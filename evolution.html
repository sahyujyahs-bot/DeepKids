~2.5 billion years

The Life Savers

The new kid, cyanobacteria, came to the rescue.

The young cyanobacteria searched within herself, unlocking an ancient secret — the power to breathe life into a dying world as a by-product of photosynthesis, mother of all inventions.

For the very first time, organisms started breaking water and learned to use the power of the sun. With each bubble of oxygen she fought back the silent invaders, paving the way for a world teeming with life.

Most books hand children "plants make oxygen." This one hands them the moment it started, and who did it.

~3.8 billion years

The First Cell — All Our Great Grandmother

The first cell, our great grandmother, the prokaryote, was born when the RNA chains took shelter in bubbles of fat floating in the ocean.

She was an enigma — the great grandmother for all life. She lived, learned and built. She built the proteins by stitching amino acids into a chain. Proteins became the commandos and she, the queen.

She could already sense and respond to the environment. She could move toward food and away from toxins. She grew tails that spinned like propellers to move. She even learned to crawl. All while also splitting, copying, multiplying.

This was no accident. This was ingenuity, wrapped in a cell wall. And still, just the beginning.

Verbatim from the spread, right down to "spinned".

~4 billion years

Thought Predates Neurons

Form has always followed function in evolution, and the mind is no exception.

One of the great delusions — even science still clings to it — is that intelligence, thought and awareness began with "higher" animals, or with neurons and the brain. But thought has been moulding life all along.

Now go back four billion years, to her: the great-grandmother cell, a prokaryote. She sensed her surroundings, made decisions, took action. She built life from scratch — none of it without thought. Same biochemistry, same ion flow, same protein triggers and gene flips. Just without the neurons, without the super-fast tunnels — through diffusion across the cell instead.

Thinking, strategising, planning — from the prokaryotes to you. Common to all life.
