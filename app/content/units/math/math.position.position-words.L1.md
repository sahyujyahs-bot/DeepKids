# Where is it? Position words
`math.position.position-words.L1`

**Summary.** A book is on your left. You walk to the other side of the room, and the same book is on your right. Did the book move? You will hide a lemon, lay a banana leaf, count steps and play a shelf game to find out how we say where things are. You will learn top and bottom, above and below, inside and outside, near and far, left and right, in front, behind and between. You will also find out which of these words change when you move, and which stay the same.

**Intro.** *The cat is on the bottom shelf, on the left. Which shape is hiding the cat?* The green triangle is hiding the cat. You needed two clues to find her. 'Bottom' told you which shelf. 'Left' told you which end. Words like these are called position words. They tell where a thing is. In this unit you will use many of them, and test which ones change when you move.

## Learning outcomes
0. I can say where a thing is with top, bottom, on, under, above and below.
1. I can say if a thing is inside or outside, and if it is near or far.
2. I can show my left hand and my right hand, and say what is on my left and on my right.
3. I can say what is in front of, behind, next to and between other things.
4. I can put a thing in its place when I hear position words, and tell someone else where to put it.
5. I can say which position words change when I move, and which stay the same.

## Terms
- **Position**: The place where a thing is. *e.g.* The position of the tumbler is on the stool.
- **Top and bottom**: The top is the highest part of a thing. The bottom is the lowest part. *e.g.* The lid is at the top of the dabba. The last murukku is at the bottom.
- **Above and below**: Above means higher than another thing. Below means lower than another thing. *e.g.* The fan is above the bed. The mat is below the bed.
- **On and under**: On means resting on top of a thing and touching it. Under means below a thing, covered by it. *e.g.* The plate is on the table. The dog sleeps under the table.
- **Inside and outside**: Inside means within the edges or walls of a thing. Outside means not within them. *e.g.* The rice is inside the pot. The spoon lies outside the pot.
- **Near and far**: Near means a short way off. Far means a long way off. *e.g.* From your bed, the pillow is near and the bus stop is far.
- **Left and right**: The two sides of your body. Most people eat with the right hand. The left is the other side. *e.g.* Tie a thread on your right wrist. The hand with no thread is your left hand.
- **In front of and behind**: In front of you is the side your face looks at. Behind you is the side your back is on. *e.g.* In the bus queue, Meena is in front of you. Your sister is behind you.
- **Between**: In the middle, with one thing on each side. *e.g.* On the plate, the sambar is between the rice and the poriyal.
- **Next to**: Beside a thing, with nothing in the way. *e.g.* Your left chappal is next to your right chappal.

## Explanations
### A position word needs two things · *defined*
**Definition:** A position word tells where one thing is, compared with another thing.

Where is the lemon? 'Under' is not an answer, because under what? You must name a second thing: under the stool. Here is a worked example. Where is the square in the row? It is between the circle and the triangle. You named two other shapes to say it. Now say where the triangle is.
- e.g. The spoon is inside the tumbler.
- e.g. The tumbler is on the stool.
- e.g. The kolam is in front of the door.
### Top, bottom, above, below, on, under · *defined*
**Definition:** Top, above and on are for the higher place. Bottom, below and under are for the lower place.

Look at the three shelves. The circle is on the top shelf, the square is in the middle, and the triangle is at the bottom. So the circle is above the square, and the triangle is below the square. Here is a worked example. What is below the circle? The square and the triangle are both lower, so both are below the circle. 'On' and 'under' are for things that touch or are very close: a cup on a table, a cat under the cot.
- e.g. The clock is above the door.
- e.g. Your feet are below your knees.
- e.g. The last idli is at the bottom of the stack.
### Inside and outside · *defined*
**Definition:** A thing is inside when it is within an edge or a wall. It is outside when it is not.

A box has walls. A kolam has a border line. A ring of thread on the floor has an edge. Each of these has an inside and an outside. Here is a worked example. In the picture, two counters are inside the ring and three are outside. To check, ask: would the counter have to cross the edge to get out? If yes, the counter is inside.
- e.g. The seeds are inside the lemon. The peel is on the outside.
- e.g. You are inside the house. The crow is outside.
- e.g. One foot inside the ring and one foot outside: you are on the edge.
### Near and far · *defined*
**Definition:** Near means few steps away. Far means many steps away. You must say near to what.

Here is a worked example. From the door, the mat is 2 steps away and the bed is 9 steps away. 2 is fewer than 9, so the mat is nearer the door. Now stand at the window, where the bed is only 1 step away. Now the bed is the near one. The bed did not move. You did. So always say where you are counting from.
- e.g. From your house, the shop is near and the sea is far.
- e.g. For a child in Kanyakumari, the sea is near.
- e.g. The Moon is far from us. The Sun is much farther.
### Left and right go with you · *defined*
**Definition:** Left and right are the two sides of your own body. When you turn, they turn with you.

Your right hand is always your right hand. But the things on your right change when you turn. Here is a worked example. You face a red circle and a blue square, and the circle is on your left. Your friend stands on the other side and faces you. For her, the circle is on the right. Both of you are correct. So when you say left or right, say whose: 'on my left' or 'on your left'.
- e.g. Two people facing each other shake right hands. The arms cross over.
- e.g. 'The third house on the left' depends on which end of the street you enter.
- e.g. On a bus, your left stays your left wherever the bus turns.
### In front, behind, between, next to · *defined*
**Definition:** In front is the way a person or a line is facing. Behind is the other way. Between is in the middle of two.

Look at the row. It is a queue, and everyone faces right. The red circle is first, so nobody is in front of her. The blue circle is behind her. The yellow circle is between the blue and the green. Here is a worked example. Who is next to the green circle? Only the yellow circle, because nobody stands on green's other side.
- e.g. In a queue for the bus, the person in front of you gets on before you.
- e.g. Your nose is between your eyes.
- e.g. A chair has a front of its own, the side you face when you sit. Behind the chair is the side of its back rest.
### Which words change when you move? · *defined*
**Definition:** Some position words are about two things only. Others are about the two things and you.

'The tumbler is on the stool' is about the tumbler and the stool. You are not in the sentence. So you can walk anywhere and it stays true. 'The book is on my left' has you in it. When you walk to the other side, it stops being true. 'The stool is near me' has you in it too. Walk away, and near becomes far.
- e.g. On, under and inside stay true when you move.
- e.g. Left and right swap when you go to the other side.
- e.g. Near turns into far when you walk away.
### Down is toward the middle of the Earth · *defined*
**Definition:** Down is the way things fall: toward the ground, and so toward the middle of the Earth.

Drop a lemon, and the lemon falls down. The Earth pulls everything toward its middle, and we call that way down. The Earth is a ball, and people live all round the ball. A child in Chennai and a child in Peru, nearly on the opposite side, both stand with feet toward the middle. So 'down' for one child points a different way from 'down' for the other. Nobody is upside down. Inside the space station, astronauts float, and they have to agree which wall to call the floor.
- e.g. The top of a coconut tree is the end farthest from the ground.
- e.g. Water in a tumbler always settles at the bottom.
- e.g. A root grows down, toward the middle of the Earth.
### Everyone does maths: the bee's dance and the root · *defined*
**Definition:** Many living beings sense positions. Honeybees also tell each other how far and which way.

A honeybee finds flowers and flies home. Inside the dark hive she dances for her sisters. She runs in a straight line and waggles her body. A longer waggle means the flowers are farther away. The way her run points tells the others which way to fly, compared with the Sun. Karl von Frisch worked this out and won a Nobel Prize in 1973. Plants sense positions too. A root senses which way is down and grows that way, even in the dark.
- e.g. A short waggle run: flowers near the hive. A long one: flowers far away.
- e.g. A run that points straight up on the comb means: fly toward the Sun.
- e.g. Lay a sprouting seed sideways. The tip of the root bends to grow down again.
### People agree on left and right, and some do without them · *defined*
**Definition:** Which side to keep to, and even whether to use the words left and right, are things people chose.

On Indian roads, vehicles keep to the left. In many other countries they keep to the right. Neither side is better. What matters is that everyone on the same road agrees. Words are chosen too. The Guugu Yimithirr people of Australia mostly say north, south, east and west where we would say left and right. A speaker may say, 'The cup is to the east of the plate.' That sentence stays true whichever way you face.
- e.g. India, Sri Lanka and Japan keep left. France and the United States keep right.
- e.g. The narrow tip of a banana leaf is often placed on the eater's left. Families chose that.
- e.g. 'The pot is by the east wall' works for everyone in the room.

## Mostly agreed
- To say where a thing is, you have to name another thing. *How we know:* Try it. Say where your nose is without naming anything else. You will say 'between my eyes' or 'above my mouth'. There is no other way.
- When two people face each other, one person's left is the other person's right. *How we know:* Anyone can test it. Tie a thread on both right wrists and stand face to face. The two threads are on opposite sides.
- Down is toward the middle of the Earth, so it points a different way in different places. *How we know:* People live all round the Earth and nobody falls off. In 1522 the ship Victoria came back to Spain after sailing right round the world.
- Even a child of three or four can work out what another person can and cannot see. *How we know:* In 1975 a scientist named Martin Hughes asked small children to hide a doll from two toy policemen. About nine in ten gave the correct answer.

## How we found out
**Aryabhata, and the sailors of the ship Victoria, 499 CE and 1522.** Long ago, many people thought that down was one direction for the whole world, like the floor of one giant room. So if the Earth were a ball, the people on the far side would hang upside down and fall off. About 1,700 years ago a Roman writer named Lactantius laughed at the round Earth for this reason. He asked who could believe in people whose feet are higher than their heads. In India, the mathematician Aryabhata saw it another way. In his book of 499 CE he compared the Earth to the round head of a kadamba flower, which is covered with tiny blossoms on every side. In the same way, he wrote, the round Earth is covered on every side by the living beings of land and water. Later, sailors settled it. In 1522 the ship Victoria came home to Spain after sailing right round the world. Nobody on the far side had fallen off. Down is not one direction. Down is toward the middle of the Earth, wherever you stand.  
*What people believed before:* Down is the same direction everywhere, so people on the far side of a round Earth would fall off. Voyages round the world showed this was wrong. People live on every side of the Earth, and each stands with feet toward the middle.

## Challenging perspectives
- **My right is the same side as your right, always.** (Many children, and grown-ups in a hurry; *rejected*)  
  For: It is true when two people stand side by side and face the same way. Then both right hands are on the same side.  
  Against: When they face each other, the right hands are on opposite sides. The test Face to Face shows it with two threads.
- **Children below seven can only think about what they themselves see.** (Jean Piaget and Bärbel Inhelder, two Swiss scientists, in the 1940s and 1950s; *debated*)  
  For: They sat children in front of a model of three mountains, with a doll on another side. Asked what the doll could see, many young children picked their own view.  
  Against: In 1975 Martin Hughes used a simpler game: hide a doll from toy policemen. Most three and four year olds could do it. The mountain test may have been too strange for them.
- **All people think of the world as left and right.** (Many people who speak languages that have these words; *debated*)  
  For: Every human body has two sides, and most languages have words for them.  
  Against: Guugu Yimithirr speakers in Australia mostly use north, south, east and west for where things are. Scientists still argue about how much this changes the way they think.

## Roots
We said where things are: on, under, inside, near, left and right. Now let us ask what a place really is.

**Deep truth.** In the Turn-Around Test, the book was on your left. You walked to the other side, and the same book was on your right. The book did not move. So left and right are not in the book. They are in the one who looks.  
*Habit it breaks:* We say 'the book is on the left' as if the left were a part of the room. But a room has no left side until someone stands in it and faces one way.  
*Evidence:* In the Turn-Around Test, 'on', 'under' and 'inside' stayed true wherever you stood. 'Left', 'right' and 'near' changed as soon as you moved.

- *who am i:* You are the middle of your own left and right, your own front and back. Wherever you go, they go with you.
- *what is life:* Every living being knows where things are. A bee dances to tell her sisters how far the flowers are. A root grows down, even in the dark.
- *what is this world:* A room has no left side and no near side by itself. It gets them when someone stands in it and looks.

Where is the lemon? You cannot say it with the lemon alone. You need a second thing: under the stool. Every position word joins one thing to another thing, or to you. So nothing has a place all by itself, only a place among the other things.

- **Isha Upanishad 5:** The Isha Upanishad speaks of the One that is in everything. It says: it moves, and it does not move. It is far, and it is near. It is inside all this, and it is outside all this. (Far and near sound like opposites. But in the Step Count the same bed was far from the door and near to the window. Both words were true of one bed.)
- **Udana 6.4 (Tittha Sutta), paraphrased:** The Buddha told of men who had been blind from birth. Each was led to a different part of one elephant. The man at the head said an elephant is like a water pot. The man at the ear said an elephant is like a winnowing basket. They began to fight. (Where each man stood decided what he found. In the Turn-Around Test, where you stood decided what was on your left. Each view was true from its own place.)
- Maths (settled): Speakers of Guugu Yimithirr, a language of Australia, mostly do not use left and right to say where things are. They use words like north, south, east and west (John Haviland, 1998, and Stephen Levinson, 1997). So people can live well without the words left and right.
- Maths (open): Is space a real thing by itself, or only our way of saying how things sit beside each other? Scientists and thinkers still argue about this.
- Other view, Isaac Newton, an English scientist, in his book of 1687: Newton said that space is real by itself. It is like one huge, still room that holds everything. Each thing has its own true place in that room, even if nobody is looking and nothing else is near. On this view, 'left' and 'near' are only our words. The book's place is a fact, with or without us.
- Practice, Where am I? (3 min): Sit still. Name one thing above you and one thing below you. Name one thing in front, one behind, one on your left and one on your right. Now close your eyes and point to the door. Open your eyes and check. Turn to face the other way. Close your eyes and point to the door again. Did the door move, or did you?
- Ask: If nobody were in the room, would the book still be on the left?
- Ask: When you turn around, does the world turn, or do you?
- Ask: The bed is near the window and far from the door. So is the bed near or far?
- Ask: Is there anything that is nowhere?

## Deep and big questions
- *deep:* You turn around, and the book goes from your left to your right. What really changed? (hint: Did the book move? Did the room move? Did you?)
- *deep:* Can you say where something is without naming any other thing? (hint: Try with your nose, the Moon, or the lemon under the stool.)
- *deep:* Which words work the same for everyone in the room, wherever they stand? (hint: Think of on, under and inside. Think of left and near.)
- *big:* Is there a top of the world? Who decided? (hint: Globes and maps usually put north at the top. The Earth floats in space with no ground under it.)
- *big:* An ant walks under the table top, upside down. For them, which way is up? (hint: The ant's feet hold the table. The Earth still pulls the ant down.)
- *big:* If you were the only thing there was, could you be anywhere? (hint: Near what? Above what? On whose left?)

## Story: The Third House on the Left
*Nila, her father, her aunt and Tank Street are made up. Buses in Madurai really are sent by other roads on the days of the big temple chariot festival.*

On Saturday morning Nila helped her father get ready. Appa could never do the button on his own right cuff.

"Give me your right hand," said Nila. She stood facing him. He held out his hand, and she reached for it with her left.

"That's funny," she said. "Your right hand is on my left side."

"Hurry up, kanna," said Appa. "We will miss the bus."

They were going to Madurai to see Meena Athai's new house for the first time. Athai had told them the way on the phone the night before. Appa had said it three times so that he would not forget. "Get down at the flower market. Walk into Tank Street. The third house on the left. Blue gate."

Nila said it too, in her head, all the way on the bus. Third house on the left. Blue gate.

But that day was the day of the temple chariot. The big road was closed, and the bus went round by small roads. It did not go to the flower market at all. It stopped beside the old temple tank, and the conductor called, "Last stop! Everybody down!"

Appa took out his phone to call Athai. The screen was black. He pressed the button. He pressed it again.

"No charge," he said. "Never mind. Look." He pointed to a board on the corner. It said TANK STREET. "We are here anyway. Come."

Tank Street was long and straight. At the far end Nila could see heaps of orange and white, and people carrying baskets. In between there were houses on both sides, each with a gate.

"Third house on the left," said Appa, and he counted. "One. Two. Three."

The third house on the left had a brown gate. A grandfather sat on the step, reading his newspaper.

"Is this Meenakshi's house?" asked Appa.

"No Meenakshi here," said the grandfather. "I have lived in this house for forty years."

Appa scratched his head. He counted again from the corner. One, two, three. It was still the brown gate.

"Maybe she said the fourth house," he said. The fourth house had a green gate, and a dog who did not want visitors. "Maybe there are two Tank Streets," said Appa. He looked hot and worried.

Nila was not counting houses. She was looking at the far end of the street, at the orange and white heaps. They were flowers. Marigolds and jasmine.

"Appa," she said. "Where is the flower market?"

"Down there, at the other end. Why?"

"Athai thought we would come in from the flower market. So she would walk up this street from that end. Like this." Nila turned around, so that her back was to the flowers. Now she was facing the way Athai would face.

"When I stand like Athai, my left hand is on that side." She pointed across the road. "But we came in from the tank. We are looking the other way. Athai's left is our right. Like your cuff this morning."

Appa looked at his right hand. He looked at the two sides of the street. He looked at Nila.

"And her third house is not our third house," said Nila. "She counts from the flower end. So we must count from there too."

They did not have to walk all the way to the market. Nila looked along the right side of the street to the last house before the flowers. She counted back toward herself. "One. Two. Three."

The third gate from the far end was blue.

They walked to it. Before Appa could knock, the door opened. Meena Athai stood there with flour on her hands.

"There you are! I rang and rang. Did you get lost? I told you, third on the left."

"It was the third on the left," said Nila. "Your left. Not ours."

Athai laughed. "Next time I will say: the blue gate, on the same side as the neem tree."

"That is better," said Nila. "The neem tree stays in one place."

**For the parent:** For the parent: 'left' is not a fact about the street. It belongs to the person who is walking, and to the way she is facing. Athai's direction had a hidden starting point, the flower market. When Nila and her father came in from the other end, left became right, and the third house had to be counted from the far end. Nila solves it by turning her body to face the way her aunt would face.

- Ask: Appa counted three houses on the left. Why was that the wrong house?
- Ask: Why did Nila turn around in the middle of the street?
- Ask: Athai said she would use the neem tree next time. Why is that a better clue than 'on the left'?
- Ask: How would you tell a friend the way to your door, so that it works from both ends of your street?

## Games
### g1 · Three in a Line (10 min)
- **rules:** Each player draws a shelf of 9 boxes, 3 rows of 3, and takes 9 beans.; On your turn, roll the dice twice. The first roll gives the row. The second roll gives the place in that row.; Say the box aloud, such as 'bottom row, on the left'. Then put a bean in that box on your own shelf.; If that box already has a bean, say the box aloud anyway. Your turn ends.; If you say the wrong box, another player may correct you. Then you put the bean in the correct box.; The first player with three beans in a line wins. The line can go across, down, or from corner to corner.; Playing alone: count how many turns you need to make a line. Play again and compare.
- **Printable board: A shelf for each player: draw 9 boxes in 3 rows, with these names:** top left; top middle; top right; middle left; middle middle; middle right; bottom left; bottom middle; bottom right
- **Printable score sheet: The dice key:** First roll gives the row. 1 or 2 = top. 3 or 4 = middle. 5 or 6 = bottom.; Second roll gives the place. 1 or 2 = left. 3 or 4 = middle. 5 or 6 = right.
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Each player draws 9 boxes, in 3 rows of 3. 2. Point to the top, middle and bottom rows. 3. Point to the left, middle and right box in a row. 4. Read the rules aloud.  
  *Parent says:* This is your shelf. Show me the top row. Show me the box on the right of the bottom row.  
  *Child mode:* Draw 9 boxes, 3 in each row. Find the top row. Find the left side.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Show the dice key. 2. Play one practice turn each. 3. Say the box aloud before the bean goes down.  
  *Parent says:* I rolled 5, then 2. Five means the bottom row. Two means the left. So I say: bottom row, on the left.  
  *Child mode:* Roll twice. The first roll is the row. The second is the place. Say the box aloud.
  - Check: You roll 1, then 6. Where does your bean go? → **Top row, on the right**
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play the game, taking turns. 2. Now and then, ask a player which box they still need for a line. 3. Ask them to name it with position words.  
  *Parent says:* Which box do you need now to make a line? Tell me its row and its place.  
  *Child mode:* Play in turns. Say each box aloud. Try to get three in a line.
  - Ask: You have top left and bottom right. Which box would finish that line? (good: The middle one.; Middle row, in the middle.)
- **s4 (1 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. When someone has three in a line, the game ends. 2. The winner says where the three beans are. 3. Count the empty boxes on each shelf.  
  *Parent says:* Tell us your three boxes. How many of your boxes are still empty?  
  *Child mode:* Three in a line wins. Say where your three beans are.
### g2 · Amma Says (10 min)
- **rules:** Tie a thread on every player's right wrist. Put a chair in the middle. Lay a ring of thread or a dupatta on the floor.; Each player starts with 5 beans. Shuffle the 16 cards. One person is the caller.; The caller takes a card and reads it. Sometimes the caller says 'Amma says' first, and sometimes not.; If the caller said 'Amma says', do the action at once. If not, stay still.; If you use the wrong side, go to the wrong place, or move when you should stay still, give up one bean.; The game ends when all 16 cards are used. The player with the most beans wins.; Playing alone: turn over a card, do the action, then check with your thread. Count how many of the 16 you got correct.
- **Printable cards: 16 action cards to cut out, or to copy on slips of paper:** Right hand on your head; Left hand on your left knee; Right hand on your left ear; Left hand above your head; Both hands behind your back; Right hand under your chin; Left hand on your right shoulder; Left hand below your right hand; Hop on your left foot; Stand behind the chair; Stand in front of the chair; Stand between the chair and the ring; Put one foot inside the ring; Jump outside the ring; Go far from the chair; Come near the chair
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Tie a thread on each right wrist. 2. Put the chair in the middle and the ring on the floor. 3. Give each player 5 beans. 4. Read the rules aloud.  
  *Parent says:* Show me your right hand. Show me your left foot. Now stand inside the ring. Now outside.  
  *Child mode:* Tie the thread on your right wrist. Show your right hand. Show your left foot.
- **s2 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play three practice cards with no beans lost. 2. For one of them, leave out 'Amma says'. 3. Show that everyone must stay still for that one.  
  *Parent says:* Amma says: right hand on your left ear. Good. Now: hop on your left foot. Did I say 'Amma says'?  
  *Child mode:* Do the action only after 'Amma says'. If you hear no 'Amma says', stay still.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play through the 16 cards. 2. Call slowly at first, then faster. 3. Let your child be the caller for the last few cards.  
  *Parent says:* Amma says: stand between the chair and the ring. Amma says: left hand on your right shoulder.  
  *Child mode:* Listen for 'Amma says'. Then do it fast. Use the correct side.
  - Note: Cards that cross the body, such as 'right hand on your left ear', are the hardest. Slow down for those.
  - Check: Amma says: stand behind the chair. Where do you go? → **To the side of the chair's back rest**
- **s4 (1 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. When the cards are finished, count each player's beans. 2. Ask which card was the hardest and why.  
  *Parent says:* Count your beans. Which card tricked you the most? Why was that one hard?  
  *Child mode:* Count your beans. Most beans wins. Which card was the hardest?

## Experiments
### e1 · The Turn-Around Test: which words change when you move? (25 min)
- **question:** If I move to a new spot, which sentences about position stay true, and which stop being true?
- **predict:** Before each move, put a bean on every sentence that you think will stop being true. Why do you think those ones will change?
- **fair test:** Nobody touches the stool or the things around it. Only the child moves. We check all six sentences after each move, in the same order.
- **safety:** Keep beans, seeds and other small things away from babies and toddlers, who could choke on them.
- **record:** A grown-up writes the six sentences in a table. After each move, put a tick if the sentence is still true and a cross if it is not. At the end the child says what they found, and the grown-up writes it under the table.
- **results table:** Sentence | My guess: will it change? | From the other side | From far away
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put the stool in the middle of the room. 2. Put the tumbler on it and the spoon in the tumbler. 3. Put the lemon under the stool. 4. Your child stands one step away, facing the stool. 5. Tie the thread on their right wrist. 6. Put the book on the floor on their left and the plate on their right.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the stool. Tell me where each thing is. Where is the tumbler? Where is the lemon? Where is the book?  
  *Child mode:* Stand one step from the stool. Say where each thing is.
  - Ask: Where is the spoon? (good: In the tumbler.; Inside the tumbler.)
  - Ask: Which side is the book on? (good: My left.; The side with no thread.)
  - Note: Write the six sentences from the chart on paper as your child says them. If left and right are shaky, point to the thread: the thread hand is the right hand.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Read the six sentences aloud. 2. Say that your child will soon walk to the other side of the stool. 3. Ask them to put a bean on each sentence that will stop being true. 4. Ask why. Do not say if they are correct.  
  *Parent says:* Soon you will walk to the other side and look back at the stool. Which sentences will stop being true? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* You will walk to the other side. Which sentences will change? Put a bean on each.
  - Ask: Why did you put a bean on that one? (good: Because I will see it from the back.; Because everything will be the other way.; The book will be on my other side.)
  - Note: Many six-year-olds put beans on 'on' or 'under' too, or on nothing at all. Accept every guess that comes with a reason.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child walks round to the other side of the stool and faces it. 2. Read each sentence. 3. Your child looks and says true or not true. 4. Write a tick or a cross for each.  
  *Parent says:* Now look. Is the tumbler still on the stool? Is the book still on your left? Check with your thread.  
  *Child mode:* Walk to the other side. Face the stool. Check each sentence. True or not true?
  - Ask: The book did not move. Why is it not on your left now? (good: Because I moved.; I am facing the other way.; My left is on that side now.)
  - Note: On, under and inside stay true. The book is now on the right and the plate on the left. The stool is still near.
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child comes back to the first spot. 2. Say they will now walk ten big steps away and turn to face the stool. 3. They guess with beans again and say why. 4. They walk, turn and check each sentence. 5. Write ticks and crosses.  
  *Parent says:* Now you will walk ten big steps away. Which sentences will stop being true this time? Why?  
  *Child mode:* Guess with beans again. Walk ten big steps away. Turn and check each sentence.
  - Ask: Which sentence changed this time? (good: The stool is not near now.; It is far.; Only the near one.)
  - Note: In a small room, go as far as you can, even into the next room. This time only 'near' changes. Left and right stay as they were at the first spot.
- **s5 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child stands at the first spot again. 2. Ask what will happen to 'on' and 'under' if they look at the stool upside down. 3. They bend over and look back between their legs.  
  *Parent says:* Bend over and look at the stool between your legs. Is the tumbler under the stool now? Why not?  
  *Child mode:* Bend over. Look at the stool between your legs. Is the tumbler still on the stool?
  - Note: The picture looks upside down, but the tumbler still rests on the stool. 'On' and 'under' go by which way things fall, not by which way your head is.
- **s6 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at the table together. 2. Ask your child to say which words stayed and which changed. 3. Ask why. 4. Write their words under the table.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the ticks and crosses. Which words stayed true every time? Which words changed? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Look at your table. Which words stayed true? Which words changed? Say why.
  - Ask: What do you think now? What did you see? Why is it so? (good: On, under and inside did not change.; Left and right changed when I went to the other side.; Near changed when I went far.; Those ones are about me. When I move, they change.)
  - Note: A good answer in your child's own words: some words are about the things only, and some are about me too.
  - Check: A cup is on a table, on your left. You walk to the other side of the table. What is true now? → **The cup is on the table, on my right**

## Activities
### a1 · Find the Lemon: clues with position words (10 min)
- **problem:** Can you find a hidden lemon from words alone? Can you give clues so good that nobody needs you to point?
- **record:** After the game, the child draws one hiding place and a grown-up writes the clue under it.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child closes their eyes. 2. Hide the lemon inside or under something. 3. Give two clues with position words. 4. Keep your hands behind your back.  
  *Parent says:* Open your eyes. The lemon is inside something. That thing is on the bottom shelf.  
  *Child mode:* Close your eyes. Then listen to the clues. Find the lemon.
  - Note: Good first clues use two position words, such as 'inside the pot, under the table'. Choose a place that is safe to reach.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Swap. You close your eyes and your child hides the lemon. 2. Your child gives the clues. 3. If they point, put their hands behind their back and ask for words.  
  *Parent says:* Now you hide the lemon. Tell me where it is with words only. No pointing.  
  *Child mode:* Now you hide the lemon. Give clues with words. Keep your hands behind you.
  - Ask: You said 'there'. Where is there? (good: Under the cot.; Behind the door.; Next to the pot.)
  - Note: Children often say 'there' or 'that side'. Each time, ask 'Which thing is it near? Is it on, under or inside?'
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Hide the lemon once more. 2. Give a broken clue: say only 'It is under.' 3. Wait for your child to ask 'Under what?' 4. Then give the full clue.  
  *Parent says:* Here is my clue. The lemon is under. Can you find it now? What else do you need to know?  
  *Child mode:* Listen to this clue: 'It is under.' Is that enough? What must you ask?
  - Ask: What was missing from my clue? (good: Under what?; You did not say the thing.; The name of the other thing.)
  - Check: Which clue tells you where the lemon is? → **It is under the blue stool.**
### a2 · Face to Face: where did my right hand go? (10 min)
- **problem:** When my friend faces me, is her right hand on the same side as mine?
- **record:** Draw two stick people facing each other. Colour both right hands red.
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Tie a thread on your right wrist and one on your child's. 2. Stand side by side, facing the same wall. 3. Both raise the thread hand.  
  *Parent says:* This is our right hand. Raise it. Are our right hands on the same side?  
  *Child mode:* Stand next to your grown-up. Raise your thread hand. Same side?
  - Note: Side by side, both right hands are toward the same wall.
- **s2 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Hands down. 2. Say that you will now turn to face each other. 3. Ask for a guess and a reason before anyone turns.  
  *Parent says:* Now we will face each other. Will our thread hands be on the same side? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Soon you will face each other. Will the thread hands be on the same side? Guess.
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: Yes, because right is right.; No, because you will turn round.; I am not sure.)
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Turn to face each other. 2. Both raise the thread hand. 3. Let your child look. 4. Then both shake right hands.  
  *Parent says:* Raise your thread hand. Look at mine. Are they on the same side now? What happened?  
  *Child mode:* Face your grown-up. Raise your thread hand. Look where the other thread is.
  - Ask: Why are the threads on opposite sides now? (good: Because you turned round.; We are looking at each other.; Your right is on my left.)
  - Note: Facing each other, your right hand is across from your child's left hand. When you shake right hands, the arms cross.
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stay face to face. 2. Call out: touch my right hand with your left hand. 3. Then: touch my left knee. 4. Take turns to call. 5. Go slowly.  
  *Parent says:* Touch my right hand with your left hand. Look for my thread before you move.  
  *Child mode:* Touch the hand your grown-up calls. Look for the thread first.
  - Check: Your friend stands facing you. Where is her right hand? → **Across from my left hand**
### a3 · Maths in life: lay the leaf for a meal (15 min)
- **problem:** Can you put each food in its place on the leaf, just by hearing where it goes?
- **safety:** A grown-up serves everything hot, such as rice, sambar and rasam. The child serves only the cool things.
- **record:** After the meal, draw the leaf from above. Draw each food in its place.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Sit where a person will eat. 2. Lay one leaf or plate in front of you. 3. On a leaf, find the narrow tip and the line down the middle. 4. Show the top half, far from you, and the bottom half, near you.  
  *Parent says:* Sit here like the person who will eat. Which half of the leaf is near you? Which half is far?  
  *Child mode:* Sit where someone will eat. Find the top half and the bottom half of the leaf.
  - Note: In many Tamil and Kerala homes the narrow tip is on the eater's left. Small foods go on the top half and rice on the bottom half. Families differ, so teach your own way.
- **s2 (4 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stay seated on the eater's side. 2. Call each place aloud, one at a time. 3. Your child puts the cool food there. 4. Use your family's places.  
  *Parent says:* Put the salt at the top, on the left. Put the pickle next to the salt, on its right.  
  *Child mode:* Listen for the place. Put each food there. Say the place aloud.
  - Ask: Where did you put the banana? Say it in words. (good: At the top, on the left.; Next to the salt.; Above the rice place.)
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Now your child stands across the leaf, as a server does. 2. Ask where the eater's left is from here. 3. Your child lays the next leaf from the server's side. 4. Sit down and check it.  
  *Parent says:* Now stand across from me, like someone serving. The salt goes on my left. Which side is that for you?  
  *Child mode:* Stand across from the eater. The salt goes on her left. Which side is that for you?
  - Ask: From where you stand, is my left on your left or on your right? (good: On my right.; The other side.; Your left is my right.)
  - Note: This is the finding from Face to Face, used for real. If the salt lands on the wrong side, sit your child in the eater's place to look.
  - Check: You stand facing a person who is eating. The salt goes on her left. Which side is that for you? → **My right**
- **s4 (3 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lay the other leaves or plates the same way. 2. Put a tumbler of water outside each leaf. 3. At the meal, ask each person to say where one food is.  
  *Parent says:* Every leaf should look the same to the person who sits at it. Check each one from their place.  
  *Child mode:* Lay the other leaves the same way. Put a tumbler outside each leaf.
### a4 · Draw What I Say (10 min)
- **problem:** Can you make a picture from words alone? Can you tell someone a picture so well that theirs matches yours?
- **record:** Keep both drawings. Write the five instructions on the back of one.
- **s1 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Give your child paper and a pencil. 2. Tie the thread on their right wrist if it helps. 3. Read the five instructions slowly, one at a time. 4. Wait for each drawing before the next.  
  *Parent says:* Draw a sun at the top, in the middle. Draw a pot at the bottom, on the left. Draw a tree on the right. Draw a bird above the tree. Draw a fish inside the pot.  
  *Child mode:* Listen and draw. Put each thing where the words say.
  - Note: Do not correct during the drawing. The picture in the chart shows roughly where each thing should end up.
- **s2 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at the drawing together. 2. Read each instruction again. 3. Your child points to the thing and says where it is.  
  *Parent says:* Where is the bird? Tell me in words. What is below the bird?  
  *Child mode:* Point to each thing. Say where it is on your paper.
  - Ask: What is at the bottom, on the left? (good: The pot.; The pot with the fish inside.)
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Swap. Your child thinks of a picture with four things. 2. They tell you where to draw each one. 3. Draw exactly what they say. 4. Make one mistake on purpose for them to catch.  
  *Parent says:* Now you tell me what to draw, and where. I will draw only what your words say.  
  *Child mode:* Now you say what to draw and where. Check your grown-up's picture for a mistake.
  - Ask: What did I get wrong? (good: I said above, and you drew it below.; The ball should be on the right.; It should be inside the box.)
  - Check: You hear: 'Draw a bird above the tree.' Where does the bird go? → **Higher than the tree**
### a5 · The Step Count: near to what? (12 min)
- **problem:** Which thing in the room is the nearest? Does the answer change if you stand somewhere else?
- **record:** Make two lists: 'From the door' and 'From the window'. In each list write the four things with their steps, nearest first.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stand at the door with your child. 2. Pick four things in the room. 3. Ask which is nearest to the door and which is farthest. 4. Write the guesses.  
  *Parent says:* From the door, which of these four is the nearest? Which is the farthest? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Stand at the door. Which thing is nearest? Which is farthest? Guess.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child walks from the door to each thing, with steps of the same size. 2. They count aloud. 3. Write the number of steps next to each thing. 4. Together, order the four from fewest steps to most.  
  *Parent says:* Walk to the shelf and count your steps. Keep your steps the same size each time.  
  *Child mode:* Walk from the door to each thing. Count your steps. Write the number.
  - Ask: Which thing took the fewest steps? So which is nearest? (good: The chair, 2 steps.; The one with the smallest number.)
  - Note: Heel-to-toe steps keep the size the same. Fewer steps means nearer.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Now stand at the window, or at the far wall. 2. Ask for a new guess: nearest and farthest from here. 3. Count the steps again. 4. Compare the two lists.  
  *Parent says:* Now we start from the window. Will the nearest thing be the same one? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Now stand at the window. Guess again. Then count your steps again.
  - Ask: The bed was far before. Now the bed is near. Did the bed move? (good: No, I moved.; It is near the window and far from the door.; It depends where I stand.)
  - Check: The shop is 3 steps from the gate and 9 steps from the well. Is the shop near or far? → **Near the gate, and farther from the well**
### a6 · Everyone does maths: dance like a honeybee (10 min)
- **problem:** Can you say 'near' or 'far', and which way, with a dance alone?
- **record:** Draw a short wavy line and write 'near'. Draw a long wavy line and write 'far'.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Tell your child how a honeybee tells her sisters where flowers are. 2. Show a short waggle walk of 2 steps and a long one of 8 steps. 3. Agree: the walk points straight at the hiding place.  
  *Parent says:* A honeybee has no words. She waggles. A long waggle means far. A short waggle means near.  
  *Child mode:* A bee waggles to tell where flowers are. Long waggle: far. Short waggle: near.
  - Note: This is simpler than the real dance. A real bee dances on the wall of the comb, in the dark. Her angle from straight up shows the angle from the Sun.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Close your eyes. Your child hides the sweet near or far. 2. They come back to the middle of the room. 3. They waggle toward the hiding place, long or short. 4. You go and look.  
  *Parent says:* No words, little bee. Dance for me. I will fly where your dance tells me.  
  *Child mode:* Hide the sweet. Come back. Waggle toward it. Use no words.
  - Ask: After I find it: how did your dance tell me it was far? (good: I waggled for a long time.; I took many steps.)
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Swap. You hide the sweet and dance. 2. Your child reads your dance and finds it. 3. Ask what the dance told them and what it did not.  
  *Parent says:* Now I am the bee. Watch my waggle. Is it near or far? Which way?  
  *Child mode:* Watch your grown-up waggle. Near or far? Which way? Go and look.
  - Ask: What did my dance leave out? (good: If it was under or inside something.; How high it was.; Exactly where.)
  - Check: A honeybee makes a very long waggle run. What is she telling her sisters? → **The flowers are far away**

## Projects
### p1 · Everyone does maths: ask a seed which way is down (25 min, 5 days)
- **problem:** A seed has no eyes and sits in the dark. If we turn the seed, will the root still find the way down?
- **safety:** Keep beans, seeds and other small things away from babies and toddlers, who could choke on them. Do not eat these seeds after the test.
- **record:** Each day from day 3, draw the tumbler and one seed with the root. Add an arrow to show which way the root tip points.
- **schedule:** day 1: Soak 8 moong seeds in water overnight. (5 min); day 2: Line the tumbler with wet tissue. Tuck the seeds between the tissue and the glass. (5 min); day 3: Look for the white roots. Draw which way they point. (5 min); day 4: Guess what the roots will do. Then lay the tumbler on its side. (5 min); day 5: Look at the root tips. Draw which way they point now. (5 min)
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Count 8 moong seeds into a cup. 2. Cover them with water. 3. Leave them overnight.  
  *Parent says:* These seeds are alive and resting. Water will wake them. Where do you think the root will come out?  
  *Child mode:* Count 8 seeds into a cup. Cover them with water. Leave them for the night.
  - Wait: Leave the seeds in water overnight.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Line the inside of the tumbler with wet tissue. 2. Tuck 6 soaked seeds between the tissue and the glass, halfway up. 3. Turn the seeds different ways. 4. Keep the tissue wet every day.  
  *Parent says:* Put each seed where we can see them through the glass. Turn some sideways and some upside down.  
  *Child mode:* Tuck the seeds between the wet tissue and the glass. Turn them different ways.
  - Note: Two seeds are spare. Keep the tumbler out of strong sun and add a spoon of water when the tissue dries.
  - Wait: Look again tomorrow.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at the seeds through the glass. 2. Find the white roots. 3. Ask which way each root points. 4. Your child draws one seed with an arrow.  
  *Parent says:* The seeds were turned in different ways. Which way did each root go? What do you notice?  
  *Child mode:* Find the white roots. Which way does each one point? Draw one.
  - Ask: The seeds faced different ways. Did the roots go different ways? (good: No, all of them go down.; Some started sideways and then went down.)
  - Note: If no root shows yet, wait one more day. In cool weather sprouting is slower.
  - Wait: Look again tomorrow.
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask what the roots will do if the tumbler lies on its side. 2. Ask for a reason. 3. Lay the tumbler on its side on a plate. 4. Do not turn it again.  
  *Parent says:* Now the roots will point sideways. What will they do by tomorrow? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess what the roots will do. Then lay the tumbler on its side.
  - Ask: What is your guess, and why? (good: They will keep going sideways, because that is how they are now.; They will turn down, because roots go down.; I do not know.)
  - Wait: Leave the tumbler on its side until tomorrow.
- **s5 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look closely at the tip of each root. 2. Your child draws one root with an arrow at the tip. 3. Ask what they think, what they saw, and why.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the tips. Which way do they point now? What do you think the root can sense?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the root tips. Which way do they point now? Draw one.
  - Ask: What did you find out about roots? (good: The tips turned down again.; The root knows which way is down.; Even lying sideways, the seed finds down.)
  - Note: The tips usually bend down within a day. If the bend is small, leave the tumbler one more day. A root senses the pull of the Earth and grows toward it.
  - Check: A sprouting seed is laid sideways. Which way does the tip of the root grow next? → **Down, toward the ground**

## Challenges
### c1 · Stretch: back to back, build the same thing (20 min)
- **problem:** Your partner cannot see what you built. Tell them so clearly that their five things end up in exactly the same places as yours.
- **your turn:** Now invent your own way of telling. Test it with a new build. If the two builds do not match, find the word that went wrong and change your way.
- **constraints:** You sit back to back. Nobody may turn round or peek until the end.; Words only. No pointing, and no touching the other person's things.; The builder may say each instruction twice. The listener may ask questions.
- **general:** Say each thing and where it goes compared with the plate: 'the tumbler is on the plate'.; Use left and right: 'the spoon is on the left of the plate'.; Go slowly, one thing at a time, and ask the listener to say it back.
- **creative:** Before you start, agree on something both of you can find, such as the window. Then say 'the spoon is on the window side of the plate'.; Name the four sides of the newspaper first: the side near you, the far side, the window side and the door side.; Fold each newspaper into four boxes and number them 1 to 4, in the same way. Then say which box each thing goes in.
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Sit back to back, each with a newspaper and the five things. 2. Your child builds something with all five. 3. They tell you how to build it. 4. Do exactly what the words say. 5. Turn round and compare.  
  *Parent says:* Build something with your five things. Then tell me how to build the same. I cannot see yours.  
  *Child mode:* Build with your five things. Tell your grown-up how to build the same.
  - Note: Follow the words exactly, even when you can guess what was meant. If your child says 'on the left', use your own left. The first builds often do not match.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at both builds. 2. Find each thing that is in a different place. 3. Ask which word caused it. 4. Read the usual ways and the creative ways aloud if your child wants ideas.  
  *Parent says:* Which things are not in the same place? Which word went wrong? How could we say it better?  
  *Child mode:* Find what does not match. Which word went wrong? Think of a better way to say it.
  - Ask: You said 'left'. We sit back to back. Is your left the same side as my left? (good: No. We face different ways.; Your left is toward the door and mine is toward the window.)
- **s3 (7 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Agree on your child's new way of telling. 2. Sit back to back again. 3. This time you build and tell, using their way. 4. Compare. 5. Then swap once more.  
  *Parent says:* We will use your new way. This time I build and you listen. Then we look.  
  *Child mode:* Use your new way. Your grown-up builds and tells. You build the same.
  - Check: Two people sit back to back. Which clue means the same side for both of them? → **On the window side**
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask your child to explain their way to someone else at home. 2. Ask if it would work over the phone, with a cousin in another town.  
  *Parent says:* Would your way work on the phone, with someone in another house? What would you have to change?  
  *Child mode:* Tell someone how your way works. Would it work over the phone?
  - Ask: Your cousin's house has no window on that side. What could you both use? (good: The side near you and the far side.; Numbers on the paper.; North and south, if we both know them.)

## Worksheets
### Where Is It? (core) (core, 12 questions)
1. (mcq) Which shape is on the top shelf, on the left?  
   Options: Blue square / Yellow circle / Green triangle  
   **Answer:** Blue square. *Why:* Top tells you the shelf. Left tells you the end. The blue square is there. The yellow circle is on the left too, but on the bottom shelf.
2. (fill) Look at the row. The shape between the circle and the triangle is a ____.  
   **Answer:** ['square', 'blue square']. *Why:* The square has the circle on one side and the triangle on the other. That is what between means.
3. (truefalse) In the picture, the red circle is above the blue square.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. The red circle is on the higher shelf, so it is above the blue square.
4. (mcq) A lemon is in a closed tiffin box. Where is the lemon?  
   Options: Inside the box / Outside the box / On top of the box  
   **Answer:** Inside the box. *Why:* The lemon is within the walls and the lid, so the lemon is inside. To get out, the lemon would have to cross the edge.
5. (sort) You stand at your front door. Sort each thing: near or far?  
   **Answer:** the doormat → Near; the Moon → Far; your chappals → Near; the Sun → Far; the door handle → Near; the stars → Far. *Why:* You can touch the doormat, the chappals and the handle from the door. The Moon, the Sun and the stars are a very long way off.
6. (match) Match each position word to its opposite.  
   **Answer:** top → bottom; inside → outside; near → far; left → right; above → below. *Why:* Each pair names two opposite places: the highest and the lowest, within and not within, a short way and a long way, the two sides, higher and lower.
7. (mcq) Which shape is at the right end of the row?  
   Options: Green triangle / Blue square / Red rectangle  
   **Answer:** Red rectangle. *Why:* The red rectangle is the last shape on the right. The green triangle is at the left end.
8. (fill) Count the shapes on the bottom shelf. There are ____ shapes.  
   **Answer:** ['4', 'four']. *Why:* Bottom means the lowest shelf. Touch each shape there once: 1, 2, 3, 4.
9. (truefalse) A cup is on a table. You walk round the table. The cup is still on the table.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. 'On' is about the cup and the table only. You moved, but they did not.
10. (draw) Draw a table. Draw a ball under the table and a cup on the table.  
   **Answer:** A table with a cup resting on its top and a ball on the floor below it.. *Why:* A strong drawing has the cup touching the top of the table, and the ball lower than the table top, between its legs.
11. (draw) Draw a box. Draw 3 dots inside the box and 2 dots outside the box.  
   **Answer:** A closed box shape with 3 dots within its lines and 2 dots beyond them.. *Why:* To check, ask of each dot: would it have to cross a line to get out? The 3 inside dots would. The 2 outside dots would not.
12. (write) Say your answer aloud. A grown-up writes it. Look around you. What is above your head? What is below your feet?  
   **Answer:** For example: the fan is above my head, and the floor is below my feet.. *Why:* A strong answer names a real thing that is higher than the head, and a real thing that is lower than the feet.
### Left, Right and In Between (stretch) (stretch, 12 questions)
1. (mcq) Which shape is second from the left?  
   Options: Red circle / Yellow circle / Green triangle  
   **Answer:** Red circle. *Why:* Start at the left end and count: the green triangle is first, the red circle is second. The yellow circle is second from the right.
2. (fill) How many shapes are on the right of the blue square? ____  
   **Answer:** ['2', 'two']. *Why:* After the blue square, going right, come the yellow circle and the red rectangle. That is 2 shapes.
3. (truefalse) You and a friend face each other. Your right hand is across from your friend's right hand.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Your friend has turned to face you, so your right hand is across from your friend's left hand.
4. (order) Put these in order, from nearest the door to farthest from the door.  
   **Answer:** ['mat: 1 step', 'chair: 4 steps', 'bed: 7 steps', 'window: 10 steps']. *Why:* Fewer steps means nearer. 1 is the fewest, then 4, then 7, then 10.
5. (mcq) Lakshmi is in a queue. 2 children are in front of her and 2 are behind. How many children in all?  
   Options: 4 / 5 / 2  
   **Answer:** 5. *Why:* 2 in front, 2 behind, and Lakshmi herself: 2 and 1 and 2 make 5. Picking 4 leaves Lakshmi out.
6. (sort) You walk to the other side of a table. Sort each word: stays true, or can change?  
   **Answer:** on → Stays true; under → Stays true; inside → Stays true; on my left → Can change; on my right → Can change. *Why:* On, under and inside are about the things only. Left and right are about you too, so they swap when you face the other way.
7. (fill) Look at the shelf. The green triangle is in the ____ row.  
   **Answer:** ['middle']. *Why:* The green triangle has one row above and one row below. That row is the middle row.
8. (mcq) On roads in India, which side do vehicles keep to?  
   Options: The left / The right / The middle  
   **Answer:** The left. *Why:* In India everyone keeps to the left. People agreed on one side so that vehicles do not meet head on. Many other countries chose the right.
9. (draw) Draw a row of three shapes: a triangle on the left, a circle on the right, a square between them.  
   **Answer:** Triangle, square, circle, from left to right.. *Why:* A strong drawing has the square in the middle, with the triangle at the left end and the circle at the right end.
10. (write) Say your answer aloud. A grown-up writes it. The book went from your left to your right. Why?  
   **Answer:** Because I walked to the other side and faced the other way. The book did not move.. *Why:* A strong answer says that the child moved, not the book. Left and right go with the person.
11. (perspective) A tumbler and a plate sit on a mat. Two children face each other across the mat.  
   **Answer:** A strong answer says that each child is correct from her own side, and offers a clue that works for both, such as 'the tumbler is on the window side'.. *Why:* The children face opposite ways, so one child's left is the other child's right. A clue that uses a fixed thing, such as the window, is the same for both.
12. (draw) Draw round both your hands on paper. Colour the right hand red.  
   **Answer:** Two hand outlines. The outline of the right hand is red.. *Why:* To check, put your hands back on the outlines. The red outline should be under the hand with the thread, your right hand.
### Position Puzzles (challenge) (challenge, 12 questions)
1. (mcq) Which shape is below the red circle and on the left of the blue square?  
   Options: Green triangle / Blue circle / Yellow rectangle  
   **Answer:** Green triangle. *Why:* Below the red circle are the green triangle and the yellow rectangle. On the left of the blue square are the green triangle and the blue circle. Only the green triangle fits both clues.
2. (fill) Six children stand in a row. Nila is 3rd from the left. From the right she is number ____.  
   **Answer:** ['4', 'four', '4th', 'fourth']. *Why:* This one goes past Class 1. Count from the right end: 1, 2, 3, and Nila is the 4th. Three children are on her right and two are on her left.
3. (truefalse) Down points the same way for a child in Chennai and a child on the far side of the Earth.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Down is toward the middle of the Earth. The two children stand on opposite sides of a ball, so their 'down' arrows point toward each other.
4. (mcq) You face a mirror and raise your right hand. Where is the raised hand in the mirror?  
   Options: On the same side as my right hand / On the side of my left hand / In the middle  
   **Answer:** On the same side as my right hand. *Why:* In a mirror, the raised hand is straight across from your raised hand, on the same side. A friend facing you is different: her right hand is across from your left.
5. (order) The pot is below the plate. The lid is above the plate. Put them in order, top first.  
   **Answer:** ['lid', 'plate', 'pot']. *Why:* The lid is above the plate, so the lid is at the top. The pot is below the plate, so the pot is at the bottom.
6. (match) Match each thing to the place where you find it.  
   **Answer:** the roof → at the top of a house; the roots → under the ground; the yolk → inside an egg; the peel → on the outside of a banana. *Why:* A roof covers the top. Roots grow down into the soil. The yolk is within the shell. The peel is the outer cover.
7. (fill) Count the shapes that are NOT in the middle row. There are ____ shapes.  
   **Answer:** ['6', 'six']. *Why:* The top row has 3 shapes and the bottom row has 3 shapes. 3 and 3 make 6.
8. (sort) Sort each one: above the ground or below the ground?  
   **Answer:** a kite → Above the ground; a carrot → Below the ground; a cloud → Above the ground; water in a well → Below the ground; a crow's nest → Above the ground; an earthworm's tunnel → Below the ground. *Why:* A kite, a cloud and a nest are higher than the ground. A carrot, well water and a worm's tunnel are lower than the ground.
9. (mcq) Which shape is in the bottom right corner?  
   Options: Green circle / Red square / Green square  
   **Answer:** Green circle. *Why:* Bottom tells you the lowest row. Right tells you the end. The green circle is there. The red square is at the bottom left, and the green square is at the top right.
10. (write) Say your answer aloud. A grown-up writes it. Tell where your toothbrush is, without pointing. Use two position words.  
   **Answer:** For example: my toothbrush is inside the blue mug, on the shelf above the tap.. *Why:* A strong answer uses two position words and names the other things, so that a visitor could find the toothbrush.
11. (draw) Draw 9 boxes, in 3 rows of 3. Draw a star in the top right box.  
   **Answer:** A 3 by 3 grid with a star in the last box of the first row.. *Why:* Top chooses the row. Right chooses the place in that row.
12. (perspective) Is the Moon near or far?  
   **Answer:** A strong answer picks a side with a reason, and sees that near and far depend on what you compare with.. *Why:* Next to a trip to the shop, the Moon is very far. Next to the Sun, the Moon is near. Near and far always need something to compare with.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) Which shape is on the bottom shelf, in the middle?  
   Options: Red circle / Red triangle / Yellow circle  
   **Answer:** Red triangle. *Why:* Bottom tells you the shelf. Middle tells you the place. The red triangle is there. The red circle is in the middle too, but on the top shelf.
2. (truefalse) A lemon is under a stool. You walk to the other side of the stool. The lemon is still under the stool.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. 'Under' is about the lemon and the stool. Neither of them moved.
3. (mcq) A coin is in your closed fist. Where is the coin?  
   Options: Outside your hand / Inside your hand / Above your hand  
   **Answer:** Inside your hand. *Why:* Your fingers close round the coin like walls. The coin is inside your hand.
4. (mcq) The well is 3 steps from the door. The gate is 8 steps from the door. Which is nearer the door?  
   Options: The well / The gate / Both the same  
   **Answer:** The well. *Why:* Fewer steps means nearer. 3 is fewer than 8, so the well is nearer the door.
5. (mcq) A thread is on your right hand. You turn around. Which hand has the thread now?  
   Options: My left hand / My right hand / Neither hand  
   **Answer:** My right hand. *Why:* Your right hand is always your right hand. It turns with you. Only the things around you change sides.
6. (fill) How many shapes are on the left of the square? ____  
   **Answer:** ['3', 'three']. *Why:* Going left from the square you meet 3 shapes: a circle, a triangle and another circle.
7. (mcq) Which shape is between the two circles?  
   Options: The triangle / The square / The rectangle  
   **Answer:** The triangle. *Why:* The triangle has a circle on each side. The square and the rectangle are at the two ends.
8. (mcq) Meena is first in the bus queue. Where are all the other people?  
   Options: In front of her / Behind her / Above her  
   **Answer:** Behind her. *Why:* The first person has nobody in front. Everyone else in the queue stands behind her.
9. (mcq) Put the spoon inside the tumbler, and the tumbler on the plate. What is at the bottom?  
   Options: The spoon / The tumbler / The plate  
   **Answer:** The plate. *Why:* The tumbler rests on the plate, so the plate is lowest. The spoon is inside the tumbler, above the plate.
10. (mcq) 'Put a bean in the top row, on the right.' Which shape does your bean cover?  
   Options: Yellow triangle / Green square / Green circle  
   **Answer:** Green square. *Why:* The top row is the highest row. Its right end is the green square. The green circle is on the right too, but in the bottom row.
11. (mcq) Which word can stop being true when you walk to the other side of a table?  
   Options: on / under / on my left  
   **Answer:** on my left. *Why:* 'On my left' has you in it. When you face the other way, the thing is on your right. On and under are about the things only.
12. (truefalse) Two friends face each other. A book on one friend's left is on the other friend's right.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. They face opposite ways, so one friend's left side is the other friend's right side.

## Applications
- A postal address says where a house is: the door number, the street, the town.
- A bus conductor tells you where to get down: 'the stop after the temple'.
- A cook follows position words: 'put the lid on the pot', 'keep the curd inside the fridge'.
- A cricket captain sets the field by telling each fielder where to stand: near the bat, or far out by the rope.
- A dance teacher calls 'right foot in front, left hand above your head', and the whole class moves together.
- A doctor asks 'where does it hurt?' and you answer with position words: 'below my knee, on the left'.
- A tailor sews the pocket on the left side of a shirt, above the waist.
- Drivers keep to the left on Indian roads, so that vehicles going opposite ways do not meet.

## Spotted around us
- The kitchen shelf. What is on the top shelf? What is at the bottom? Why are the heavy vessels kept low?
- A kolam at a doorstep. It is drawn outside the house, in front of the door. Find the dot in the middle.
- A banana leaf at a wedding meal. Look where the salt, the pickle and the rice are put. Is it the same on every leaf?
- A road in your town. Watch which side the buses and autos keep to. Is it your left when you walk the other way?
- Everyone does maths: honeybees. A bee who finds flowers dances in the hive. A longer waggle tells her sisters that the flowers are farther away.
- Everyone does maths: a sprouting seed. However the seed lies, the root grows down and the shoot grows up.
- A house gecko on the ceiling. They walk upside down, with their feet above and their back below.
- The Moon in the evening sky. Is it above the neem tree or below the top of the tree? Look again an hour later.

## Open questions
- **Is space a real thing by itself, or only a way of saying how things sit beside each other?** Newton said space is real by itself. Leibniz, who lived at the same time, said it is only the order of things. Scientists and thinkers still argue.
- **How do birds who fly across the world know which way to go?** Scientists know that many birds use the Sun, the stars and the Earth's magnetism. How a bird's body senses that magnetism is still not settled.
- **Does the universe have a middle or an edge?** Nobody has found a middle or an edge. Nobody knows if space goes on for ever.
- **Do people who speak with north and south think about places differently from people who speak with left and right?** Some tests suggest they do, and other scientists read the same tests differently. The argument is still going on.

## Connected maths
- Look at the shelf. Go to the row below the top row. How many shapes are in that row? **It is the middle row, and 3 shapes are in that row.**. The row below the top row is the middle row. Count the shapes there: 1, 2, 3. (queues `math.number-sense.counting.L1`)
- From the door, the bed is 6 steps away and the shelf is 9 steps away. Which is nearer the door? How many steps nearer? **The bed is nearer, by 3 steps.**. 6 is fewer than 9, so the bed is nearer. From 6, count on to 9: 7, 8, 9. That is 3 steps. (queues `math.number-sense.comparing-ordering.L1`)
- Five children stand in a row. Meena is in the middle. How many children are on her left? How many are on her right? **2 on her left and 2 on her right.**. Take Meena out of the 5, and 4 are left. The middle has the same number on each side. 2 and 2 make 4. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L1`)
- Kavya and Farhan face each other across a mat. Kavya sees the plate on the left and the tumbler on the right. What does Farhan see on his left? **Farhan sees the tumbler on his left.**. Farhan faces the other way. So Kavya's right is Farhan's left. The tumbler is on Kavya's right, so it is on Farhan's left. (queues `math.position.directions-and-grids.L2`)
- In a game of Three in a Line, your shelf has 9 boxes. The top row is full, and one more bean is in the bottom row. How many boxes are still empty? **5 boxes are empty.**. The top row holds 3 beans and the bottom row holds 1. That is 4 beans. 9 take away 4 is 5. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L1`)
