# My day: what comes before, what comes after
`math.time.daily-time.L1`

**Summary.** You can look out of the window and tell that it is morning. Can you look out and tell that it is Tuesday? You will put your own day in order with cards, and test which things can swap places. You will learn the four parts of a day, the words before and after, and the seven days of the week. You will also find out which parts of time come from the Sun, and which names people chose.

**Intro.** *Three skies from one day are mixed up. Which one comes first after you wake up: A, B or C?* C comes first. The Sun comes up in the morning. Then comes A, when the Sun is high. Then comes B, when it is dark and the stars are out. So the order is C, A, B. What comes after B? C comes again, because the next morning comes. A day has an order, and the order goes round and round. In this unit you will put your own day in order.

## Learning outcomes
0. I can tell morning, afternoon, evening and night apart, and say one thing I do in each.
1. I can put the things I do in a day in order, using first, next, then and last.
2. I can say what comes before and what comes after something in my day.
3. I can say the seven days of the week in order, and the day before and after any day.
4. I can use yesterday, today and tomorrow correctly.
5. I can say which parts of time come from the Sun, and which names people chose.

## Terms
- **Day**: The bright time when the Sun is up. A day can also mean one whole day and night together. *e.g.* We played all day. There are seven days in a week.
- **Morning**: The first part of the day. It starts when the Sun comes up. *e.g.* In the morning you wake up, brush your teeth and eat breakfast.
- **Afternoon**: The part that starts in the middle of the day, around lunch time. The Sun is high and it is hot. *e.g.* In the afternoon you eat lunch and rest.
- **Evening**: The part of the day when the Sun goes down and the light fades. *e.g.* In the evening you play outside, and the lamps are lit.
- **Night**: The dark part, after the Sun has gone down and before it comes up again. *e.g.* At night you eat dinner, hear a story and sleep.
- **Before**: Earlier than. It comes first. *e.g.* You wash your hands before you eat.
- **After**: Later than. It comes next. *e.g.* You wash your plate after you eat.
- **Order**: What comes first, what comes next and what comes last. *e.g.* The order of a bath is: pour water, rub soap, pour water again, dry.
- **Week**: Seven days in a row. People gave each of the seven days a name. *e.g.* Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday make one week.
- **Today**: This day, the one you are in now. *e.g.* Today I am drawing my day cards.
- **Yesterday**: The day before today. It is over. *e.g.* Yesterday we ate dosa. That was one sleep ago.
- **Tomorrow**: The day after today. It has not come yet. *e.g.* Tomorrow we will go to the market. That is one sleep away.

## Explanations
### The four parts of a day · *defined*
**Definition:** A day has four parts. They always come in the same order: morning, afternoon, evening, night.

Morning starts when the Sun comes up. Afternoon starts in the middle of the day, around lunch time, when the Sun is high. Evening is when the Sun goes down. Night is the dark part. After night, morning comes again. Here is a worked example. Kavya eats lunch, and the Sun is high. So it is afternoon. Which part comes next? Evening comes next. Now you try: you are in bed and the stars are out. Which part is it, and which part comes next? The four parts are not the same length. Night is the longest part. You sleep through most of it. People also do not all agree on where one part stops. Is 4 o'clock afternoon or evening? Families say it differently.
- e.g. Morning: the Sun is low, crows call, and you brush your teeth.
- e.g. Afternoon: the Sun is high, your shadow is short, and you eat lunch.
- e.g. Evening: the sky turns orange and lamps are lit.
- e.g. Night: it is dark, the stars are out, and you sleep.
### Before and after · *defined*
**Definition:** Before means earlier. After means later.

Put three things in a row: brush your teeth, eat breakfast, wash your plate. Here is a worked example. Look at 'eat breakfast' in the middle. What comes before it? Brush your teeth. What comes after it? Wash your plate. Now look again. Eating breakfast is after brushing. It is also before washing the plate. So one thing can be before and after at the same time. It depends on what you compare it with. Now you try: what do you do just before you sleep? What do you do just after you wake up?
- e.g. You put on socks before shoes.
- e.g. You dry yourself after a bath.
- e.g. Lunch is after breakfast and before dinner.
### First, next, then, last · *defined*
**Definition:** Putting things in order means saying what happens first, what happens next, and what happens last.

Here is a worked example. Meenakshi gets ready for a bath. First she fills the bucket. Next she pours water. Then she rubs soap. Last she dries herself. Some orders cannot change. You cannot dry yourself before you pour the water. You cannot brush your teeth before you wake up. Other orders are chosen by people. Some families bathe before breakfast. Some bathe after breakfast. Both days work. Now you try: tell how you eat a banana, with first, next and last.
- e.g. First peel the banana. Next eat it. Last put the peel in the bin.
- e.g. First cut the lemon. Next squeeze it. You cannot squeeze first.
- e.g. Bath then breakfast, or breakfast then bath. Both work.
### The seven days of the week · *defined*
**Definition:** A week is seven days in a row. Each day has a name, and the names always come in the same order.

The days are Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday. After Saturday, Sunday comes again. So the week goes round like a ring. Here is a worked example. Today is Thursday. Which day comes after it? Say the days in order: Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. Friday comes after Thursday. Which day comes before it? Wednesday. Now you try: which day comes after Saturday? In Tamil the days are named after the Sun, the Moon and five planets. Sunday is Nyayiru, which means the Sun. Monday is Thingal, which means the Moon. Many calendars in India start the week on Sunday. In some countries the calendar starts the week on Monday. Both have the same seven days in the same order.
- e.g. After Monday comes Tuesday.
- e.g. Before Sunday comes Saturday.
- e.g. Seven days after a Friday, it is Friday again.
### Yesterday, today and tomorrow · *defined*
**Definition:** Today is the day you are in. Yesterday is the day before today. Tomorrow is the day after today.

Yesterday is one sleep back. Tomorrow is one sleep ahead. Here is a worked example. Today is Wednesday. Yesterday is the day before, so yesterday was Tuesday. Tomorrow is the day after, so tomorrow will be Thursday. Now you try: today is Saturday. What was yesterday? What will tomorrow be? These three words move. When you wake up tomorrow, you will call that day today. The day you are in now will be called yesterday.
- e.g. Today is Monday. Yesterday was Sunday. Tomorrow is Tuesday.
- e.g. The dosa you ate one sleep ago was yesterday's dosa.
- e.g. Your birthday is tomorrow. That means one more sleep.
### Why morning and night come · *defined*
**Definition:** The Earth is a big ball that turns round. It turns once in one whole day and night.

The Sun shines all the time. It lights up one half of the Earth. The other half is dark. The Earth turns, and you turn with it. When your place turns towards the Sun, it is morning for you. When your place faces the Sun fully, it is the middle of the day. When your place turns away, it is evening, and then night. So the Sun does not go anywhere at night. Your side of the Earth has turned away from it. This also means that it is not morning everywhere at once. When children in Chennai eat breakfast at 8 in the morning, children in Mexico are getting ready for bed.
- e.g. Hold a ball near a lamp. One half is lit and one half is dark.
- e.g. Turn the ball slowly. A dot on it goes into the light and out again.
- e.g. You can call a cousin far away and ask: is it day or night where you are?
### Who chose seven days? · *defined*
**Definition:** A week is a number of days that people agreed to count together. Today almost everyone counts in sevens.

A day comes from the Earth turning. Nobody chose that. A week is different. Nothing in the sky goes round in exactly seven days. People chose seven a very long time ago, and nobody is sure why. Other people chose other numbers. Long ago in the city of Rome, the market came every 8 days. In France, in the year 1793, the rulers made a week of 10 days. People there had to work nine days before a rest day. After some years France went back to seven days. People also chose where a day starts. Clocks and phones start a new day in the middle of the night. In the Indian calendar called the panchangam, the day starts at sunrise. In the Jewish and Islamic calendars, the day starts in the evening.
- e.g. A week of 7 days: Sunday to Saturday.
- e.g. A market week of 8 days in old Rome.
- e.g. A week of 10 days in France, from 1793.
### Other beings keep time too · *defined*
**Definition:** Many living beings keep the time of day inside their bodies, without any clock.

A honeybee remembers the time of day when she found food. In 1929 a scientist, Ingeborg Beling, put out sugar water at the same time each day. The bees learnt the time. The next day they came at that time, even when the dish was empty. A rooster crows before the Sun comes up. In 2013 scientists in Japan kept roosters in dim light all day and night. The roosters still crowed about once a day, at their own morning. A tamarind tree folds their small leaves at night and opens them in the day. The four o'clock flower opens late in the afternoon. All of them keep the parts of the day. As far as anyone knows, none of them keeps a week of seven days.
- e.g. Crows in a town fly to their sleeping trees before dark.
- e.g. A hibiscus flower opens in the morning and closes by evening.
- e.g. A touch-me-not plant folds their leaves at night.
- **Many scientists:** They call this a body clock. They describe it as chemicals inside each cell that rise and fall once a day.
- **Another way to say it:** The bee knows what time it is and chooses when to fly. Both ways describe the same thing that bees do.

## Mostly agreed
- The Earth turns round once in a day. That is why morning comes, then night, then morning again. *How we know:* In 1851 Léon Foucault hung a heavy ball on a long wire in Paris. As it swung, the line of its swing slowly turned over the floor. The floor was turning with the Earth.
- It is not morning everywhere at once. When it is morning in India, it is night on the other side of the Earth. *How we know:* You can test it with a phone call to someone far away. When it is 8 in the morning in Chennai, it is 8:30 at night in Mexico City.
- The Sun does not make a week. People chose to count days in sevens. *How we know:* Nothing in the sky goes round in exactly seven days. People have also used weeks of 8 days and 10 days.
- Bees know the time of day without a clock. *How we know:* In 1929 Ingeborg Beling fed bees at the same time each day. The next day the bees came at that time, even though the dish was empty.

## How we found out
**Aryabhata, and much later Léon Foucault, The year 499, and 1851.** For a very long time, almost everyone thought the Earth stood still. They thought the Sun went round the Earth once a day, and that this made morning and night. It looks that way from your window. About 1,500 years ago an Indian mathematician, Aryabhata, said something different. He wrote a book called the Aryabhatiya. He says in it that he was 23 years old in the year 499. In this book he said that the Earth turns. He gave an example. A person in a moving boat sees the trees on the bank go backward. In the same way, he said, the stars only seem to move west. Most people then did not agree with him. They said the sky turned. In 1851 a French scientist, Léon Foucault, hung a heavy ball on a long wire in Paris and let it swing. Through the day, the line of the swing slowly turned over the floor. The floor was turning under the ball, because the Earth turns. Now anyone could see it.  
*What people believed before:* The Earth stands still, and the Sun goes round it once a day. Aryabhata said the Earth turns. Foucault's swinging ball showed it.

## Challenging perspectives
- **The Sun goes round the Earth once every day.** (Almost everyone, for thousands of years; *rejected*)  
  For: It looks that way. The Sun comes up on one side, crosses the sky and goes down on the other side. Nobody notices the Earth moving.  
  Against: Aryabhata wrote in the year 499 that the Earth turns. In 1851 Foucault's swinging ball showed the ground turning under it.
- **The week has seven days because of the Moon.** (Some people who study calendars; *debated*)  
  For: The Moon takes about seven days to grow from a half Moon to a full Moon. People long ago watched the Moon closely.  
  Against: It takes a little more than seven days, so a week does not stay in step with the Moon. And other peoples used 8 or 10 days.
- **A new day starts at 12 o'clock at night, for everyone.** (Clocks, phones and most countries; *accepted*)  
  For: Clocks and phones all over the world change the date at 12 o'clock at night. It helps that everyone uses the same rule.  
  Against: It is a rule people agreed on. The panchangam starts the day at sunrise. The Jewish and Islamic calendars start it in the evening.

## Roots
We put our day in order and went round the week. Now let us ask who made morning, and who made Monday.

**Deep truth.** In the Window Test, the sky showed you that it was morning. It did not show you that it was Monday. The Sun makes morning and night. People made Monday. Long ago, people chose to count days in sevens and gave each day a name.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think Monday is just as real as morning. But morning comes from the Sun. Monday comes from people agreeing with each other.  
*Evidence:* In the Window Test you could tell the part of the day from the light. For the name of the day, you had to ask a person or look at a calendar. And people have used weeks of 8 days and 10 days.

- *who am i:* Your body knows night and morning with no clock. You get sleepy when it is dark, and you wake when it is light.
- *what is life:* Bees, roosters and tamarind trees keep the time of day too. None of them needs a name for the day.
- *what is this world:* The Earth keeps turning, so it is always morning somewhere. When you wake up in India, children in Mexico are going to bed.

The Earth turns round and round, and it does not stop. That one turning gives morning, afternoon, evening and night. People cut the turning into pieces and gave the pieces names, like Monday and week. The turning is one thing. The pieces and the names are ours.

- **Aitareya Brahmana 3.44:** This old Indian text says the Sun never really sets or rises. When people think the Sun is setting, it is making night here and day on the other side. (The Sun is always shining. Night comes to us when our side of the Earth turns away from the Sun.)
- **Thirukkural 481:** Thiruvalluvar, the Tamil poet, says a crow can beat an owl in the daytime. So a king who wants to win must choose the right time. (The day belongs to the crow and the night to the owl. Each part of the day is the right time for someone.)
- **Ecclesiastes 3:1:** This book of the Bible says that for everything there is a season, and a time for every purpose under heaven. (Your day has an order too. There is a time to wake, a time to eat, a time to play and a time to sleep.)
- Maths (settled): In 1729 a French scientist, Jean-Jacques de Mairan, kept a plant in a dark cupboard. Most people think the plant was a touch-me-not. Their leaves still opened by day and folded at night. So living beings keep the time of day inside themselves.
- Maths (open): Nobody is sure why people first chose seven days for a week. There are several ideas, and none is proved.
- Other view, Many people who study calendars: They say Monday is real too. The day we call Monday really comes, and the Sun really rises on it. Only the name is chosen. It is like naming a dog Raja. The dog is real, whatever name you give. Some of them also say seven was not just made up. The Moon takes about seven days to grow from a half Moon to a full Moon.
- Practice, One day in one minute (3 min): Curl up small on the floor. This is night. Now breathe in slowly and stretch your arms up, like the Sun coming up. This is morning. Hold your arms high. This is afternoon. Breathe out and bring your arms down slowly. This is evening. Curl up again. This is night. Do it once more with your eyes closed. Could you feel where morning stopped and afternoon began?
- Ask: Does a crow know it is Sunday? How could you find out?
- Ask: If everyone forgot the names of the days, would the days still come?
- Ask: Where does evening stop and night begin? Who decides?
- Ask: You slept all night. How do you know that time went by?

## Story: The Day Without a Name
*Yazhini, Arul and Ammamma are made up. Weekly markets, called sandhai, that come on the same day every week are real in Tamil Nadu. So are wall calendars with one sheet to tear off for each day.*

Yazhini was six, and it was the summer holidays. She was staying at Ammamma's house in a village near Thanjavur. Her cousin Arul was staying there too. He was eight.

Before she left, Amma had said, "I will come on Friday, by the morning bus."

At home, Yazhini always knew what day it was. Monday was the day of the drawing class. Saturday was the day Appa made dosas. But here, every day was the same. They woke up. They fed the hens. They climbed the guava tree. They slept. The days had lost their names.

One morning Yazhini woke up with a question. "Ammamma, is it Friday today?"

Ammamma was grinding batter. "I have lost count, kanna. Look at the calendar."

The calendar hung by the door. It was the kind with one small sheet for each day. But nobody had torn off a sheet since the holidays began. It still showed the day the children had come.

"Look at your phone," said Yazhini.

"The phone is at the repair shop," said Ammamma.

Arul laughed. "You don't need a phone. Just look outside. The sky will tell you."

So the two of them went out to the front step and looked.

The Sun was low, just above the coconut trees. The air was cool. The crows were making a great noise. A hen walked past with her chicks behind her.

"It is morning," said Yazhini.

"I know it is morning," said Arul. "Wait." He screwed up his eyes and looked at the sky for a long time. "It looks like a Thursday."

"What does a Thursday look like?"

Arul opened his mouth and shut it again. He could not say.

Yazhini looked up too. The sky was blue, the same as yesterday. The Sun was the same Sun. The crows did not look like Friday crows or Thursday crows. They only looked like crows.

"The sky does not say," she said slowly. "The sky only says morning."

"Then nobody knows," said Arul, and he went to look for a guava.

Yazhini sat down on the step. If the Sun did not tell her the name of the day, who could?

People could. People had names for days. So she had to think about people.

Then she remembered the sandhai. That was the market where they had bought the big jackfruit. Ammamma had said something then. "The sandhai comes only on Wednesday. If we miss it, we wait a whole week."

So the market day was a Wednesday. Yazhini was sure of that. She picked up a tamarind seed from the ground and put it on the step. "Wednesday," she said. "The market."

What came after the market? They came home. They ate jackfruit until their hands were sticky. They slept.

Then came the day it rained. She and Arul made paper boats and raced them in the drain. She put down a second seed, after the first. "Thursday. The boats."

What came after the boats? They slept again. And then she woke up with her question.

She put down a third seed. "Today."

She touched the seeds one by one. "Wednesday. Thursday." Her finger stopped on the third seed. "Friday. Today is Friday!"

Arul came back with a guava. "How do you know? Maybe there were two boat days."

"There was one boat day," said Yazhini. "It rained one time. One sleep before the boats, and one sleep after."

Arul looked at the three seeds. He counted the sleeps on his fingers. Then, far away on the main road, a bus horn sounded.

They ran. Ammamma came after them, wiping her hands. The morning bus stopped under the neem tree, and Amma stepped down with her blue bag.

That afternoon Arul stood in front of the calendar. "Show me how you did it," he said.

So Yazhini showed him. They tore off one sheet for each day that had gone, and said what they did on that day. The hen day. The guava day. The market. The boats. When they stopped, the sheet on top said Friday.

"And tomorrow?" asked Yazhini.

Arul thought. "Saturday."

"And yesterday was?"

"Thursday. The boats."

That evening the crows flew home over the coconut trees, the same as every evening. Yazhini watched them go. Nobody had told the crows that it was Friday. But Yazhini knew.

**For the parent:** For the parent: the sky can tell us the part of the day, but not the name of the day. The name comes from people. So Yazhini works it out from something people do on a fixed day, the Wednesday market. Then she counts the sleeps forward, one seed for each day. That is the unit's maths: order, before and after, and counting on along the week.

- Ask: Arul looked at the sky to find the day's name. Why did that not work?
- Ask: What could Yazhini tell from the sky, and what could she not tell?
- Ask: How do you find out the name of the day at your home?
- Ask: If today is Friday, what day was two sleeps ago? How do you know?

## Games
### g1 · Fill My Day (10 min)
- **rules:** Each player has a day mat with four boxes in a row: morning, afternoon, evening, night. To make one by hand, draw four boxes and write the four words.; Shuffle the 16 cards. Put them in a pile with the backs facing down, so nobody sees the answers.; On your turn, take the top card. Read it aloud, or ask a grown-up to read it. Say which part of the day it belongs to.; Turn the card over to check. If you are right and that box on your mat is empty, put the card in the box.; If that box is already full, or you named the wrong part, put the card at the bottom of the pile.; The first player to fill all four boxes says their day aloud: 'In the morning I… In the afternoon I…' and wins.; Playing alone: count how many turns you need to fill your mat. Play again and try to need fewer.
- **Printable cards: 16 day cards to cut out. The part of the day is on the back of each card:** The Sun comes up (back: Morning); Wake up (back: Morning); Brush your teeth (back: Morning); Eat breakfast (back: Morning); Eat lunch (back: Afternoon); The Sun is high in the sky (back: Afternoon); The hottest time of the day (back: Afternoon); Rest after lunch (back: Afternoon); The Sun goes down (back: Evening); Play outside before dark (back: Evening); Lamps and lights come on (back: Evening); Birds fly home (back: Evening); Stars come out (back: Night); Eat dinner (back: Night); Bedtime story (back: Night); Sleep (back: Night)
- **Printable board: A day mat: 4 boxes in a row (one mat for each player):** 1. Morning; 2. Afternoon; 3. Evening; 4. Night
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut out the 16 cards, or write them on slips of paper. 2. Draw a day mat for each player. 3. Shuffle the cards. Put them in a pile with the answers facing down. 4. Read the rules aloud.  
  *Parent says:* Each card is something that happens in a day. You need one card for each part of the day.  
  *Child mode:* Take a day mat. Put the cards in a pile, answers facing down.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play one practice turn each. 2. Take a card and read it. 3. Ask which part of the day it belongs to, and why. 4. Turn it over to check. 5. Place it or put it back.  
  *Parent says:* This card says 'Lamps and lights come on'. Which part of the day is that? How do you know?  
  *Child mode:* Take a card. Say which part of the day it is. Say how you know.
  - Note: Sunrise and sunset are the cards children mix up most. Ask: is the day starting or ending?
  - Check: The card says 'The Sun comes up'. Which box does it go in? → **Morning**
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Shuffle again and play the game. 2. Each player says the part of the day before placing a card. 3. Now and then, ask a player which parts they still need.  
  *Parent says:* You have morning and night. Which parts do you still need? Which of them comes first?  
  *Child mode:* Play the game. Say the part of the day for every card you take.
  - Ask: You have morning and night. Which two parts are missing? (good: Afternoon and evening.; The two in the middle.)
- **s4 (1 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. When someone fills all four boxes, they read their day aloud in order. 2. Every other player reads the cards they have, in order too.  
  *Parent says:* Your mat is full. Tell me your day, from morning to night.  
  *Child mode:* Your mat is full. Say your day in order, from morning to night.
### g2 · Round the Week (10 min)
- **rules:** Lay the seven squares in a ring, in order. Each player puts a button on Sunday. Put a small heap of beans in the middle.; On your turn, roll the dice. Before you move, say the day you will land on. Count on through the days to find it.; Now move your button one square for each dot. If you land on the day you said, stay there. If not, go back to where your turn began.; Each time you land on Sunday or go past Sunday, take one bean. One bean means one whole week.; The first player to have 3 beans wins.; Playing alone: count how many rolls you need to win 3 beans. Play again and try to need fewer.
- **Printable board: 7 day squares to cut out and lay in a ring, in this order:** Sunday ★; Monday; Tuesday; Wednesday; Thursday; Friday; Saturday
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut out the seven squares, or write the days on slips of paper. 2. Lay them in a ring, in order. 3. Give each player a button. 4. Read the rules aloud.  
  *Parent says:* This is a race round the week. Every time you come round to Sunday, you win a bean.  
  *Child mode:* Lay the seven days in a ring. Put your button on Sunday.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play one practice turn each. 2. Show how to count on. 3. If you are on Friday and roll 3, touch the next three squares and say Saturday, Sunday, Monday.  
  *Parent says:* I am on Friday and I rolled 3. I count on: Saturday, Sunday, Monday. I will land on Monday.  
  *Child mode:* Roll the dice. Count on through the days. Say where you will land.
  - Note: Counting the square you are standing on as 'one' is the most common slip. The first step always lands on the next day.
  - Check: You are on Friday and you roll 3. Where do you land? → **Monday**
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play the game. 2. Each player says the day before moving. 3. Give a bean each time a button reaches or passes Sunday. 4. Now and then, ask how many days are left until Sunday.  
  *Parent says:* You are on Thursday. How many more days until Sunday? What roll do you need?  
  *Child mode:* Play the game. Say your day before you move. Take a bean at Sunday.
  - Ask: You are on Thursday. How many days until Sunday? (good: 3.; Friday, Saturday, Sunday. That is 3.)
- **s4 (1 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. When someone has 3 beans, the game ends. 2. Ask each player which day they finished on. 3. Ask which roll would bring a player back to the same day.  
  *Parent says:* Who has three beans? Which day did you finish on? Could one roll of the dice ever bring you back to the same day?  
  *Child mode:* The first to win 3 beans wins. Which day did you finish on?
  - Note: No roll of one dice brings you back to the same day. That needs seven steps, and a dice stops at six.

## Experiments
### e1 · The Day Cards Test: can the order of my day be changed? (25 min)
- **question:** If we swap two things in our day, does the day still work?
- **predict:** Before each swap, guess: will the day still work, or will it go wrong? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** We swap only two cards at a time, and they must be next to each other. After each swap we put the two cards back, so every swap starts from the same day.
- **record:** A grown-up draws the table. For each swap, write the two cards, the guess and what you found. At the end, the child says what they found, and the grown-up writes it under the table.
- **results table:** The two cards we swapped | My guess: works or goes wrong | What we found
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut a sheet of paper into 8 cards. 2. Ask your child to tell you eight things they did yesterday, from waking up to sleeping. 3. Write one or two words on each card. 4. Your child draws a small picture on each card.  
  *Parent says:* Tell me eight things you did yesterday. Start with waking up. End with going to sleep.  
  *Child mode:* Think of yesterday. Say eight things you did. Draw one on each card.
  - Note: If your child is stuck, use these eight: wake up, brush teeth, bath, breakfast, lunch, play, dinner, sleep. Stick figures are fine. The drawing should take about half a minute for each card.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Shuffle the cards. 2. Ask your child to lay them in a row from left to right, in the order of the day. 3. Your child reads the row aloud with first, next, then and last.  
  *Parent says:* These are all mixed up. Put them in the order you did them. Then read me your day.  
  *Child mode:* The cards are mixed up. Lay them in a row, in order. Read your day aloud.
  - Ask: What did you do first? What did you do last? (good: First I woke up.; Last I slept.)
  - Ask: What did you do just before lunch? What did you do just after lunch? (good: Before lunch I had a bath.; After lunch I played.)
  - Note: Do not correct the order yet. If two cards are in an order you did not expect, ask about them in the next step.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Draw the table. 2. Point at the first two cards. 3. Ask for a guess and a reason, and write the guess. 4. Swap the two cards. 5. Your child acts out the new order, or tells it. 6. Write what you found. 7. Put the cards back.  
  *Parent says:* I will swap these two cards. Will your day still work, or will it go wrong? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Look at your first two cards. Guess: can they swap? Now swap them and act it out.
  - Ask: What happened when you tried the new order? (good: I cannot brush my teeth when I am asleep.; It does not work.; It still works.)
  - Note: With 'wake up' and 'brush teeth', the swap goes wrong: nobody brushes while asleep. Accept any guess that comes with a reason. Let the acting out give the answer.
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Test four more swaps the same way. 2. You choose two pairs. Your child chooses two pairs. 3. Each time: guess and reason, swap, act it out, write, put back. 4. Try bath with breakfast. Try dinner with sleep.  
  *Parent says:* Now you choose two cards that are next to each other. Guess first. Will the day still work? Why?  
  *Child mode:* Choose two cards next to each other. Guess first. Then swap them and see.
  - Ask: Which swaps went wrong? Which swaps still worked? (good: Bath and breakfast can swap.; Dinner and sleep cannot swap.; Wake up has to be first.)
  - Note: Bath and breakfast can swap, and many families do it each way. Dinner and sleep cannot. Your child may say that a swap 'works' when you expected 'goes wrong'. Ask them to act it out. Their reason matters more than the answer.
- **s5 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Draw four boxes on the second sheet. Write morning, afternoon, evening, night. 2. Your child puts each card in its box. 3. Count the cards in each box together.  
  *Parent says:* A day has four parts. Which part does each card belong to? How do you know?  
  *Child mode:* Here are four boxes for the four parts of the day. Put each card in its box.
  - Ask: How did you know that card belongs to the morning? (good: The Sun has just come up.; I do it after I wake up.; It is before lunch.)
  - Ask: Which part of your day has the most cards? (good: Morning has the most.; Morning has 4 cards.)
  - Note: Morning usually has the most cards, because getting ready takes many small steps. If a card could go in two boxes, let your child choose and say why.
  - Check: You eat lunch and the Sun is high in the sky. Which part of the day is it? → **Afternoon**
- **s6 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lay the eight cards in a ring, in order. 2. Ask what comes after the sleep card. 3. Look at the table together. 4. Ask your child what they think, what they saw and why. 5. Write their words under the table.  
  *Parent says:* Look at our table. Which swaps went wrong? Which worked? What is different about them?  
  *Child mode:* Put your cards in a ring. What comes after sleep? Now look at your table. Which swaps worked?
  - Ask: What do you think now? What did you see? Why is it so? (good: Some cards cannot swap.; Wake up has to come before everything.; Bath and breakfast can swap, because we choose that.; After sleep, I wake up again.)
  - Note: A strong answer has two kinds of order in it. One kind cannot change. The other kind is chosen by the family. Read the card 'First, next, then, last' after this step, not before.
  - Check: Which two things can swap places, and the day still works? → **Bath and breakfast**

## Activities
### a1 · The Window Test: what can the sky tell you? (10 min)
- **problem:** Can you tell the part of the day by looking outside? Can you tell the day of the week?
- **safety:** Never look straight at the Sun. Look at where the light and the shadows fall.
- **record:** Draw two columns: 'The sky told me' and 'People told me'. Write or draw each clue in one column.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Hide the clock and the phone. 2. Stand at the window with your child. 3. Ask which part of the day it is. 4. Ask for the clues.  
  *Parent says:* Do not look at a clock. Look outside. Is it morning, afternoon, evening or night? How can you tell?  
  *Child mode:* Look out of the window. Which part of the day is it? Find three clues.
  - Ask: What clues did you find? (good: The Sun is low.; The shadows are long.; It is getting dark.; The crows are noisy.; It is hot and bright.)
  - Note: Good clues are the height of the Sun, how bright it is, how long the shadows are, and what birds are doing. Remind your child not to look straight at the Sun.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stay at the window. 2. Ask which day of the week it is. 3. Ask your child to find a clue outside. 4. Write down the guess.  
  *Parent says:* Keep looking outside. Which day of the week is it? What can you see that tells you?  
  *Child mode:* Keep looking. Which day of the week is it? Can you see a clue outside?
  - Ask: Does the Sun look different on a Sunday? (good: No.; It looks the same every day.; I cannot tell from the Sun.)
  - Ask: Is there any clue at all? (good: The shop is shut, so maybe it is Sunday.; The school van came, so it is not Sunday.; No, nothing.)
  - Note: The sky gives no clue at all. Your child may find clues from people: a shut shop, a school van, a weekly market. Notice together that every one of these clues comes from people.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Show the calendar or phone and check the day. 2. Draw two columns on the paper. 3. Sort each clue: did the sky tell us, or did people tell us? 4. Ask who told us the name of the day.  
  *Parent says:* Let us check. Today is this day. Who told us the name of the day? The sky, or people?  
  *Child mode:* Check the day on a calendar. Now sort your clues. Did the sky tell you, or did people?
  - Ask: What could the sky tell you? What could it not tell you? (good: It told me it is morning.; It could not tell me it is Tuesday.; Only the calendar knows the day.)
  - Note: This step is where the Roots card comes from. The part of the day comes from the Sun. The name of the day comes from people. Let your child say it in their own words.
  - Check: Which of these can you find out just by looking at the sky? → **That it is morning**
### a2 · The Week Ring: yesterday, today, tomorrow (15 min)
- **problem:** Which day comes after Saturday? And where do yesterday and tomorrow go when today moves?
- **record:** Draw a ring of seven circles. Write the first letter of each day in the circles. Colour today.
- **s1 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Write one day name on each sheet, in big letters. 2. Say the seven days together, twice. 3. Your child lays the sheets in a ring, in order. 4. Saturday and Sunday end up next to each other.  
  *Parent says:* Let us say the days: Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday. Now lay them in a ring, in that order.  
  *Child mode:* Say the seven days. Lay the seven sheets in a ring, in order.
  - Note: If your child does not read yet, draw a small Sun on Sunday and say each name as you lay the sheet. Saying the days in order is the first skill. Do not hurry it.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Check today's day on the calendar. 2. Your child stands on today. 3. Ask what tomorrow is. Your child hops one sheet forward and says the day. 4. Go back to today. Ask what yesterday was. Your child hops one sheet back.  
  *Parent says:* You are standing on today. Tomorrow is one sleep ahead. Hop to tomorrow. What day is it?  
  *Child mode:* Stand on today. Hop one sheet forward. That is tomorrow. Say the day.
  - Ask: What did we do yesterday? What will we do tomorrow? (good: Yesterday we went to the park.; Tomorrow we will see Paatti.)
  - Note: Tie each hop to something real that happened yesterday or will happen tomorrow. That makes the words stick.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Call out a day. Your child jumps to it. 2. Ask for the day after it and the day before it. 3. Do this five times. 4. Make sure one of the days is Saturday.  
  *Parent says:* Jump to Saturday. Which day comes after Saturday? Hop and see.  
  *Child mode:* Jump to the day you hear. Then hop to the day after it.
  - Ask: Is there a last day of the week? (good: No, it goes round.; After Saturday it is Sunday again.; Saturday is last on the calendar, but then it starts again.)
  - Note: Many children think nothing comes after Saturday, because the calendar row ends there. The ring shows that Sunday comes next.
  - Check: Today is Saturday. What day is tomorrow? → **Sunday**
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child stands on today again. 2. Ask them to guess where seven hops forward will end. 3. They hop seven times, saying each day. 4. Ask what they notice.  
  *Parent says:* Stand on today. If you hop forward seven times, where will you land? Guess first, then try.  
  *Child mode:* Stand on today. Guess where seven hops will take you. Now hop and count.
  - Ask: Where did you land? Why? (good: On the same day.; There are seven days, so seven hops go all the way round.)
  - Note: Seven hops bring your child back to the same day. That is why next week's Monday is seven sleeps after this Monday.
### a3 · Maths in the kitchen: the lemon juice robot (15 min)
- **problem:** A recipe is a list of steps in order. What happens to the lemon juice if the order is wrong?
- **safety:** Only a grown-up uses the knife.
- **record:** Stick or copy the six cards in the final order on a sheet. Put a star on the steps that could swap.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Write one step on each card: cut the lemon, squeeze the lemon, pour the water, add sugar and salt, stir, drink. 2. Your child draws a small picture on each. 3. Shuffle the cards.  
  *Parent says:* These six cards make lemon juice. But they are mixed up. I am a robot. I only do what the cards say.  
  *Child mode:* Draw a picture on each card. These six steps make lemon juice.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child lays the six cards in a row. 2. Do a pretend run with no food. Act out each card in order, like a robot. 3. If a step cannot be done, stop and say 'Robot stuck'. 4. Your child fixes the order.  
  *Parent says:* Lay the cards in the order I should do them. First we do a pretend run, with no lemon.  
  *Child mode:* Lay the cards in a row. The robot will act them out. Fix the row if the robot gets stuck.
  - Ask: Why did the robot get stuck? (good: You cannot squeeze a lemon before it is cut.; You cannot stir when nothing is in the tumbler.; You cannot drink first.)
  - Note: Play it straight. If 'squeeze' comes before 'cut', try to squeeze a whole lemon and fail. If 'drink' comes first, lift an empty tumbler. The pretend run wastes no food.
- **s3 (5 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Now run it for real. 2. You cut the lemon. 3. Your child squeezes, pours, adds sugar and salt, and stirs, reading each card. 4. Your child tells the order with first, next, then and last.  
  *Parent says:* Now we make it for real. Read me the first card. What comes next?  
  *Child mode:* Now make the juice. Read each card in order. A grown-up cuts the lemon.
  - Note: Let your child do every step except the cutting. Ask 'What comes next?' before each card.
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Drink the juice together. 2. Point at the cards. 3. Ask which two cards could swap places and still give good juice. 4. Ask which could never swap.  
  *Parent says:* Which two cards could swap places, and the juice would be just as good? Which could never swap?  
  *Child mode:* Drink your juice. Which two cards could swap? Which could never swap?
  - Ask: Why can water and sugar swap, but not cut and squeeze? (good: It all gets mixed anyway.; The lemon must be open before the juice comes out.; Stirring has to come after everything is in.)
  - Check: Which step must come before 'squeeze the lemon'? → **Cut the lemon**
### a4 · Which takes longer? Count the claps (10 min)
- **problem:** Some things in your day are over quickly and some take long. How can you tell which takes longer without a clock?
- **record:** Write the four jobs and the number of claps for each. Then write them again in order, shortest first.
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Name the four jobs: put on both chappals, fold the towel, drink half the water, write your name. 2. Ask which will take longest and which will be quickest. 3. Write the guesses.  
  *Parent says:* Here are four jobs. Which one will take the longest? Which will be the quickest? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the four jobs. Guess which takes longest. Guess which is quickest.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Say 'go'. Clap slowly and evenly, about one clap each second, and count aloud. 2. Stop when the job is done. 3. Write the number of claps. 4. Do all four jobs. Clap at the same speed each time.  
  *Parent says:* When I say go, start the job. I will clap and count until you finish.  
  *Child mode:* Start when you hear 'go'. Do the job. Listen to the claps being counted.
  - Note: The claps must be the same speed for every job, or the numbers cannot be compared. Clapping along with a ticking clock helps. Your child should not rush: this is not a race.
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Read the four numbers together. 2. Your child puts the jobs in order, from fewest claps to most. 3. Compare with the guesses. 4. Ask why the claps had to be the same speed.  
  *Parent says:* Which job had the most claps? So which took the longest? Put them in order, shortest first.  
  *Child mode:* Look at your four numbers. Which job took longest? Put the jobs in order.
  - Ask: What would go wrong if I clapped fast for one job and slowly for another? (good: The numbers would not be fair.; The fast one would get more claps.; We could not compare.)
  - Check: Folding a towel took 14 claps. Putting on chappals took 6 claps. Which took longer? → **Folding the towel**
### a5 · Everyone keeps time: a morning and evening watch (10 min)
- **problem:** People have clocks. How do crows and flowers show that they know the part of the day?
- **safety:** Never look straight at the Sun.
- **record:** Fold a sheet in half. Draw the morning on one half and the evening on the other half.
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. In the morning, stand at the window with your child for five minutes. 2. Look for birds, flowers and leaves. 3. Your child draws what each one is doing.  
  *Parent says:* What are the birds doing now? Are the flowers open or closed? Draw what you see.  
  *Child mode:* Watch for five minutes. What are the birds doing? Are flowers open? Draw them.
  - Note: Any living being will do: crows, pigeons, mynas, sparrows, a hibiscus or a tulsi in a pot, a tree across the road. If you have a tamarind or a touch-me-not nearby, look at their leaves.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Before dark, go back to the same place for five minutes. 2. Your child draws what has changed. 3. Ask who told the birds it was evening.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the same birds and plants. What is different now? Who told them it is evening?  
  *Child mode:* Watch again. What has changed since morning? Draw it. Who told the birds it is evening?
  - Ask: Do the crows have a clock? How do they know when to fly home? (good: They see the light going.; They feel it.; They just know.)
  - Note: In many towns crows fly together to their sleeping trees before dark. A hibiscus flower that was open in the morning is often closed by evening.
  - Check: A hibiscus flower opens in the morning and closes by evening. What does this show? → **The plant keeps the time of day**
  - Wait: Do this step in the evening of the same day

## Projects
### p1 · My Week Strip: seven days, one picture a day (35 min, 7 days)
- **problem:** A day that is over is easy to forget. How can you keep a week so that you can look back at it in order?
- **record:** The strip is the record. Keep it on the wall or the fridge for the week.
- **schedule:** day 1: Draw seven boxes on the strip and write the days, starting from today. Draw one thing from today in the first box. (5 min); day 2: Move the marker to today. Say what you did yesterday. Draw one thing from today. (5 min); day 3: Move the marker. Say yesterday, today and tomorrow. Draw today's picture. (5 min); day 4: Move the marker. Count the days done and the days left. Draw today's picture. (5 min); day 5: Move the marker. Say what happened two days ago. Draw today's picture. (5 min); day 6: Move the marker. Guess what you will draw tomorrow. Draw today's picture. (5 min); day 7: Draw the last picture. Read the whole week aloud in order. (5 min)
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Tape two sheets end to end. 2. Draw seven boxes in a row. 3. Write today's day above the first box, and the next six days in order. 4. Your child draws one thing from today in the first box.  
  *Parent says:* This strip will hold one whole week. Today goes in the first box. What happened today that you want to keep?  
  *Child mode:* Draw seven boxes in a row. Draw one thing from today in the first box.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Move the marker to the second box. 2. Point at the first box and ask what day that was, and what happened. 3. Your child draws one thing from today.  
  *Parent says:* The marker is on today. Look at the box before it. What do we call that day now?  
  *Child mode:* Move the marker to today. What did you draw yesterday? Now draw today.
  - Ask: Yesterday you called this box 'today'. What do you call it now? (good: Yesterday.; It moved.)
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Move the marker to the third box. 2. Your child points to yesterday, today and tomorrow on the strip, and names each day. 3. Your child draws today's picture.  
  *Parent says:* Show me yesterday. Show me today. Show me tomorrow. What are their names?  
  *Child mode:* Move the marker. Point to yesterday, today and tomorrow. Draw today's picture.
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Move the marker to the fourth box. 2. Count the boxes with pictures. 3. Count the empty boxes. 4. Your child draws today's picture.  
  *Parent says:* How many days of our week are done, with today? How many are left?  
  *Child mode:* Move the marker. Count the days done. Count the days left. Draw today.
  - Check: A week has 7 days. 4 days are done. How many days are left? → **3**
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening
- **s5 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Move the marker to the fifth box. 2. Ask what happened two days ago. Your child finds the box by hopping back two. 3. Your child draws today's picture.  
  *Parent says:* What did we do two days ago? Hop back two boxes from today and see.  
  *Child mode:* Move the marker. Hop back two boxes. What happened that day? Draw today.
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening
- **s6 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Move the marker to the sixth box. 2. Ask what your child thinks will go in tomorrow's box. 3. Your child draws today's picture.  
  *Parent says:* One box is left. What do you think you will draw in it tomorrow? Can you be sure?  
  *Child mode:* Move the marker. Guess what you will draw tomorrow. Draw today's picture.
  - Ask: You can look back at yesterday's box. Can you look at tomorrow's box? (good: No, it is empty.; Tomorrow has not come yet.; I can only guess.)
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening
- **s7 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child draws the last picture. 2. Your child reads the whole strip aloud, naming each day. 3. Ask what day comes after the last box.  
  *Parent says:* Your week is full. Read it to me, day by day. What day comes after the last box?  
  *Child mode:* Draw the last picture. Read your whole week aloud. What day comes next?
  - Ask: What day comes after the last box? (good: The same day as the first box.; It starts again.)
  - Wait: Do this step the next evening

## Challenges
### c1 · Stretch: what day will it be after 10 sleeps? (20 min)
- **problem:** Your cousin is coming to stay after 10 sleeps. What day of the week will that be? Find a way that also works for 15 sleeps and 21 sleeps.
- **safety:** Keep seeds, beans and buttons away from babies and toddlers, who could choke on them.
- **your turn:** Now invent your own way. Use it for 15 sleeps and for 21 sleeps. Check each answer by hopping round the Week Ring.
- **constraints:** You must check your answer in a second way.; Your way must work when you start from any day of the week.; You may use the Week Ring, beans, paper and a pencil.
- **general:** Stand on today on the Week Ring. Hop forward 10 times and say each day.; Write the days in one long line and count along the line.; Put one bean on each day sheet, going round, until 10 beans are down.
- **creative:** Seven sleeps always bring back the same day. So 10 sleeps is one whole week and 3 more sleeps. Hop only 3.; Put the beans in a row of 7 and a row of what is left. Only the short row matters.; Make a small table: after 7 sleeps, the same day. After 8, one day on. After 9, two days on.
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lay the Week Ring. 2. Your child stands on today. 3. Ask what day it will be after 10 sleeps. 4. Let them find it in their own way. 5. Write the answer.  
  *Parent says:* Your cousin comes after 10 sleeps. What day will that be? Find out any way you like.  
  *Child mode:* Stand on today. Find the day after 10 sleeps. Use any way you like.
  - Note: Most children hop ten times round the ring. That is a good first way. Many lose count near seven or eight, which is why a second way is needed.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask your child to hop exactly 7 times from today. 2. Ask where they landed. 3. Try 7 hops from two other days. 4. Ask what 7 hops always do.  
  *Parent says:* Hop seven times from today. Where do you land? Try from another day. What do you notice?  
  *Child mode:* Hop 7 times from today. Where do you land? Try from another day.
  - Ask: What do seven hops always do? (good: They bring me back to the same day.; Seven sleeps is one whole week.)
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lay 10 beans in a row. 2. Ask your child to push 7 of them together into one week. 3. Count the beans left over. 4. Your child hops only that many from today. 5. Compare with the first answer.  
  *Parent says:* Here are 10 sleeps. Push seven of them together. That is one week, and it brings the same day. How many sleeps are left?  
  *Child mode:* Lay 10 beans. Push 7 together. Count what is left. Hop only that many.
  - Note: 10 is 7 and 3. Your child does not need to write this as a sum. Seeing the beans in a row of 7 and a row of 3 is enough.
  - Check: Today is Wednesday. What day is it after 10 sleeps? → **Saturday**
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask for the day after 15 sleeps. 2. Your child lays 15 beans and makes weeks of 7. 3. They hop what is left. 4. Try 21 sleeps. 5. Check one answer on a real calendar.  
  *Parent says:* Now try 15 sleeps. How many whole weeks can you make? How many sleeps are left over?  
  *Child mode:* Now try 15 sleeps. Make rows of 7 beans. Hop what is left over.
  - Ask: What happened with 21 sleeps? (good: Nothing was left over.; It is the same day as today.; It made three whole weeks.)
  - Note: 15 beans make two rows of 7 and 1 left over, so the answer is one day on. 21 beans make three rows of 7 with none left, so it is the same day as today.

## Worksheets
### My Day (core) (core, 13 questions)
1. (mcq) A day has 4 parts. The first part is shaded. Which part is it?  
   Options: Morning / Evening / Night  
   **Answer:** Morning. *Why:* Morning is the first part of the day. It starts when the Sun comes up.
2. (order) Put the four parts of a day in order. Start with morning.  
   **Answer:** ['Morning', 'Afternoon', 'Evening', 'Night']. *Why:* The Sun comes up in the morning. It is high in the afternoon. It goes down in the evening. Then it is night.
3. (truefalse) Look at the row. 'Brush teeth' comes before 'eat breakfast'.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. 'Brush teeth' is in box 1 and 'eat breakfast' is in box 2. Box 1 comes before box 2.
4. (fill) Count the boxes. How many days are in one week? ____  
   **Answer:** ['7', 'seven']. *Why:* There are 7 boxes, one for each day: Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday.
5. (mcq) Which day comes just after Monday?  
   Options: Sunday / Tuesday / Wednesday  
   **Answer:** Tuesday. *Why:* Say the days in order: Sunday, Monday, Tuesday. Tuesday comes just after Monday. Sunday comes before Monday.
6. (mcq) Sunday is day 1 and Monday is day 2. Which day is the arrow on?  
   Options: Tuesday / Wednesday / Thursday  
   **Answer:** Wednesday. *Why:* The arrow is on 4. Count the days: Sunday 1, Monday 2, Tuesday 3, Wednesday 4.
7. (match) Match each thing to its part of the day.  
   **Answer:** The Sun comes up → Morning; Lunch time → Afternoon; The Sun goes down → Evening; Stars are out → Night. *Why:* The Sun comes up in the morning and goes down in the evening. Lunch is in the afternoon. Stars show at night, when it is dark.
8. (truefalse) Yesterday is the day after today.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Yesterday is the day before today. It is over. The day after today is tomorrow.
9. (sort) Sort each thing: do you do it before you eat, or after you eat?  
   **Answer:** Wash my hands → Before I eat; Wash my plate → After I eat; Serve the food → Before I eat; Rinse my mouth → After I eat. *Why:* You wash your hands and serve the food first. You wash the plate and rinse your mouth when the eating is over.
10. (fill) Each dot is one sleep until Pongal. How many sleeps are there? ____  
   **Answer:** ['5', 'five']. *Why:* Count the dots: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. There are 5 sleeps until Pongal.
11. (draw) Draw one thing you do in the morning and one thing you do at night.  
   **Answer:** Two drawings that fit the two parts, such as brushing teeth for morning and sleeping for night.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows something that really belongs to each part. A Sun or a Moon in the picture helps to show which part it is.
12. (write) Say your answer aloud. A grown-up writes it. What did you do yesterday? Say three things in order.  
   **Answer:** Three things from yesterday, told in the order they happened, with words like first, next and last.. *Why:* A strong answer keeps the real order and uses order words: first, next, then, last.
13. (draw) Draw the sky in the evening. Draw where the Sun is.  
   **Answer:** A Sun low in the sky, or half hidden, with orange or fading light.. *Why:* In the evening the Sun goes down. A strong drawing puts the Sun low, not high.
### Round the Week (stretch) (stretch, 12 questions)
1. (fill) A week has 7 days. The shaded days are over. How many days are left? ____  
   **Answer:** ['5', 'five']. *Why:* 2 boxes are shaded and 5 are not. 7 take away 2 leaves 5.
2. (mcq) Today is Wednesday. What day was yesterday?  
   Options: Tuesday / Thursday / Monday  
   **Answer:** Tuesday. *Why:* Yesterday is the day before today. The day before Wednesday is Tuesday. Thursday is tomorrow.

   Options: Monday / Tuesday / Wednesday

   **Answer:** ['Wednesday', 'wednesday']. *Why:* Seven sleeps bring the same day. One more sleep takes you one day on, to Wednesday.

   Options: 1 / 2 / 3  
   **Answer:** 2. *Why:* Count on from Monday: Tuesday is 1, Wednesday is 2. Picking 3 means Monday was counted as a sleep.

   **Answer:** ['Thursday', 'Friday', 'Saturday', 'Sunday', 'Monday']. *Why:* After Thursday come Friday and Saturday. Then the week goes round to Sunday and Monday.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) The Sun is going down and the sky is orange. Which part of the day is it?  
   Options: Morning / Evening / Afternoon  
   **Answer:** Evening. *Why:* It is evening. The Sun goes down in the evening. In the morning the Sun comes up.
2. (mcq) A day has 4 parts. Morning and afternoon are over. Which part comes next?  
   Options: Evening / Night / Morning  
   **Answer:** Evening. *Why:* Evening comes next. The order is morning, afternoon, evening, night. Picking night skips a part.
3. (order) Put these in order, from first to last.  
   **Answer:** ['Pick a banana', 'Peel the banana', 'Eat the banana', 'Put the peel in the bin']. *Why:* You must pick it before you can peel it, and peel it before you eat it. The peel goes in the bin last.
4. (mcq) You are making lemon juice. What must you do first?  
   Options: Stir / Cut the lemon / Drink  
   **Answer:** Cut the lemon. *Why:* Cut the lemon first. You cannot squeeze, stir or drink before the lemon is open.
5. (mcq) Look at the row. What comes just before 'eat breakfast'?  
   Options: Wash plate / Brush teeth / Nothing  
   **Answer:** Brush teeth. *Why:* 'Brush teeth' is in the box just before 'eat breakfast'. 'Wash plate' comes after it.
6. (truefalse) Lunch comes after breakfast and before dinner.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. The meals go breakfast, lunch, dinner. Lunch is in the middle, so it is after one and before the other.
7. (mcq) Which day comes just after Thursday?  
   Options: Wednesday / Friday / Saturday  
   **Answer:** Friday. *Why:* Friday comes just after Thursday. Wednesday comes before Thursday.
8. (mcq) Which day comes just before Sunday?  
   Options: Monday / Saturday / Friday  
   **Answer:** Saturday. *Why:* Saturday comes just before Sunday. The week goes round, so Sunday follows Saturday. Monday comes after Sunday.
9. (fill) Look at the boxes. One week has ____ days.  
   **Answer:** ['7', 'seven']. *Why:* There are 7 boxes. A week has seven days, from Sunday to Saturday.
10. (mcq) Today is Tuesday. What day will tomorrow be?  
   Options: Monday / Wednesday / Thursday  
   **Answer:** Wednesday. *Why:* Tomorrow is the day after today. After Tuesday comes Wednesday. Monday was yesterday.
11. (mcq) Today is Monday. What day was yesterday?  
   Options: Sunday / Tuesday / Saturday  
   **Answer:** Sunday. *Why:* Yesterday is the day before today. Before Monday comes Sunday.
12. (mcq) Which of these did people choose?  
   Options: The Sun coming up / The name Wednesday / The sky getting dark  
   **Answer:** The name Wednesday. *Why:* People chose the name Wednesday. The Sun coming up and the sky getting dark happen without anyone choosing.
13. (truefalse) You can tell that it is Friday by looking at the Sun.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. The Sun looks the same on every day. It can show you the part of the day, but not the name of the day.

## Applications
- A cook follows a recipe in order. The dosa batter is ground first, left overnight, and cooked the next morning.
- A bus or train timetable says which days a bus runs and at what part of the day.
- A doctor says when to take medicine: one in the morning after food, one at night.
- A farmer waters the plants in the early morning or the evening, not in the hot afternoon.
- A weekly market sets up on the same day every week, so buyers and sellers know when to come.
- A school or a class has a timetable: music on Tuesday, drawing on Friday.
- A weaver sets up all the long threads first. Only after that can the cross threads go in.
- A cricket match has an order: the toss, the first innings, the break, the second innings.

## Spotted around us
- A kolam at the doorstep. In many Tamil homes someone draws it early in the morning, before the day's work starts.
- A wall calendar with one sheet for each day. Someone tears off yesterday's sheet every morning.
- Your shadow. In the morning and evening your shadow is long. In the middle of the day your shadow is short.
- Crows before dark. In many towns they fly together to the trees where they sleep.
- A weekly market, called a sandhai in Tamil and a haat in the north and east. It comes on the same day every week.
- A tamarind tree at night. The small leaves are folded shut. In the morning they open again.
- The four o'clock flower. The flowers open late in the afternoon and close by the next morning.
- A mosque's call to prayer. It comes five times a day, from before sunrise to night, so people hear the parts of the day.

## Open questions
- **Why did people first choose seven days for a week?** The seven-day week is thousands of years old. There are several ideas about where it came from. One is the Moon. Another is the seven lights people saw moving in the sky. No record settles it.
- **What is time?** Everyone uses time. But scientists and thinkers still do not agree on what it is. They also ask why it only goes one way, from before to after.
- **How does a bee remember what time of day she found food?** Scientists have known for about a hundred years that bees do this. How the bee's body joins a time to a place is still being studied.
- **Does any other animal keep something like a week?** Many living beings keep the time of day and the seasons. Nobody has clearly shown a wild animal counting days in sevens. It is hard to test.

## Connected maths
- Today is Tuesday. Amma comes home on Friday. How many sleeps until she comes? Sunday is day 1, so Tuesday is day 3. **3 sleeps.**. Count on from Tuesday. Wednesday is 1. Thursday is 2. Friday is 3. So there are 3 sleeps. (queues `math.number-sense.counting.L1`)
- You have lessons on 5 days of the week. The other days are rest days. How many rest days are there in a week? **2 rest days.**. A week has 7 days. Take away the 5 lesson days. Count on from 5 to 7: 6, 7. That is 2 days. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L1`)
- In Count the Claps, putting on chappals took 6 claps. Folding the towel took 14 claps. How many more claps did the towel take? **8 more claps.**. Count on from 6 to 14: 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. That is 8 claps. So the towel took 8 claps longer. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.word-problems.L1`)
- The days go Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and then again. Today is Wednesday. What day is it 7 sleeps from now? Guess, then say why. **Wednesday again.**. The days make a pattern of seven that repeats. Seven hops go once round the whole ring. So you land on the day you started from. (queues `math.patterns.repeating-patterns.L1`)
