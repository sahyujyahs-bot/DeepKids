# Zero: the number for none
`math.number-sense.zero.L1`

**Summary.** You eat the last laddu on the plate. How many laddus are on the plate now? Is there a number for that? You will take beans away one by one until none are left, and find the number that comes before 1. You will hop back to zero on a number line, play games with a zero dice, and keep score in corridor cricket. You will also find out that zero is always zero of something.

**Intro.** *What number tells how many laddus are on plate C?* Plate A has 3 laddus and plate B has 1. Plate C has none. The number for none is zero, and we write it as 0. Zero is a number, just like 1, 2 and 3. It answers the question 'how many?' when there are none. In this unit you will find zero yourself, with beans and a plate.

## Learning outcomes
0. I can say zero when there are none of the things I am counting, and write it as 0.
1. I can show that taking away the last thing leaves zero.
2. I can find zero on the number line, one step before 1.
3. I can count back to zero from any number up to 10.
4. I can say that zero is fewer than 1, 2, 3 and every other counting number.
5. I can say what there is zero of, because zero is always zero of something.

## Terms
- **Zero**: The number that tells how many when there are none. We write it as 0. *e.g.* You eat both bananas in the bowl. Now there are zero bananas in the bowl.
- **None**: Not even one. *e.g.* There were 4 murukkus in the tin. Now there are none. The number for none is zero.
- **Empty**: With none of the things it usually holds. *e.g.* An empty water bottle has zero water in it. It still has air in it.
- **Take away**: To remove some things from a group, so that fewer are left. *e.g.* There are 3 idlis on the plate. You take away 1 idli. Now 2 are left.
- **Fewer**: Not as many. *e.g.* 2 vadas are fewer than 3 vadas. Zero vadas are fewer than 1 vada.
- **Number line**: A line with the numbers written along it in order, the same distance apart. *e.g.* You write 0 to 10 on slips of paper and lay them in a line on the floor. Zero is at the start.
- **Count back**: To start from a number and say the numbers backwards, one fewer each time. *e.g.* You count back from 3: 2, 1, 0.
- **Countdown**: Counting back to zero to show when something will start. *e.g.* Before a rocket lifts off, people count back: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0.

## Explanations
### Zero is the number for none · *defined*
**Definition:** Zero is the number that tells how many when there are none. We write it as 0.

Count the laddus on a plate: 1, 2, 3. Now look at an empty plate. How many laddus are on it? There are none, and the number for none is zero. Here is a worked example. Two bananas are in a bowl. Amma eats one, so 1 is left. You eat one, so 0 are left.
- e.g. A new notebook has 0 pages written on.
- e.g. When everyone has gone to school and work, there are 0 chappals by the door.
- e.g. In Sanskrit, zero is called shunya. The word means empty.
### One fewer than 1 is zero · *defined*
**Definition:** When you take away the last thing, zero are left. One take away one is zero.

Each time you take one thing away, the number goes down by one. Here is a worked example. Three beans are on a plate. Take one away, and 2 are left. Take one away, and 1 is left. Take the last one away, and 0 are left. We can write the last step as a sum: 1 take away 1 is 0.
- e.g. One dosa is on your plate. You eat it. Zero dosas are on your plate.
- e.g. Five children are in the lift. All five get out. Zero children are in the lift.
- e.g. A full tube of toothpaste goes down a little each day, until zero is left.
### Zero on the number line · *defined*
**Definition:** On a number line, zero is one step before 1. It is where counting starts.

On a number line, each step to the right is one more. Each step to the left is one fewer. Here is a worked example. Put your finger on 3. Step left to 2, and then to 1. Step left once more, and you are on 0. So zero sits one step to the left of 1. Before you have counted anything, you are at zero.
- e.g. A countdown is hopping left along the number line: 3, 2, 1, 0.
- e.g. A stopwatch shows 0 before you press start.
- e.g. At a petrol pump, the meter is set to 0 before the petrol starts to flow.
### Zero is fewer than one · *defined*
**Definition:** Zero is fewer than 1, and fewer than every other counting number.

Fewer means not as many. 1 vada is fewer than 2 vadas. 0 vadas is fewer than 1 vada. You cannot have fewer vadas than none. So when you count things, zero is the smallest answer you can get. This is simplified. Later you will meet numbers below zero. They are used for other jobs, such as naming the floors under the ground in a tall building.
- e.g. A plate with 0 idlis has fewer idlis than a plate with 1 idli.
- e.g. A team with 0 runs has fewer runs than a team with 1 run.
- e.g. If two tiffin boxes both have 0 biscuits, neither has fewer. They have the same.
### Zero is always zero of something · *defined*
**Definition:** Zero tells how many of one kind of thing. It does not mean that nothing at all is there.

Zero answers a question, and the question always names a thing. How many beans are on the plate? Zero. How many plates are there? One. A bowl holds 3 bananas and no mangoes. It has 0 mangoes and 3 fruits. So before you say zero, say what you are counting.
- e.g. Your hand has 0 feathers and 5 fingers.
- e.g. An empty tumbler has 0 water. It is full of air.
- e.g. In the day you can count 0 stars in the sky, apart from the Sun. The stars are still there. The Sun's light hides them.
### Where zero goes is sometimes a choice · *defined*
**Definition:** People choose where to start counting. Some start at 0 and some start at 1.

Look at the buttons in a lift. In India the ground floor is often marked 0 or G, and the floor above it is 1. In the United States the ground floor is usually called floor 1. The building is the same. Only the choice is different. Ages work like this too. In India a new baby is 0 years old until the first birthday. In the old Korean way, a new baby was called 1 year old.
- e.g. A lift in Chennai: 0, 1, 2, 3. A lift in New York: 1, 2, 3, 4. Both have four floors.
- e.g. In June 2023, South Korea changed its rule. Now a new baby there is 0 years old too.
- e.g. The first second of a race starts at 0 on the stopwatch, not at 1.
### Is none a number? · *debated*
A number tells how many things there are. But when there are none, there is nothing to count. So is zero really a number? People answered this in two ways. Today nearly everyone uses zero as a number, because sums with zero work every time. The argument about what zero really is has not ended.
- e.g. The Romans wrote numbers with letters: I for 1, V for 5, X for 10. They had no letter for zero.
- e.g. In India, writers first used a dot for zero, and later a small circle.
- e.g. The Maya people of Central America drew a shell for zero.
- **Many people long ago:** A number counts things. None is not a thing. So you do not need a number for it. You can just say 'there are none'.
- **Brahmagupta, an Indian mathematician, in 628 CE, and mathematicians today:** Zero is a number like 1, 2 and 3. You can add it and take it away. Brahmagupta wrote down rules for doing sums with zero.
### Stretch: adding zero, and taking away zero · *defined*
**Definition:** When you add zero to a number, the number stays the same. When you take zero away from a number, the number stays the same.

Here is a worked example. Five beans are on a plate. Tip an empty cup over the plate. The cup had zero beans, so no bean falls out. There are still 5 beans, and we write 5 + 0 = 5. Now take zero beans away. You take none, so there are still 5. We write 5 − 0 = 5.
- e.g. You have 6 marbles. Your sister gives you 0 more. You still have 6.
- e.g. A batter has 4 runs. The next ball is a dot ball, which means 0 runs. She still has 4.
- e.g. 0 + 0 = 0. Two empty plates together still hold no beans.

## Mostly agreed
- Zero is a number. It tells how many when there are none. *How we know:* Sums with zero work every time. 3 take away 3 is 0. 3 and 0 more is 3. Anyone can check this with beans. Brahmagupta wrote the first known rules for sums with zero in 628 CE.
- Small children learn zero later than they learn 1, 2 and 3. *How we know:* In 1986 two scientists, Henry Wellman and Kevin Miller, asked children aged 3 to 7 about zero. Many of the younger children knew the sign 0 but still thought 1 was the smallest number.
- People in different places invented a sign for zero by themselves. *How we know:* Writers in old Babylon used two slanted marks for an empty place. The Maya of Central America drew a shell. Writers in India used a dot and later a circle. These peoples lived far apart.
- Some other animals treat none as fewer than one. *How we know:* In 2018, honeybees trained to fly to the card with fewer shapes chose an empty card over a card with shapes more often than not (Howard and others, 2018). In 2021, scientists showed two trained crows screens of dots, and sometimes an empty screen. One crow clearly treated the empty screen as closest to one dot (Kirschhock and others, 2021).

## How we found out
**Writers in Babylon, the Maya, and Brahmagupta in India, About 300 BCE to 876 CE.** Long ago, people wrote numbers without any sign for none. About 2,300 years ago, writers in Babylon, in today's Iraq, began to use two small slanted marks to show an empty place inside a number. It was a gap-filler. Nobody did sums with it. Far away in Central America, the Maya drew a shell for zero. In India, writers used a dot. Then, in 628 CE, the Indian mathematician Brahmagupta wrote a book that gave rules for zero. A number added to zero stays the same. Zero added to zero is zero. He treated zero as a number, like 1, 2 and 3. Later, Indian writers drew zero as a small circle. A temple wall in Gwalior, dated 876 CE, shows the numbers 270 and 50 written that way. From India, the numerals travelled to the Arab world and then to Europe. The word zero comes from the Arabic word sifr.  
*What people believed before:* A number must count something, so none cannot be a number. Brahmagupta showed this was wrong in 628 CE. He wrote rules for adding and taking away zero, and the rules worked like the rules for any other number.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Zero is nothing, so it is not a real number.** (Many grown-ups, and many people long ago; *rejected*)  
  For: You cannot hold zero beans in your hand. When there are none, there is nothing to point at.  
  Against: Zero does every job a number does. It answers 'how many?'. It has a place on the number line. You can add it and take it away. An empty plate is also not nothing: the plate is still there.
- **Zero was invented in India and nowhere else.** (A common belief; *debated*)  
  For: The first known rules for doing sums with zero as a number were written in India, by Brahmagupta in 628 CE. The numerals the world uses today, with 0 in them, came from India.  
  Against: Writers in Babylon and the Maya of Central America had their own signs for an empty place, without learning from India. So more than one people thought of a sign for zero.
- **Only humans understand none.** (A common belief; *debated*)  
  For: Only humans are known to write zero and do sums with it.  
  Against: Trained honeybees chose an empty card as fewer than one. A trained crow treated an empty screen as the number next to one. Scientists still argue about how much this is like our zero.

## Roots
We took beans away until none were left, and found the number zero. Now let us think about what zero really tells us.

**Deep truth.** In the Empty Plate Test, you took away the last bean. The plate had zero beans, zero coins and zero elephants. But it was still 1 plate. So zero does not mean that nothing is there. Zero means none of the thing you chose to count.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think zero means nothing. But something is always there: the plate, the table, the air. Zero only answers the question we asked, such as 'how many beans?'  
*Evidence:* In the Empty Plate Test, the two empty plates had zero beans and zero coins. You could still count 2 plates.

- *who am i:* You have zero wings and zero tails. You also have 2 hands and 1 nose. Nobody is zero of everything.
- *what is life:* Honeybees were trained to choose the card with fewer shapes. They chose an empty card over a card with one shape. Bees deal with none too.
- *what is this world:* An empty tumbler has zero water in it. But it is full of air. It is hard to find a place that is empty of everything.

Start with an empty plate. That is zero beans. Put one bean on it. That is one. Add one more, and one more, and you have many. Every count starts at zero, even when we do not say it. Take the beans away again, and you are back at zero.

- **Chandogya Upanishad 6.12:** In the Chandogya Upanishad, a father asks his son to break open a tiny banyan seed. The son says he sees nothing inside. The father says the great banyan tree grows from what he cannot see. (The son counted zero things in the seed. But the seed was not empty. Zero told only what he was able to see.)
- **Brahmasphutasiddhanta 18.30:** The mathematician Brahmagupta wrote in 628 CE that a number added to zero stays the same kind of number, and that zero added to zero is zero. (This is what you saw when you tipped the empty cup over 4 beans. Brahmagupta treated zero as a number you can do sums with.)
- Maths (settled): Honeybees trained to choose the card with fewer shapes went on to choose an empty card over cards with shapes (Howard and others, 2018). So treating none as fewer than one does not need words or numerals.
- Maths (open): Is there any place with truly nothing in it? Physicists have found that space with all the air taken out still has energy in it. Whether a place with nothing at all could exist is not known.
- Other view, Many people long ago, and some thinkers today: They say a number tells how many things there are. When there are no things, there is nothing to count. So zero is not a number like 3. It is only a mark that says 'nothing here'. The Romans had no numeral for zero, and they still built roads and bridges that stand today.
- Practice, Listen for zero sounds (3 min): Sit still and close your eyes. For one minute, listen. Each time you hear a new sound, put one finger down. A fan, a crow, a pressure cooker, your own breath. Now listen for a moment with zero sounds. Open your eyes. Did you find one? Was it ever really silent?
- Ask: Is an empty tumbler really empty?
- Ask: When you take the last bean away, where does the number go?
- Ask: Can you think of nothing at all? Try it. What happened?
- Ask: In the daytime you count zero stars. Are there zero stars?

## Story: The Ring on the Slate
*Thangam, Paatti and Mani are made up. Madurai is well known for its jasmine. Nobody knows who first drew zero as a round sign. In India, the oldest zero written as a small circle that carries a date is on a temple wall in Gwalior, from 876 CE. That writing counts 50 flower garlands to be given each day.*

Thangam's grandmother sold jasmine near the big temple in Madurai. Every morning Paatti got up at four o'clock, while the street was still dark. She went to the flower market, bought a basket of buds, and tied them into strings.

Jasmine does not keep. A string tied in the morning is brown by the next day. So Paatti had to guess well. If she tied too many strings, some went to waste. If she tied too few, people walked away with empty hands.

That was why Thangam had a job. Every evening she counted the strings that nobody had bought. She wrote the number on the slate by the door. Paatti read it at four in the morning, when Thangam was fast asleep.

"If the slate says 6, I buy fewer buds," Paatti told her. "If it says 1, I buy a little more."

On Monday the slate said 4. On Tuesday it said 2.

On Wednesday a wedding party came down the street. The aunties bought jasmine for every girl's plait. By sunset the basket was empty.

Thangam picked up the chalk to do her job. She looked into the basket. There was not one string to count. So she wrote no number. She left the slate clean and went to bed happy.

At four in the morning Paatti held the lamp up to the slate. It was blank.

"The child fell asleep before she counted," Paatti said to herself. Thangam had done that once last week, and that day six strings had been left over. "I had better be careful." She bought only a small basket of buds.

Thursday was the day before the festival. By five o'clock every string was sold, and people were still coming. A girl in a green pavadai asked for two strings. A man asked for five. Paatti had to shake her head again and again.

"Paatti, why did you tie so few?" Thangam asked. "Yesterday we sold every single one!"

"Did we? The slate was blank. I thought you forgot to count."

"I did not forget. I looked in the basket. There was nothing to count, so there was nothing to write."

Paatti put down the basket. "Kanna, think. When you forget, the slate is blank. When everything is sold, the slate is blank. At four in the morning, how can I tell one blank slate from the other?"

Thangam thought about that all the way home. A blank slate did not say 'none are left'. A blank slate did not say anything at all. She needed a mark that meant: I looked, I counted, and there are none.

She looked at the empty basket in her lap. From above it was a round rim with nothing inside.

That evening the basket was empty again. Thangam took the chalk and drew a ring on the slate. It was round like the rim of the basket, with nothing inside it.

Her little brother Mani came to look. "Why did you draw that? You said there are no strings. So do not write anything."

"If I write nothing, Paatti does not know if I counted," said Thangam. "The ring is how many are left. I counted, and this is the number."

"Then it is a number for no strings," said Mani slowly.

"Yes. And Paatti needs it as much as she needs a 4 or a 6."

At four in the morning Paatti held the lamp up to the slate. She saw the ring, and she smiled. She came home from the market with the biggest basket of buds that Thangam had ever seen.

On the festival day they sold jasmine until the stars came out. That evening Thangam counted the strings that were left. There were three. She rubbed out the ring and wrote 3.

A few days later Thangam fell asleep straight after dinner. In the morning the slate was blank. This time Paatti knew what a blank slate meant. She woke Thangam with a tickle. "You forgot to count," she said. "Come, we will count them together."

**For the parent:** For the parent: writing zero is not the same as writing nothing. A blank slate could mean 'I forgot' or 'none are left', so it tells Paatti nothing. The ring means 'I counted, and there are none'. Zero is a number because it is an answer to 'how many?'.

- Ask: Why could Paatti not tell what the blank slate meant?
- Ask: What did the ring tell Paatti that a blank slate could not?
- Ask: Mani said to write nothing when there are no strings. What would you say to him?
- Ask: Where at home would a zero be more useful than a blank space?

## Games
### g1 · Empty the Plate (10 min)
- **rules:** Make the zero dice. Stick a small piece of paper over the 6 on a dice and write 0 on it.; Each player has a plate with 15 beans.; On your turn, roll the zero dice. Take that many beans off your plate. Then count your beans and say how many are left.; If you roll 0, take no beans. Say 'Zero. I take none.'; If you roll more than you have, take all your beans.; The first player with an empty plate shouts 'Zero!' and wins.; Playing alone: count how many rolls you need to empty the plate. Play again and try to need fewer.
- **Printable score sheet: A strip to keep track of how many beans are left after each turn (one for each player):** Start: 15; After turn 1: ___; After turn 2: ___; After turn 3: ___; After turn 4: ___; After turn 5: ___; After turn 6: ___; After turn 7: ___; After turn 8: ___
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Make the zero dice together. 2. Each player counts 15 beans onto a plate. 3. Read the rules aloud.  
  *Parent says:* This dice has a zero on it. What do you think you will do if you roll zero?  
  *Child mode:* Make the zero dice. Count 15 beans onto your plate.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Take one practice turn each. 2. Roll, take the beans off, then count what is left and say it aloud. 3. Roll until someone gets a 0, and show what to do.  
  *Parent says:* I rolled 3. I take 3 beans off. Now I count what is left: 12. Your turn.  
  *Child mode:* Roll the dice. Take that many beans off. Count what is left. Say it.
  - Note: The usual slip is to think that rolling zero empties the plate. Taking zero away changes nothing.
  - Check: You have 7 beans on your plate. You roll 0. How many beans do you have now? → **7**
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Play the game. 2. Each turn, the player says how many beans are left. 3. When a player has 5 or fewer, ask what they need to roll to reach zero.  
  *Parent says:* You have 2 beans left. Which rolls will empty your plate?  
  *Child mode:* Play the game. Say how many beans are left each turn. First to zero wins.
  - Ask: You have 2 beans. Which rolls will empty your plate? (good: 2, 3, 4 or 5.; Anything but 0 and 1.)
  - Note: A game takes about six or seven turns for each player.
- **s4 (1 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. When someone's plate is empty, the game ends. 2. Ask each player how many beans they have left.  
  *Parent says:* Who reached zero first? How many beans does everyone else have?  
  *Child mode:* The first empty plate wins. How many beans do you have left?

Play again and try to need fewer.

  *Parent says:* How many pairs do you have? Now put the dot cards in order, starting with zero.  
  *Child mode:* Count your pairs. Then lay the dot cards in order, from 0 to 5.

## Experiments
### e1 · The Empty Plate Test: is there a number for none? (25 min)
- **question:** When the last bean is taken away, is there still a number for how many beans are on the plate? Where does that number go?
- **predict:** Before the last bean is taken, guess: is there a number for how many will be left? What is it, and why do you think so?
- **fair test:** We use the same plate and the same 5 beans all the way through. We take away exactly one bean each time. We count what is left every time, even when it looks easy.
- **safety:** Keep beans, coins and buttons away from babies and toddlers, who could choke on them.
- **record:** A grown-up draws the table. For each step, write the guess and the count. At the end, the child says what they found, and the grown-up writes it under the table.
- **results table:** What I did | My guess | What I counted
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Draw the table with three columns. 2. Put 5 beans on one plate. 3. Ask your child to count them, touching each bean. 4. Write 5.  
  *Parent says:* How many beans are on this plate? Touch each one as you count.  
  *Child mode:* Put 5 beans on a plate. Touch each bean and count.
  - Ask: What was the last number you said? So how many beans are there? (good: 5.; Five, because that was the last number.)
  - Note: Keep the second plate, the cup and the coins to one side for now.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask your child to take one bean away. 2. Before they count, ask how many will be left. Write the guess. 3. Count and write. 4. Do this again until 1 bean is left. 5. Now stop. Ask for a guess and a reason before the last bean goes. 6. Take the last bean. Write what your child says.  
  *Parent says:* One bean is left. If you take it away, how many beans will be on the plate? Is there a number for that? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Take one bean away. Count what is left. Do it again and again. What will you say when the plate is empty?
  - Ask: The plate is empty now. How many beans are on it? (good: None.; Nothing.; Zero.; No beans.)
  - Note: Children say 'nothing', 'none', 'no beans' or 'zero'. Write down the exact word. Do not give the word zero yet if your child has not said it. The next step shows why a number is needed.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Write 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 0 on six slips of paper. Hide the 0 slip. 2. On the table, lay a row of 5 beans, then rows of 4, 3, 2 and 1 under it. Put the right slip beside each row. 3. Ask what the next row looks like. 4. Ask where the empty plate belongs: after 5 or before 1? Ask why. 5. Bring out the 0 slip. Your child puts it in place.  
  *Parent says:* Each row has one bean fewer than the row before it. What does the next row look like? Does the empty plate go after 5 or before 1? Why?  
  *Child mode:* Make rows of 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 beans. What does the next row look like? Put the 0 slip where it belongs.
  - Ask: Why does the empty row go next to the row of 1? (good: Because it is one less than 1.; Each row loses one bean, and then there are no beans.; It has the fewest.)
  - Note: The next row has no beans at all. It goes next to the row of 1, because it has one bean fewer than 1. Some children put it after 5 because zero is the newest number they learnt. Point at the pattern of one fewer each time and let them move it.
  - Check: Rows of beans go 3, 2, 1. Each row has one bean fewer. How many beans are in the next row? → **0**
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put 4 beans on the plate. 2. Show the cup, held so that your child cannot see inside. It is empty. 3. Ask: if I tip this cup over the plate, will there be more beans, fewer beans or the same? Ask why. Write the guess. 4. Tip the cup. Count. Write 4. 5. Let your child look inside the cup.  
  *Parent says:* There are 4 beans. I will tip this cup over the plate. Will there be more beans, fewer beans or the same? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Put 4 beans on the plate. Tip the empty cup over them. More beans, fewer, or the same? Count to check.
  - Ask: How many beans were in the cup? How do you know? (good: Zero, because nothing fell out.; None. There are still 4 on the plate.)

### a2 · Floor Number Line: count down to zero and lift off (12 min)
- **problem:** Where is zero on the number line, and how do you get there?
- **record:** Draw your number line from 0 to 10 on paper. Draw the hops from 3 back to 0.
- **s1 (3 min)**

  *Parent says:* Here is zero. Where does it go on our line? Why there?  
  *Child mode:* Lay the numbers 1 to 10 in a line on the floor. Now where does 0 go?
  - Ask: Why does 0 go before 1? (good: It is one less than 1.; It is the smallest.; You count back 2, 1, 0.)
  - Note: Zero goes one step before 1, with the same gap as the others. Tape the slips so that they do not slide on tiles.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child stands on 5. 2. They hop back one slip at a time and say each number they land on. 3. On 0 they jump up and shout 'Lift off!'. 4. Do it again from 8 and from 10.

  - Note: The number of hops to zero is the same as the number your child started on. Let them notice it after two or three goes. Do not point it out the first time.
  - Check: You stand on 3 and hop back one step at a time. Which numbers do you say? → **2, 1, 0**

  *Child mode:* Stand on 10. Roll the zero dice. Hop back that many steps. If you roll 0, stay still.
  - Ask: You rolled 0. Where are you now? Why? (good: In the same place.; Zero hops means I do not move.)
  - Note: Rolling zero feels like a wasted turn. That is the idea: hopping zero steps leaves you on the same number. It takes about four rolls to get from 10 to 0.
### a3 · Maths in cricket: score one over, dot balls and all (15 min)
- **problem:** A cricket scorer writes something for every ball, even a ball with no runs. Why?
- **safety:** Play in a corridor or a room with nothing breakable nearby. Bowl underarm and gently.
- **record:** Draw two rows of six boxes, one row for each batter. Write the runs for each ball in a box. For a ball with zero runs, put a dot. Write each total at the end of the row.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stand the bottle at one end of the corridor. Your child bats in front of it. 2. Agree on the runs. A miss, or a hit that does not go past the bowler, is 0 runs. A hit that goes past the bowler is 1 run. A hit that reaches the far wall is 2 runs. 3. Draw two rows of six boxes on the paper.  
  *Parent says:* An over is six balls. We will write the runs for every ball. If you get zero runs, we put a dot. Cricket scorers call that a dot ball.  
  *Child mode:* Set up the bottle as stumps. Draw two rows of six boxes. A miss is 0 runs. Past the bowler is 1. The far wall is 2.
  - Note: In real cricket, the scorer makes a dot in the book for a ball with no runs. That is where the name 'dot ball' comes from. If the ball hits the bottle, simply score it as 0 runs and play on, so that everyone faces six balls.
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Bowl six balls underarm to your child. 2. After each ball, your child says the runs and writes them in the next box, with a dot for zero. 3. For each run, they drop one bean in the cup. For a dot ball they drop no bean and say 'zero'.  
  *Parent says:* How many runs was that? Write it. How many beans go in the cup?  
  *Child mode:* Bat six balls. Write the runs after each ball. A dot for zero. One bean in the cup for each run.
  - Ask: That was a dot ball. How many beans go in the cup? (good: None.; Zero beans.)
  - Note: Watch for a skipped box after a miss. Every ball gets a box, even when the box holds only a dot. Otherwise nobody knows how many balls have been bowled.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Swap. You bat six balls and your child bowls and keeps the score in the second row. 2. Use a second cup, or tip the first one out after counting its beans.  
  *Parent says:* Now I bat and you are the scorer. Do not forget my dot balls.  
  *Child mode:* Now you bowl and keep score. Write the runs for every ball. Dots too.
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Count the beans for each batter and write the totals. 2. Count the dots in each row. 3. Ask the questions below.  
  *Parent says:* Count the dots in your row. How many runs did all those dot balls add to your total?  
  *Child mode:* Count the beans for your total. Count your dots. How many runs did the dots add?
  - Ask: Why does the scorer write a dot, and not just leave the box empty? (good: So we know the ball was bowled.; Or we lose count of the six balls.; An empty box could mean we forgot.)
  - Note: The dots add zero runs, however many there are. A batter who is out without scoring any run is said to be 'out for a duck'. The name is short for 'duck's egg'. People say that is because a 0 on the scoreboard looks like an egg.
  - Check: A batter scores 1, 0, 0, 2 on four balls. How many runs in all? → **3**

### Zero and None (core) (core, 12 questions)
1. (fill) Count the dots in row B. There are ____ dots in row B.  
   **Answer:** ['0', 'zero']. *Why:* Row B has no dots. The number for none is zero, and we write 0.
2. (mcq) 2 bananas are in the bowl. You eat both. How many bananas are left in the bowl?  
   Options: 2 / 1 / 0  
   **Answer:** 0. *Why:* 2 take away 2 leaves none. The number for none is 0. Picking 1 means taking away only one banana.
3. (truefalse) Zero is the number that tells how many when there are none.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. Zero answers 'how many?' when there are none of the things you are counting.
4. (fill) Count back: 3, 2, 1, ____.  
   **Answer:** ['0', 'zero']. *Why:* Each number is one fewer than the one before. One fewer than 1 is 0.
5. (mcq) Which plate has zero vadas?  
   Options: Plate A / Plate B / Plate C  
   **Answer:** Plate B. *Why:* Plate B has no vadas, so it has zero. Plate A has 2 and plate C has 4.
6. (match) Match each number to its dots.  
   **Answer:** 2 → ● ●; 0 → (no dots); 4 → ● ● ● ●; 1 → ●. *Why:* Count the dots in each group. The group with no dots matches 0.
7. (perspective) A plate is empty. Amma asks how many laddus are on it.  
   **Answer:** A strong answer picks a side and gives a reason. For example: zero, because it answers 'how many?'. Arun is right that there is nothing to touch or point at.. *Why:* Today zero is used as a number everywhere. But for a very long time, many people thought as Arun does.
8. (order) Put these numbers in order, smallest first.  
   **Answer:** ['0', '1', '3', '5']. *Why:* Zero is the smallest, because none is fewer than 1. Then come 1, 3 and 5.
9. (mcq) 1 idli is on your plate. You eat it. How many idlis are on your plate now?  
   Options: 1 / 0 / 2  
   **Answer:** 0. *Why:* One fewer than 1 is 0. Picking 1 means the idli was not taken away.
10. (truefalse) There are 0 elephants in your kitchen.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True, unless you live in a very unusual home. There are none, and the number for none is zero.
11. (draw) Draw a plate with 3 laddus. Next to it, draw a plate with 0 laddus. Write the number under each plate.  
   **Answer:** One plate with exactly 3 laddus and the numeral 3, and one empty plate with the numeral 0.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the empty plate too. Zero laddus still needs a plate and a number.
12. (write) Say it aloud. You take the last bean off a plate. Why is zero the right number for the plate now?  
   **Answer:** Because there are no beans left on it. Zero is the number for none.. *Why:* A strong answer says that zero tells how many when there are none.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) Which number tells how many laddus are on an empty plate?  
   Options: 1 / 0 / There is no number for that  
   **Answer:** 0. *Why:* Zero is the number for none. Picking 'no number' means thinking that numbers are only for things you can see.
2. (fill) Look at the picture. Row B has ____ dots.  
   **Answer:** ['0', 'zero']. *Why:* Row B has no dots, so the number is zero.
3. (mcq) 1 idli is on the plate. You eat it. How many idlis are left?  
   Options: 1 / 0 / 2  
   **Answer:** 0. *Why:* One fewer than 1 is 0. Taking away the last one leaves zero.
4. (truefalse) If you take away all the beans on a plate, zero beans are left.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. When all are taken away, none are left, and the number for none is zero.
5. (mcq) Where is zero on the number line?  
   Options: One step before 1 / After 10 / Between 1 and 2  
   **Answer:** One step before 1. *Why:* Zero is one fewer than 1, so it sits one step before 1. Picking 'after 10' may come from the 0 in the numeral 10.
6. (mcq) You stand on 1 and hop back one step. Where do you land?  
   Options: On 2 / On 1 / On 0  
   **Answer:** On 0. *Why:* One step back from 1 is 0. Picking 2 means hopping forward.
7. (fill) Count back: 4, 3, 2, 1, ____.  
   **Answer:** ['0', 'zero']. *Why:* Each number is one fewer. One fewer than 1 is 0.
8. (mcq) A countdown goes 3, 2, 1. What comes next?  
   Options: 0 / 4 / 1 again  
   **Answer:** 0. *Why:* A countdown counts back to zero. After 1 comes 0.
9. (mcq) Which plate has fewer vadas?  
   Options: Plate A / Plate B / They are the same  
   **Answer:** Plate A. *Why:* Plate A has 0 vadas and plate B has 1. Zero is fewer than 1. Picking 'the same' means not counting the empty plate.
10. (order) Put these three numbers in order, starting with the smallest.  
   **Answer:** ['0', '1', '2']. *Why:* Zero is the smallest of the three, then 1, then 2.
11. (mcq) A table has zero spoons on it. Which is true?  
   Options: Nothing at all is on the table / No spoons are on it. Other things may be / The table is gone  
   **Answer:** No spoons are on it. Other things may be. *Why:* Zero spoons tells you only about spoons. A cloth or a tumbler may still be there. Zero is always zero of something.
12. (truefalse) A room with zero chairs must have nothing at all in it.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Zero chairs means no chairs. The room may still have a mat, a fan and a window.

## Applications
- A cricket scorer writes a dot for every ball with zero runs, so that all six balls of the over are counted.
- A petrol pump meter is set to 0 before each fill, so that you pay only for your own petrol.
- A shopkeeper checks that the weighing scale shows 0 before putting the rice on it.
- A rocket countdown ends at zero. Zero is the moment the rocket lifts off.
- A stopwatch starts at 0, so that it shows only the time of this race.
- A lift uses 0 or G for the ground floor.
- A doctor writes 0 when a child has no fever days this week. A blank space could mean nobody checked.
- Our way of writing numbers needs 0. Without it, 10 and 1 would look the same. You will meet this in the unit on tens and ones.

## Spotted around us
- Lift buttons. Look for the 0 or the G. Is there a button below it?
- A cricket scoreboard at the start of a match. Both teams have 0 runs.
- The petrol pump. Before the petrol flows, the person at the pump shows you that the meter says 0.
- A medu vada or a bangle. They are round with a hole in the middle, like the numeral 0.
- The weighing scale at a vegetable shop. It should show 0 before anything is put on it.
- Everyone does maths: honeybees. In a test in 2018, bees were trained to fly to the card with fewer shapes. When one card was empty, they chose the empty card more often than not.
- Everyone does maths: crows. In 2021, scientists trained two crows to tell small numbers of dots apart. One of the choices was a screen with no dots. One crow mixed up zero dots with 1 dot more often than with 2 dots. That is what you would expect if none comes just before one for her.
- The sky in the daytime. You can count zero stars, apart from the Sun. At night, count again.

## Open questions
- **Was zero found or made?** Some thinkers say zero was always there, waiting to be noticed. Others say people made it, as they make words. Nobody has found a test that settles it.
- **How much do bees and crows understand about none?** The tests so far used a few trained animals. Nobody knows whether wild bees or crows use the idea of none in their daily lives.
- **Who first wrote zero as a round ring, and why round?** The oldest written zeros in India are dots. Small circles come later. No old book says who chose the circle or why.
- **Is there any place in the universe with truly nothing in it?** Even the space between the stars has a little gas, light and energy in it. Physicists do not agree on whether a place with nothing at all could exist.

Plate C has 1. Plate A has 2. Zero comes before 1, and 1 comes before 2. (queues `math.number-sense.comparing-ordering.L1`)
