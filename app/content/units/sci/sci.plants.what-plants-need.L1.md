# What plants need: light and water
`sci.plants.what-plants-need.L1`

**Summary.** Methi seeds sprout even under an upturned bowl, in the dark. But they come up yellow, not green. Plants need water to grow, and light to make their green colour and their food. Your child grows methi on three plates: dry, wet in the light, and wet in the dark. Then they watch the yellow sprouts turn green in the light.

**Intro.** *Peel a cabbage, one leaf at a time. The outside leaves are green. The leaves deep inside are pale, almost white. Why?* It is the same cabbage, and the same plant. But the inside leaves grew wrapped up in the dark. The outside leaves grew in the sunlight. So does a plant need light to be green? And what else does a plant need? In this unit we grow methi seeds in the light and in the dark, and with and without water, and we watch what happens.

## Learning outcomes
0. By the end I can say two things a plant needs to grow: water and light.
1. By the end I can say what happens to methi sprouts in the dark: they come up yellow and thin.
2. By the end I can say that a plant makes their own green colour, and their own food, using light.
3. By the end I can tell when a plant needs water: the leaves droop and the soil is dry.
4. By the end I can make a guess, look carefully over a few days, and say what I saw.

## Terms
- **Need**: A need is something a living being must have to live and grow. *e.g.* You need water and food. A plant needs water and light.
- **Sprout**: A sprout is a very young plant that has just come out of a seed. *e.g.* Soak methi or moong seeds, and in a day or two, tiny sprouts come out.
- **Wilt**: A plant wilts when they are short of water. The leaves and stem go soft and droop. *e.g.* A tulsi plant left without water on a hot day wilts. After a drink, the leaves stand up again.
- **Sunlight**: Sunlight is the light that comes from the Sun. Plants use light to make their food. *e.g.* A sunny windowsill gets lots of sunlight. A closed cupboard gets none.

## Explanations
### Plants need water · *defined*
**Definition:** Every plant needs water to live and grow. A dry seed only starts to sprout once they soak up water.

A plant drinks water through their roots. The water keeps the stems and leaves firm. Without water they wilt: the leaves droop and go soft. A dry seed waits for months, and starts to sprout once they soak up water. Too much water is not good either, because roots that sit in water for days can rot.
- e.g. Methi seeds on a dry plate stay just as they are. On a wet plate, they sprout in a day or two.
- e.g. A tulsi plant wilts on a hot day without water, and stands up again after a drink.
- e.g. Rice fields in Thanjavur and Kerala are kept full of water while the young rice plants grow.
### Plants need light · *defined*
**Definition:** Green plants make their own food in their leaves, using light, air and water.

Humans and other animals eat food. Plants make their own food in their green leaves, and they need light to do it. A seed has a little food stored inside, so a sprout can grow for a few days in the dark. But they come up yellow and thin, and without light they grow weak.
- e.g. Methi under an upturned bowl sprouts, but comes up yellow and thin.
- e.g. The inside leaves of a cabbage grow in the dark and are pale.
- e.g. House plants grow best near a window.
### A sprout makes their green in the light · *debated*
The green in a leaf is made by the plant. Methi makes their green only when they sense light. In the dark a sprout stays yellow and stretches tall. Put it in the light, and within a day it turns green. Scientists agree that light sets this off. They argue about how to describe what the plant does.
- e.g. Yellow methi sprouts from under the bowl turn green within a day on the windowsill.
- e.g. Moong sprouts sold in a closed packet are white and yellow, and turn green if left in the light.
- e.g. Farmers tie leaves over a cauliflower so the white part stays white.
- **Plant scientists who study how plants sense:** The sprout senses the light and chooses to change. The sprout makes green leaves only when there is light to use them. That is a sensible choice.
- **Other plant scientists:** Light sets off a chemical switch inside the sprout. The switch works the same way every time. They call this a response, and they do not call it a choice.

## Mostly agreed
- Plants need water to live and grow. *How we know:* Seeds without water do not sprout. Plants without water wilt and, after many days, dry up. Farmers everywhere water their crops or plant them when the rains come.
- Green plants need light to make their food. *How we know:* Plants kept in the dark grow yellow and weak, and in the end they die. Plants in the light keep growing. Scientists have tested this in many experiments.
- Plants make their green colour in the light. *How we know:* Sprouts of methi, moong and many other plants grow yellow in the dark and turn green within a day when light reaches them.

## How we found out
**Joseph Priestley, an English scientist (1771); Jan Ingenhousz, a Dutch doctor working in England (1779), Priestley's mint jar in 1771; Ingenhousz's tests in the summer of 1779, published the same year.** In 1771, Joseph Priestley, an English scientist, put a sprig of mint in a closed glass jar. A candle had burned out in that jar, and the air inside was used up. After some days, a candle could burn in the jar again. The mint had freshened the air. But when other people tried this, sometimes it worked and sometimes it did not. Nobody knew why. In the summer of 1779, Jan Ingenhousz, a Dutch doctor working in England, did more than 500 tests. He put leaves under water in glass jars. In sunlight, tiny bubbles came out of the green leaves. In the shade and at night, no bubbles came. He found that plants freshen the air only in light, and only with their green parts. In the dark, plants use up air, just as humans and other animals do. Today we know the bubbles were oxygen, which green leaves give out while they make food using light.  
*What people believed before:* That plants freshen the air all the time, in the light and in the dark. Ingenhousz showed that green leaves do it only in light, and that in the dark plants use up air like humans and other animals.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Plants eat soil, so a plant in soil does not need light.** (A very common idea, among children and grown-ups; *rejected*)  
  For: Plants grow in soil, and their roots go deep into it.  
  Against: Plants make their own food in their leaves, using light, air and water. Roots take in water and tiny bits called minerals from the soil. A plant in good soil but kept in the dark still grows yellow and weak.
- **The more water you give a plant, the better they grow.** (A common idea, especially when we love our plants; *rejected*)  
  For: Plants need water, and a plant without water wilts.  
  Against: Roots need air as well as water. If the soil stays soggy for days, the roots cannot get air and can rot. Most pot plants grow best when the soil is damp, not soaking.
- **Seeds need light to sprout.** (A common guess before people try it; *rejected*)  
  For: Plants need light to grow well, so it seems that seeds would need light too.  
  Against: Methi, moong and many other seeds sprout in the dark, using the food stored inside the seed. A few kinds of seed, like some lettuce seeds, do need light to sprout.

## Roots
We have seen methi grow yellow in the dark and green in the light. Now we ask what that tells us about plants, and about ourselves.

**Deep truth.** Under the bowl, our methi sprouts came up yellow. After one day in the light, the same sprouts turned green. Nobody added any colour, so the methi made the green themselves. A plant makes their green when they sense light.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think of colours as fixed, like the green of a green crayon. But a leaf's green is something the plant makes, and only when light reaches them.  
*Evidence:* In the three plates test, methi under the bowl came up yellow while the same seeds by the window came up green. After a day uncovered on the windowsill, the yellow sprouts turned green.

- *who am i:* Your body senses light too. Your eyes see it, and your skin slowly turns darker in strong sun.
- *what is life:* Being alive means sensing what is around you and answering it. The methi sensed the light and made their green.
- *what is this world:* The same Sun lights the methi, the crow and you. Nearly all the food we eat comes from plants, which use that light.

The Sun lights the methi, and the methi answers by turning green. The Sun lights your eyes, and you answer by seeing colours. It lights your skin, and your skin slowly darkens. Each of us senses the light and answers it in our own way.

- **Rig Veda 1.115.1:** The Sun is the self of all that moves and all that stays still. (Plants stay in one place, yet they grow, turn green and make food with the Sun's light. Humans and other animals, who move, eat what plants make.)
- **Thirukkural 16, by Thiruvalluvar:** Unless drops of rain fall from the sky, not even a blade of green grass will be seen. (Our dry plate showed this. With no water, the methi seeds did not sprout at all.)
- Science (settled): Plants have light sensors in their leaves, stems and roots. These tell a sprout if there is light, and what colour the light is.
- Science (open): Plants feel light. What that feeling is like for a plant, nobody knows yet.
- Other view, Many plant scientists: Many plant scientists say the methi turns green because light flips a chemical switch inside the sprout. The switch works the same way every time, so they call it a response, with no choosing.
- Practice, Light on your eyelids (3 min): Sit facing a bright window, but not the Sun itself. Close your eyes. What colour do you see through your eyelids? Now cover your eyes with your hands. What colour now? Take your hands away. Feel the light come back. The methi felt the light come back too, when we lifted the bowl.
- Ask: When the methi turned green, who decided: the methi, or the light?
- Ask: Your skin darkens in the sun, and methi turns green. How are they alike? How are they different?
- Ask: If you lived under a bowl for four days, what would you miss most?

## Deep and big questions
- *deep:* The yellow methi sprouts turned green in the light. Who made the green? (hint: Nobody painted the sprouts. Think about what changed, and what stayed the same.)
- *deep:* How does a sprout under a bowl know there is no light? (hint: Plants have light sensors all over their bodies. You have light sensors too. Where are yours?)
- *big:* We need food, and plants make food. What would we eat if there were no sunlight? (hint: Think about rice, dal, bananas and milk. Where did each one come from?)
- *big:* Why do the leaves of a plant on a shelf lean towards the window? (hint: Think about where the light comes from. What does the plant need from the light?)

## Story: The Red Bucket on the Balcony
*Lakshmi, Kannan and Paati are made up. Methi sprouts grown in the dark really are yellow, and really turn green within about a day in the light.*

Lakshmi lived in a flat in Mysuru, on the third floor. She was six. Her little brother Kannan was four. Their grandmother, Paati, lived with them.

On the balcony there was an old steel tray full of soil. One Sunday, Paati gave Lakshmi a handful of methi seeds. The seeds were small, hard and yellow-brown. 'Scatter them all over the soil,' said Paati. 'Then sprinkle a little water on top.'

Lakshmi scattered the seeds. She sprinkled the water with her fingers, like rain. 'How long will they take?' she asked.

'Keep the soil damp, and you will see,' said Paati.

Kannan was watching. He had a red plastic bucket from the beach. He turned the bucket upside down on one corner of the tray. 'This is my bucket's house,' he said. 'Nobody can move it.'

Paati laughed and let it be.

Every morning, Lakshmi sprinkled water on the tray. On Tuesday, she saw tiny white roots poking out of the seeds. On Wednesday, little stems stood up. On Thursday, each sprout opened two small round leaves. They were bright green, like new bangles.

On Friday morning, Kannan wanted his bucket back. He lifted it up, and he shouted. 'Akka! Come and see! Ghost plants!'

Lakshmi ran out. Under the bucket, there were sprouts too. But they were not green at all. They were pale yellow, and very tall and thin. Some of them had fallen over.

'Are they sick?' asked Kannan. 'Are they a different plant?'

Lakshmi looked closely. The seeds under the bucket were the same methi seeds. The soil under the bucket was the same soil, and it was still damp. She thought hard. What was different?

'It was dark under the bucket,' she said slowly. 'The bucket kept the sunlight out.'

Paati nodded. 'So what should we do now?'

'Leave the bucket off,' said Lakshmi. 'Let the light in, and see what happens.'

That night, Kannan did not want to sleep. 'Will the ghost plants still be there?' he asked. 'Will they be sad?'

Next morning, Lakshmi and Kannan ran to the balcony before breakfast. The pale corner was not pale any more. The tall, thin sprouts had turned light green, like the inside of a cucumber. By the evening, they were as green as the rest.

'Who painted them?' asked Kannan.

'Nobody,' said Lakshmi. 'The methi made the green themselves. They only make it when they feel the light.' Kannan put his hand flat over one sprout, like a tiny roof. 'Now it is dark for you,' he whispered. Then he took his hand away. 'And now it is light again.'

Paati smiled. 'Plants feel the light, just as your skin feels the sun. In a week, we will pick the methi leaves and put them in the dal.'

Kannan put his bucket away in the cupboard. 'My bucket's house can be in there,' he said. 'The methi needs the sun more than my bucket does.'

**For the parent:** Lakshmi works out that the only difference under the bucket was the dark. When the light comes back, the methi makes their own green.

- Ask: Why were the sprouts under the bucket yellow?
- Ask: The soil under the bucket was still damp. Why does that matter?
- Ask: What do you think Kannan's sprout felt under his hand?

## Experiments
### e1 · Three Plates of Methi: dry, light and dark (32 min, 6 days)
- **question:** Do methi seeds need water to sprout? What happens to methi sprouts that get water but no light?
- **predict:** Guess first. Which plate will sprout? Which sprouts will be green, and which will not? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** All three plates get the same seeds, from one packet, on the same tissue, in the same place. Plate 1 and plate 2 are different only in water. Plate 2 and plate 3 are different only in light.
- **record:** Draw the three plates on day 5. Colour the sprouts the colour you see. Draw plate 3 again on day 6.
- **results table:** Plate | Day 2: any sprouts? | Day 5: what colour? | Day 6: after a day in the light
- **schedule:** day 1: Set up the three plates. Guess what will happen. (10 min); day 2: Look at plates 1 and 2. Add 2 spoons of water to plates 2 and 3. Lift the bowl only for a moment. (3 min); day 3: Look at plate 2. Add 2 spoons of water to plates 2 and 3. (3 min); day 4: Add 2 spoons of water to plates 2 and 3. Look at plate 2. (3 min); day 5: Lift the bowl. Compare all three plates. Leave plate 3 uncovered on the windowsill. (8 min); day 6: Look at plate 3 again. Have the yellow sprouts changed colour? (5 min)
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Fold a tissue onto each plate. 2. Pour 3 spoons of water on plates 2 and 3. Leave plate 1 dry. 3. Sprinkle one teaspoon of methi seeds on each plate, spread out. 4. Draw a small label for each plate (a crossed-out drop, a sun and a drop, a bowl and a drop) and put it beside the plate.  
  *Parent says:* Here are three plates of methi seeds. Plate 1 stays dry. Plates 2 and 3 get water. Help me spread the seeds out.  
  *Child mode:* Put a tissue on each plate. Wet plates 2 and 3. Keep plate 1 dry. Sprinkle seeds on each plate.
  - Note: Use seeds from one packet for all three plates. The tissue on plates 2 and 3 should be wet but with no puddle.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put all three plates on a bright windowsill. 2. Turn the steel bowl upside down over plate 3, so no light gets in.  
  *Parent says:* Plate 3 goes under the bowl. Now it is dark in there. Plates 1 and 2 stay in the light.  
  *Child mode:* Put the plates by the window. Cover plate 3 with the bowl. Now plate 3 is in the dark.
  - Note: Choose a bright place out of hot afternoon sun. A steel bowl in strong sun gets hot and can cook the seeds. Set the bowl flat on the sill, so no light slips in under the edge.
- **s3 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Ask your child to guess for each plate, and to say why. You write the guesses down.  
  *Parent says:* Which plate will sprout? What colour will the sprouts be? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Which plate will sprout? Green or not? Why?
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: The dry seeds won't grow, because seeds need water.; The ones under the bowl won't grow, because there's no light.; The ones in the light will be green.)
  - Note: Any guess is fine. Many children guess that the seeds under the bowl will not sprout at all. Don't say what will happen.
- **s4 (9 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at plates 1 and 2. 2. Add 2 spoons of water to plates 2 and 3. Lift the bowl only for a moment to add the water.  
  *Parent says:* Look closely at plate 1 and plate 2. What has changed?  
  *Child mode:* Look at plate 1. Look at plate 2. What is different? Add water to plates 2 and 3.
  - Ask: What do you see on plate 2? (good: Tiny white roots coming out.; The seeds got bigger.)
  - Note: Wet methi seeds usually show tiny white roots within one or two days. The dry seeds stay the same. Days 3 and 4 are the same: look, then water plates 2 and 3. The tissue must stay wet, never dry.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 2. Do the same on days 3 and 4 (3 minutes each day).
- **s5 (8 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lift the bowl off plate 3. 2. Put the three plates side by side. 3. Look at the colour and the height of the sprouts on each plate. 4. Draw them. 5. Leave plate 3 uncovered on the windowsill.  
  *Parent says:* Let's lift the bowl. What do you see? How are plate 2 and plate 3 different?  
  *Child mode:* Lift the bowl. Look at plate 3. Look at plate 2. What is different?
  - Ask: Plates 2 and 3 both got water. So why are they different? (good: Plate 3 was in the dark.; There was no light under the bowl.)
  - Note: Sprouts from the dark are yellow, taller and thinner, and may lean over. If plate 3 has no sprouts, the seeds were too dry or too hot: try again with more water, out of the sun.
  - Check: The sprouts under the bowl had water, but no light. What colour were they? → **Yellow**
  - Wait: Come back on day 5.
- **s6 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at plate 3 again, after a day in the light. 2. Put it next to your drawing from yesterday.  
  *Parent says:* Yesterday these sprouts were yellow. Look at them now. What happened? Who made the change?  
  *Child mode:* Look at plate 3 again. Are the sprouts still yellow? What changed?
  - Ask: Why did the sprouts turn green? (good: Because they got light.; The methi made the green when the light came.)
  - Note: Most yellow sprouts turn light green within a day in the light, and greener the day after. If they still look yellow, wait one more day. Methi needs water to sprout, and light to turn green.
  - Wait: Come back on day 6.

### p1 · Grow Methi to Eat (25 min, 12 days)

### Light and Water (stretch) (stretch, 11 questions)
1. (mcq) Anu, a girl in Class 1, puts methi seeds on wet tissue under a clear glass bowl. What colour will the sprouts be?  
   Options: Green / Yellow / No sprouts at all  
   **Answer:** Green. *Why:* Light goes through clear glass, so the sprouts still get light. They will be green.
2. (match) Draw a line from each sign to what the plant is short of.  
   **Answer:** Tall, thin, yellow sprouts → Light; Droopy leaves and dry soil → Water; Pale leaves inside a cabbage → Light; Soft brown roots in soggy soil → Too much water. *Why:* Yellow, thin sprouts and pale leaves mean little light. Droopy leaves and dry soil mean little water. Roots in soggy soil cannot get air.
3. (sort) Sort these: does light go through, or is the light blocked?  
   **Answer:** a clear glass → Light goes through; a steel plate → Light is blocked; a clear plastic bag → Light goes through; a piece of cardboard → Light is blocked. *Why:* Light passes through clear glass and clear plastic. Steel and cardboard block it, so a sprout under them is in the dark.
4. (truefalse) The pale leaves deep inside a cabbage are pale because little light reached them.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. The inside leaves grow wrapped up by the outer leaves. With little light, they make little green.
5. (draw) Draw a plant who has water but no light. Next to it, draw a plant who has light but no water.  
   **Answer:** A tall, thin, yellow plant in damp soil, in a dark place. A droopy plant with dry soil in the sun.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the two different signs: no light gives yellow and thin, no water gives droopy and dry.

   **Answer:** The methi will sprout, but they will be yellow and thin. Put them by a window, so they get light and turn green.. *Why:* A strong answer says what will happen in the dark and gives a way to fix it.
7. (label) Draw a methi sprout. Then label the parts: root, stem, leaf. Write each word, or ask a grown-up to write it.  
   **Answer:** The root is the white part at the bottom. The stem is in the middle. The leaves are at the top.. *Why:* The root takes in water. The stem holds the leaves up. The leaves catch the light.
8. (count) Draw 5 green sprouts and 3 yellow sprouts. Which colour did you draw more of?  
   **Answer:** There are more green sprouts: 5 is more than 3.. *Why:* 5 is more than 3. A strong answer counts the drawing and says which colour has more.
9. (order) Put the steps of the three plates test in order.  
   **Answer:** ['Wet the tissue on plates 2 and 3', 'Put the seeds on the plates', 'Cover plate 3 with a bowl', 'Lift the bowl and look']. *Why:* First wet the tissue and add the seeds, then cover plate 3. Look at the end.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) What do plants need to grow?  
   Options: Light and water / Sweets and toys / Milk and biscuits  
   **Answer:** Light and water. *Why:* Plants need light and water. They make their own food, so they do not need sweets or milk.
2. (mcq) Methi seeds sat on a dry plate for two days. What happened?  
   Options: They sprouted / Nothing, they stayed dry seeds / They turned green  
   **Answer:** Nothing, they stayed dry seeds. *Why:* Seeds need water to sprout. Dry seeds stay as they are.
3. (mcq) Methi sprouts grew under an upturned steel bowl. What did they look like?  
   Options: Short and green / Tall, thin and yellow / Blue  
   **Answer:** Tall, thin and yellow. *Why:* In the dark, methi sprouts come up yellow, tall and thin.
4. (mcq) The yellow sprouts were put on the windowsill for a day. What happened?  
   Options: They turned green / They turned red / They stayed yellow for ever  
   **Answer:** They turned green. *Why:* In the light, the methi made their green. Most sprouts turn green within a day.
5. (mcq) Who made the green in the methi leaves?  
   Options: The water / The methi plant / The steel plate  
   **Answer:** The methi plant. *Why:* The plant makes their own green, when they sense light.
6. (mcq) Where do plants make their food?  
   Options: In their green leaves / In the soil / In the kitchen  
   **Answer:** In their green leaves. *Why:* Plants make food in their green leaves, using light, air and water. Many people think plants eat soil, but the roots only take in water and minerals.
7. (mcq) A plant has droopy leaves and dry soil. What does the plant need?  
   Options: Water / Darkness / A new pot  
   **Answer:** Water. *Why:* Droopy leaves and dry soil mean the plant is short of water.
8. (mcq) Is a soggy pot that never dries good for a plant?  
   Options: Yes, more water is always better / No, roots need air too  
   **Answer:** No, roots need air too. *Why:* Roots in soggy soil for days cannot get air and can rot. Damp soil is just right.
9. (mcq) Before you lifted the bowl, what did a good scientist do first?  
   Options: Guessed what would happen, and said why / Threw the seeds away / Painted the sprouts  
   **Answer:** Guessed what would happen, and said why. *Why:* Scientists guess first, then look carefully, then say what they saw.
10. (mcq) Two plates of methi seeds get the same water. One is under a bowl, one is in the light. Why give them the same water?  
   Options: So that light was the only difference / Because we had extra water / So the bowl would float  
   **Answer:** So that light was the only difference. *Why:* When only one thing is different, we know that thing made the change. Here, the only difference was light.

## Applications
- Farmers water their fields, or sow seeds when the monsoon rains come.

- Astronauts on the International Space Station grow lettuce under bright lamps, because plants need light.

## Open questions
- **What is it like for a plant to feel light?** Plants have light sensors, and they answer the light. But nobody knows what feeling light is like for a plant.
- **Why are most plants green, and not black?** A black leaf could take in more of the light. Scientists have several ideas about why leaves are green, and they still argue about it.
- **Could we grow plants on Mars?** Mars gets less sunlight, and the soil and air are very different. Scientists are testing plants in Mars-like soil on Earth, but nobody has grown a plant on Mars yet.
