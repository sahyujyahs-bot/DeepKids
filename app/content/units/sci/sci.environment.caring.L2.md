# Caring for our environment: why some litter traps animals, and what we can do
`sci.environment.caring.L2`

**Summary.** A loop of wet newspaper tears at one gentle pull, but a loop cut from a milk packet only stretches and holds. That is why plastic litter can trap a bird, a cow or a turtle, and paper usually cannot. Your child soaks loops of paper, cloth and plastic in water and tries to pull each one apart. Then we find out how litter harms the animals who live around us, and the real ways families, villages and cities in India care for their environment.

**Intro.** *On the day after a kite festival, people in many Indian cities find birds caught in kite string on trees and balconies. Why can't the bird just break the string and fly away?* Some string is so strong that a bird cannot break it, however hard the bird pulls. Strong plastic litter can trap animals the same way. Our environment is everything around us: the air, the water, the land and every living being. In this unit we test which kinds of litter can trap an animal. Then we learn what people in India do to care for the places we share.

## Terms
- **Environment**: Our environment is everything around us: air, water, land, plants, animals and people. *e.g.* Your building, the road, the neem tree outside, the crows and the drain are all part of your environment.
- **Litter**: Litter is rubbish left in the wrong place, outside a bin. *e.g.* A chips packet on the pavement and a plastic bag in a lake are litter.
- **Trapped**: An animal is trapped when they are caught in something and cannot get free. *e.g.* A pigeon with kite string wound round their foot is trapped.
- **Plastic**: Plastic is a material made by people, mostly from oil. Most plastic is strong, light and does not soak up water. *e.g.* Milk packets, carry bags, straws and nylon string are made of plastic.
- **Habitat**: A habitat is the place where a living being lives and finds what they need. *e.g.* A lake is a habitat for fish, frogs, ducks and lotus plants.
- **Protect**: To protect means to keep someone or something safe from harm. *e.g.* Women in Assam protect the nests of big storks in their village trees.

## Explanations
### What our environment is · *defined*
**Definition:** Our environment is everything around us. It has living parts and non-living parts.

The living parts are plants, animals, fungi and tiny microbes. People are animals too, so we are part of it. The non-living parts are air, water, soil, rock and sunlight. Every being needs the others to live.
- e.g. A crow needs trees for a nest and clean water to drink.
- e.g. A neem tree needs soil, sunlight, rain and air.
- e.g. You need clean air, clean water and food that grew in soil.
### How litter harms animals · *defined*
**Definition:** Litter harms animals in two main ways. They get trapped in it, or they eat it.

Birds get caught in kite string and in loops of plastic. Cows on Indian streets eat plastic bags that have food scraps inside. Vets have taken heaps of plastic out of cows' stomachs. Turtles in the sea can swallow floating bags.
- e.g. A pigeon with kite string wound round a foot.
- e.g. A cow eating a plastic bag with vegetable peels in it.
- e.g. A dog cutting a paw on broken glass.
### Why plastic is the most dangerous litter · *defined*
**Definition:** Plastic stays strong in water and does not rot away quickly. So a plastic loop or string can trap an animal for a long time.

Water makes paper soft and weak, so a wet paper loop tears. Plastic does not soak up water, so it stays strong. Paper and cotton rot away in weeks or months. Most plastic lasts for many years, breaking only into smaller and smaller pieces.
- e.g. A wet paper bag tears, but a wet plastic bag does not.
- e.g. Cotton kite string breaks more easily than nylon string, which is a plastic.
- e.g. A plastic bag can float in a lake for years.
### How long plastic lasts · *debated*
Everyone agrees that most plastic lasts much longer than paper, cloth or peels. But nobody has watched a plastic bag for hundreds of years, because plastic bags are only about 60 years old. So the numbers are careful guesses.
- e.g. A plastic bag left in sunlight cracks into small bits, but the bits stay.
- e.g. A plastic bottle in the sea can last a very long time.
- **Many scientists:** Plastic breaks into tiny pieces in sunlight and waves, and those pieces may last hundreds of years.
- **Other scientists:** Some plastics may break down faster than we thought, for example in strong sunlight. The time depends on the kind of plastic and where it is.
### Ways we can care for our environment · *defined*
**Definition:** We care for our environment when we keep litter out of it, use less plastic, and protect the beings who live in it.

Put litter in a bin, and sort it into wet and dry waste. Snip every loop before you throw it away, so it cannot trap an animal. Carry a cloth bag. Keep a bowl of water for birds in summer. Plant trees and look after them.
- e.g. Snip a rubber band before it goes in the bin.
- e.g. Fly kites with cotton string, not nylon string.
- e.g. Pick up kite string left on the terrace after the festival.

## Mostly agreed
- Litter harms many animals, by trapping them or when they eat it. *How we know:* Vets and bird rescuers in India treat birds cut by kite string and cows sick from eating plastic bags. Scientists find plastic in the stomachs of many sea birds and turtles.
- Plastic lasts far longer in water and soil than paper, cloth or peels. *How we know:* In your wet loop test, the paper tore and the plastic held. Tests that bury things in soil find peels and paper rotting in weeks, while plastic looks the same.
- Nylon kite string is dangerous to birds and people. *How we know:* In 2017 India's National Green Tribunal, a court for the environment, banned nylon and other plastic kite string across the country, because it was hurting birds, other animals and people.

## How we found out
**Jennifer Lavers, an Australian scientist, with Hayley Charlton-Howard, Alex Bond and Jack Rivers-Auty, Years of study on Lord Howe Island, and the report in 2023.** Flesh-footed shearwaters are sea birds who nest on Lord Howe Island, off the coast of Australia. The parents fly over the sea and bring back food for their chicks. But they also pick up floating bits of plastic, and feed those to their chicks too. Scientists found plastic in the stomachs of most of the chicks. For a long time, many people thought that if a chick stayed alive, the plastic did no real harm. Birds swallow small stones too, such as light pumice stones that float on the sea, and the stones seem to do no harm. Jennifer Lavers, an Australian scientist, had studied these birds for years. With Alex Bond, Hayley Charlton-Howard and Jack Rivers-Auty, she looked at the stomachs of chicks under a microscope. In 2023 they reported that plastic left scars inside the stomach. The more plastic, the more scars. Pumice did not leave scars like that. They named the new illness plasticosis. So plastic harms a bird even when the bird does not die.  
*What people believed before:* Many people thought that if a sea bird chick swallowed plastic and stayed alive, the plastic did no real harm, like the small stones birds swallow.

## Challenging perspectives
- **A little bit of litter does no harm.** (A common idea; *rejected*)  
  For: One wrapper looks small, and a sweeper may pick it up.  
  Against: One loop of string can trap one bird. Many people each dropping a little make a lot of litter, and rain carries it into drains and lakes.
- **Paper bags are always better for the environment than plastic bags.** (Many people; *debated*)  
  For: Paper rots away in weeks and does not trap animals for years.  
  Against: Making a paper bag uses trees, water and energy, often more than a thin plastic bag. A cloth bag used many, many times is better than both. Scientists still argue about which bag is best overall.
- **Caring for the environment is only the government's job.** (Some people; *debated*)  
  For: Cities have sweepers, laws and big trucks for waste. A family is small.  
  Against: Laws help a lot, but ordinary people have also saved forests, birds and beaches. The women of Reni village saved their forest, and the women of the Hargila Army saved a rare stork.

## Roots
We have tested which wet loops tear and which hold, and learned how litter traps animals. Now we ask what that tells us about the things we throw away and the beings who meet them.

**Deep truth.** In your bowl, the wet paper loop tore at the first gentle pull. The wet plastic loop only stretched and held. A bird caught in paper can pull free, but not from plastic. So what litter is made of decides how much harm it can do.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think all litter is the same: just mess that looks untidy. But a wrapper, a peel and a rubber band are not the same to the animal who meets them. The material decides whether it rots away, tears, or traps.  
*Evidence:* In the Wet Loop Test, the dry paper held and the wet paper tore, so the water made it weak. The wet cloth and the wet plastic held for all five pulls. In 2023, scientists found that plastic scarred the stomachs of sea bird chicks, while pumice stones did not.

- *who am i:* You are one of the many animals who share this place. What you drop, another animal meets.
- *what is life:* A pigeon feels the string tighten and decides to pull. Every being senses danger and tries to get free.
- *what is this world:* The world is shared. A lake belongs to the fish, the ducks, the lotus and the people who live by it.

A piece of litter does not stop mattering when it leaves our hand. It goes on to meet someone: a cow by the road, a pigeon on a roof, a turtle in the sea. Each of them senses it, and decides what to do: eat it, pull against it, or pass it by. What happens next depends on what the litter is made of. So caring for the environment means thinking about the beings who will meet what we leave behind.

- **Bhagavata Purana 7.14.9:** Look on deer, camels, donkeys, monkeys, mice, snakes, birds and flies as you would look on your own children. (A parent would never leave a trap where their child plays. Snipping a loop is caring for a bird the way we care for our own family.)
- **Karaniya Metta Sutta (Sutta Nipata 1.8), a teaching of the Buddha:** Whatever living beings there are, weak or strong, big or small, seen or unseen, near or far, may they all be well. (We rarely see the cow at the dump or the turtle in the sea who meets our litter. They are the unseen and far-away beings this teaching asks us to wish well.)
- Science (settled): Plastic does not soak up water, so it stays strong when wet. Most plastic lasts many years in water and soil.
- Science (open): How long plastic lasts, and what tiny plastic bits do inside animals, are still being studied.
- Other view, Some people who study waste: Some people say that one family snipping loops makes only a small difference. Most plastic litter comes from how things are made, packed and sold, and from places with no proper waste collection. They say laws, companies and good city waste systems matter more than one family's habits. Others reply that families, villages and laws work best together.
- Practice, Feel the Loop (3 min): Put a rubber band loosely round two of your fingers. Close your eyes. Breathe in slowly, and out slowly, three times. Now gently spread your fingers. Feel the band hold them. Think of a bird who feels this round their foot. Now take the band off and snip it open. How does the strip feel now?
- Ask: When you throw something away, who do you think meets it next?
- Ask: Does a cow searching a rubbish heap know which things are safe to eat?
- Ask: Who does a lake belong to?

## Story: The String on the Balcony
*Meenakshi, Arun, Amma and Dr. Rehana are made up. Kite flying at Sankranti is a big festival in Hyderabad and in many parts of India. Nylon kite string was banned in India in 2017 because it hurts birds and people. If you find a trapped bird, call an animal rescue group or a vet, and do not try to pull the string yourself.*

Meenakshi lived on the sixth floor of a building in Hyderabad. She was seven. Her little brother Arun was five.

It was Sankranti, the kite festival. All day, the sky was full of kites. Red kites, green kites and kites with long paper tails flew over every roof.

Arun loved the kites. "I want to fly one too!" he said.

The next morning, Meenakshi heard a strange flapping sound on the balcony. She looked out through the glass door.

A grey pigeon was sitting on the railing. A long piece of kite string was wound round one of the pigeon's feet. The other end was caught on a nail in the wall. The pigeon flapped and pulled, flapped and pulled. But the string did not break.

"Amma!" Meenakshi called. "A pigeon is caught!"

Amma came quickly. She looked at the string. "We must not pull it," she said. "Pulling can cut the pigeon's foot. Let us call Dr. Rehana."

Dr. Rehana was a vet who lived in the next building. She looked after hurt birds and other animals. She came up in ten minutes, with a towel and a small pair of scissors.

She put the towel gently over the pigeon. The pigeon went still under the soft dark cloth. Then she snipped the string, bit by bit, until the foot was free.

"This foot is only a little red," said Dr. Rehana. "She was lucky. You called me fast."

Arun looked at the string in her hand. "Why didn't the pigeon just break it?" he asked. "She pulled so hard."

"Feel it," said Dr. Rehana. Arun pulled the string. It did not break. It cut into his fingers a little, so he stopped.

"This string is made of nylon," said Dr. Rehana. "Nylon is a kind of plastic. It is very strong, even when it is wet. A bird cannot break it."

She took the towel off. The pigeon stood up, shook her feathers, and flew to the next roof.

That evening, Meenakshi had an idea. She cut a strip from an old newspaper and a strip from an empty milk packet. She tied each one into a loop and put both loops in a bowl of water.

After ten minutes she took out the paper loop and pulled it gently. It tore at once. Then she pulled the plastic loop. It stretched and went thin, but it did not tear.

"Look, Arun," said Meenakshi. "A wet paper loop could never trap a bird. But a plastic loop could."

Arun pulled the plastic loop too. "It is like the kite string," he said.

The next day, Meenakshi went up to the terrace with Amma. Bits of kite string hung from the water tank and the TV dish. They lay in tangles on the floor.

Meenakshi showed Arun how to pick them up and wind them round a stick. "Every bit we pick up is one less trap," she said.

They filled a whole bag. Arun counted the sticks of wound string. There were eleven.

"Next Sankranti," said Arun, "can I fly a kite with cotton string?"

"Yes," said Amma. "And we will fly it in the middle of the day, when fewer birds are flying. And we will pick up every bit of string when we finish."

On the railing, the grey pigeon was back, pecking at a few grains of rice. Both her feet were free.

**For the parent:** A loop that a bird cannot break is a trap. Plastic stays strong even when wet, so plastic string and loops can trap animals. Picking them up keeps the birds safe.

- Ask: Why did Dr. Rehana snip the string instead of pulling it?
- Ask: Why did the paper loop tear but the plastic loop did not?
- Ask: What could Meenakshi's family do at the next Sankranti to keep birds safe?

## Experiments
### e1 · The Wet Loop Test: which litter could a bird pull free from? (27 min)
- **question:** After a soak in water, which loops tear when you pull them gently: paper, cloth or plastic? Which loops stay whole?
- **predict:** Guess first. Which wet loop will tear: paper, cloth or plastic? Which will stay whole? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Every strip is the same size: two fingers wide and as long as a school ruler. Every wet loop soaks for the same 10 minutes. You pull every loop the same gentle way, up to 5 pulls. We also keep one dry paper loop, so we can see what the water did to the paper.
- **safety:** A grown-up cuts the strips. Pull gently with your fingers. Stop if a loop pinches or cuts into your fingers.
- **record:** Fill in the table. Then draw the four loops after the test, showing which ones tore.
- **results table:** Loop | My guess: tear or stay whole? | Number of pulls before it tore (stop at 5) | What it looked like at the end
- **s1 (5 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut two newspaper strips, one cloth strip and one milk-packet strip, all the same size. 2. Help your child tie each strip into a loop with one knot.  
  *Parent says:* These loops are like litter. A bird or a cow could get a foot or a neck caught in a loop. Let's find out which loops they could break.  
  *Child mode:* A grown-up cuts four strips. Tie each one into a loop.
  - Note: Make the strips two fingers wide and about 30 centimetres long. Tie the paper loop loosely, so it does not tear while you knot it.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Hold up each loop. Ask for a guess and a reason for each. Write the guesses in the table.  
  *Parent says:* We will soak three loops in water, like litter in a lake. Which wet loop will tear when you pull it? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Which wet loop will tear? Why do you think so?
  - Ask: Which wet loop will tear? Why? (good: The paper, because water makes paper soft.; None of them, because they are all strong.; The cloth, because it soaks up water.)
  - Note: Any guess is fine. Write it down with the reason. The test will answer it.
- **s3 (10 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put the paper, cloth and plastic loops in the bowl. 2. Push them under the water. 3. Keep the second paper loop dry on the table. 4. Wait 10 minutes.  
  *Parent says:* While we wait, let's look. Which loop is soaking up water? Which one is floating? Draw the bowl.  
  *Child mode:* Put three loops in the water. Wait 10 minutes. Keep one paper loop dry.
  - Note: Use the 10 minutes to draw the bowl, or to read the story. The plastic strip may float, so push it under now and then.
  - Wait: 10 minutes
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Start with the dry paper loop. 2. Your child hooks the loop over two fingers of each hand. 3. They pull their hands apart gently, up to 5 times. 4. Do the same with each wet loop. 5. Write the number of pulls.  
  *Parent says:* Pull gently, like a bird trying to get free. Count each pull. Stop at 5, or when the loop tears.  
  *Child mode:* Hook a loop over your fingers. Pull gently. Count each pull.
  - Note: The wet paper usually tears at the first or second pull. The dry paper, the cloth and the plastic usually hold for all 5 pulls. The plastic stretches and goes thin, but stays whole. If the plastic pinches, stop.
- **s5 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Fill in the table together. Ask your child three things: what they think, what they saw, and why.  
  *Parent says:* Which loop tore? Which loops stayed whole? Look at the two paper loops. What did the water do to the paper?  
  *Child mode:* Look at your table. What did you find? Why?
  - Check: Which wet loop tore when you pulled it? → **The paper loop**
  - Think: A pigeon gets a foot caught in a wet plastic loop in a puddle. Can she pull free?
### e2 · Bag in the Bucket: does a floating plastic bag look like a living animal? (15 min)
- **question:** In water, does a thin plastic bag look and move like a floating jellyfish? Do paper and cloth look like that too?
- **predict:** Guess first. Which one will look most like a floating jellyfish: the plastic bag, the paper or the cloth? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Each thing goes into the same bucket of water. You stir the water the same way, three slow turns with a spoon. You watch each one from the side for the same time, one minute.
- **record:** Draw each thing in the water, and the jellyfish picture next to them.
- **results table:** Thing in the water | My guess: looks like a jellyfish? | Does it float, sink or drift? | Does it look like a jellyfish? Draw it.
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Show a picture of a jellyfish. Ask for a guess and a reason.  
  *Parent says:* Sea turtles eat jellyfish. Which of these three will look most like a jellyfish in water? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the jellyfish picture. Guess which thing will look like a jellyfish. Why?
  - Ask: Which one will look like a jellyfish? Why? (good: The plastic bag, because it is thin and see-through.; The cloth, because it is white and soft.)
- **s2 (9 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put the plastic bag in the water. Push it under once. 2. Stir three slow turns. 3. Watch from the side for one minute. 4. Take it out. Do the same with the paper, then the cloth.  
  *Parent says:* Watch it from the side, like a turtle under the water. Does it float, sink or drift? Does it look alive?  
  *Child mode:* Put in one thing at a time. Stir slowly. Watch from the side.
  - Note: A thin bag usually puffs open and drifts slowly in the middle of the water, much like a jellyfish. The newspaper goes soggy and sinks or tears. The handkerchief sinks or lies flat.
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Compare your child's drawings with the jellyfish picture.  
  *Parent says:* Which one looked most like a jellyfish? What might a hungry turtle do if she saw it?  
  *Child mode:* Which one looked most like a jellyfish? Why might a turtle eat it?
  - Check: Which one drifted and looked most like a jellyfish? → **The thin plastic bag**

## Activities
### a1 · Loop Hunt: find the traps in our dry waste (15 min)
- **safety:** Use only clean, dry waste. Leave out anything sharp, such as broken glass or tins. Wash hands with soap after.
- **record:** Make a tally mark for each loop you find and snip.
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Spread one day's clean dry waste on a newspaper. Your child looks for anything shaped like a loop or a long string.  
  *Parent says:* Look for loops and strings. A bird's foot or a dog's nose could get stuck in them.  
  *Child mode:* Spread the dry waste out. Look for loops and strings.
  - Note: Common loops are rubber bands, mask ear loops, the rings on packets, plastic strapping from parcels and pieces of string.
- **s2 (7 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Your child snips each loop open, so it becomes a straight strip. Make a tally mark for each one.  
  *Parent says:* Snip it open. Now it is a strip, not a loop. Can a bird's foot get stuck in it now?  
  *Child mode:* Snip each loop open. Make a tally mark for each one.
  - Ask: Why does snipping help? (good: A strip cannot close round a foot.; An animal can pull free from a strip.)
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Count the tally marks together. Put the waste back in the dry waste bin.  
  *Parent says:* How many traps did we find in one day? How many might we find in a week?  
  *Child mode:* Count your marks. Put the waste back in the dry bin.
### a2 · Spot the Care: three places near home (15 min)
- **safety:** Look at litter, but do not touch it. Stay with a grown-up outside.
- **record:** Draw a table with three rows: the place, a smiling or sad face, and one way to make it better.
- **s1 (3 min)**

## Worksheets
### Caring for our environment (core) (core, 10 questions)
1. (mcq) Remember the wet loop test, where you soaked loops in water and pulled them? Which wet loop tore?  
   Options: The paper loop / The plastic loop / The cloth loop  
   **Answer:** The paper loop. *Why:* Water soaks into paper and makes it weak. A strong answer adds that the dry paper did not tear, so the water made the difference.
2. (draw) Draw the four loops after the wet loop test. Show which ones tore and which stayed whole.  
   **Answer:** A drawing with the wet paper loop torn, and the dry paper, cloth and plastic loops whole. The plastic loop may look stretched.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the torn wet paper and the stretched but whole plastic.
3. (match) Draw a line from each piece of litter to the harm it can do.  
   **Answer:** Kite string → Traps a bird's foot; Plastic bag with peels → A cow swallows it; Broken glass → Cuts a dog's paw; Floating bag in the sea → A turtle swallows it. *Why:* String and loops trap. Bags with food smells get eaten. Glass cuts. Turtles may think a floating bag is a jellyfish.
4. (truefalse) Our environment is only the trees and the animals. People are not part of it.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Our environment is everything around us, living and non-living. People are animals, so we are part of it too.
5. (sort) Sort these into living and non-living parts of the environment.  
   **Answer:** A neem tree → Living; A crow → Living; You → Living; Rain water → Non-living; Soil → Non-living; Sunlight → Non-living. *Why:* Trees, crows and people grow, feed and have young. Water, soil and sunlight are not alive, but every living being needs them.
6. (write) Say it to a grown-up, who writes it down: why is a plastic loop more dangerous to a bird than a paper loop?  
   **Answer:** Plastic stays strong when wet, so a bird cannot break free. Paper tears when wet, so a bird could break it.. *Why:* A strong answer uses the wet loop test as evidence.
7. (count) Priya, a girl in Class 2, found these in her family's dry waste: 3 rubber bands, 2 masks with 2 loops each, and 1 packet with no loops. How many loops did she need to snip?  
   **Answer:** 7. *Why:* 3 rubber bands make 3 loops. 2 masks with 2 loops each make 4 loops. 3 + 4 = 7. The packet has no loops.
8. (fill) To keep birds safe, we ___ every loop open before it goes in the bin.  
   **Answer:** snip. *Why:* A snipped loop becomes a strip, which cannot close round a foot or a neck.
9. (perspective) Two friends in Chennai are going shopping for vegetables.  
   **Answer:** Either side, with a reason. Divya is right that paper rots away and tears when wet, so it is less likely to trap an animal. Ammu is right that a bag used many times makes the least waste.. *Why:* A strong answer gives a reason from the unit for one side, and fairly says what the other side has right.
10. (label) Look at the picture of a balcony. Label two things that could harm a bird, and one thing that helps a bird.  
   **Answer:** Harm: kite string on the railing, a rubber band on the floor. Help: a bowl of clean water.. *Why:* A strong answer finds the loops or strings as traps and the water bowl as care.
### Caring for our environment (stretch) (stretch, 10 questions)
1. (write) Arun, a boy of 5, says, 'The pigeon could break the kite string if she pulled harder.' What would you tell him? Use what you found in the wet loop test.  
   **Answer:** Nylon string is a plastic. In the test, the plastic loop only stretched and did not tear, even when wet. So pulling harder would not break it, and it could cut her foot.. *Why:* A strong answer uses the plastic loop as evidence, and says that plastic stays strong when wet.
2. (mcq) In the wet loop test, why did we keep one paper loop dry?  
   Options: To see what the water did to the paper / Because there was not enough water / To have a spare loop  
   **Answer:** To see what the water did to the paper. *Why:* The dry loop held and the wet loop tore. The only difference was the water, so the water made the paper weak.
3. (mcq) Why does a cow eat a plastic bag on the street?  
   Options: The bag smells of the food scraps inside it / Cows like the colour of plastic / Cows need plastic to grow  
   **Answer:** The bag smells of the food scraps inside it. *Why:* Food scraps thrown in plastic bags smell like food. The cow swallows the bag with the food, and the plastic stays in their stomach.
4. (draw) In the bag-in-the-bucket test, a thin bag drifted in the water. Draw a sea turtle seeing a floating bag and a jellyfish side by side.  
   **Answer:** A drawing of a turtle with a puffed-open bag and a jellyfish that look alike.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows why the turtle could mix them up: both are thin, pale and drifting.
5. (order) Put in order how a wrapper dropped on a road can reach a lake.  
   **Answer:** [1, 0, 2, 3]. *Why:* First the wrapper is dropped, then rain washes it into a drain, the drain flows to the lake, and an animal meets it there.
6. (truefalse) Plastic lasts much longer than paper or banana peels.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. Paper and peels rot away in weeks or months. Most plastic lasts many years, breaking only into smaller bits.
7. (perspective) After Sankranti, a family in Ahmedabad talks about kites.  
   **Answer:** Any view, with a reason. Hetal is right that birds get hurt. The grandmother is right that the festival matters, and that cotton string and picking up help. The rescuer gives facts that help us choose safer ways.. *Why:* A strong answer uses a fact from the unit and is fair to another view.
8. (label) Label the parts of this lake that are living and the parts that are non-living.  
   **Answer:** Living: fish, ducks, frog, lotus. Non-living: water, mud, stones, sunlight.. *Why:* A strong answer labels at least three of each.
9. (odd) Which one is the odd one out, and why? Kite string, rubber band, banana peel, mask ear loop.  
   **Answer:** Banana peel. *Why:* The other three are loops or strings that can trap an animal. A banana peel rots away and cannot trap anyone.
10. (write) Name one thing your family could do this week to care for the environment. Say why it helps.  
   **Answer:** For example: snip every loop before it goes in the bin, so no animal gets trapped. Or carry a cloth bag, so we use fewer plastic bags.. *Why:* A strong answer names a real action and says which harm it stops.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) What is our environment?  
   Options: Everything around us: air, water, land and all living beings / Only forests far away / Only the trees on our street  
   **Answer:** Everything around us: air, water, land and all living beings. *Why:* Our environment is everything around us, living and non-living, including us.
2. (mcq) Which of these is a non-living part of the environment?  
   Options: Rain water / A sparrow / A tulsi plant  
   **Answer:** Rain water. *Why:* Water is not alive, but every living being needs it. The sparrow and the tulsi are alive.
3. (mcq) What are the two main ways litter harms animals?  
   Options: They get trapped in it, or they eat it / It makes them too cold, or too hot / It makes them sleep, or sing  
   **Answer:** They get trapped in it, or they eat it. *Why:* Loops and strings trap animals. Bags, caps and wrappers get swallowed.
4. (mcq) In a test, a wet paper loop tore at the first pull. What did a wet plastic loop do?  
   Options: It stretched but stayed whole / It tore at once too / It melted  
   **Answer:** It stretched but stayed whole. *Why:* Plastic does not soak up water, so it stays strong when wet. That is why a plastic loop can trap a bird.
5. (mcq) Why is plastic litter more dangerous to animals than paper litter?  
   Options: Plastic stays strong in water and lasts for years / Plastic is heavier than paper / Plastic is always brightly coloured  
   **Answer:** Plastic stays strong in water and lasts for years. *Why:* Wet paper goes weak and rots away. Plastic stays strong and lasts, so it can trap or harm animals for a long time.
6. (mcq) Why might a sea turtle swallow a floating plastic bag?  
   Options: The bag drifts like a jellyfish, which turtles eat / Turtles eat plastic to grow / The bag tastes sweet  
   **Answer:** The bag drifts like a jellyfish, which turtles eat. *Why:* A thin bag in water puffs open and drifts like a jellyfish. Many scientists think turtles mix them up.
7. (mcq) You find a rubber band in your dry waste. What should you do before it goes in the bin?  
   Options: Snip it open / Tie it in a knot / Wash it  
   **Answer:** Snip it open. *Why:* A snipped band is a strip, which cannot close round an animal's foot or beak.
8. (mcq) Which bag makes the least litter?  
   Options: A cloth bag used again and again / A new plastic bag each day / A new paper bag each day  
   **Answer:** A cloth bag used again and again. *Why:* A bag used hundreds of times means hundreds fewer bags thrown away.
9. (mcq) In 1974, what did Gaura Devi and other women of Reni village do?  
   Options: They held on to the trees so they could not be cut down / They planted rice / They built a dam  
   **Answer:** They held on to the trees so they could not be cut down. *Why:* This was part of the Chipko movement. 'Chipko' means 'to hug' or 'to stick to'. The loggers went away.
10. (mcq) Who leads the Hargila Army of women in Assam?  
   Options: Purnima Devi Barman, a scientist who studies birds / A king / A football team  
   **Answer:** Purnima Devi Barman, a scientist who studies birds. *Why:* Purnima Devi Barman and thousands of village women protect the nests of the greater adjutant stork, called hargila in Assamese.
11. (truefalse) Nylon kite string is banned in India because it hurts birds and people.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. In 2017 the National Green Tribunal banned nylon and other plastic kite string across India.

## Applications

## Open questions
- **How long does a plastic bag really last?** Plastic bags are only about 60 years old, so nobody has watched one for hundreds of years. Scientists make careful guesses, and they do not all agree.
- **How often do sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish?** Scientists find plastic in many turtles' stomachs, but they cannot easily watch turtles choosing food in the deep sea.
- **What do the tiny bits of broken plastic do inside animals, and inside us?** Tiny plastic bits are now found in water, soil, animals and people. Scientists are still finding out how much harm they do.

## Connected maths
- In the wet loop test, Lakshmi pulled 4 loops. 1 loop tore. How many loops stayed whole? **3 loops.**. 4 loops take away the 1 that tore leaves 3. 4 - 1 = 3. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L2`)
- In the Loop-Free Week, Kavya's family snipped 3 loops on Monday, 5 on Tuesday and 2 on Wednesday. On which day did they snip the most? How many loops in all? **Tuesday. 10 loops in all.**. 5 is the biggest number, so Tuesday. 3 + 5 + 2 = 10. (queues `math.data.sorting-and-tallies.L2`)
- One home in a building snips 2 loops a day. There are 5 homes in the building, and each snips 2 loops a day. How many loops do they snip in one day? **10 loops.**. 5 groups of 2 make 10. 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10. (queues `math.multiplication.equal-groups.L2`)
