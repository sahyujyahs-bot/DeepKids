# Where food comes from: food from plants and food from animals
`sci.food.where-food-comes-from.L1`

**Summary.** A mango tree never eats, yet the tree grows big and makes mangoes. Plants make their own food from sunlight, air and water. Animals, including us, cannot do that, so we eat plants and other animals. Some of our food comes from plants, like rice, coconut and palak. Some comes from animals, like milk, eggs and honey. In this unit you make pretend flower nectar from sugar water and find out how bees turn nectar into thick honey.

**Intro.** *Hold a spoon of honey and tip it slowly. The honey creeps off the spoon. Now tip a spoon of water. The water runs off at once. Bees make honey from a thin, watery juice inside flowers. How do they make it so thick?* Every food we eat comes from somewhere. Most of it comes from plants or from animals. In this unit you sort your lunch into food from plants and food from animals. You find out that plants make their own food, and animals must eat. Then you test what happens to thin sugar water under a fan, the way bees make honey.

## Learning outcomes
0. By the end I can sort foods into food from plants and food from animals, and give two examples of each.
1. By the end I can say that plants make their own food from sunlight, air and water, and that animals, including us, eat plants or other animals.
2. By the end I can say how bees make honey from flower nectar, using what I saw in my sugar water test.
3. By the end I can say where milk, eggs and honey come from, and what the animals who make them eat.
4. By the end I can make a guess, say why, watch carefully and say what I saw.

## Terms
- **Food**: Food is what a living being takes in to grow, move and stay alive. *e.g.* Rice, a banana and a glass of milk are all food.
- **Food from plants**: Some of our food is a part of a plant: a seed, a fruit, a leaf, a root or a stem. *e.g.* Rice is a seed. A mango is a fruit. Palak is a leaf. A carrot is a root.
- **Food from animals**: Some of our food comes from animals. Animals make milk, eggs and honey, and some people also eat fish and meat. *e.g.* A cow makes milk. A hen lays eggs. Honey bees make honey.
- **Nectar**: Nectar is a thin, sweet juice that flowers make inside them. Bees and butterflies drink nectar. *e.g.* A bee pushes her head into a flower to drink the nectar.
- **Evaporate**: When water evaporates, it goes into the air as tiny bits too small to see. *e.g.* Wet clothes on a line dry because the water evaporates into the air.

## Explanations
### Plants make their own food · *defined*
**Definition:** A green plant makes food in their leaves, from sunlight, air and water.

A plant does not eat. Roots drink water, and leaves take in air. Sunlight gives the leaves the energy to make sugar. That sugar is the plant's food.
- e.g. A mango tree makes all the food in every mango from sunlight, air and water.
- e.g. Sugarcane stores sweet juice in the stem. The plant made that sugar in the leaves.
- e.g. In the 1930s, Janaki Ammal, a plant scientist from Kerala, helped breed sweeter sugarcane in Coimbatore.
### Animals, including us, must eat · *defined*
**Definition:** Animals cannot make food from sunlight. So every animal eats plants, or eats other animals, or both.

Humans are animals too. A cow eats grass. A hen pecks grain and insects. A crow eats almost anything. You eat rice, dal, vegetables, and maybe milk, eggs or fish.
- e.g. A goat eats leaves. A tiger eats deer. A crow eats seeds, insects and leftovers.
- e.g. A baby drinks milk from their mother before they can eat anything else.
- e.g. A child in Kolkata may eat rice and fish. A child in Madurai may eat idli and sambar.
### Food from plants · *defined*
**Definition:** Much of our food is a part of a plant: seeds, fruits, leaves, roots, stems and even flowers.

Rice, wheat, ragi and dal are seeds. Mangoes, bananas and tomatoes are fruits. Palak and curry leaves are leaves. Carrots are roots. Banana flower is a flower.
- e.g. A dosa is made from rice and urad dal. Both are seeds.
- e.g. Coconut is the fruit of the coconut palm.
- e.g. In the 1940s, Kamala Sohonie, the first Indian woman to earn a PhD in science, studied neera. Neera is the sweet sap of palm trees.
### Food from animals · *defined*
**Definition:** Animals give us milk, eggs and honey. Some people also eat fish and meat.

A cow, buffalo or goat makes milk for her calf or kid. A hen lays eggs. Honey bees make honey to feed their hive. Curd, ghee and paneer are all made from milk. Many families eat no meat, and some eat no eggs or honey either.
- e.g. In Rajasthan and Kutch, some families drink camel milk.
- e.g. Most eggs from a shop cannot hatch. There was no rooster, so no chick began inside.
- e.g. A cow makes milk from the grass she eats. So the milk began as a plant.
### How bees make honey · *defined*
**Definition:** Honey bees drink flower nectar, carry it home, and dry it into thick honey.

A worker bee sucks up nectar and carries the nectar home in her body. At the hive, the bees mix it with juices from their own bodies. They spread it in the wax cells and fan their wings over it. The moving air makes water evaporate. When the honey is thick, they close the cell with wax.
- e.g. All the honey-making bees in a hive are female. The male bees make no honey.
- e.g. A worker bee makes only a small spoon's worth of honey in her whole life.
- e.g. In Kerala, people also collect cheruthen, honey from tiny bees who do not sting.
### Do bees and flowers choose? · *debated*
Bees choose which flowers to visit, and they remember where the best ones are. A honey bee can tell her sisters where flowers are by dancing on the comb. Flowers make nectar and bright colours, and bees carry their pollen. Some scientists say this is all instinct, not choosing.
- e.g. Karl von Frisch, an Austrian scientist, found that honey bees dance to show the way to flowers.
- e.g. A bee visits the same kind of flower again and again on one trip.
- e.g. Flowers that bees visit grow fruits and seeds.
- **Many scientists who study bee behaviour:** Bees learn, remember, decide and tell each other where food is. Flowers sense and respond too. Both are minds at work.
- **Many other scientists:** Bees and flowers follow instincts built over a very long time. They prefer words like 'behaviour' and 'response' to 'choose'.

## Mostly agreed
- Green plants make their own food from sunlight, air and water. *How we know:* A plant in a dark cupboard goes pale and weak. A plant in light grows, though nobody feeds the plant.
- Every animal, including people, gets food by eating plants, other animals, or both. *How we know:* Watch any animal for a while: a crow, an ant, a cow, a cat. Each one is looking for food or eating.
- Honey bees make honey from flower nectar. *How we know:* Beekeepers see bees going into flowers and coming home. When there are few flowers, the bees make little honey.

## How we found out
**Luis Méndez de Torres, Charles Butler and Jan Swammerdam, 1586, 1609, and Swammerdam's dissections between 1669 and the 1670s.** For a very long time, people in Europe believed that a king bee ruled every hive. The Greek thinker Aristotle wrote about the king bee about 2,350 years ago. He also wrote that honey falls from the air, and the bees only gather it. Beekeepers watched their bees closely, and saw them drinking from flowers. In 1586, a Spanish beekeeper named Luis Méndez de Torres wrote that the big bee in the hive is a female who lays eggs. In 1609, the English beekeeper Charles Butler said the same in a book called The Feminine Monarchie. Many people still did not believe it. Then, around 350 years ago, the Dutch scientist Jan Swammerdam cut open the big bee and looked inside with a microscope. He found the parts that make eggs. The king was a queen, the mother of the whole hive. Today we know more. The queen lays the eggs. The worker bees, who are all female too, gather nectar and make the honey. The male bees make no honey at all.  
*What people believed before:* That a male king bee rules the hive, and that honey falls from the air. Swammerdam's microscope showed the big bee makes eggs, so she is a mother, and watching bees showed honey is made from flower nectar.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Honey falls from the air like dew, and bees only gather it.** (Aristotle, a Greek thinker, about 2,350 years ago; *rejected*)  
  For: Bees sometimes gather sweet drops from leaves, and the drops seem to appear from nowhere.  
  Against: Bees drink nectar from flowers and dry it in the hive. The sweet drops on leaves are mostly made by tiny insects sucking the plant.
- **Every food comes from a plant or an animal.** (A common idea; *rejected*)  
  For: Almost everything on our plate does come from a plant or an animal.  
  Against: Salt comes from sea water or from the ground. Water was never alive. Mushrooms are fungi, which are neither plants nor animals.
- **Milk comes from the shop.** (What many small children think; *rejected*)  
  For: We buy milk in a packet or from a milk booth.  
  Against: The shop only sells the milk. A cow, a buffalo or a goat made the milk, from the plants she ate.

## Roots
We have sorted foods from plants and foods from animals, and turned thin sugar water thick. Now we ask what honey tells us about how plants, animals and we are joined.

**Deep truth.** Our thin sugar water, left under the fan, turned thick and sticky like honey. The moving air took its water away. Bees make honey in a similar way, fanning flower nectar until most water is gone. So honey is food from a plant and from an animal at once.  
*Habit it breaks:* We sort every food into two boxes, plant or animal, as if each food has one maker. Honey needs both: a flower made the nectar, and bees changed it into honey.  
*Evidence:* In the sugar water test, the open drop under the fan turned thick and barely moved when tilted, while the covered drop stayed runny. Bees dry nectar in the comb by fanning it with their wings.

- *who am i:* You are an animal too. Like every animal, you must eat what plants and other animals made.
- *what is life:* Life passes food along. A flower makes nectar, a bee makes honey, and you eat the honey.
- *what is this world:* A world where flowers and bees feed each other. The bee gets nectar, and the flower gets pollen carried to the next flower.

Sunlight falls on a leaf, and the plant makes sugar. The flower turns some of that sugar into nectar. A bee drinks the nectar, and bees dry the nectar into honey, and you eat the honey. The same sunlight has become a plant's food, a bee's food and your food.

- **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.5.1 (the Madhu Vidya, the teaching of honey):** This earth is honey for all beings, and all beings are honey for this earth. (In honey, a flower feeds the bees and the bees help the flower make seeds. Each one is food and help for the other, as the teaching says.)
- **Dhammapada, verse 49:** A bee gathers nectar from a flower without harming the flower's colour or smell, and flies away. A wise person moves through the village in the same way. (A bee takes nectar but leaves the flower whole, and carries the flower's pollen on. Taking food does not have to mean harming.)
- Science (settled): Many fruits and vegetables grow only when bees and other insects carry pollen between their flowers.
- Science (settled): A honey bee dances on the comb to tell her sisters which way the flowers are, and how far.
- Other view, Many scientists who study evolution: They say bees and flowers do not mean to help each other. Each one does what helps their own life, and the help to the other happens along the way.
- Practice, One Slow Drop (3 min): Put one drop of honey, or a crumb of jaggery, on your tongue. Close your eyes. Let it melt slowly. Think of the flower that made the sweet juice. Think of the bee, or the sugarcane field. Breathe in slowly, and breathe out slowly, three times. Now open your eyes.
- Ask: If honey is from a flower and from bees, are there other foods that came from more than one living being?
- Ask: The bees made the honey for their own hive. What do you think the bees would say about us eating it?
- Ask: A plant makes food from sunlight, and you eat the plant. Is some of that sunlight now inside you?

## Deep and big questions
- *deep:* Honey is made by bees from flower nectar. Is honey food from a plant, food from an animal, or both? (hint: Think about who made the nectar, and who made it thick.)
- *deep:* A cow makes milk from the grass she eats. Is the milk made of grass? (hint: Think about what goes into the cow, and what comes out.)
- *big:* Plants make their own food. If you could make food from sunlight, how would your day be different? (hint: Think about meals, cooking and going to the shop.)
- *big:* Bees make honey for their own hive. When we take honey, who should it belong to? (hint: Think about the bees, the flowers and the beekeeper.)

## Story: The Hum Under the Water Tank
*Divya, her Ajji (her grandmother) and her Appa (her father) are made up. The bee facts are true: rock bees build one large open comb, often under ledges and water tanks on tall buildings in Indian cities; they sting if disturbed; they move to a new place after some months; worker bees carry nectar home and dry it into honey by fanning; bees carry pollen between flowers.*

Divya lived on the fourth floor of a tall building in Bengaluru. She was six. One morning she heard a soft, steady hum outside her window.

Under the water tank of the next building hung a huge brown shape. The shape was as big as a door, and the shape was moving.

'Ajji!' she called. Ajji, her grandmother, came slowly with her glasses.

'Rock bees,' said Ajji. 'They have built a honeycomb. Thousands of bees are sitting on the comb, keeping it safe.'

Appa, her father, came to look. 'We must call someone to burn it,' he said. 'Bees sting.'

'Rock bees sting if you disturb them,' said Ajji. 'So we will not disturb them. We will watch from inside, with the window shut. Rock bees move on after some months.'

So every day, Divya watched through the glass. Bees flew away from the comb in a stream. Other bees flew back.

'Where do they go?' she asked.

'To the flowers,' said Ajji. 'Look at the drumstick tree in the park, and the yellow flowers on the gulmohar road. Inside each flower is a drop of sweet juice called nectar. A bee drinks the nectar and carries the nectar home inside her.'

'Is it honey already?' asked Divya.

'Not yet. Nectar is thin and watery. The bees spread it in little wax rooms in the comb. Then they fan their wings over it, hour after hour. The moving air dries it. Slowly it turns thick. Then it is honey.'

Appa put down his phone. 'I did not know that,' he said. 'I thought honey just came in a bottle.'

Divya laughed. 'Appa, the bottle cannot make honey. Only bees can.'

That night at dinner, Divya looked at her plate. There were dosas, coconut chutney and a small cup of curd.

'Ajji, did bees make my dosa?'

'No,' said Ajji. 'A dosa is rice and urad dal. Both are seeds from plants. The coconut grew on a coconut palm.'

'And the curd?'

'The curd was milk. A cow made the milk from the grass she ate.'

Divya put a leaf drawing next to the dosa and the chutney. She put a cow drawing next to the curd. 'Food from plants, and food from animals,' she said.

'And honey?' asked Appa.

Divya thought for a long time. 'Honey is both,' she said. 'The flower made the nectar. The bees made the nectar into honey.'

Ajji smiled. 'And the flower got something back. When a bee drinks, yellow pollen sticks to her. She carries the pollen to the next flower, and that helps the flowers make seeds.'

The weeks went by. The rains came. One morning the hum was gone. The rock bees had flown away, all together, to a new home somewhere else.

Only the empty comb was left, pale and full of little holes.

Divya pressed her nose to the window. 'Thank you for not burning them, Appa,' she said.

'Thank Ajji,' said Appa. 'She taught me to look first.'

Divya tried it at home the next day. She made sugar water, thin like nectar. She put a little on a plate under the fan. By morning it was thick and sticky, just like honey.

'The fan was my bee,' she said.

**For the parent:** For the parent: Divya sorts her dinner into food from plants and food from animals, and then sees that honey belongs to both. A flower made the nectar, and the bees dried it into honey.

- Ask: Where did the bees fly to every day, and why?
- Ask: How do bees make thin nectar into thick honey?
- Ask: Why did Divya say honey is both?

## Experiments
### e1 · Nectar to Honey: what turns thin sugar water thick? (24 min, 2 days)
- **question:** We leave thin sugar water out overnight, one drop under a fan and one drop covered. Which one will turn thick, like honey?
- **predict:** Guess first. Tomorrow, will the drop under the fan be runny like water, or thick like honey? What about the covered drop? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Both drops are the same sugar water, the same size, on the same kind of plate, in the same room. Only one thing is different: one drop is open to the moving air, and one is covered by a katori.
- **record:** Draw the two plates after you tilted them. Show which drop ran and which one stayed.
- **results table:** Which drop | How the drop looks and feels | What happens when we tilt the plate
- **schedule:** day 1: Make sugar water. Feel how runny it is. Guess. Put one drop under the fan, and cover one drop with a katori. (10 min); day 1: In the evening, peep at both drops without touching them. (3 min); day 2: Touch both drops. Tilt both plates. Say what you saw and why. (11 min)
- **s1 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put 4 spoons of water in a cup. 2. Your child adds 1 spoon of sugar. 3. Stir until the water is clear again. 4. Your child tastes a drop on a clean finger.  
  *Parent says:* This is pretend nectar. Flower nectar is thin and sweet like this. Taste it. Is it runny like water, or thick like honey?  
  *Child mode:* Stir the sugar into the water. Taste one drop. Is it thin or thick?
  - Ask: How is this sugar water like honey? How is it different? (good: Both are sweet.; The sugar water is runny. Honey is thick.)
  - Note: If you have honey, let your child tip a spoon of honey too, and see how slowly it moves.
- **s2 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Ask your child to guess, and to say why. Write the guess down, or draw it.  
  *Parent says:* One drop will stay under the fan all night. One drop will hide under a katori. Which one will turn thick? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Which drop will turn thick? Why?
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: The fan one, because the fan will dry it.; Both will stay the same, because nothing is added.)
  - Note: Any guess with a reason is a good guess. Do not say which is right. The test will show.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Put half a spoon of sugar water on each plate. 2. Spread each drop with the back of the spoon, to the size of a coin. 3. Cover one drop with an upside-down katori. 4. Put both plates on a high table under the fan.  
  *Parent says:* Both drops are the same. Only one has a cover. Now we leave them all night.  
  *Child mode:* Put a drop on each plate. Cover one drop with a katori.
  - Note: Sugar brings ants. If ants visit your home, stand each plate on an upside-down bowl inside a tray with a little water. Ants do not cross the water.
  - Wait: Leave both plates under the fan until tomorrow morning.
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* In the evening, look at both drops together. Do not touch them yet.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the two drops. Has either one changed? Is one smaller?  
  *Child mode:* Look at both drops. Has one changed?
  - Wait: Leave both plates under the fan overnight.
- **s5 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lift the katori. 2. Your child touches each drop with a clean fingertip. 3. Tilt each plate slowly, and watch each drop. 4. Put a drop of honey on a plate and tilt that too.  
  *Parent says:* Touch each drop. Now tilt the plates slowly. Which drop runs? Which one stays?  
  *Child mode:* Touch each drop. Tilt each plate. Which drop runs?
  - Ask: What happened to the drop under the fan? What happened to the covered drop? (good: The fan drop went thick and sticky. It hardly moved.; The covered drop was still wet and ran down the plate.)
  - Note: The open drop is usually thick, sticky and smaller. It may even dry into a hard, shiny crust. The covered drop stays runny, and you may see tiny water drops inside the katori. In very damp weather, leave the plates one more day.
- **s6 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask your child what they think, what they saw and why. 2. Then tell them how bees make honey.  
  *Parent says:* The moving air took water out of the open drop. The water evaporated. Bees fan their wings over nectar in the same way, until the nectar turns thick. That is honey.  
  *Child mode:* The fan dried the open drop. Bees fan nectar with their wings. Then nectar turns into honey.
  - Ask: Why did the covered drop stay runny? (good: The katori kept the moving air away.; The water could not go off into the room.)
  - Check: The open drop turned thick, and the covered drop stayed runny. Why? → **Moving air made the water evaporate from the open drop**

## Challenges
### c1 · Be a Bee: which tongue carries the most nectar? (20 min)
- **problem:** A bee must carry nectar home from many flowers. Which tool carries the most sugar water from a cup to a bottle cap in 10 dips?
- **your turn:** Make your own bee tongue from things at home. Give your tool 10 dips. Did your tool beat the paintbrush?
- **constraints:** Use only things from home.; Each tool gets exactly 10 dips.; Dip, carry and drip each time. No pouring.
- **general:** A spoon, because a spoon can scoop.; A straw, with a finger over the top to hold the water in.; A strip of cloth, which soaks up the water.
- **creative:** A paintbrush, whose hairs hold water between them, like a real honey bee's hairy tongue.; Two cotton buds tied together.; A fold of paper shaped like a little cup.
- **c1s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Read the challenge to your child. Show the cup of sugar water and the four bottle caps.  
  *Parent says:* You are a bee. This cup is a flower full of nectar. These caps are your hive. Which tool will carry the most?  
  *Child mode:* You are a bee. Which tool carries the most nectar home?
  - Ask: Which tool do you think will carry the most? Why? (good: The spoon, because the spoon is the biggest.; The cloth, because cloth soaks up water.)
- **c1s2 (10 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child gives each tool 10 dips into the cup and drips each one into its own cap. 2. Line up the caps.  
  *Parent says:* Dip, carry, drip. Ten times. Count with me.  
  *Child mode:* Dip, carry, drip. Do it ten times with each tool.
- **c1s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Look at the four caps together. Which cap is the fullest?  
  *Parent says:* Which cap has the most nectar? Which tool did that?  
  *Child mode:* Which cap is the fullest?
  - Ask: Why did that tool carry the most? (good: It held lots of drops between the hairs.; It was big.)
  - Note: Results differ with the tools you have. The spoon often carries the most, and the paintbrush often beats the straw and the cotton bud.
- **c1s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Tell your child about a real bee's tongue.  
  *Parent says:* A real honey bee has a long, hairy tongue. Nectar sticks between the hairs, like water on your paintbrush. Then she sucks the nectar up.  
  *Child mode:* A real bee's tongue is hairy. Nectar sticks to the hairs.
  - Check: How does a honey bee's tongue pick up nectar? → **Nectar sticks between the tiny hairs on her tongue**

## Worksheets
### Food from Plants, Food from Animals (core) (core, 12 questions)
1. (sort) Sort these foods. Which came from a plant, and which came from an animal?  
   **Answer:** rice → From a plant; milk → From an animal; a banana → From a plant; an egg → From an animal; palak → From a plant; curd → From an animal. *Why:* Rice, bananas and palak are parts of plants. Milk and eggs are made by animals, and curd is made from milk.
2. (match) Draw a line from each food to the plant or animal who made that food.  
   **Answer:** honey → honey bees; milk → a cow; an egg → a hen; a coconut → a coconut palm. *Why:* Bees make honey from nectar. A cow makes milk. A hen lays eggs. A coconut grows on a coconut palm.
3. (mcq) Which one of these did a plant make?  
   Options: An egg / A mango / A glass of milk  
   **Answer:** A mango. *Why:* A mango is the fruit of the mango tree. Eggs and milk are made by animals.
4. (draw) Draw your two plates from the sugar water test, the morning after. Show which drop ran when you tilted the plate.  
   **Answer:** The open drop thick and still. The covered drop running down the plate.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the open drop staying put and the covered drop running in a line.
5. (truefalse) A green plant makes their own food, using sunlight, air and water.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. Leaves use the energy in sunlight to make sugar from air and water. The sugar is the plant's food.
6. (write) Say out loud, and a grown-up writes it down: why did the drop under the fan turn thick?  
   **Answer:** The moving air made the water evaporate. Less water was left, so the sugar water turned thick.. *Why:* A strong answer says the water went into the air, and uses what the child saw: the open drop got smaller and stickier.
7. (perspective) Kavya, aged 7, and her brother Arun, aged 5, live in Chennai. They are eating honey on dosa.  
   **Answer:** A strong answer picks a side with a reason, then sees the other side. The flower made the nectar, and the bees dried the nectar into honey. So both are right.. *Why:* Honey needs both a plant and an animal. Some foods do not fit only one group.
8. (odd) Three of these grew on a plant. Which one did not?  
   Options: An idli / A coconut / An egg / A tomato  
   **Answer:** 2. *Why:* An idli is made from rice and urad dal, which are seeds. A coconut and a tomato are fruits. A hen laid the egg.
9. (count) Lalitha's lunch is rice, sambar, curd, a banana and a glass of buttermilk. How many of these came from an animal?  
   **Answer:** 2. *Why:* Curd and buttermilk are both made from milk, so 2 came from an animal. Rice, sambar and the banana came from plants.
10. (label) Here is the journey of honey. Write or say what is in each box: flower, bee, honeycomb, honey jar.  
   **Answer:** ['where the nectar is made: flower', 'who carries the nectar home: bee', 'where the nectar is dried: honeycomb', 'what we eat: honey jar']. *Why:* The flower makes nectar. A worker bee carries the nectar home. Bees dry the nectar in the honeycomb. Then it is honey.
11. (order) Put these in order, from first to last.  
   **Answer:** ['A flower makes nectar.', 'A bee drinks the nectar and flies home.', 'Bees fan the nectar until it is thick.', 'We eat the honey.']. *Why:* Nectar is made first, then carried, then dried into honey, and last of all eaten.
12. (draw) Draw one food from a plant and one food from an animal that you ate today.  
   **Answer:** Any two true examples, such as a dosa and a glass of milk.. *Why:* A strong drawing names both foods and can say who made each one.
### Honey, Milk and Eggs (stretch) (stretch, 11 questions)
1. (mcq) In the sugar water test, why did the drop under the katori stay runny?  
   Options: The katori kept the moving air away, so the water could not evaporate / The katori added water / The katori made the sugar disappear  
   **Answer:** The katori kept the moving air away, so the water could not evaporate. *Why:* Under the katori, the air stayed still and damp, so very little water could leave. Nothing was added or taken away.
2. (write) A hen eats grain. A cow eats grass. Where did the food inside an egg and inside milk first come from?  
   **Answer:** From plants. The hen made the egg from the grain she ate, and the cow made the milk from the grass she ate.. *Why:* Animals make milk and eggs from the food they eat, and that food was mostly plants.
3. (truefalse) Every food we eat comes from a plant or an animal.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. Salt comes from sea water or from the ground, and water was never alive. A mushroom is a fungus, neither a plant nor an animal.
4. (sort) Sort these dishes: from plants only, from animals only, or both?  
   **Answer:** plain rice → Plants only; a boiled egg → Animals only; curd rice → Both; payasam made with milk → Both. *Why:* Plain rice is a seed. A boiled egg is from a hen. Curd rice and payasam mix rice with milk, so they are both.
5. (perspective) Meera keeps honey bees in wooden boxes in Kodagu. Her friend Salma does not eat honey.  
   **Answer:** A strong answer gives a reason for one side and a fair reason for the other: the beekeeper's bees help flowers make fruit, and the honey was made for the bees' own hive.. *Why:* This is a real choice families make. Both sides care about the bees.
6. (draw) In the Be a Bee challenge, which tool carried the most sugar water? Draw that tool and its bottle cap.  
   **Answer:** A drawing of the best tool and a full cap.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the tool and how full its cap was, and the child can say why that tool held more.
7. (mcq) Why do bees fan their wings over the honeycomb?  
   Options: To dry the nectar into honey / To keep the flowers away / To make the honey colder  
   **Answer:** To dry the nectar into honey. *Why:* The moving air from their wings makes water evaporate from the nectar, until it is thick honey.
8. (match) Match each food to the plant part we eat.  
   **Answer:** rice → seed; a mango → fruit; palak → leaf; a carrot → root. *Why:* Rice grains are seeds. A mango is a fruit. Palak is a leaf. A carrot is a root.
9. (odd) Three of these are made from milk. Which one is not?  
   Options: Ghee / Paneer / Jaggery / Curd  
   **Answer:** 2. *Why:* Ghee, paneer and curd are all made from milk. Jaggery is made from sugarcane juice, so jaggery comes from a plant.
10. (fill) Bees make honey from the sweet ____ inside flowers.  
   **Answer:** ['nectar']. *Why:* Nectar is the thin sweet juice that flowers make. Bees carry nectar home and dry it into honey.
11. (write) A plant makes their own food from sunlight. You cannot. So where does all your food come from?  
   **Answer:** From plants, from animals, and a little, like salt and water, from the Earth.. *Why:* A strong answer says that people, like other animals, must eat food that plants or other animals made.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) Which of these is food from an animal?  
   Options: An idli / A cup of milk / A banana  
   **Answer:** A cup of milk. *Why:* A cow, buffalo or goat makes milk. Idli is made from seeds, and a banana is a fruit.
2. (mcq) Which of these is food from a plant?  
   Options: An egg / Curd / A coconut  
   **Answer:** A coconut. *Why:* A coconut is the fruit of the coconut palm. An egg comes from a hen, and curd is made from milk.
3. (mcq) Which of these is neither from a plant nor from an animal?  
   Options: Salt / Rice / Ghee  
   **Answer:** Salt. *Why:* Salt comes from sea water or from the ground. Rice is a seed, and ghee is made from milk.
4. (mcq) How does a mango tree get food?  
   Options: The tree makes food in the leaves, from sunlight, air and water / The tree eats insects / The tree eats the soil  
   **Answer:** The tree makes food in the leaves, from sunlight, air and water. *Why:* Green plants make their own food in their leaves. The soil gives water and a little of some minerals, not food.
5. (mcq) A goat eats leaves all day. Why does the goat need to eat?  
   Options: Goats, like all animals, get their food by eating / Goats eat only for fun / Goats make food from sunlight  
   **Answer:** Goats, like all animals, get their food by eating. *Why:* Animals, including goats and people, cannot make food from sunlight. So they eat plants, or other animals, or both.
6. (mcq) What do honey bees make honey from?  
   Options: Flower nectar / Rain water / Mud  
   **Answer:** Flower nectar. *Why:* Bees drink nectar from flowers, carry it home and dry it into honey.
7. (mcq) In the sugar water test, which drop turned thick by the next morning?  
   Options: The open drop under the fan / The drop under the katori / Both stayed the same  
   **Answer:** The open drop under the fan. *Why:* Moving air took water away from the open drop. The covered drop kept its water and stayed runny.
8. (mcq) Who makes the honey in a beehive?  
   Options: The female worker bees / The male bees / The beekeeper  
   **Answer:** The female worker bees. *Why:* The worker bees, who are all female, gather nectar and make honey. Male bees make no honey. The beekeeper only takes some out.
9. (mcq) What does a cow eat to make her milk?  
   Options: Grass and other plants / Milk / Stones  
   **Answer:** Grass and other plants. *Why:* A cow makes milk from the grass, hay and other plants she eats.
10. (mcq) Where does an egg come from?  
   Options: A hen lays the egg / Eggs grow on trees / Eggs are made in shops  
   **Answer:** A hen lays the egg. *Why:* A hen lays eggs. The shop only sells them.
11. (mcq) Before you left the drops under the fan, what did you do first?  
   Options: Made a guess and said why / Ate the sugar / Washed the plates  
   **Answer:** Made a guess and said why. *Why:* Scientists guess first and give a reason. Then the test shows if the guess was right.

## Applications
- Beekeepers in Kodagu, Kerala and many other places keep honey bees in wooden boxes. The bees make honey and also carry pollen to coffee, coconut and fruit flowers.
- India makes more milk than any other country. Most of it comes from cows and buffaloes kept by small farming families.
- Farmers grow rice, wheat, ragi, dal and vegetables, which make up most of the food eaten in India.
- Honey keeps for a very long time without spoiling, because it has so little water in it.

## Open questions
- **How does a bee know which flowers still have nectar?** Scientists have found that bees use colour and smell, and that bees leave scent marks on flowers they have emptied. How bees put all these clues together is still being worked out.
- **How many kinds of bees live in India?** Scientists have named hundreds of kinds of bees in India, but many small wild bees have never been studied. Nobody has counted them all.
- **Do bees feel happy?** In 2016, scientists in London found that bumblebees given a sweet treat acted more hopeful about a new flower. Some scientists say this shows feelings. Others say it is too early to tell.
