# How plants sense light, gravity and touch, and move in answer
`sci.plants.plant-senses.L2`

**Summary.** Put a cup of young moong shoots by a window, and by the next morning they all lean towards the glass. Nobody pushed them. Plants sense the world around them and respond to it, mostly by growing. A shoot grows towards the light. A root grows down, even when the seed is lying the wrong way up. A touch-me-not folds their leaves a second after you touch them. In this unit you test all three, and find out how a plant can move with no muscles.

**Intro.** *Find a plant that grows by a window or on a balcony. Which way are their leaves facing: into the room, or out towards the light?* Plants have no eyes and no muscles. Yet they know where the light is, which way is down, and when something touches them. To sense something means to notice it. To respond means to do something because of it. Plants do both, all day long. Before you read on, make a guess. If you turn a plant round so the leaves face away from the window, will they stay that way, or turn back? You are going to find out.

## Learning outcomes
0. By the end I can say that plants sense light, which way is down, and touch, and that they respond to each one.
1. By the end I can show that a shoot bends towards the light, and turns back if the light comes from the other side.
2. By the end I can show that roots grow down, whichever way the sprout is lying.
3. By the end I can say how a touch-me-not responds to a touch, and that they fold their leaves at night too.
4. By the end I can say that most plants move by growing, so their movements are slow.
5. By the end I can guess first, give a reason, count what I see, and say what my test showed.

## Terms
- **Sense**: To notice something, such as light, a touch, or which way is down. *e.g.* A moong shoot senses the light coming in from the window.
- **Respond**: To do something because of what you sensed. *e.g.* A touch-me-not responds to a touch by folding their leaves.
- **Shoot**: The part of a young plant that grows up out of the soil: the stem and the first leaves. *e.g.* A moong shoot is a thin stem with two small leaves at the top.
- **Phototropism**: A plant growing towards the light. 'Photo' means light. Say it 'foe-toe-TROPE-izm'. *e.g.* Moong shoots by a window bend towards the glass. That is phototropism.
- **Gravity**: The pull of the Earth that makes things fall down. *e.g.* A dropped spoon falls to the floor because of gravity. Roots grow in the direction gravity pulls.
- **Root tip**: The very end of a root. It senses which way is down. *e.g.* When a sprout lies on their side, the root tip curves round and grows down.
- **Touch-me-not**: A small plant whose leaves fold up when touched. Many people call them chhui-mui or lajjavati. In Malayalam they are thottavadi, and in Tamil thottal chinungi. *e.g.* Touch the tip of one leaf, and the small leaflets fold up, pair after pair.
- **Leaflet**: One of the small parts that together make one leaf on some plants. *e.g.* One touch-me-not leaf has many leaflets in pairs, like the teeth of a comb.
- **Signal**: A message passed from one part to another. *e.g.* When you touch one leaflet, a signal runs along the leaf, and the next leaflets fold too.

## Explanations
### A shoot grows towards the light · *defined*
**Definition:** Phototropism means a plant growing towards the light. The tip of a shoot senses which side the light comes from, and the shoot bends that way.

The tip of a young shoot senses light. When the light comes from one side, the plant sends a growth chemical to the shady side of the stem. That side grows longer than the lit side. A stem that is longer on one side curves towards the shorter side, so the shoot leans towards the light. In 1880, Charles Darwin and his son Francis put tiny caps over the tips of grass seedlings. With their tips covered, the seedlings did not bend to the light. So the tip is the part that senses the light.
- e.g. Moong shoots by a window lean towards the glass within a day.
- e.g. A money plant on a windowsill turns their leaves to face outside.
- e.g. Young sunflower plants turn to face the Sun as it crosses the sky.
### Roots grow down, and shoots grow up · *defined*
**Definition:** Roots grow down, in the direction gravity pulls. Shoots grow up, away from it. This happens whichever way a seed is lying.

Inside the cells of a root tip are tiny heavy grains made of starch. Whichever way the root is lying, the grains sink to the lowest side of each cell, like sand in a bottle. That tells the root which way is down. The root then grows more on the upper side, so the tip curves down. Your ears have tiny crystals that do a similar job. They help you know which way is down, even with your eyes shut.
- e.g. A sprout laid with the root pointing up curves the root round to grow down.
- e.g. A seed planted upside down still sends the root down and the shoot up.
- e.g. A potted plant knocked onto their side slowly turns the shoot tips upwards again.
### How a touch-me-not folds their leaves · *defined*
**Definition:** At the base of each leaflet is a small swelling full of water. A touch sends a signal through the leaf. Water moves quickly out of the cells on one side of each swelling, and the leaflets fold together.

The swelling is called a pulvinus (say 'PUL-vin-us'). When its cells on one side are full of water, the swelling is firm and holds the leaflet open. A touch starts an electrical signal, which runs along the leaf. Water rushes out of the cells on one side, so that side goes soft, and the leaflets fold in a second or two. A strong touch makes the whole leaf droop down at the stalk. After about a quarter of an hour, sometimes longer, water moves back and the leaflets open again. A touch-me-not also folds their leaves every night and opens them in the morning.
- e.g. A light touch on one leaflet folds a few pairs.
- e.g. A firm tap folds the whole leaf, and the stalk drops down.
- e.g. After dark, the leaves are folded even when nobody has touched them.
### Why does a touch-me-not fold? · *idea*
Nobody knows for certain why touch-me-not plants fold. Scientists have a few ideas, and more than one may be true.
- e.g. A folded plant looks thin and dry, so a goat or an insect may pass them by.
- e.g. Folded leaves show the small prickles on the stems more clearly.
- e.g. Folding may protect the leaves from heavy rain and strong wind.
- **Many plant scientists:** Folding protects the plant from animals that eat leaves. A plant that suddenly looks small and dry, with prickles showing, is less tempting.
- **Other plant scientists:** Folding also protects the leaves from damage by heavy rain, strong wind or cold nights.
### Do plants feel and remember? · *debated*
Plants feel, remember and decide. A root decides which way to grow, and a touch-me-not can learn that a harmless shake is harmless and remember it for weeks. J.C. Bose, a scientist in Kolkata, measured electrical signals running through touch-me-not plants over 100 years ago. Many other scientists call these responses automatic. They say that sensing something is not the same as feeling it. Nobody knows for sure what being a plant is like.
- e.g. Monica Gagliano's touch-me-not plants stopped folding for a harmless drop, but still folded when shaken.
- e.g. A root grows round a stone and finds the way down again.
- e.g. A climber's tendril coils round a stick that touches them.
- **Scientists who study how plants behave:** Plants feel touch, light and gravity, remember what happened, and decide how to grow. Their signals work like messages in our own nerves.
- **Many other plant scientists:** Plants sense and respond, but this happens automatically, by chemicals and water moving. They do not use the words 'feel' or 'remember' for plants.

## Mostly agreed
- A shoot grows towards light that comes from one side. *How we know:* Moong shoots by a window lean towards the glass within a day. Turn the cup round, and they bend back. Darwin's seedlings with covered tips did not bend, so the tip senses the light.
- Roots grow down and shoots grow up, whichever way a seed is lying. *How we know:* In the root box, sprouts laid pointing up, down, left and right all curved their roots to point down by the next day.
- A touch-me-not folds their leaflets within seconds of a touch, and opens them again later. *How we know:* Touch one leaf and watch: the leaflets fold pair after pair. Look again after about a quarter of an hour, and they are open.
- Most plants move by growing, which is slow. A touch-me-not folds fast because water moves quickly out of cells. *How we know:* A shoot takes hours to bend, so you only see it if you look again later. Time-lapse films, made by taking a photo every few minutes, show plants bending and turning all the time.

## How we found out
**Monica Gagliano and her team, in Australia, 2014.** Many people thought a touch-me-not folds their leaves every time they are touched, the way a bell rings every time you press it. In 2014, a scientist named Monica Gagliano tested this in Australia. She grew touch-me-not plants in pots. Then she dropped each pot gently, about 15 centimetres, onto soft foam, again and again. At first, the plants folded their leaves every time. After a few drops, many of them stopped folding. Were they just tired out? She tested that too. She shook the same plants from side to side, which was new to them. They folded at once. So they still had the energy to fold. They had stopped folding for the drop, because the drop did them no harm. Four weeks later, many of the plants still did not fold when dropped. They had remembered. Some scientists still argue about whether to call this learning and memory.  
*What people believed before:* That a touch-me-not folds every single time, like a bell that rings whenever it is pressed. Gagliano's plants stopped folding for a harmless drop, still folded when shaken, and many kept this up for four weeks.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Plants cannot move.** (A common first guess; *rejected*)  
  For: A plant stays in one pot and does not walk away. Most of the time you see no movement at all.  
  Against: Plants move all the time, by growing. Shoots bend to the window, roots turn down, and leaves turn to face the light. A touch-me-not folds in a second. We miss most of it because it is slow.
- **The light pulls the shoot towards the window.** (A common first guess; *rejected*)  
  For: The shoots end up pointing at the light, as if something tugged them there.  
  Against: Light does not pull. The shoot's tip senses the light, and the shady side of the stem grows longer, so the shoot bends. The plant does the moving.
- **A sunflower in full bloom follows the Sun across the sky every day.** (Many people, and many books; *rejected*)  
  For: Young sunflower plants really do turn from east to west during the day, and back again at night.  
  Against: In 2016, Stacey Harmer and her team in America found that once the big flower opens, most sunflowers stop turning and face east. East-facing flowers warm up faster in the morning and get more visits from bees.
- **A touch-me-not can learn and remember.** (Monica Gagliano and her team, 2014; *debated*)  
  For: Her plants stopped folding when they were dropped again and again with no harm. They still folded when shaken in a new way, so they were not just tired. Many still did not fold for the drop four weeks later.  
  Against: Some scientists say this is not real memory, only a change in how the plant reacts. They want more tests.

## Roots
We have watched moong shoots turn to the light, roots turn down, and a touch-me-not fold. Now we ask what these tell us about plants, and about ourselves.

**Deep truth.** Our straight moong shoots leaned to the window within a day. When we turned the cup, they bent back towards the light. Nobody moved them: each shoot bent by growing more on the shady side. So a plant's shape is a record of where their light came from.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think of a plant's shape as fixed, like the shape of a chair. But a plant builds their shape day by day from what they sense. A bend in a stem shows where the light was, and a curve in a root shows where down was.  
*Evidence:* In the window test, straight shoots leaned to the window within a day. After the cup was turned, their tips turned back, and many stems were left shaped like the letter S. In the root box, a root that pointed up made a hook as it turned down.

- *who am i:* You sense light, touch and which way is down too. Tiny crystals in your ears tell you where down is, a little like the grains in a root tip.
- *what is life:* Living beings sense what is around them and respond. A shoot responds to light by bending, and a touch-me-not responds to a touch by folding.
- *what is this world:* The world reaches a plant as light, the pull of the Earth, touch and water. Each plant grows their own shape from what reaches them.

Every moong seed in a packet is much the same. Planted in different places, each one grows a different shape, bent by their own light, their own 'down' and their own touches. Everything is conscious, plants too: a shoot senses the light and decides which way to grow, and a touch-me-not remembers a harmless drop. One kind of seed, one Sun and one Earth give countless different plants.

- **Mahabharata, Shanti Parva, the talk between the sages Bhrigu and Bharadvaja:** Bhrigu says that trees have senses. They wither in heat, so they feel touch. A creeper winds round a tree and finds the way, so plants can sense where to go. They drink water through their roots. (Our moong shoots found the window, and a climber finds a stick. The old Indian epic asked whether plants sense the world, and gave the same kind of evidence we used: watch what the plant does.)
- **Tattvartha Sutra, chapter 2 (Umasvati, a Jain teacher):** Plants are living beings with one sense, the sense of touch. So Jains take great care not to harm them without need. (A touch-me-not shows the sense of touch clearly. Science has since found that plants also sense light, gravity and water. Both say a plant is a living someone who senses.)
- **J.C. Bose, The Nervous Mechanism of Plants (1926):** Bose found that a touch on a touch-me-not starts an electrical signal that travels through the plant, like a message along a nerve. (When you touched one leaflet, the folding ran along the leaf. That running fold is the signal Bose measured in Kolkata.)
- Science (settled): A shoot bends to light because a growth chemical, auxin, gathers on the shady side and makes it grow longer.
- Science (debated): Whether a plant's sensing comes with feeling, the way ours does, is still argued about.
- Other view, Many plant scientists: They explain bending, root turning and folding fully by chemicals, water and growth. They call these automatic responses, and do not say the plant decides or remembers.
- Practice, Turn to the light (3 min): Sit near a window and close your eyes. Keep them closed. Can you feel which side the light and warmth come from? Slowly turn your face towards it, like a shoot. Stay still and breathe slowly three times. Now open your eyes. Were you facing the window?
- Ask: If your shape grew from everything you sensed, what would it show?
- Ask: A touch-me-not senses touch, and you do too. Is a plant's touch like yours?
- Ask: Who decides which way the moong shoot grows: the shoot, the light, or both?

## Deep and big questions
- *deep:* A moong shoot has no eyes. How does the shoot know where the window is? (hint: Think about which part of the seedlings Darwin covered.)
- *deep:* If you grew moong seeds in space, where nothing falls down, which way would the roots grow? (hint: Think about what tells a root which way is down.)

## Story: The Shoots That Turned Round
*Nila, Siddharth and Ammamma are made up. Thottal chinungi is the Tamil name for the touch-me-not, and the plant grows wild on empty plots and roadsides in many Indian towns.*

Nila was seven. She lived on the fourth floor of a building in Chennai, with her grandmother and her little brother Siddharth. Siddharth was five. Everyone called their grandmother Ammamma.

One Monday evening, Ammamma put a handful of green moong in a bowl of water. "Soak them tonight," she said. "Tomorrow, Nila, you can plant them."

On Tuesday, Nila pressed twelve swollen moong seeds into a katori of soil. She gave them two spoons of water. Then Ammamma turned an old steel vessel upside down over the katori. "Leave them in the dark for three days," she said.

On Friday, Nila lifted the vessel. Twelve thin shoots stood straight up, pale yellow, like tiny candles. Each one had two small folded leaves at the top.

"Now put them on the kitchen windowsill," said Ammamma.

Nila set the katori by the window. The light came in through the glass, from one side only.

On Saturday morning, Siddharth ran into her room. "Nila! Your plants are falling over!"

Nila hurried to the kitchen. The shoots were not lying down. They were leaning, all twelve of them, towards the window. They had turned a little green, too.

"The fan blew them over," said Siddharth.

"The fan is in the hall," said Nila. "There is no fan in the kitchen."

Siddharth thought hard. "Then the window pulled them."

Nila looked at the shoots for a long time. "Let's test it," she said. "If something knocked them over, they will stay bent the way they are. Let's turn the katori round, so they lean away from the window. Then we will see what they do."

She stuck a red bindi on the side of the katori that faced the glass. Then she turned the katori halfway round. Now the bindi faced the room, and every shoot leaned away from the light.

"Tomorrow we look," said Nila.

That evening, Siddharth peeped at the katori. "The tops are lifting up!" he said.

On Sunday morning, both of them stood at the windowsill. Every shoot had turned. The tips were pointing at the window again. Low down, each stem still leaned the old way. Higher up, it curved back towards the glass, like a wiggly letter S.

"They turned round by themselves," said Siddharth. "Nobody touched them."

"They sensed the light," said Nila. "Then they grew towards it."

"But they have no eyes," said Siddharth.

Ammamma came in with her coffee. "No eyes," she agreed. "The tip of each shoot senses the light. Then the shady side of the stem grows a little longer than the bright side. When one side grows longer, the stem bends. That is how a plant moves. Slowly, by growing."

Siddharth bent down and looked at one wiggly shoot. "So this low bend is from yesterday," he said slowly. "And the top bend is from today."

"Yes," said Nila. "The shoot keeps a record of where the light was."

"Can any plant move fast?" asked Siddharth.

Ammamma smiled. "When I was a girl in Palakkad, we had a plant called thottavadi near our well. Here in Chennai people call it thottal chinungi. Touch the leaves, and they fold up shut."

"Like a door?" asked Siddharth.

"Like a door," said Ammamma. "There is one growing on the empty plot near the bus stop."

After breakfast, the three of them walked down to the plot. Low among the grass grew a small plant with feathery leaves and fluffy pink flowers.

Nila showed Siddharth how to touch the tip of one leaf with one finger, very gently, and keep his hand away from the prickly stem. The tiny leaflets folded together, one pair after another, all the way along the leaf. Siddharth gasped.

"Now wait," said Nila. "Don't touch again. Let's see when they open."

They sat on the low wall and counted the buses. After seven buses, about a quarter of an hour, the leaflets had opened again.

On the way home, Siddharth was quiet. Then he said, "The thottal chinungi folds fast. The moong turns slowly. But they both know when something happens to them."

"They both sense," said Nila. "And they both respond."

That night, Nila drew the wiggly moong shoots in her notebook. Next to them she drew an arrow for yesterday's light and an arrow for today's.

**For the parent:** Nila sees that nobody moved the moong shoots. They sensed where the light was and turned themselves by growing, so the bends in their stems show where the light came from.

- Ask: Why did Nila turn the katori round?
- Ask: If the fan had knocked the shoots over, what would Nila have seen the next morning?
- Ask: The touch-me-not folds fast and the moong turns slowly. How are they the same?

The next player has a turn.; Check a pair by turning the cards over: a pair has the same number on the back.; When all the cards are taken, the player with the most pairs wins.

## Experiments
### e1 · The Window Test: will moong shoots turn back to the light? (30 min, 7 days)
- **question:** Which way do moong shoots grow when light comes from one side? If we turn the cup round, will they stay as they are, or turn back?
- **predict:** Your straight shoots will stand by a window for a day. Which way do you think they will lean? Then we turn the cup round. What will they do then? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** We use one cup of shoots, with the same soil and the same water all through. The only thing we change is which side of the cup faces the window. A bindi marks the side that faced the glass first, so we always know how the cup was turned. We count every shoot, not just one, because one shoot on their own could be a fluke.
- **record:** Draw the cup three times: when it came out of the dark, after one day by the window, and the day after you turned it. Draw an arrow for where the light came from. Write how many shoots leaned to the window each time.
- **results table:** When | Leaning to the window | Standing straight | Leaning away from the window | My drawing
- **schedule:** day 1: In the evening, soak 15 moong in a bowl of water overnight. (2 min); day 2: Fill the cup with soil. Press 12 to 15 soaked moong just under the surface. Give 2 spoons of water. Cover the cup with the upside-down vessel. (5 min); day 4: Peep under the vessel. If the soil feels dry, add 1 spoon of water. Cover again. (2 min); day 5: Lift the vessel. Count the shoots and draw them. Mark the cup with a bindi. Put the cup by a window, bindi towards the glass. Guess what will happen. (9 min); day 6: Count and draw. Then turn the cup round so the bindi faces the room. Guess again. (7 min); day 7: Count and draw once more. Compare all three drawings and say what the shoots did. (7 min)
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. In the evening, put 15 whole moong in a bowl. 2. Cover them with water. 3. Leave them overnight.  
  *Parent says:* These moong seeds are going to drink water tonight. Tomorrow we plant them.  
  *Child mode:* Put 15 moong in a bowl. Cover them with water. Leave them overnight.
  - Note: Whole green moong sprouts reliably. Split moong dal will not grow. Start this four days before you want to do the window test.
  - Wait: Leave them overnight
- **s2 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Fill the cup with soil. 2. Your child presses 12 to 15 soaked moong just under the soil. 3. Give 2 spoons of water. 4. Turn the vessel upside down over the cup.  
  *Parent says:* We plant the moong and cover them. In the dark, they will grow straight up. Then we can see clearly if they bend.  
  *Child mode:* Press the moong into the soil. Give 2 spoons of water. Cover the cup with the vessel.
  - Note: Growing them in the dark matters. Shoots grown near a window lean before the test starts. In a warm room, the shoots are usually 3 to 6 cm tall after 3 days. If they are shorter than 2 cm, wait one more day.
  - Wait: Leave them for 3 days, adding a spoon of water if the soil is dry
- **s3 (9 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Lift the vessel together. 2. Count the shoots and draw them. 3. Stick a bindi on the side of the cup. 4. Put the cup on a windowsill or a table right by a window, bindi towards the glass. 5. Ask for a guess and a reason.  
  *Parent says:* Look, they grew straight up in the dark. Now they will stay by the window until tomorrow. Which way do you think they will lean? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Lift the cover. Count the shoots. Draw them. Put the cup by the window, bindi to the glass. Which way will they lean? Say why.
  - Ask: Why do you think they will lean that way? (good: Plants like light, so they will lean to the window.; They will stay straight, because nothing will push them.; I saw a plant on our balcony leaning out.)
  - Note: Choose a window that gives most of the room's daylight, so the light comes from one side. Keep the cup away from the fan. Write down your child's guess and reason, word for word. Don't say if it is right. The shoots are pale because they grew in the dark. They turn green in the light, too.
  - Wait: Leave the cup by the window until tomorrow
- **s4 (7 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at the shoots together. 2. Count how many lean to the window, stand straight, or lean away. 3. Your child draws them. 4. Turn the cup halfway round, so the bindi faces the room. 5. Ask for a new guess.  
  *Parent says:* Count them with me. How many lean to the window? Now I turn the cup round. They all lean away from the light. What will they do by tomorrow? Why?

### e2 · The Root Box: which way will four roots grow? (20 min, 4 days)
- **question:** If moong sprouts lie with their roots pointing four different ways, which way will each root grow?
- **predict:** One root points up, one down, one left and one right. Which way will each root grow by tomorrow? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** All four sprouts are in the same box, on the same wet paper, in the same place. Only the way each sprout lies is different. We use sprouts with roots about as long as your fingernail, so that a bend is easy to see.
- **record:** Draw the box with the four sprouts as they were at the start. On the next day, draw them again, with the new part of each root.
- **results table:** Sprout | Way the root pointed at the start | My guess | Way the root tip points the next day
- **schedule:** day 1: Soak a spoonful of moong overnight. (3 min); day 2: Drain the moong and wrap them in a wet cloth for a day, until the roots are 1 to 2 cm long. (2 min); day 3: Set up the root box with four sprouts pointing four ways. Guess. Leave it standing up. (10 min); day 4: Look at each root tip. Draw them. Count how many point down. (5 min)
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Two days before, soak a spoonful of moong overnight. 2. Next morning, drain them and wrap them in a wet cloth.  
  *Parent says:* We are sprouting moong, the way we do for sundal or a salad. This time we need the little roots.  
  *Child mode:* Soak the moong overnight. Then wrap them in a wet cloth.
  - Note: In a warm room, roots are 1 to 2 cm long after about a day in the cloth. Many families already sprout moong this way, so you may have some ready.
  - Wait: Leave them for a day, until the roots are 1 to 2 cm long
- **s2 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Fold a wet paper and press it against one side of the box. 2. Your child slides 4 sprouts between the paper and the plastic. 3. Point one root up, one down, one left and one right. 4. Draw an arrow on the plastic beside each root, showing the way it points.  
  *Parent says:* Let's give each sprout a different way to point. One root up, one down, one left, one right. Draw an arrow for each one.  
  *Child mode:* Slide four sprouts in. Point the roots up, down, left and right. Draw an arrow beside each one.
  - Note: Keep the paper wet, but don't leave a puddle. The wet paper holds the sprouts against the plastic. If one slips, push the sprout back gently.
- **s3 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Close the lid. 2. Stand the box on one edge, so you can see the sprouts from the side. 3. Ask for a guess and a reason.  
  *Parent says:* Which way will each root grow by tomorrow? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Close the box. Stand it up. Which way will each root grow? Say why.
  - Ask: Why do you think the root pointing up will grow that way? (good: Roots grow down, so it will turn round.; It will keep going up, because it is already pointing up.)
  - Note: Write down the guesses. Put the box in a warm place out of strong sun. The lid keeps the paper from drying out.
  - Wait: Leave the box standing up until tomorrow
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Look at each root tip together. 2. Compare it with the arrow you drew. 3. Count how many tips now point down. 4. Your child draws them.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the tip of each root. Which way is it pointing now? How many point down?  
  *Child mode:* Look at each root tip. Which way does it point now? Count how many point down.
  - Ask: The root that pointed up: what did it do? (good: It curved round and grew down.; It made a hook shape.)
  - Note: Usually all four tips point down by the next day. The root that pointed up makes a hook. The one that already pointed down grows straight on. Then read the card on how a root knows which way is down. For more, turn the box a quarter of the way round and look again the next day.
  - Check: A sprout lay with the root pointing up. The next day, which way did the root tip point? → **Down**

### a2 · Touch-Me-Not Tests: what makes them fold? (if you can get a touch-me-not) (20 min)

  *Child mode:* You will try four touches. Which one will fold the most? Say why.

- **s2 (8 min)**

  - Check: You touched the tip of one leaf. Leaflets further along the leaf folded too. What does that show? → **A signal travelled along the leaf**

  - Note: A touch-me-not folds their leaves every night and opens them in the morning. If the plant grows outside on a plot, go only with a grown-up, or skip this step and look early in the morning instead.

   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. In the root box, the root that pointed up curved round and grew down.

   **Answer:** Light from one side → the shoot bends towards it; Lying on their side → the root turns down; A finger touch → the touch-me-not folds; Night → the touch-me-not folds until morning. *Why:* Each response matches what the plant sensed: light, gravity, touch, or the dark of night.

   **Answer:** Because they sensed the light on the window side and grew towards it again.. *Why:* A strong answer says the shoots sensed where the light was and turned themselves by growing. Nobody moved them.

   Options: A shoot bending to a window / A root turning down / A touch-me-not folding / A money plant turning their leaves

1. (mcq) Nila put a cup of straight moong shoots by a window. The next day, which way did they lean?  
   Options: Towards the window / Away from the window / They fell flat  
   **Answer:** Towards the window. *Why:* The shoots sensed the light and grew towards it. Picking 'fell flat' may mean thinking the shoots were knocked over, but they stood up, only leaning.
2. (mcq) Which part of a shoot senses the light?  
   Options: The tip / The roots / The seed  
   **Answer:** The tip. *Why:* Darwin covered the tips of seedlings, and they stopped bending to the light. Picking 'roots' mixes up light with gravity.
3. (mcq) A moong sprout lies with the root pointing up. What will the root do?  
   Options: Keep growing up / Curve round and grow down / Stop growing  
   **Answer:** Curve round and grow down. *Why:* Roots grow in the direction gravity pulls. Picking 'keep growing up' means thinking a root just carries on the way it points.
4. (mcq) What helps a root tip know which way is down?  
   Options: Tiny heavy grains that sink to the lowest side of each cell / The root sees the floor / Water pulls the root  
   **Answer:** Tiny heavy grains that sink to the lowest side of each cell. *Why:* The grains always sink to the lowest side, whichever way the root lies. Roots have no eyes, so 'sees the floor' cannot be right.
5. (mcq) What does a touch-me-not do when you touch the tip of one leaf?  
   Options: The leaflets fold up, pair after pair / The leaf turns red / The plant grows taller  
   **Answer:** The leaflets fold up, pair after pair. *Why:* A signal runs along the leaf, water moves out of cells at the base of each leaflet, and the leaflets fold.
6. (mcq) When else does a touch-me-not fold their leaves, with nobody touching them?  
   Options: At night / At noon in bright sun / Never  
   **Answer:** At night. *Why:* A touch-me-not folds their leaves every night and opens them in the morning.
7. (mcq) How does a moong shoot bend towards the light?  
   Options: The shady side of the stem grows longer, so the stem curves / The plant walks on their roots / The wind blows the shoot over  
   **Answer:** The shady side of the stem grows longer, so the stem curves. *Why:* The plant sends a growth chemical to the shady side. That side grows longer, so the stem curves towards the light.
8. (mcq) Why can't we see a moong shoot bending, even if we stare at the shoot?  
   Options: The shoot bends by growing, and growing is slow / The shoot moves only when nobody is looking / Shoots never bend  
   **Answer:** The shoot bends by growing, and growing is slow. *Why:* Growing takes hours. We see the bend only if we look again later, or watch a time-lapse film.
9. (mcq) Ravi, a boy in Bhubaneswar, turned his money plant pot round, so the leaves faced the room. What will happen in a week?  
   Options: The leaves slowly turn back to face the window / The leaves fall off / Nothing changes  
   **Answer:** The leaves slowly turn back to face the window. *Why:* The plant senses which side the light comes from, and turns the leaves to face it again.
10. (mcq) Monica Gagliano dropped touch-me-not plants gently, again and again, with no harm. What happened after a few drops?  
   Options: Many stopped folding their leaves for the drop / They folded faster each time / They lost all their leaves  
   **Answer:** Many stopped folding their leaves for the drop. *Why:* The plants learned the drop was harmless. They still folded when shaken in a new way, so they were not just tired.
11. (mcq) Divya guessed the moong shoots would lean to the window. What is the best way to check her guess?  
   Options: Put shoots by a window and look the next day / Ask a friend what they think / Guess again, harder  
   **Answer:** Put shoots by a window and look the next day. *Why:* A test with real shoots shows what really happens. Asking or guessing again cannot tell us.
12. (mcq) Which of these do plants sense?  
   Options: Light, gravity and touch / Only water / Nothing at all  
   **Answer:** Light, gravity and touch. *Why:* Plants sense light, which way is down, touch, water and more. Picking 'nothing at all' may mean thinking that a plant that stays still senses nothing.

## Applications
- Gardeners turn pots on a windowsill every few days, so the plant grows straight instead of leaning to one side.
- Farmers sow seeds without worrying which way up they land, because roots find their way down and shoots find their way up.
- On the International Space Station, where nothing falls, plants have no 'down' to sense. Their shoots grow towards the lamps instead.
- Climbers like bottle gourd and beans are given a stick or a string to climb, because their tendrils and stems coil round what they touch.
- People who grow sunflowers for seeds know that grown flower heads face east, and plan their rows with that in mind.

## Spotted around us
- Money plants and tulsi on balconies and windowsills, with their leaves turned towards the open sky.
- Touch-me-not growing low on empty plots, roadsides and the edges of school grounds, with small pink puffball flowers.
- Sunflower fields in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra, with nearly all the open flowers facing the same way.
- A plant growing through a crack in a wall, leaning out towards the light.
- Bottle gourd or bitter gourd on a terrace or a balcony grille, their tendrils coiled round the bars.

## Open questions
- **Does a touch-me-not feel a touch, the way we feel one?** Plants feel touch and remember it. Scientists have measured the electrical signal a touch starts. Many still argue about what the plant experiences, because nobody can ask a plant.
- **How exactly does the growth chemical know to move to the shady side of a shoot?** Scientists have found the light-sensing parts in the tip and the growth chemical, called auxin. How the light sends auxin to the shady side is still being studied.
- **How do roots choose between gravity, water and a stone in the way, all at once?** Roots sense many things at the same time. How a root tip weighs them up and decides which way to grow is still being worked out.

## Connected maths
- In the window test, 12 moong shoots came up. After a day by the window, 9 leaned to the glass and the rest stood straight. How many stood straight? Did most shoots lean? **3 stood straight. Yes, most leaned: 9 is far more than 3.**. 12 take away 9 is 3. 9 shoots leaned and only 3 did not, so most of them leaned. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L2`)
- Tara made a tally of leaflet pairs that folded. Fingertip: |||| |||. Feather: |||. Drop of water: ||||. Gentle blow: |. Which touch folded the most? - A moong root was 2 cm long, pointing up. The next day the root had grown 1 cm more, and the new part curved down. If the root keeps growing 1 cm a day, how long will the root be after 4 days in all? **6 cm.**. 2 cm to start, then 1 cm each day for 4 days: 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6. (queues `math.length.standard-units-length.L2`)
- In the turn-the-pot project, Anu counted the leaves facing the window: 2 on day 1, 5 on day 3, 8 on day 5. If the pattern goes on, how many will face the window on day 7? **11 leaves.**. The number goes up by 3 each time: 2, 5, 8, so next is 11. A real plant may slow down once nearly all the leaves face the light. (queues `math.patterns.number-patterns.L2`)
