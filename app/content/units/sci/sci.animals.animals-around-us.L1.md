# Animals around us: what they eat, where they live, how they move
`sci.animals.animals-around-us.L1`

**Summary.** Ants live in the cracks of our walls, a gecko waits by the tube light, and a crow calls from the window. Every one of these animals has to find food, a safe place to live and a way to move. Humans are animals too. Your child hunts for the animals who share their home, then tests their own teeth on five foods. A mouth fits the food an animal eats, and that is true for a crow, a cow and you.

**Intro.** *Who lives in your home? Count everyone. Did you count the ants, the gecko and the spider?* Most families count only the people. But many other animals live in a flat too. Ants live in tiny cracks, a gecko hides behind a cupboard, and a spider sits in a corner. In this unit we find the animals in our home, and then we look at our own teeth.

## Terms
- **Animal**: An animal is a living being who has to eat food to live. Most animals can move about. *e.g.* An ant, a crow, a cow and you are all animals.
- **Food**: Food is what a living being eats to live, grow and move. *e.g.* Rice and dal are food for you. Grass is food for a cow. A moth is food for a gecko.
- **Home**: An animal's home is the place where they rest, hide and stay safe. *e.g.* Ants make their home in a crack in the wall. A pigeon rests on a window ledge.
- **Teeth**: Teeth are the hard white parts in your mouth that bite and chew food. *e.g.* Your front teeth bite a piece off a carrot. Your back teeth chew it up.
- **Beak**: A beak is a bird's hard mouth. Birds have no teeth, so the beak picks up and breaks the food. *e.g.* A pigeon picks up grains with her short beak.

## Explanations
### Humans are animals too · *defined*
**Definition:** An animal is a living being who has to eat food to live. Plants make their own food using light. Animals find their food.

An ant, a crow, a cow and a child are all animals. All of them eat food, breathe air and grow. Your body has bones, blood and a heart, like a cat's or a cow's. So when you count the animals in your home, count yourself too.
- e.g. You eat, breathe, grow and move. So does the crow at the window.
- e.g. A cat, a cow and a human mother all feed their babies milk.
- e.g. An ant is much smaller than you, but an ant eats, breathes and moves too.
### Every mouth fits a food · *defined*
**Definition:** Animals eat many different foods. The shape of an animal's mouth fits the food they eat.

Ants eat sugar and crumbs. A gecko eats moths and mosquitoes. A cow eats grass, and a crow eats almost anything. Each mouth fits the food: a cow has wide, flat back teeth to grind grass, a gecko has tiny teeth to grip an insect, and a pigeon has a short beak to pick up grains. You have thin front teeth to bite and wide back teeth to chew, because you eat many kinds of food.
- e.g. A cow has no front teeth on top, only a hard pad. She grips grass between her bottom teeth and that pad.
- e.g. A butterfly has a long, thin tube for a mouth. She sips the sweet juice from flowers.
- e.g. You bite a carrot with your front teeth and chew it with your back teeth.
### Every animal has a home place · *defined*
**Definition:** Animals live where they can find food and stay safe.

An animal's home is near their food and keeps them safe from danger, rain and heat. Ants live in cracks in the walls, close to the crumbs in the kitchen. A gecko hides behind a cupboard in the day and comes out near the lights at night, because insects fly to the light. A spider makes a web in a quiet corner, where insects fly in. So one flat is home to many animals.
- e.g. Ants live inside a crack near the kitchen, close to the food.
- e.g. A gecko waits near the tube light at night, where the moths come.
- e.g. A squirrel makes a nest of grass high up in a tree, away from cats.
### Animals move in many ways · *defined*
**Definition:** Animals move to find food, to find a home and to get away from danger.

A pigeon walks, nodding her head with each step. A sparrow hops on both feet together. Ants walk fast on six legs. A gecko climbs straight up a wall, because the tiny hairs under each toe cling to the wall. A snake has no legs, and slides along on her belly scales. You walk and run on two legs.
- e.g. A pigeon walks on the ledge. A sparrow hops.
- e.g. A gecko runs up the wall and across the ceiling.
- e.g. You walk on two legs, and a cat walks on four.
### Do the animals around us choose? · *debated*
A gecko waits by the tube light every night, because moths come there. A crow tries a new food, then comes back for more. An ant tastes a crumb and decides to carry the crumb home. Animals sense, choose and remember. A gecko learns where the moths come, and a crow learns which window has rice. Some scientists use a different word for this. They call it instinct, and say the animal is born knowing what to do.
- e.g. A gecko comes back to the same tube light every night.
- e.g. A crow learns which family puts out rice in the morning.
- e.g. Ants find a sugar spill again the next day.
- **Scientists who study animal minds:** Animals sense the world, choose what to do and remember what worked. A gecko chose a good place to wait for moths.
- **Other scientists:** Much of what small animals do is instinct. They are born knowing how to do it, and they do it the same way each time.

## Mostly agreed
- Humans are animals. *How we know:* Humans eat, breathe and grow like other animals. Our bodies have bones, blood and a heart, like a cat's. Scientists have found that humans and all other animals came from the same ancestors, long ago.
- An animal's mouth fits the food they eat. *How we know:* Animals that eat grass, like cows and goats, have wide, flat back teeth for grinding. Animals that hunt, like cats, have sharp, pointed teeth. Birds that crack seeds have short, thick beaks.
- Many wild animals live in towns and cities, right next to us. *How we know:* Crows, pigeons, sparrows, squirrels, geckos, ants and spiders live in Indian cities. When people count the birds in a city, they find dozens of kinds.

## How we found out
**Mary Anning, an English fossil hunter, with her brother Joseph; William Buckland, who named coprolites, The ichthyosaur skeleton about 1811 to 1812; coprolites named in 1829.** About 200 years ago, in Lyme Regis, a seaside town in England, a girl called Mary Anning hunted for fossils. Fossils are the remains of living beings from long ago, turned to stone. Mary's family was poor, and she sold fossils to visitors to earn money. Her brother Joseph dug up the skull of an ichthyosaur, and Mary found the rest of the skeleton, and she grew up to be a famous fossil hunter. Ichthyosaurs were big sea animals with long jaws full of pointed teeth. Pointed teeth are good for gripping slippery fish. On the same beach, people found odd round stones. They called them bezoar stones, and nobody knew what they were. Mary noticed that these stones often lay inside the belly of an ichthyosaur skeleton. When she broke them open, she found tiny fish bones and fish scales inside. The stones were fossil poo! In 1829, the scientist William Buckland named them coprolites. Now people could see what ichthyosaurs ate: fish, just as their teeth suggested. Mary was not allowed to join the Geological Society of London, because she was a woman.  
*What people believed before:* That the 'bezoar stones' on the beach were just odd stones. Mary Anning found them inside ichthyosaur skeletons, broke them open and found fish bones and scales: they were fossil poo, which showed what ichthyosaurs ate.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Humans are not animals.** (A very common idea, among children and grown-ups; *rejected*)  
  For: Humans talk, cook, read and build cities. No other animal does all of these.  
  Against: Humans eat, breathe, grow and have babies, like other animals. Our bodies have the same parts as other mammals. Every animal has their own special skills. A bat finds food with sound, and a human cooks.
- **A house gecko is poisonous.** (A common belief in many Indian homes; *rejected*)  
  For: Geckos look strange and move fast, and people fear a gecko falling into food.  
  Against: The house geckos in Indian homes have no poison. They do not harm people. But throw away food if a gecko falls into the pot. A gecko's body can carry germs, like any animal's.
- **Pigeons and crows are pests and do not belong in cities.** (Some people in cities; *debated*)  
  For: Pigeon droppings make balconies dirty, and breathing in dust from old droppings can make some people ill. Crows sometimes snatch food.  
  Against: Crows eat scraps and dead animals, which helps keep streets clean. Birds lived in these places before the buildings came. Many families share food with crows every day.

## Roots
We have found the animals in our home and tested our own teeth. Now we ask what our mouths tell us about ourselves and the other animals.

**Deep truth.** In our chewing test, front teeth bit the food and back teeth ground it up. Curd needed almost no chewing, and carrot needed the most. Humans have both kinds of teeth because humans eat many kinds of food. So an animal's mouth shows what that animal eats.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think of teeth as just for eating, the same in everyone. But every animal's mouth is shaped by their food, so looking in a mouth tells you how that animal lives.  
*Evidence:* In the chewing test, thin front teeth bit the carrot and the chapati, and wide back teeth chewed every food except the curd. The hard carrot needed many more chews than the soft banana.

- *who am i:* You are an animal with teeth for many foods. Your mouth tells the story of what humans eat.
- *what is life:* Every animal must find food. The ant, the crow, the gecko and you each have a body that fits your own food.
- *what is this world:* One flat is home to many animals. Each one lives where they find their food and a safe place.

The ant, the crow, the gecko and you all need food. Each of you gets it in a different way: jaws, a beak, tiny gripping teeth, front and back teeth. One need, food, has given each animal a different mouth. So the animals in one small flat show many answers to the same need.

- **Zhuangzi, chapter 2 (Discussion on Making All Things Equal):** Zhuangzi, a Chinese thinker, wrote that people eat the meat of farm animals, deer eat grass, centipedes eat snakes, and owls and crows eat mice. Then he asked: which of them knows the right taste? (Our home hunt found the same thing. Each animal eats a different food, and each mouth fits their own food. No one food is right for everyone.)
- **Thirukkural 322, by Thiruvalluvar:** To share your food and to care for all living beings is the best of all the teachings. (Every animal in our home needs food, as we do. Many families share a little rice with the crows each day.)
- Science (settled): An animal's teeth or beak fit the food they eat. Scientists can tell what a fossil animal ate by looking at their teeth.
- Science (open): Animals sense, choose and remember. What eating feels like to a gecko or an ant, nobody knows.
- Other view, Many biologists: Many biologists say a gecko waiting by the light is acting on instinct, set off by the light and the moving insects. They say the gecko is born knowing how to hunt, and they would not call it a choice.
- Practice, One slow bite (3 min): Take one raisin or a small piece of fruit. Close your eyes. Bite it slowly with your front teeth. Feel your tongue move it to the back. Feel your back teeth chew. Before you swallow, think of the crow and the ant, who are eating somewhere too.
- Ask: A crow has no teeth, and you have about twenty. Who has the better mouth?
- Ask: Why might one animal love a food that another animal leaves alone?
- Ask: What do you think the crow at the window learns about you?

## Deep and big questions
- *deep:* Why does a gecko live in our home, and not in the forest? (hint: Think about the lights, the insects that come to them, and the cracks behind the cupboards.)
- *big:* Our flat is home to people, ants, a gecko and a spider. Whose home is it? (hint: Who was here first? Who pays the rent? Who needs the flat?)

## Story: Only Four of Us
*Ponni, Selvan, Amma and Paati are made up. Everything the animals do in the story is true of crows, ants, spiders and house geckos in Indian homes.*

Ponni lived in a flat in Coimbatore, on the fourth floor. She was six. She lived with her mother, Amma, her grandmother, Paati, and her little brother Selvan.

One morning, Ponni drew a picture of her family. 'Only four of us live here,' she said.

Amma smiled. 'Are you sure? Look around today, and count again.'

Ponni went to the kitchen first. By the sugar dabba, she saw a thin black line. It was a line of tiny ants. One ant held a white grain of sugar in her jaws. Ponni bent down close. The ants walked fast on their six legs, all the way to a crack under the tiles. Then they went inside, one by one.

'They live in the wall,' said Ponni. 'And they eat our sugar.' She drew the ants on her picture.

After lunch, Paati put a small ball of rice on the window ledge. 'Kaa, kaa,' called Paati. Soon a crow landed on the grill. She held the rice under one foot. She pecked it into pieces with her strong black beak. Then she flew away with a piece of rice, to the tall coconut tree across the road.

'The crow lives in that tree,' said Paati. 'She comes here every day for her rice.' Ponni drew the crow too.

In the evening, Ponni went into the bathroom. High in the corner, there was a fine web. A brown spider sat in the middle, very still. A tiny fly was caught in the web. The spider walked over on long, thin legs.

'Amma! The spider has caught her dinner,' said Ponni. She drew the spider and her web.

That night, the tube light was on in the hall. Moths flew round and round the light. Selvan pointed up and squealed. A gecko was on the wall, right beside the light. The gecko stayed very still. Then, quick as a blink, the gecko ran forward and snapped up a moth.

'Will the gecko bite us?' asked Selvan. 'Is the gecko poisonous?'

'No,' said Amma. 'Our gecko has no poison. The gecko eats the moths and the mosquitoes. In the day, the gecko hides behind the cupboard, in the dark and safe.'

'How does the gecko walk on the ceiling?' asked Ponni.

'The gecko's toes have millions of tiny hairs,' said Amma. 'They cling to the wall, so the gecko does not fall.'

Ponni drew the gecko next to the light. Then she looked at her picture. There were four people, a crow, a spider, a gecko and so many ants that she could not count them.

At dinner, Paati made dosa, with a carrot salad on the side. Ponni bit a piece of carrot with her front teeth. Crunch. Then she chewed it with her back teeth, round and round.

'Paati,' said Ponni, with her mouth full. 'I bite with my front teeth, like the gecko snaps. And I chew with my back teeth. Which animal chews like me?'

'A cow chews grass all day with her flat back teeth,' said Paati. 'Every animal has a mouth for their own food. The crow has a beak for rice, and you have teeth for dosa and carrots.'

Ponni put down her dosa. She picked up her pencil. In the middle of her picture, she wrote one more word next to her own face. The word was 'animal'.

'Amma,' she said. 'Not only four of us live here. Lots of animals live here. And I am one of them.'

**For the parent:** Ponni finds that her flat is home to many animals, each with their own food, home and way of moving, and that she is one of the animals too.

- Ask: Which animals did Ponni find in her flat? What did each one eat?
- Ask: Why did Selvan ask if the gecko is poisonous? What did Amma say?
- Ask: Why did Ponni write 'animal' next to her own face?

## Experiments
### e1 · The Chewing Test: which teeth do you use for each food? (25 min)
- **question:** Which teeth do you use to bite and to chew each food? Which food needs the most chews?
- **predict:** Guess first. Which food will need the most chews? Which will need no chewing at all? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Every solid piece is about the size of the top of your thumb. Curd is one small spoon. You chew in your usual way, and the grown-up counts every chew the same way. A grown-up does the same test, so we can compare.
- **safety:** Sit down to eat, and stay with your child the whole time. Use small pieces and a thin carrot stick. Use only foods your child already eats, and wash hands first. A grown-up cuts the carrot.
- **record:** Draw your mouth, with thin front teeth and wide back teeth. Next to each food, draw a tally of the chews.
- **results table:** Food | Which teeth bit it? | How many chews? | Which teeth chewed it?
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Stand at a mirror together. 2. Smile wide. 3. Look at the front teeth, then open wide and look at the back teeth. 4. Touch them gently with a clean finger.  
  *Parent says:* Look at your front teeth. Are they thin or wide? Now look right at the back. What shape are those teeth?  
  *Child mode:* Look at your front teeth. Are they thin? Look at your back teeth. Are they wide and bumpy?
  - Ask: How are the front teeth and back teeth different? (good: The front ones are thin and flat, like a knife.; The back ones are big and wide and bumpy.)
  - Note: Many six-year-olds have a gap where a milk tooth fell out. That is fine. A bigger tooth is coming. Ask your child to notice if biting is harder there.
- **s2 (3 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut a thumb-sized piece of banana, carrot and chapati. 2. Put one spoon of curd and a small pinch of puffed rice on the plate. 3. Ask your child to guess, and to say why.  
  *Parent says:* Which food will need the most chews? Which will need none? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Which food needs the most chews? Which needs none? Why?
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: The carrot is hard, so it needs lots of chews.; Curd is soft, so I can just swallow it.)
  - Note: Write down the guess. Don't say what will happen.
- **s3 (10 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child eats one food at a time. 2. Before each one, ask which teeth will bite. 3. Count every chew aloud, and make a tally. 4. Ask which teeth did the chewing.  
  *Parent says:* Take a bite. Did your front teeth or your back teeth bite it? Now chew, and I will count.  
  *Child mode:* Bite the food. Which teeth bit it? Now chew. Your grown-up counts.
  - Ask: Which teeth chewed the carrot? (good: My back teeth.; The big ones at the back.)
  - Ask: Did you need to chew the curd? (good: No, I just swallowed it.; Only a little.)
  - Note: Put a finger on your child's cheek near the back teeth while they chew. They can feel the jaw working. Most children find the curd needs almost no chewing and the carrot needs the most.
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Now swap. A grown-up eats the same five foods. Your child counts the grown-up's chews.  
  *Parent says:* Now it is my turn. You count my chews. Will I need more chews or fewer?  
  *Child mode:* Now count your grown-up's chews. Are they more or fewer than yours?
  - Ask: Was the order the same for both of us? (good: Yes, curd was fewest and carrot was most.; You needed fewer chews, but the carrot still needed the most.)
  - Note: A grown-up's chew counts are usually a little different, but the order of the foods is often the same. That shows the food, not only the person, decides the chewing.
- **s5 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Look at the tally together. Ask your child to say what they found.  
  *Parent says:* What did you find? What did your front teeth do, and what did your back teeth do?  
  *Child mode:* Tell your grown-up what you found. What do front teeth do? What do back teeth do?
  - Ask: What do your front teeth do, and what do your back teeth do? (good: Front teeth bite pieces off.; Back teeth chew the food up small.)
  - Check: Which teeth bite a piece off a carrot? → **Front teeth**
  - Think: A cow eats grass all day. Why might she need to chew so much?

  *Child mode:* Sit very still. Find the gecko. What is the gecko doing?

  - Note: Geckos often wait still for a long time, then dash. If no gecko comes tonight, try another night, or look for small black-and-white droppings near the light.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Talk about what you saw. Look at how the gecko held on to the wall.  
  *Parent says:* How did the gecko move? How do you think the gecko stays on the wall without falling?  
  *Child mode:* How did the gecko move? How does the gecko not fall?
  - Ask: How does a gecko climb a wall? (good: Tiny hairs on the toes cling to the wall.; The toes have millions of tiny hairs that hold on.)
  - Think: The gecko chose this light. How did the gecko know the moths would come?
### a3 · Walk or Hop? Watch a bird on the ledge (8 min)
- **record:** Draw how the bird moved: one-two-one-two footprints for walking, or pairs side by side for hopping.
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* An hour before, put a few rice grains on the ledge or the balcony floor. Then stand back from the window and wait quietly. Watch how the bird moves.  
  *Parent says:* Watch the bird's feet. Does the bird put one foot, then the other? Or both feet together?  
  *Child mode:* Watch the bird's feet. One foot, then the other? Or both feet together?
  - Ask: Did the bird walk or hop? (good: The pigeon walked, one foot then the other.; The sparrow hopped.)
  - Note: Pigeons and mynas walk, one foot at a time. Pigeons nod their head with each step. Sparrows hop with both feet together. Crows walk and also hop. Stay behind the window grill and never lean out. Birds may not come in five minutes. If none comes, watch again later in the day, or on a short walk outside.
- **s2 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Now your child tries it. Walk across the room like a pigeon, then hop like a sparrow.  
  *Parent says:* Now you try. Walk like the pigeon. Now hop like a sparrow. Which is easier for you?  
  *Child mode:* Walk like a pigeon. Now hop like a sparrow. Which is easier?
  - Ask: Which was easier for you, walking or hopping? Why? (good: Walking, because my legs are long.; Hopping makes me tired fast.)

### c1 · Invent an Animal Who Could Live in Our Flat (20 min)
- **problem:** Invent an animal who could live in your flat without anyone feeding them. Decide what they eat, where they hide and how they move. Then make them out of dough or paper.

- **general:** A gecko eats insects that come to the lights, hides behind cupboards, and climbs walls with clinging toes.; Ants eat crumbs, live in cracks and walk on six legs.; A spider eats insects caught in her web and lives in a quiet corner.
- **creative:** An animal with a long, thin tongue for licking up spilt sugar from inside a dabba.; A flat animal who slides under doors and eats dust and dead skin.; An animal with sucker feet who lives on the bathroom tiles and eats the black mould in the grout.

## Worksheets
### Animals Around Us (core) (core, 10 questions)
1. (sort) Colour the animals green. Cross out the things that are not animals.  
   **Answer:** crow → Animal; ant → Animal; a girl → Animal; a stone → Not an animal; a gecko → Animal; a spoon → Not an animal. *Why:* Crows, ants, geckos and girls are all animals. They eat, breathe and grow. A stone and a spoon were never alive.
2. (match) Draw a line from each animal to the food they eat.  
   **Answer:** Cow → Grass; Gecko → Moth; Pigeon → Grains; Butterfly → Flower. *Why:* A cow grinds grass, a gecko snaps up insects, a pigeon picks up grains and a butterfly sips nectar from flowers.
3. (draw) Draw your mouth, open wide. Colour the front teeth that bite red. Colour the back teeth that chew blue.  
   **Answer:** Thin front teeth coloured red, wide back teeth coloured blue.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows the front teeth thin and the back teeth wide, as the child saw in the mirror.
4. (mcq) Remember the chewing test? Which food needed almost no chewing?  
   Options: Carrot / Curd / Chapati  
   **Answer:** Curd. *Why:* Curd is soft, so it needs almost no chewing. Carrot and chapati are tougher, so the back teeth must chew them many times.
5. (match) Draw a line from each animal to their home.  
   **Answer:** Ants → A crack in the wall; Spider → A web in a corner; Crow → A nest in a tall tree. *Why:* Ants live in cracks near food, spiders build webs where insects fly, and crows nest high in trees.
6. (truefalse) A crow has teeth.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. A crow has a beak and no teeth. The beak picks up and breaks the food.
7. (label) Say or write how each animal moves: walk, hop, climb or slither.  
   **Answer:** ['pigeon: walk', 'sparrow: hop', 'gecko: climb', 'snake: slither']. *Why:* Pigeons walk one foot at a time, sparrows hop with both feet, geckos climb with clinging toes and snakes slither on their belly scales.
8. (count) Count the animals in this picture of a kitchen: three ants, a gecko, a girl and a cup.  
   **Answer:** 5. *Why:* Three ants, one gecko and one girl make 5 animals. The cup is not an animal. Did you remember to count the girl?
9. (write) Say it to a grown-up, who writes it down: why does a gecko wait by the tube light at night?  
   **Answer:** Moths and other insects fly to the light. The gecko waits there to catch them.. *Why:* A strong answer links the place (the light) to the food (the insects that come to it).
10. (perspective) Two sisters in Chennai see a gecko on the kitchen wall.  
   **Answer:** A strong answer picks a side and gives a reason, such as 'Nandini, because the gecko eats mosquitoes that bite us.' Then the child is fair to the other side: Kayal is right that a gecko should not walk on our food.. *Why:* Both sisters have a point. The gecko helps by eating insects and has no poison. But any animal walking on food can carry germs, so food is kept covered.
### Animals Around Us (stretch) (stretch, 10 questions)
1. (mcq) A cow has wide, flat back teeth. What food are those teeth best for?  
   Options: Grinding grass / Catching moths / Sipping flower juice  
   **Answer:** Grinding grass. *Why:* Wide, flat back teeth grind tough grass. Catching moths needs speed and small gripping teeth. Sipping needs a tube.
2. (odd) Which one is the odd one out? Think about what they eat.  
   Options: Gecko / Spider / Cow / Crow  
   **Answer:** 2. *Why:* The gecko, the spider and the crow all eat insects. The cow eats grass and plants.
3. (draw) Draw a bird's footprints for walking, and a bird's footprints for hopping.  
   **Answer:** Walking: one-two-one-two, one foot ahead of the other. Hopping: two footprints side by side, in pairs.. *Why:* A strong drawing shows that a walking bird puts one foot ahead, and a hopping bird lands with both feet together.
4. (order) Put the chewing test in order.  
   **Answer:** ['Look at your teeth in a mirror', 'Guess which food needs the most chews', 'Take a bite', 'Count the chews']. *Why:* First we look, then we guess, then we bite, then we count.
5. (write) Say it to a grown-up, who writes it down: a crow has no teeth. How does a crow eat a big ball of rice?  
   **Answer:** The crow holds the rice under one foot and pecks it into small pieces with the beak.. *Why:* A strong answer says that the beak and the foot do the work that teeth do for us.
6. (sort) Sort these animals: do they live inside our flat, or outside?  
   **Answer:** ants → Often inside the flat; gecko → Often inside the flat; cow → Usually outside; squirrel → Usually outside; spider → Often inside the flat; crow → Usually outside. *Why:* Ants, geckos and spiders find food and hiding places inside. Cows, squirrels and crows need the street and the trees, but they visit our windows.
7. (truefalse) Every animal moves on legs.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. A snake moves with no legs, using their body and belly scales. An earthworm stretches and squeezes. A fish swims with fins.
8. (perspective) Three friends in Pune talk about who owns their building.  
   **Answer:** Any side can be argued well. A strong answer gives a reason, such as 'Grace, because the ants and the gecko live here too and need the building.' Then it is fair to another friend, such as 'Isha is right that people built it.'. *Why:* This is a real question with no single answer. People built the building, other animals live in it too, and many animals lived on this land before.
9. (truefalse) A butterfly bites flowers with her teeth.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. A butterfly has no teeth. She sips sweet nectar from flowers through a long, thin tube.
10. (mcq) Why do humans have thin front teeth and wide back teeth?  
   Options: Humans eat many kinds of food / Humans only eat grass / Humans only eat insects  
   **Answer:** Humans eat many kinds of food. *Why:* Front teeth bite pieces off and back teeth chew them up. Humans eat many kinds of food, so we need both.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) Which of these is an animal?  
   Options: A stone / A crow / A chair  
   **Answer:** A crow. *Why:* A crow is an animal. A crow eats, breathes, grows and moves. A stone and a chair were never alive.
2. (mcq) Are humans animals?  
   Options: Yes, humans are animals / No, humans are not animals / Only grown-ups are animals  
   **Answer:** Yes, humans are animals. *Why:* Humans are animals. We eat, breathe, grow and have babies like other animals.
3. (mcq) What does a gecko eat?  
   Options: Grass / Insects, like moths and mosquitoes / Rice  
   **Answer:** Insects, like moths and mosquitoes. *Why:* A gecko eats insects. That is why a gecko waits near a light at night, where insects come.
4. (mcq) What does a pigeon mostly eat?  
   Options: Grains and seeds / Grass / Flies  
   **Answer:** Grains and seeds. *Why:* A pigeon picks up grains and seeds with a short beak.
5. (mcq) Where do the ants in a kitchen usually live?  
   Options: In tiny cracks in the walls and floor / On top of the fan / Inside the tap  
   **Answer:** In tiny cracks in the walls and floor. *Why:* Ants live in small cracks, close to the food in the kitchen.
6. (mcq) Why does a spider build a web in a corner?  
   Options: Insects fly there and get caught / Spiders like to look out of the window / The web keeps the spider warm  
   **Answer:** Insects fly there and get caught. *Why:* A spider eats insects. The web catches the insects that fly into that corner.
7. (mcq) How does a sparrow move on the ground?  
   Options: The sparrow hops with both feet together / The sparrow slithers / The sparrow swims  
   **Answer:** The sparrow hops with both feet together. *Why:* Sparrows hop with both feet together. Pigeons walk one foot at a time.
8. (mcq) How does a gecko climb up a wall?  
   Options: Tiny hairs on the toes cling to the wall / The gecko flies / The gecko jumps from the floor  
   **Answer:** Tiny hairs on the toes cling to the wall. *Why:* A gecko's toes have millions of tiny hairs that cling to the wall, so the gecko can climb and even walk on the ceiling.
9. (mcq) In the chewing test, which teeth chewed the food up small?  
   Options: The front teeth / The back teeth / No teeth  
   **Answer:** The back teeth. *Why:* Wide back teeth chew and grind the food. Thin front teeth bite pieces off.
10. (mcq) Why did each piece of food in the chewing test have to be the same size?  
   Options: So the food was the only difference / To save food / Because big pieces taste better  
   **Answer:** So the food was the only difference. *Why:* If one piece were bigger, it might need more chews just because of its size. With the same size, only the kind of food is different.

## Applications

## Open questions
- **What does a gecko feel when the gecko catches a moth?** Geckos sense, choose and learn. But nobody knows what being a gecko feels like from the inside.
- **How many kinds of animals live in one city flat?** Scientists who search homes carefully find many tiny animals, like mites, that most people never see. Nobody has counted them for most Indian homes.
- **Why have house sparrows become rare in some Indian cities?** Scientists have several ideas, such as fewer holes in new buildings for nests and fewer insects to feed the chicks. They still argue about which matter most.
