# Seeds: how a seed grows into a new plant
`sci.seeds-and-life-cycles.seeds-and-germination.L1`

**Summary.** Plant a chana seed, and for days nothing shows above the soil. But under the soil, the seed is busy. A seed is a tiny living plant, and when the seed gets water, the seed grows a root first, down into the soil. Then a shoot comes up into the light. Your child plants five chana seeds and gently digs one up each day to see what is happening underneath.

**Intro.** *Hold a dry chana seed. Is this hard little ball alive? Could a whole plant come out of this seed?* Many plants you know, from the tulsi on the balcony to the big neem tree on the road, began as a seed. Seeds look like small stones. But put a seed in damp soil, and a new plant grows out of the seed. In this unit we plant seeds and watch. We also peek under the soil, to find out what a seed does before we can see anything.

## Learning outcomes
0. By the end I can say that a seed grows into a new plant.
1. By the end I can say what a seed needs to start growing: water.
2. By the end I can say which part comes out of a seed first: the root.
3. By the end I can say what the root does and what the shoot does.
4. By the end I can plant a seed, look after the seed, and say what I saw each day.
5. By the end I can say that a seed grows into the same kind of plant as the plant that made the seed.

## Terms
- **Seed**: A seed is a tiny living plant packed with food, inside a hard coat. *e.g.* Chana, rajma, moong and the black seeds in a papaya are all seeds.
- **Sprout**: To sprout is to start growing. A sprout is also the young plant that comes out of a seed. *e.g.* Moong soaked overnight and kept on a wet cloth sprout in a day.
- **Root**: The root is the part of a plant that grows down into the soil. A root drinks water and holds the plant in place. *e.g.* Pull up a weed gently, and the thin white threads at the bottom are the roots.
- **Shoot**: The shoot is the part of a plant that grows up into the light. The stem and leaves grow from the shoot. *e.g.* A new chana plant pushes a curled green shoot up out of the soil.
- **Seed leaves**: Seed leaves are the first leaves that open on a young plant. They often look different from the plant's later leaves. *e.g.* A young pumpkin plant opens two round, smooth seed leaves first.

## Explanations
### A seed is a tiny living plant · *defined*
**Definition:** A seed holds a tiny baby plant and a store of food, wrapped in a seed coat.

A seed looks like a small stone, but the seed is alive. Inside the hard coat there is a tiny baby plant, with a store of food next to the baby plant. A dry seed waits, sometimes for years. When the seed gets water, the seed swells and starts to grow.
- e.g. A dry chana seed in a jar stays the same for months. Soaked overnight, the chana is fat and soft by morning.
- e.g. Rice, wheat, chana and dal are seeds that people eat.
- e.g. A mango stone has the mango tree's seed inside.
### The root comes out first · *defined*
**Definition:** When a seed sprouts, the root nearly always comes out first and grows down. The shoot comes later and grows up.

The root comes out first, through the seed coat. The root grows down into the soil, drinks water and holds the plant in place. Then the shoot grows up, curled like a bent finger so the soil does not scrape the tender tip. So for the first days, most of the growing happens under the soil, where we cannot see it.
- e.g. Your chana grew a white root before any green showed above the soil.
- e.g. Moong sprouts in the kitchen have a white tail. That tail is the root.
- e.g. A young peepal plant growing out of a wall crack sends roots deep into the crack.
### A seed grows into the same kind of plant as the plant that made the seed · *defined*
**Definition:** A seed grows into the same kind of plant as the plant that made the seed.

Plant a pumpkin seed, and you get a pumpkin plant. Plant a chana seed, and you get a chana plant. The size of the seed does not decide the plant. The kind of seed does.
- e.g. Seeds from a pumpkin grow into pumpkin plants.
- e.g. A tamarind seed grows into a tamarind tree.
- e.g. A mango stone grows into a mango tree, never a coconut palm.

## Mostly agreed
- Seeds are alive. Each seed holds a tiny baby plant. *How we know:* Open a soaked chana or rajma and you can see the baby plant at one edge. Dry seeds kept for months still sprout when they get water.
- Seeds need water to start growing. *How we know:* Dry seeds in a jar never sprout. The same seeds sprout within a few days once they are kept damp.
- In almost all seeds, the root comes out first. *How we know:* People who study seeds watch thousands of them sprout. The root comes out first and grows down. Scientists even use the root coming out as the sign that a seed has sprouted.

## How we found out
**Sarah Sallon, a doctor and scientist, and Elaine Solowey, a plant scientist, in Israel, The seeds were found in the 1960s. Planted in January 2005. Reported in the journal Science in 2008..** In the 1960s, archaeologists digging at Masada, an old fort on a hill in Israel, found date seeds in a jar. The seeds were about 2,000 years old. Most people thought seeds that old must be long dead. The seeds sat in a drawer for about 40 years. Then a doctor and scientist, Sarah Sallon, wondered if they could still grow. She asked a plant scientist, Elaine Solowey, to try. In January 2005, Elaine Solowey soaked three of the seeds and planted them. After about eight weeks, one seed sent up a shoot. The team named the young date palm Methuselah, after a very old man in the Bible. Scientists tested a small piece of a leftover seed from the same place, and found the seed was about 2,000 years old. In 2008 the team wrote about it in the journal Science. At that time, Methuselah was the oldest seed ever known to grow into a plant. The tiny baby plant inside had stayed alive and waited for water for 2,000 years.  
*What people believed before:* That seeds thousands of years old must be dead. One 2,000-year-old date seed planted by Elaine Solowey sprouted and grew into a palm.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Seeds are not alive. They are like little stones.** (A very common idea, among children and grown-ups; *rejected*)  
  For: A dry seed does not move, eat or grow. A dry seed looks and feels like a small stone.  
  Against: A pebble in damp soil stays a pebble. A seed in damp soil swells, grows a root and becomes a plant. Seeds are alive, and they wait until they get water.
- **A big seed grows into a big plant.** (A common guess; *rejected*)  
  For: Some big seeds, like a coconut, do grow into tall plants.  
  Against: A banyan seed is tiny, smaller than a grain of rai, and a banyan becomes a huge tree. A rajma seed is big, and a rajma plant stays small. The kind of seed decides, not the size.
- **Leaves grow first, and roots come later.** (A common guess, because leaves are what we see first; *rejected*)  
  For: When we plant a seed, the first thing we see is a green shoot coming up.  
  Against: Under the soil, the root came out days before. Dig up a sprouting chana and you see a long root and no leaves yet.

## Roots
We planted chana, dug them up day by day, and saw what a seed does under the soil. Now we ask what that tells us about plants, and about ourselves.

**Deep truth.** Our chana showed nothing above the soil for days. Under the soil, each one had already grown a root. A seed grows a root down first, to drink and hold on. Only then does the shoot come up into the light.  
*Habit it breaks:* We judge whether something is growing by what we can see. Above the soil nothing seemed to happen, yet the first days of a plant's life were busy underground.  
*Evidence:* In Peek Under the Soil, the dug-up seeds showed a root that got longer each day: a tiny tip on seed 1, a root as long as a fingernail on seed 2, and a long root on seed 3. A curled shoot came last.

- *who am i:* You also grew out of sight, for nine months before you were born.
- *what is life:* A living seed senses water and which way is down, and chooses where to grow, even in the dark.
- *what is this world:* Under every pot, field and forest, roots are growing where we cannot see. Much of the world's living happens out of sight.

A seed starts in the dark. The root senses which way is down and grows towards water. The shoot grows up towards the light. So one tiny seed becomes two parts going two ways, down into the soil and up into the air.

- **Chandogya Upanishad 6.12:** Uddalaka asks his son Shvetaketu to break open a banyan fruit, and then one of its tiny seeds. Shvetaketu says he sees nothing inside. Uddalaka says the great banyan tree grows from that very fine thing which he cannot see. (Our chana showed nothing on top for days, yet a whole root was growing. A big plant starts from something too small, or too hidden, to see.)
- **Tao Te Ching 64:** A tree as wide as your arms can reach grows from a tiny shoot. A journey of a thousand miles begins with one step. (Every big tree began as one small seed growing one small root. The start is small and slow, and it is still the start.)
- Science (settled): In almost all flowering plants, the root comes out of the seed first. Scientists use the root coming out as the sign that a seed has sprouted.
- Science (open): Roots sense which way is down, where water is, and what they touch, and they change where they grow. What that sensing is like for a plant, nobody knows yet.
- Other view, Many plant scientists: Many plant scientists say a root grows down because of a fixed response to gravity that works the same way every time. They would not call this choosing.
- Practice, Feel your roots (2 min): Stand still, with bare feet on the floor. Close your eyes. Press your feet down. Feel the floor pushing back. Now, very slowly, lift your arms up, like a shoot. Keep your feet pressed down. Breathe in, and breathe out. Your feet hold on, and your arms reach up.
- Ask: If we had waited five days before looking, what would we have missed?
- Ask: A tulsi on the balcony has roots too. Where are they, and what are they doing right now?
- Ask: What did your chana need from you, and what did the chana do by itself?

## Deep and big questions
- *big:* For days, nothing showed above the soil. But the seed was busy. What else might be busy where we cannot see? (hint: Think about your own body, the ground under a building, and the sky at night.)
- *deep:* How does a root know which way is down, in the dark under the soil? (hint: You know which way is down even with your eyes shut. How do you know?)
- *deep:* A dry seed can wait in a jar for a year. Is a seed in a jar asleep, or awake and waiting? (hint: What wakes you up in the morning? What wakes a seed?)
- *big:* Every tree you know started as one small seed. Where did all the wood come from? (hint: The seed was tiny. Think about what the plant took in, every day, for years.)

## Story: The Tree on the Terrace Wall
*Leela, Varun and Paati are made up. Peepal and banyan plants really do sprout from cracks in walls in Indian cities, from seeds dropped by birds, and their roots grow deep into the cracks.*

Leela lived in a flat in Chennai, at the top of an old building. She was six. Her little brother Varun was four. Every evening, they went up to the terrace with their grandmother, Paati, to take the dry clothes off the line.

One evening, Leela saw something green on the wall of the water tank. A tiny plant was growing out of a crack in the cement. The plant had three small leaves. Each leaf was shaped like a heart, with a long pointed tip.

'Paati, look! Who planted a plant up here?' asked Leela.

Paati bent down and looked. 'Nobody planted this one,' she said. 'This is a peepal. A peepal grows into a very big tree.'

Varun laughed. 'A big tree? That's tiny! And there's no soil here. Only a wall.'

Paati pointed to the old peepal tree down on the road. The tree's branches were full of small round figs. Crows and koels sat in the branches, eating them. 'Every fig is full of tiny seeds,' said Paati. 'The birds eat the figs. Then they fly away and leave droppings, and the seeds come out in the droppings. One seed fell into this crack. The rain came. And the seed began to grow.'

Leela looked at the tiny plant again. 'How can such a little seed hold on to a wall?' she asked.

'Let's look,' said Paati. She took a hairpin out of her bun. Very gently, she scraped away some of the loose dust from the crack. Leela and Varun leaned in close.

Down in the crack, there was a thin white root. The root went down and down, much further than the plant was tall. Then the root turned a corner and went deeper still.

'The root is longer than the whole plant!' said Leela.

'Yes,' said Paati. 'When a seed wakes up, the root comes out first. The root finds water and holds on tight. Only then does the shoot grow up into the light. So most of this little peepal is hidden inside the wall.'

Varun was quiet for a moment. Then he said, 'If the root keeps going, will the wall crack?'

'It might,' said Paati. 'Peepal roots are strong. In a few years, a peepal root can split a wall.'

Leela did not want the peepal to break their tank. But she did not want to pull out the little plant either. 'Can we move the peepal to a pot?' she asked.

So the next morning, they filled an old paint tin with soil. Paati worked the little plant out of the crack, very slowly, so the long root came out whole. Leela held the pot, and Varun poured water from a steel tumbler.

Every evening after that, Leela checked the peepal. For a week, nothing changed on top. 'Is she sad?' asked Varun.

'She is busy underneath,' said Leela. 'The root comes first. You'll see.'

On the ninth day, a new red leaf unrolled at the top. Varun ran to tell Paati. 'The peepal is growing up now!' he shouted.

**For the parent:** Leela sees that the little peepal's root is much longer than the plant above, and understands that a seed grows down first, to find water and hold on, before the shoot grows up.

- Ask: Who planted the peepal on the terrace wall?
- Ask: Why was the root longer than the plant?
- Ask: For a week, nothing changed on top of the pot. What do you think the peepal was doing?

## Games
### g1 · Whose Seed? A memory game (15 min)
- **rules:** Cut out the 16 cards. Colour the border of each card: brown for seeds and green for plants.; Mix the cards and lay them face down in rows.; On your turn, turn over one brown card and one green card. Read them out loud.; If the seed grows into that plant, keep the pair and have another turn.; If not, turn both cards back over. The next player has a turn.; When all the pairs are found, each player counts their pairs. The player with the most pairs wins, or everyone wins together if it is a tie. Then lay the pairs out and look: which tiny seed made the biggest plant?
- **Printable cards: 16 cards to cut out. Each card has its name on it.:** A mango stone; A mango tree [picture]; A coconut [picture]; A coconut palm [picture]; A chana seed; A chana plant [picture]; A tamarind seed; A tamarind tree [picture]; A pumpkin seed; A pumpkin plant [picture]; A sunflower seed; A sunflower [picture]; A tiny banyan seed; A huge banyan tree [picture]; A chilli seed; A chilli plant [picture]
- **s1 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut out the 16 cards. 2. Colour the borders: brown for seeds, green for plants. 3. Mix the cards and lay them face down.  
  *Parent says:* Each brown card is a seed. Each green card is a plant. Can you find each seed and their own plant?  
  *Child mode:* Brown cards are seeds. Green cards are plants. Find the pairs.
- **s2 (8 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Take turns. Each player turns one brown and one green card. Read both out loud.  
  *Parent says:* What did you turn over? Does this seed grow into this plant? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Turn one brown card and one green card. Do they go together?
  - Ask: Can a mango stone grow into a coconut palm? (good: No. A mango seed grows into a mango tree.; Each seed grows into their own kind.)
  - Note: Let the child say why before you check. If they are unsure, ask what fruit the seed came from.
- **s3 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Lay out all the pairs. Count each player's pairs. Look at the banyan pair.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the banyan seed and the banyan tree. Which seed is smallest? Which plant is biggest?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the tiny banyan seed. Look at the huge banyan tree. Are they a pair?
  - Note: The banyan pair is the surprise. Banyan seeds are tiny, about a millimetre across.
  - Check: A banyan seed is tiny. What does a banyan seed grow into? → **A huge tree**
### g2 · Root Down, Shoot Up: a moving game (10 min)
- **rules:** The caller puts the ten cards in the order they are printed, with Dry seed on top.; The caller reads the front of the top card out loud.; Everyone moves the way the back of the card says. The caller reads the back if needed.; After a few cards, the caller says 'Freeze!' Everyone stays still. The caller asks one player what their body is doing, and which part of the plant does that.; Swap callers after all ten cards, and play again. This time the players say what comes next before the caller reads the card.
- **Printable cards: 10 call cards for the caller:** Dry seed (back: Curl up small and keep still. A dry seed waits.); Water! (back: Puff out your cheeks and stretch a little. The seed soaks up water and swells.); Root! (back: Push one foot down and out. The root comes out first and grows down.); Root drinks (back: Make a slurping sound. The root drinks water from the soil.); Root holds on (back: Press both feet hard into the floor. The root holds the plant in place.); Shoot! (back: Bend your back and push up slowly, head down, like a bent finger. The shoot comes up curled.); Into the light (back: Straighten up and lift your head. The shoot reaches the light.); Seed leaves (back: Open both hands wide above your head. The seed leaves open.); No water (back: Droop down slowly. A young plant with no water wilts.); Rain (back: Stand up tall again. A drink helps the young plant stand up.)
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Clear a space on the floor. Put the cards in order, with the Dry seed card on top.  
  *Parent says:* You are a seed. Curl up small. I will read a card, and you move.  
  *Child mode:* Curl up small. You are a dry seed.
- **s2 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Read the cards one at a time. Do the moves together the first time.  
  *Parent says:* Root! Which way does the root grow? Show me with your feet.  
  *Child mode:* Listen to the card. Move like that part of the plant.
  - Note: If the child shoots up before the root, say 'Root first!' and let them try again.
- **s3 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Say Freeze. Ask what each player is.  
  *Parent says:* Freeze! What is your body doing? Which part of the plant does that? What is that part doing for the plant?  
  *Child mode:* Freeze! What is your body doing? Which part of the plant does that?
  - Check: What does the root do for a young plant? → **Drinks water and holds on**

## Experiments
### e1 · Peek Under the Soil: what does a seed do first? (33 min, 6 days)
- **question:** After we plant a seed, what happens under the soil before anything shows on top?
- **predict:** Guess first. Under the soil, what will the seed grow first: a root, a leaf, or nothing yet? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** All five seeds come from one packet, are soaked together, and are planted in the same soil at the same depth. The only difference is how many days each seed has had in the soil.
- **record:** Each day, draw the seed you dug up, as big as your hand. On day 5, put the drawings in a row.
- **results table:** Seed | Day we looked | What we saw | My drawing
- **schedule:** day 0: In the evening, soak 6 chana seeds in a bowl of water. (2 min); day 1: Plant 5 seeds, each next to a numbered stick. Guess what will happen. (10 min); day 2: Dig up seed 1. Look, draw, and put the seed back. (4 min); day 3: Dig up seed 2. Look, draw, and put the seed back. (4 min); day 4: Dig up seed 3. Look, draw, and put the seed back. (4 min); day 5: Dig up seed 4. Look at seed 5 from the top. Put all your drawings in a row. (5 min); day 6: Look for seed 5's shoot coming up. If it is not up, look again each day. (4 min)
- **s1 (2 min)**  
  *Parent does:* In the evening, put 6 whole chana seeds in a bowl. Cover them with water.  
  *Parent says:* We are going to plant chana seeds tomorrow. Tonight they soak in water. Feel how hard they are now.  
  *Child mode:* Feel the hard chana. Put them in the water. They soak all night.
  - Note: Use whole chana, not dal. Split dal is damaged where the baby plant sits, so dal does not sprout.
  - Wait: Plant the seeds tomorrow morning.
- **s2 (8 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Fill the box with soil, 3 fingers deep. 2. Push 5 sticks into the soil, in a row, a hand apart. 3. Next to each stick, push one seed down one finger-joint deep. 4. Cover with soil. 5. Sprinkle water until the soil is damp. 6. Put the box by a bright window, out of hot afternoon sun.  
  *Parent says:* Feel the chana now. What has changed since last night? Now let's plant each seed next to a stick.  
  *Child mode:* Feel the chana. Are they hard or soft? Plant one seed next to each stick.
  - Ask: What changed after the night in water? (good: They got bigger.; They are soft now.; They drank the water.)
  - Note: The sticks tell you where each seed is, so you can dig up the right one. Keep the spare seed in the bowl to compare.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Ask your child to guess, and to say why. Write the guess down.  
  *Parent says:* Tomorrow we dig up seed 1. What will the seed have done under the soil? A root, a leaf, or nothing yet? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Under the soil, what will the seed grow first? Why?
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: Nothing, because the soil is dark.; A leaf, because plants have leaves.; A root, because roots go in the soil.)
  - Note: Any guess is fine. Many children guess that a leaf comes first, or that nothing happens in the dark. Don't tell them what will happen.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 2.
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Next to stick 1, lift the soil gently with a spoon. 2. Lay seed 1 on the damp cloth. 3. Look closely and draw. 4. Put the seed back in the hole, cover the seed with soil and water a little.  
  *Parent says:* Let's dig up seed 1 very gently. What do you see? Is anything coming out?  
  *Child mode:* Dig up seed 1 gently. Look closely. What is coming out?
  - Ask: What does seed 1 look like? (good: Fat and soft.; The skin is cracking.; A tiny white tip is coming out.)
  - Note: Handle the seed by the sides, not the white tip. Put the seed back quickly, root down. Most replanted seeds keep growing.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 3.
- **s5 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* On day 3, dig up seed 2 the same way. Draw it and put the drawing next to yesterday's drawing.  
  *Parent says:* Here is seed 2. Which way is the white part growing? Up or down?  
  *Child mode:* Dig up seed 2. Which way is the white part going?
  - Ask: What is the white part? (good: The root.; The root, going down.)
  - Note: On day 3 the root is usually as long as a fingernail. In a cold room it may be a day slower.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 4.
- **s6 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Dig up seed 3 the same way. Put the drawing next to the others.  
  *Parent says:* Here is seed 3. Is the root longer than yesterday? Is anything else coming out?  
  *Child mode:* Dig up seed 3. Is the root longer? What else is coming out?
  - Ask: What is different from seed 2? (good: The root is longer.; There are little side roots.; A curled shoot is starting.)
  - Note: Seed 3 usually has a long root, often with little side roots.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 5.
- **s7 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* On day 4, dig up seed 3 the same way. Draw it and put the drawing in the row.  
  *Parent says:* Here is seed 3. Is the root longer than yesterday? Is anything new coming out?  
  *Child mode:* Dig up seed 3. Is the root longer? Is anything new coming out?
  - Note: On day 4 the root is usually long, with little side roots, and a curled shoot may be starting. Everything may be a day slower in a cold room.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow, on day 6.
- **s8 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. On day 5, dig up seed 4. 2. Look at the top of the soil by stick 5. 3. Put all four drawings in a row, from seed 1 to seed 4.  
  *Parent says:* Seed 5 is coming up now. But was the seed doing nothing before today? What did you find out?  
  *Child mode:* Look at all your drawings. What came out first? What came next?
  - Ask: What came out first, the root or the shoot? (good: The root came first.; The root, then the shoot.)
  - Ask: Above the soil, what could we see? (good: Nothing, or only a tiny bump.; Nothing, but underneath there was a long root.)
  - Note: Ask for the claim (the root comes first), the evidence (the four drawings) and the reason (the root needs to drink and hold on). Chana can need 6 to 10 days to show above the soil, so if seed 5 is not up, wait a few more days.
  - Check: Under the soil, what did the chana seed grow first? → **A white root**
  - Wait: Come back on day 6.
- **s9 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* On day 6, look at the top of the soil by stick 5 for a green shoot. If it is not up yet, look again each day. Ask your child to say what they found out.  
  *Parent says:* Look at seed 5. Is a green shoot up yet? For days nothing showed on top, but was the seed doing nothing? What did you find out?  
  *Child mode:* Look at seed 5. Was the seed busy before we saw anything? Say what you found.
  - Ask: What did you find out? (good: The root comes first, under the soil.; The seed was growing before we could see anything.; First the root goes down, then the shoot comes up.)
  - Note: Ask for the claim (the root comes first), the evidence (the four drawings) and the reason (the root needs to drink and hold on). Chana can take 6 to 10 days to show above the soil, so wait a few days if seed 5 is not up.
### e2 · Big Seed, Small Seed: who sprouts first? (20 min, 3 days)
- **question:** Does a big seed sprout faster than a small seed?
- **predict:** Guess first. Which seed will show a root first: the big rajma, the middle moong, or the tiny rai? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** All three kinds soak for the same time, sit on the same wet tissue on one plate, and get the same water in the same place. Only the kind of seed is different.
- **record:** Each day, put a tick next to every seed that has a root.
- **results table:** Seed | Day 2: root? | Day 3: root?
- **schedule:** day 0: In the evening, soak each kind of seed in a separate bowl. (3 min); day 1: Lay the seeds on wet tissue in three rows. Guess which will show a root first. (8 min); day 2: Look at each row. Count the seeds with a root. Wet the tissue. (4 min); day 3: Look again. Which row won? Do all three grow the root first? (5 min)
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* In the evening, put 6 rajma, 6 moong and 6 rai in three small bowls. Cover each with water.  
  *Parent says:* Look at the three kinds of seed. Which is the biggest? Which is the smallest?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the three seeds. Which is biggest? Which is smallest?
  - Note: Do not eat these seeds raw: raw rajma is poisonous. Small seeds must stay out of mouths and noses, so keep the plate away from small children. Soaked rai goes slimy. That is normal.
  - Wait: Lay them out tomorrow morning.
- **s2 (8 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Wet the tissue on the plate. 2. Pour each bowl through a tea strainer to drain the seeds. 3. Lay them in three rows. 4. Ask for a guess and a reason.  
  *Parent says:* Which seed will show a root first? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Put the seeds in three rows. Which will show a root first? Why?
  - Ask: Why do you think so? (good: The rajma, because rajma are biggest and strongest.; The rai, because they are small and quick.)
  - Note: Many children guess the big rajma, because big seems strong. Don't say what will happen. Keep the tissue wet, with no puddle.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow.
- **s3 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Look at each row. Count the seeds with a white root. Sprinkle water on the tissue.  
  *Parent says:* Count the seeds with a root in each row. Which row has the most?  
  *Child mode:* Count the seeds with a white root in each row.
  - Note: Moong and rai usually show roots first, often within a day. Rajma are usually a day or two slower. If rajma win in your home, that is a real result too.
  - Wait: Come back tomorrow.
- **s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Look again. Talk about who was first, and what came out first on every seed.  
  *Parent says:* Who was first? Did the big seed sprout faster? And on every seed, what came out first?  
  *Child mode:* Who was first? On every seed, what came out first?

   **Answer:** A mango stone → A mango tree; A pumpkin seed → A pumpkin plant; A coconut → A coconut palm. *Why:* Each seed grows into the same kind of plant as the plant the seed came from.
4. (truefalse) A dry seed is not alive.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. A dry seed is alive and waiting. Give the seed water, and the seed grows.
5. (label) Colour the root brown and the shoot green.  
   **Answer:** The white part going down is the root. The curled part going up is the shoot.. *Why:* The root grows down to drink and hold on. The shoot grows up into the light.

## Applications
- Farmers sow seeds of rice, ragi and cotton when the soil is damp, often just as the monsoon rains come.
- Families sprout moong, chana and methi in the kitchen to eat.
- People plant mango, jackfruit and tamarind seeds to grow trees that give fruit for many years.
- Seed banks keep seeds of many crops dry and cold, so the crops can be grown again in the future.

## Spotted around us
- A small peepal or banyan plant growing out of a crack in a wall or on an old building, in almost any Indian city.

- Young green paddy seedlings in a nursery bed, before farmers in Andhra Pradesh, Bengal or Punjab plant them out in the fields.
- A tomato or chilli plant coming up in a pot from a seed in the kitchen waste, though nobody planted a seed there.

## Open questions
- **How does a seed know when the time is right to wake up?** Scientists know that water, warmth and light can wake seeds. But how a seed weighs all of these together, and decides, is still being studied.
- **How long can a seed stay alive and wait?** Seeds can wait a very long time, but nobody knows the longest a seed can wait.
- **What is it like for a root to feel the soil?** Roots sense water, touch and which way is down, and they change where they grow. Nobody knows what that sensing is like for the plant.
