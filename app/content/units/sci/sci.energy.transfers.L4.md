# Energy on the move: how it passes from thing to thing, changes form, and spreads out
`sci.energy.transfers.L4`

**Summary.** A marble rolls down a slope, knocks a cup along the table, and then everything is still. Where did the energy go? In this lesson you follow energy as it passes from one thing to another and as it changes form, in a torch, a kettle and a rolling marble. You find that it is never used up. It spreads out, mostly as warmth, and that is why things stop.

**Intro.** *Slide one coin into another coin that is lying still. The first coin stops dead, and the second one shoots away. What passed between them?* Nothing you can see went from the first coin to the second. Yet the second coin now has the movement that the first one had. Scientists say that energy passed from one coin to the other. Now clap your hands once, hard. Your hands were moving, and then they stopped. This time the movement did not pass to another moving thing. It turned into a sound and a sting of warmth. Energy does these two things all day long: it passes along, and it changes form. Today you catch it doing both.

## Learning outcomes
0. I can tell an energy transfer, where energy passes from one thing to another, from an energy change, where energy changes form.
1. I can draw an energy chain with arrows for a torch, a kettle and a marble on a slope, from where the energy comes from to where it ends up.
2. I can explain that energy is never used up. It spreads out, mostly as warmth, and I can say where it went when a thing stops.
3. I can test fairly how the start height of a rolling marble changes how far it pushes a cup, and give my claim, my evidence and my reasoning.
4. I can sort the energy that comes out of a machine into the useful part and the spread-out part, and say who decides which is useful.
5. I can explain that in science, work means a force moving a thing, and that doing work is one way to transfer energy.
6. I can say what physicists mean by energy, and name one thing about it that nobody knows yet.

## Terms
- **Energy**: A number that scientists can work out for anything that moves, glows, is hot, is lifted or is stretched. When things happen, the number moves from place to place, but the total stays the same. *e.g.* A marble at the top of a slope has energy because of its height.
- **Energy transfer**: Energy passing from one thing to another thing. *e.g.* A moving coin hits a still coin, and the still coin moves off with the energy.
- **Energy change**: Energy turning from one form into another form. It is also called an energy transformation or an energy conversion. *e.g.* In a torch bulb, electrical energy changes into light.
- **Form of energy**: One of the ways we notice energy: movement, heat, light, sound, electrical, or stored. *e.g.* A ringing bell shows energy in the form of sound.
- **Movement energy**: The energy a thing has because it is moving. Scientists call it kinetic energy. *e.g.* A rolling marble has movement energy. The faster it rolls, the more it has.
- **Stored energy**: Energy that waits in a thing because of where it is, how it is shaped, or how its atoms are joined. Scientists call it potential energy. *e.g.* A marble held at the top of a slope has stored energy. So does the cell in a torch.
- **Energy chain**: A row of words and arrows that shows where energy comes from, what it passes through, and where it ends up. *e.g.* Cell → wires → bulb → light and warmth in the room.
- **Work**: In science, work is done when a force moves a thing. Doing work passes energy to that thing. *e.g.* When you push a swing and it moves, you do work on the swing.
- **Friction**: The force between two surfaces that rub. It slows the sliding and turns movement energy into warmth. *e.g.* A paper cup sliding on a table is slowed by friction, and both get a tiny bit warmer.
- **Useful energy**: The part of the energy that does the job we wanted. *e.g.* For a bulb, the useful energy is the light.
- **Spread-out energy**: The part of the energy that goes where we did not want it, usually as warmth or sound. Scientists say this energy is dissipated. Many books call it wasted energy. *e.g.* A mixer grinder makes noise and a warm jar. That is spread-out energy.
- **Conservation of energy**: The rule that energy is never made and never destroyed. It only moves and changes form. *e.g.* Add the light, the warmth and the sound from a torch, and you get exactly the energy that left the cell.
- **Energy flow diagram**: A drawing where the width of each arrow shows how much energy goes that way. Engineers call it a Sankey diagram. *e.g.* For a kettle, the arrow to the water is wide and the arrow to the air is narrow.
- **Joule**: The measure used for counting energy. Lifting a 1 litre bottle of water by 1 metre takes about 10 joules. *e.g.* A small marble rolling off a 10 centimetre slope carries about five thousandths of a joule.

## Explanations
### Energy does two things: it passes along, and it changes form · *defined*
**Definition:** An energy transfer is energy passing from one thing to another. An energy change is energy turning from one form into another.

Watch a carrom striker hit a coin. The striker slows down and the coin speeds off. The energy was movement before, and it is movement after. Only the owner changed. That is a transfer. Now watch the coin slide to a stop on the board. Its movement did not pass to another moving thing. It turned into warmth in the board and the coin. That is a change of form. Most real events do both at once. A hot dosa on a steel plate passes heat to the plate, which is a transfer. A torch turns the stored energy of its cell into light, which is a change. When you look at anything that happens, ask two questions. Where was the energy before, and where is it now? Was it the same form before and after, or a new one?
- e.g. A hot cup of tea warms your hands: heat passes from the cup to your hands, and it is heat all the way.
- e.g. A clap: the movement of your hands changes into sound and a little warmth.
- e.g. A carrom striker hits a coin: movement passes from the striker to the coin, and a little changes into the click of the hit.
- **Most school books, including the Cambridge and NCERT books:** Energy comes in forms, such as movement, heat, light, sound, electrical and stored. It is transferred from thing to thing and it changes from form to form.
- **The Institute of Physics in Britain, and teachers who follow its guide:** Speaking of forms can make energy sound like a stuff that keeps changing its costume. It is clearer to say that energy sits in stores, such as a lifted thing or a hot thing, and is shifted between stores by pathways, such as a push, an electric current, heating, or light and sound.
- **Richard Feynman, a physicist, in his lectures of 1963:** Both ways of speaking are ways of keeping accounts. Energy is a number that we can work out before and after. Nobody has seen a blob of it going from one place to another.
### An energy chain: follow the energy · *defined*
**Definition:** An energy chain is a row of things joined by arrows. It shows where the energy came from, what it passed through, and where it ended up.

To draw an energy chain, start with the thing that is happening and ask where its energy came from. Then ask the same question again, and again. Write each answer in a box, and join the boxes with arrows that point the way the energy went. Above each arrow you can write how it went: by a push, by a wire, by heating, by light or by sound. A good chain also follows the energy forwards, after the job is done, until it has spread out into the room. Here is one for a pressure cooker whistle. Gas flame → hot steel → hot water → steam under pressure → the weight lifts and the steam rushes out → sound and warm, damp air in the kitchen. Almost every chain, followed far enough back, reaches the Sun. Almost every chain, followed far enough forward, ends in warmth spread through the air.
- e.g. Sun → paddy plant → rice → idli → your arm → a thrown ball → warmth in the air and the ground.
- e.g. Falling water at Idukki dam in Kerala → turbine → generator → wires → the fan in a flat → moving air → warmth.
- e.g. Cell → wire → bulb → light → the wall it lands on gets a tiny bit warmer.
### Inside a torch: stored, then electrical, then light · *defined*
**Definition:** A torch changes the stored chemical energy of its cell into electrical energy, and then into light and a little warmth.

A torch cell holds stored energy in its chemicals. Nothing happens while the switch is off, because the path is broken. When you close the switch, the chemicals in the cell begin to react. The reaction pushes electric charge round the loop of metal, and the cell's stored energy becomes electrical energy. In the bulb, the electrical energy changes into light. Some always changes into warmth as well. Hold the front of a torch that has been on for five minutes and you can feel it. The light then crosses the room and lands on a wall or on your book. A little bounces into your eyes, which is how you see. The rest is taken in by whatever it lands on, and warms it by an amount too small to feel. So the whole chain is: stored chemical energy → electrical energy → light and warmth → warmth in the room.
- e.g. An LED torch front feels slightly warm after five minutes.
- e.g. A phone torch runs the same chain from the phone's battery.
- e.g. A dynamo torch that you squeeze starts the chain with the movement of your hand.
- **The everyday picture, used in most school books:** Electrical energy flows along the wire from the cell to the bulb, like water through a pipe. This picture gives the right amounts and is good for drawing chains.
- **John Henry Poynting, a physicist in Birmingham, in 1884:** The energy does not ride along inside the wire. The cell sets up an electric and magnetic field in the space around the wires, and the energy travels through that space into the bulb. The wires guide it. Physicists still work it out this way today.
### Inside a kettle: electrical energy becomes heat · *defined*
**Definition:** An electric kettle changes electrical energy into heat in a metal coil, and the coil transfers the heat to the water.

At the bottom of an electric kettle is a metal heating coil. Electric current is pushed through it. The metal resists the current, and the electrical energy changes into heat. That is a change of form. The hot coil then passes its heat to the water that touches it. That is a transfer. The warmed water rises, cooler water sinks to take its place, and soon all the water is hot. Listen while this happens. The kettle rumbles, so a little energy has become sound. Hold your hand near the side, without touching. The wall is warm, so some heat is passing through to the air. At the end, steam carries energy out of the spout. In a good kettle, about four fifths of the electrical energy ends up in the water. A pot on a gas stove runs a different chain: the stored chemical energy of the gas changes into heat in the flame.
- e.g. An electric iron and a geyser make heat in the same way as a kettle.
- e.g. A kettle switches itself off when steam warms a small metal strip that bends and opens the switch.
- e.g. An hour after the tea is made, all the energy from the kettle has spread out into the kitchen air.
### A marble rolling down a slope: stored energy becomes movement · *defined*
**Definition:** A thing held high has stored energy. As it rolls down, the stored energy changes into movement energy.

Lift a marble to the top of a slope and hold it. Your arm did work to lift it, and that energy now waits as stored energy of height. Let go. The marble rolls faster and faster. At each moment it has a little less height and a little more movement. The stored energy is changing into movement energy, and the marble also spins as it rolls, which takes a share. At the bottom, nearly all the energy is movement. If the marble starts twice as high, it stores twice the energy, so it arrives with twice the movement energy. It does not arrive twice as fast, because movement energy grows faster than speed does. If the marble now hits a cup, its movement energy is transferred to the cup. A swing, a slide in the park and water rushing down from a dam all work the same way.
- e.g. A child at the top of a slide has stored energy, and at the bottom she has movement energy and a warm seat.
- e.g. A cyclist freewheeling down the ghat road from Ooty turns height into speed, and the brakes turn the speed into warmth.
- e.g. A swing changes stored energy into movement energy and back again, twice on every swing.
### Work: a push that moves a thing transfers energy · *defined*
**Definition:** In science, work is done when a force moves a thing. The work done is the energy transferred to that thing.

When you push a toy car and it rolls, you have done work on the car. Energy has passed from your body to the car. When you lift a bucket, you do work on the bucket, and the energy waits in it as stored energy of height. The harder the push and the further the thing moves, the more work is done. Lifting a 1 litre bottle of water by 1 metre takes about 10 joules of work. Lifting it 2 metres takes about 20 joules. If the thing does not move, no work is done on it, however hard you push. Lean on a wall with all your strength. The wall does not move, so in the scientist's meaning you have done no work on the wall. Yet you feel tired. Your muscles have been changing the stored energy of your food into warmth the whole time.
- e.g. Pulling a suitcase along a railway platform is work, because a force moves the suitcase.
- e.g. Holding a heavy school bag perfectly still does no work on the bag.
- e.g. Drawing water from a well with a rope and bucket is work, and the lifted water stores the energy.
- **Everyday speech in every language:** Work is anything that takes effort: homework, cooking, thinking, guarding a gate all night. By this meaning, holding a heavy bag for an hour is hard work.
- **Physics, since Gaspard-Gustave Coriolis gave the word this meaning in France in 1829:** Work is a force moving a thing through a distance. It counts only the energy passed to the thing. Holding a bag still passes no energy to the bag, so the work is zero.
- **Biologists who study muscle:** A muscle that holds still is not resting. Its tiny fibres keep pulling and letting go, many times a second, and each pull changes food energy into warmth. So the body spends energy even when the physicist's work is zero.
### Why things stop: friction changes movement into warmth · *defined*
**Definition:** Friction is the force between rubbing surfaces. It changes movement energy into warmth in both surfaces.

The cup in your test slid a little way and stopped. A ball rolled along the floor always stops. A swing left alone swings lower and lower. In each, the movement energy has not vanished. Wherever two surfaces rub, friction changes movement into warmth. Wherever a thing pushes through air, the air is stirred and warmed. Rub your palms together quickly ten times and you feel it at once. The amounts in the marble test are far too small to feel. A marble rolling off a 10 centimetre slope carries about five thousandths of a joule. That would warm a single drop of water by less than a tenth of a degree. With bigger things you can feel it. The brakes of a cycle are warm after a long hill. A drill bit is hot after it bores a hole. A spacecraft coming back into the air glows, because its huge movement energy changes into heat.
- e.g. Cycle brake blocks are warm after a long downhill ride.
- e.g. A squash ball warms up to about 45 degrees Celsius during a game, from being squashed again and again.
- e.g. A shooting star is a grain of space dust whose movement energy changes into heat and light in the air.
- **What our eyes and hands tell us:** The movement ended and a different thing, warmth, appeared in its place. Movement and heat look like two separate forms of energy.
- **The closer look, built by Count Rumford in 1798, James Joule in the 1840s and Ludwig Boltzmann in the 1870s:** Warmth is movement too. The atoms of the cup and the table never stop jiggling, and friction makes them jiggle faster. So the one orderly movement of the whole cup has become the scattered movement of countless atoms, in every direction.
### Energy can travel without any thing travelling with it · *defined*
**Definition:** Sound and light carry energy from place to place as waves. The air or the string shakes where it is. It does not travel along with the energy.

When you flick a line of touching coins, the last coin leaves and the middle coins hardly move. The energy went through them as a quick squash, passed from coin to coin. Sound travels the same way. A drum skin pushes the air next to it. That air pushes the next bit of air, and so on, all the way to your ear. No air travels from the drum to you. Only the shaking does. In a string phone, the shaking travels along a tight thread. Light also carries energy, and it needs nothing at all to travel through. Sunlight crosses 150 million kilometres of nearly empty space in about eight minutes, and warms your face when it arrives. So energy can be transferred in three ways you can test at home: by a push or a pull, by heating, and by waves of sound and light.
- e.g. A temple bell in Madurai shakes the air, and the shaking reaches you across the street.
- e.g. A wave crosses a pond while a floating leaf only bobs up and down in its place.
- e.g. Sunlight on a terrace in May makes the floor too hot for bare feet.
### Useful energy and spread-out energy · *defined*
**Definition:** Useful energy is the part that does the job we wanted. Spread-out energy is the part that goes elsewhere, mostly as warmth and sound.

No machine turns all the energy it is given into the form we want. A fan is meant to move air, but its motor also grows warm and hums. A mixer grinder is meant to grind, but it also roars and warms the chutney. An old bulb with a glowing wire turns only about a twentieth of its electrical energy into light. The rest becomes heat. An LED bulb turns about a third or more into light, so it gives the same light for far less electricity. You can draw this with an energy flow diagram. One wide arrow comes in. It splits into an arrow for the useful part and arrows for the rest, and each arrow is as wide as its share. Many books call the rest wasted energy. It is not destroyed. It has spread into the room as warmth, where it is very hard to collect and use again.
- e.g. A petrol car turns only about a quarter to a third of the fuel's energy into moving the car.
- e.g. A phone charger feels warm, because some electrical energy becomes heat on the way to the battery.
- e.g. A person cycling turns about a quarter of the food energy she spends into movement, and the rest into body warmth.
- **Engineers:** The job decides. Any energy that does not do the job the machine was built for is wasted, and a better machine wastes less.
- **Biologists:** For a living body, the warmth is not waste. A sparrow, a dog and a child all stay alive because the energy changes inside them keep them warm. The same warmth that an engineer calls waste in a motor is the very thing a body needs.
- **A family in a hill town such as Kodaikanal or Shimla on a winter night:** When you want a warm room, every appliance is a heater. The warmth from the bulb, the television and the fridge is all useful. Useful is a word about what we want, and not about the energy.
### Energy is never used up: it spreads out · *defined*
**Definition:** The total amount of energy never changes. Energy only moves and changes form. This rule is called the conservation of energy.

People say that a cell is finished, that the petrol is used up, or that a child has run out of energy. Scientists have measured what really happens, for nearly two hundred years, in every kind of event. They have never once found energy that vanished or energy that appeared from nowhere. When a torch cell goes flat, the energy that was stored in it now exists as a very slight warmth in the room. When your marble and cup came to rest, all the energy you gave the marble was in the table, the cup and the air. So what runs out is not energy. What runs out is energy gathered in one place, in a form that can make things happen. Once it has spread out as gentle warmth, it is all still there, but it will not gather itself again. That is why we need fuel, food and sunlight every day, even though energy is never destroyed.
- e.g. A flat cell weighs the same as a new one, and its energy has become warmth spread through the house.
- e.g. After a firecracker, the stored chemical energy is in warm air, fading sound and scattered light.
- e.g. The warmth of every fan, bus and cooking fire in a city finally leaves the Earth as heat rays into space.
- **Everyday speech:** Energy is something you have and then use up, like money or water in a tank. This matches what we feel, because the useful, gathered energy really does run down.
- **Julius Robert Mayer in 1842, James Joule in the 1840s and Hermann von Helmholtz in 1847:** Energy is conserved. Count every form, including the warmth, and the total before and after is always the same. Every careful test since has agreed.
- **Some cosmologists today, such as Sean Carroll and Tamara Davis:** For things on Earth the rule holds exactly. For the whole universe, where space itself is stretching, there is no agreed way even to add up the total, so some say the rule cannot be stated for the universe as a whole.
### Living beings are energy changers too · *defined*
**Definition:** Every living being takes in energy in one form and changes it into other forms to stay alive.

A leaf takes in light and changes it into the stored chemical energy of sugar. A parrot eats a guava and changes that stored energy into wing movement, body warmth and a loud call. A firefly changes stored chemical energy into light with very little warmth, which is why people call it cold light. An electric eel changes the energy of her food into electrical energy strong enough to stun a fish. Your own body runs a long chain all day: food → stored energy in your muscles and liver → movement, warmth, the tiny electrical signals in your nerves, and your voice. The tiny living beings in idli batter change some of the stored energy of the batter into warmth as they feed and multiply. So the energy chains in this lesson do not only run through torches and kettles. They run through every living being, and through you.
- e.g. A mango tree in Salem changes sunlight into the sugar of mangoes.
- e.g. A honeybee shivers her flight muscles to change honey into warmth for the hive.
- e.g. Your voice changes the movement of your breath and vocal folds into sound.
### What do physicists mean by energy? · *debated*
Here is what we know so far. Energy is not a thing you can see, pour or hold. It is a number that can be worked out for any object or event. There is one rule for working it out from speed, another from height, another from temperature, another from light, and more. The surprise is this. When anything happens, the numbers change in each place, but when you add them all up, the total is always the same as before. Physicists say energy is the name of that total. In 1918 the mathematician Emmy Noether proved why there must be such a number. If the laws of nature are the same today as they were yesterday, then something must stay constant, and that something is energy. What is still missing is an answer to the plain question: what is energy, underneath the sums? Nobody knows. Different people answer in different ways.
- e.g. You can work out the energy of a rolling marble from its mass and its speed.
- e.g. You can work out the energy of a lifted marble from its mass and its height.
- e.g. Add up every form before and after the marble hits the cup, and the two totals match.
- **Richard Feynman, in The Feynman Lectures on Physics, 1963:** Energy is a number that does not change when something happens. It is a strange fact about nature and a rule for keeping accounts. Physics today has no knowledge of what energy is.
- **Emmy Noether, a mathematician in Göttingen, in 1918:** Energy is what stays the same because the laws of nature do not change from one moment to the next. Its conservation is a mirror of that sameness in time.
- **Friedrich Herrmann and the Karlsruhe Physics Course:** Energy can be thought of as a real quantity that flows from place to place, always carried by something, such as an electric current, moving parts, or light. Thinking of it as flowing makes its paths easy to follow.
- **The Shakta and Kashmir Shaiva traditions of India:** Everything that happens is the play of one power, called shakti, which shows itself in every form and is never separate from awareness. This is a teaching about what the world is, and it is not a measurement.

## Mostly agreed
- A blunt borer turning in metal can bring cold water to the boil, again and again. *How we know:* In 1798, in Munich, Count Rumford had a blunt borer turned inside a brass cannon barrel standing in water. In about two and a half hours the water boiled, and it could be done again and again. The heat came from the turning.
- When energy seems to be missing, it has always turned up somewhere unexpected. *How we know:* Around 1930, atoms that gave out an electron seemed to lose some energy without trace. Wolfgang Pauli guessed that an unseen particle was carrying it away. Clyde Cowan and Frederick Reines caught that particle, the neutrino, in 1956.
- A braking train can hand its movement back to the overhead line instead of losing it in hot brakes. *How we know:* Delhi Metro trains run their motors backwards as generators when they slow down. Meters on the line show electricity flowing back for other trains to draw on.
- Old-style lamps are better heaters than lights. *How we know:* Light meters and heat meters placed around such bulbs show that nearly all the energy leaves as heat rays and warm air. This is why India's UJALA scheme, begun in 2015, replaced them with LED bulbs.
- A ball that bounces gets warmer. *How we know:* Heat cameras show a squash ball warming to about 45 degrees Celsius in play. Each bounce changes a share of the movement energy into warmth inside the rubber, which is why each bounce is lower.
- Every claimed self-running engine has failed when tested. *How we know:* Patent offices and science academies have tested claims for centuries. The Royal Academy of Sciences in Paris stopped examining such machines in 1775, because not one had worked.

## How we found out
**Bhaskara II, Count Rumford, Julius Robert Mayer, James Joule and Hermann von Helmholtz, About 1150 for the wheel, and 1775 to 1847 for the answer.** About 1150, the Indian astronomer Bhaskara II described a wheel with curved spokes half filled with mercury. He reasoned that one side would always be heavier, so the wheel would turn for ever. It was a sensible idea, because a heavy side does fall. For the next six hundred years, inventors in the Arab world and in Europe drew and built such wheels. Every one slowed and stopped. In 1775 the Royal Academy of Sciences in Paris refused to examine any more of them. Nobody could yet say why they failed. In 1798 Count Rumford showed that rubbing makes heat without end. In 1842 a German ship's doctor, Julius Robert Mayer, wrote that movement and heat are one thing in two forms. In the 1840s James Joule measured it. In 1847 Hermann von Helmholtz put it all together. Energy is never made or lost, so a wheel that loses a little to friction on each turn must stop.  
*What people believed before:* For centuries many clever people thought that a well-made wheel could keep itself turning for ever. Every wheel that was built stopped, and by 1847 scientists could say why: friction turns a little movement into warmth on every turn, and nothing puts it back.

## Challenging perspectives
- **When a moving thing stops, its energy is gone.** (Most people, in daily speech.; *rejected*)  
  For: The ball is still. The cell is flat. Nothing more happens, and nothing can be seen to have taken the energy.  
  Against: Each time the warmth and sound have been measured, they add up to exactly the energy that seemed lost. The energy is all still there, spread too thinly to notice.
- **Energy does not really change form. It only moves from one store to another.** (The Institute of Physics in Britain, following the science educator Robin Millar and others, since about 2005.; *debated*)  
  For: Talk of forms can lead children to think of energy as a stuff that turns into light, then into heat. Stores and pathways keep attention on where the energy is and how much.  
  Against: Forms are used by most books in the world, including in India, and by many working scientists. Critics say that stores and pathways are only new labels for the same sums, and that light and sound do not fit well as either.
- **Energy is a real quantity that flows, like a kind of stuff.** (Friedrich Herrmann and the Karlsruhe Physics Course in Germany, since the 1980s.; *debated*)  
  For: Energy is conserved, and you can say how much crosses any surface each second, exactly as you can for water. Treating it as flowing helps people follow it.  
  Against: Feynman and many others answer that energy is only a number in the accounts. You cannot tag a bit of energy and follow that same bit, and how much there is depends on who is measuring.
- **In the whole universe, energy is not conserved, because space is stretching.** (Some cosmologists, such as Sean Carroll.; *debated*)  
  For: Light from far galaxies is stretched as space grows, and each bit of light loses energy with nothing nearby gaining it. The energy of empty space seems to grow as space grows.  
  Against: Other physicists say the missing energy can be counted as energy of gravity, and that the total may even be exactly zero, as Edward Tryon proposed in 1973. Einstein's theory gives no single agreed way to add it up.
- **Energy is conserved only on average, and not in each single event between atoms.** (Niels Bohr, Hendrik Kramers and John Slater, in 1924.; *rejected*)  
  For: The first results on light and atoms were so strange that giving up exact conservation looked like a way out.  
  Against: In 1925 Walther Bothe and Hans Geiger in Berlin, and Arthur Compton and Alfred Simon in Chicago, watched single collisions of light with electrons. Energy balanced in each one. Bohr gave the idea up.
- **A wheel can be built that turns for ever with nothing put in.** (Bhaskara II in India about 1150, Villard de Honnecourt in France about 1235, and hundreds of inventors since.; *rejected*)  
  For: A wheel that is heavier on one side should always be falling, and a falling wheel turns.  
  Against: Every such wheel that was built slowed and stopped. Friction changes its movement into warmth, and nothing puts that energy back. The heavy side is also balanced by more weights on the light side.

## Roots
You have watched a marble pass its energy to a cup, and the cup pass it to the table. Now we ask what that tells us about the things of the world, and about ourselves.

**Deep truth.** In the Marble and the Cup test, the marble stopped and the cup slid. Then the cup stopped, and the table grew a tiny bit warmer. The energy did not stay with any of them. Each one held it for a moment and passed it on. So a marble, a cup or a body is a place that energy is passing through.  
*Habit it breaks:* We say 'the marble's energy' and 'my energy', as if each thing owned a share and kept it. That way of speaking splits the world into owners and their belongings. The test shows that nothing keeps what it is given.  
*Evidence:* The cup slid further when the marble started higher, though nobody touched the cup. The height came from your arm, your arm's energy from breakfast, and breakfast from the Sun. After the cup stopped, the same amount had moved on into the table and the air.

- *who am i:* The energy that lifts your arm was sunlight a few months ago, and it will be warmth in the air a few minutes from now. You are one of the places where it is passing through, and you are awake while it passes.
- *what is life:* A living body is a chain of energy changes that never stops. Food comes in, and movement, warmth, thought and voice go out. When the passing stops, the body stops.
- *what is this world:* From the Sun to a leaf to a parrot to the warm air, one amount is handed along and never grows or shrinks. The things we name are the stations on its way.

Follow any chain in this lesson and you never find a place where the energy begins, and you never find a place where it ends. You only find where it was before, and where it went next. The torch, the kettle, the marble, the potter's wheel and your own hand are all joined by one handing-on that has no gap in it. Science counts the amount and finds it always the same. That one amount, showing up as light in one place and warmth in another, is a clear picture of the One appearing as many. Consciousness is the ground from which all of it arises, and the energy in a marble and the energy in the hand that lifts it are one movement of that ground. Many scientists do not accept this last step, and their view is given below.

- **Chandogya Upanishad 6.1.4:** The sage Uddalaka tells his son Shvetaketu that by knowing one lump of clay, everything made of clay is known. The pot and the plate are different names that speech gives. What is real in all of them is the clay. (Light, sound, movement and warmth are names for what energy is doing. In every one of them the amount is the same energy, as every pot is the same clay.)
- **Tao Te Ching 42:** Laozi says that the Way gives rise to one, one gives rise to two, two gives rise to three, and three gives rise to the ten thousand things. (One store of sunlight becomes rice, then a push, then a spinning wheel, a sound and a warm hand. One energy becomes many happenings.)
- **Bhagavad Gita 2.22:** Krishna tells Arjuna that as a person takes off worn clothes and puts on new ones, so the one who lives in the body leaves worn bodies and enters new ones. (The Gita speaks of the self. Energy offers a picture of the same shape: it leaves one form and takes another, and is itself never worn out.)
- Science (settled): In every event ever measured, the total energy before equals the total energy after.
- Science (settled): Emmy Noether proved in 1918 that energy is conserved because the laws of nature do not change from one moment to the next.
- Science (debated): Energy may be a real thing that flows, or only a number in our accounts. Physicists hold both views.
- Science (open): What energy is, underneath the sums, is not known. Whether it has anything to do with consciousness is not a question physics can test today.
- Other view, The Buddha, in the Anattalakkhana Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 22.59), and many physicists today: The Buddha taught that there is change after change with no lasting self or substance underneath. On this view there is no one clay that takes many shapes. There is only the passing-on. Many physicists say something close to this about energy: it is a number that balances, and not a thing or a ground. They add that nothing in the measurements points to consciousness.
- Practice, The hand-on (3 min): Sit facing each other. Rub your palms together fast, ten times. Now press your warm palms gently against the other person's palms and close your eyes. Feel the warmth pass across. Breathe slowly three times. Ask yourself quietly: where was this warmth this morning? Where will it be tonight?
- Ask: If nothing keeps the energy it is given, in what way is the energy in your body yours?
- Ask: Is energy one thing wearing many forms, or only many changes with nothing underneath? How could anyone tell?
- Ask: The amount of energy never changes, but its form always does. Is there anything about you that is like that?

## Story: The Wheel That Kept Slowing Down
*A made-up story. Manamadurai, near Madurai in Tamil Nadu, is a real town known for its clay pots and for the ghatam, a clay pot played as a drum. Many potters there still spin a heavy wheel with a stick. Valli, Kabilan and Fathima are imagined.*

Kabilan was nine, and he had come from Chennai to Manamadurai for the Pongal holidays. Manamadurai is a town near Madurai that is known for its clay pots. His aunt Valli made pots there, as her mother had done before her.

On the first morning he sat on an upturned basket in her shed and watched. Valli Athai set the end of a long stick into a notch on her heavy wheel. She pushed the wheel round with the stick, faster and faster, nine or ten times. Then she laid the stick down. The wheel went on spinning by itself.

"You are not pushing it now," said Kabilan. "Why is it still turning?"

"I gave it my push," she said, wetting her hands. "The wheel is holding it for me."

She pressed her thumbs into a lump of grey clay in the middle of the wheel. A wall of clay rose between her fingers like something growing. But Kabilan was watching the wheel. With every press of her hands, it turned a little slower. Before the pot was done, she had to pick up the stick and spin the wheel again.

"It keeps running out," he said. "Where does the spinning go?"

"Where do you think it goes?"

"Nowhere. It just gets finished. Like my phone battery."

Valli Athai did not say he was wrong. She spun the wheel up again and lifted the pot off. "Come here. Lay your palm flat on the wooden rim while it turns. Press gently."

He pressed. The rim slid under his hand, and the wheel slowed at once, as if he had pulled a brake. In a few turns it stopped.

"Now put that palm on your cheek," she said. "Then the other one."

The palm that had touched the wheel was warm. It was not hot, but it was clearly warmer than his other hand.

"There is some of the spinning," she said. "It went into your hand. When I work, it goes into my hands and the clay. Some goes into the peg that the wheel sits on. Put your ear near the peg next time. You will hear a low hum, and the hum is some of it too. Some goes into the air that the wheel stirs."

Kabilan looked at his palm. "So it did not finish. It got out."

"It moved on," said Valli Athai. "It always moves on. Nothing in this shed keeps it for long. Not the wheel, and not me."

He thought about that. "Then where did you get it from?"

"From four idlis and a cup of sambar. And the rice and the dal got it from the Sun." She pointed with her chin at the yard, where forty wet pots stood drying. "The same Sun is working on those right now."

In the evening a girl called Fathima came from the next lane. She was eleven, and she had come to try a new ghatam. A ghatam is a clay pot that is played as a drum. Fathima sat cross-legged, set the pot against her stomach, and began to play. Her fingers, her wrists and the heels of her hands flew over the clay. The shed filled with sound, sharp at the neck of the pot and deep at its belly.

"Hold the side," she told Kabilan, without stopping. He laid two fingers on the pot. The clay was trembling.

Then she lifted her hands, and the sound died away.

"And now?" said Kabilan. "Where did the sound go?"

"You tell me, city boy," said Fathima. "You are the one with the warm hand."

He thought of the wheel. "Your hands shook the pot. The pot shook the air. The shaking went out through the door and got smaller and smaller." He stopped. "Does it turn into warmth as well?"

"That is what my science teacher says," said Fathima. "When the shaking of the air has spread out so far that nobody can hear it, it is warmth. This whole lane is warmer because I played. It is warmer by so little that no thermometer in Manamadurai could show it."

Kabilan turned to his aunt. "Then let us get it back. We can collect the warmth from the air and use it to spin the wheel. You would never need the stick."

Valli Athai smiled, but she did not laugh. "People have tried to build such a wheel for eight hundred years," she said. "Not one has worked. The warmth is all still here. But it is spread thin, like one spoon of salt stirred into the whole temple tank. I can spin this wheel with four idlis. I cannot spin it with the warm air of the shed."

"Why not? It is the same amount."

"It is the same amount," she agreed. "Keep asking why. The people who study this are still asking why the world runs only one way."

That night Kabilan lay on a mat near the shed. The wheel stood still in the dark. He counted on his fingers. Sun, rice, idli, his aunt's arm, the stick, the wheel, the pot, his warm palm. Then he thought of Fathima's playing, which was now a warmth too small to feel, somewhere in the air around him. He wondered where it would go next.

**For the parent:** When the wheel slowed, its energy was not finished. It had passed into warmth in the hand, the peg and the air, and into sound. Energy moves on and spreads out. It does not disappear, but once it is spread thin it cannot easily be gathered again.

- Ask: A spinning wheel slows down by itself. Name three places its energy could have gone.
- Ask: Why can a potter spin a wheel with the energy of four idlis, but not with the same amount of energy spread through warm air?
- Ask: Does anything keep its energy for ever? What would you look for to find out?

## Experiments
### e1 · The Marble and the Cup: does twice the height give twice the push? (35 min)
- **question:** If a marble starts twice as high on a slope, does it push a paper cup twice as far?
- **predict:** Before you roll anything, guess. From the 20 mark, will the cup slide the same distance as from the 10 mark, a bit further, twice as far, or more than twice as far? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Only the starting mark of the marble changes: 10, 20 and 30 centimetres up the ramp. The ramp, the marble, the cup, the table and the cup's starting place stay the same. The marble is let go and never pushed. Each mark is tried three times, because one roll can go crooked, and the middle distance of the three is used.
- **safety:** A grown-up cuts the card and the doorway in the cup. Keep marbles away from children under five, and pick them up from the floor when you finish.
- **record:** Fill in the table. Then draw three bars for the three middle slides, side by side, and write one sentence each for your claim, your evidence and your reasoning.
- **results table:** Start mark on the ramp | Height of the mark above the table | Cup slide, try 1 | Cup slide, try 2 | Cup slide, try 3 | Middle slide
- **s1 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Show your child the folded card, the marble and the cup. Explain that the marble will roll into the cup from three heights. Write down their guess and their reason.  
  *Parent says:* The marble will roll down and push the cup. From the 20 mark it starts twice as high as from the 10 mark. How far will the cup slide then? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* The marble will push the cup. Guess how far from each mark. Say why.
  - Ask: Why do you think it will slide that far? (good: Because higher means faster.; Because it has more energy at the top.; Because the cup is light.)
  - Note: Do not correct the guess. Common guesses are 'the same', 'a bit more' and 'much more, because it is faster'. All are worth testing. Write the guess and the reason above the table.
- **s2 (8 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* A grown-up cuts the card strip and a doorway in the cup rim, about 3 centimetres wide and 3 centimetres high. Fold the card into a V. Mark 10, 20 and 30 centimetres from the bottom end. Rest the top on the books and tape the bottom to the table. Stand the cup upside down with its doorway touching the end of the ramp. Draw round the cup.  
  *Parent says:* Let us build it. I will do the cutting. You make the marks at 10, 20 and 30. Now measure how high each mark is above the table.  
  *Child mode:* A grown-up cuts. You mark 10, 20 and 30 on the ramp. Draw round the cup.
  - Note: With the top end 10 centimetres high, the three marks are about 3, 7 and 10 centimetres above the table. Let your child measure them with the ruler held upright, and write them in the table. The 20 mark is twice as high as the 10 mark, and the 30 mark is three times as high. Roll the marble once to check that it enters the doorway. If it misses, straighten the ramp and tape it again.
  - Check: The ramp is straight. The 10 mark is 3 centimetres above the table. About how high is the 30 mark? → **9 or 10 centimetres**
- **s3 (12 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Hold the marble on the 10 mark and let go without pushing. Measure from the pencil outline to the nearest edge of the cup. Put the cup back on its outline. Do this three times. Then do three rolls from the 20 mark and three from the 30 mark.  
  *Parent says:* Put the marble on the mark. Just open your fingers. Do not push. Now measure how far the cup slid from its outline, and write it down.  
  *Child mode:* Let the marble go. Do not push. Measure the slide. Put the cup back each time.
  - Ask: What did you hear when the marble hit the cup? (good: A click.; A tap.; It was louder from the top mark.)
  - Note: From the lowest mark the cup may slide 3 to 6 centimetres, and from the top mark 10 to 18. Yours will depend on your cup and table, so write what you see. If the cup falls over, lower the book pile to 6 centimetres and start again. If the cup hardly moves, try a smoother surface or a lighter cup. If the marble bounces out without pushing, turn the doorway squarely to the ramp. Do not count a roll where the marble missed the door. With two or more children, one lets go, one measures and one writes, and they swap after each mark.
- **s4 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* For each mark, find the middle of the three slides. Write it in the last column. Draw three bars, one for each middle slide, using one square of the page for each centimetre.  
  *Parent says:* Look at your three numbers for the 10 mark. Which one is in the middle? That is the one we keep. Now draw a bar for each mark.  
  *Child mode:* Find the middle number for each mark. Draw three bars.
  - Ask: The 20 mark is twice as high as the 10 mark. Is its bar about twice as tall? (good: Yes, nearly.; A bit less than twice.; Ours was more than twice.)
  - Note: The middle value is used because one odd roll then changes nothing. Now compare. Many families find the 20 mark gives roughly twice the slide of the 10 mark, and the 30 mark roughly three times. It will not be exact. If your bars show something else, that is your real result, so keep it and talk about what might have caused it.
  - Check: From the 10 mark the slides were 4, 6 and 5 centimetres. Which number do you keep? → **5**
- **s5 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Ask your child to write three sentences: what I think, what I saw, and why. Then ask where the marble's energy is now that the cup has stopped. Rub a palm hard on the table ten times and feel the palm.  
  *Parent says:* Tell me three things. What do you now think happens when the marble starts higher? What did you see? Why do you think it happens? Then tell me this. The cup has stopped. Where is the energy now?  
  *Child mode:* Write what you think, what you saw, and why. The cup stopped. Where did its energy go?
  - Note: Only after your child has answered, give the idea. A higher start stores more energy. Rolling down changes it into movement. The hit transfers it to the cup. Friction with the table then changes it into warmth, and each centimetre of sliding takes about the same amount. So twice the energy lasts about twice as far. The click was a little of the energy leaving as sound. Say plainly what the test cannot show: you did not measure the warmth. It is far too small to feel, about five thousandths of a joule. The palm rub lets you feel the same change with a bigger amount. Ask what else your child would test, such as a heavier marble or a rougher table.
  - Think: You gave the marble its height with your arm. Where did your arm get that energy, and where is that energy now?
### e2 · The Tired Swing: does a sail make a pendulum run down sooner? (25 min)
- **question:** A swinging bunch of keys slowly swings less and less. If it has to push more air, does it run down sooner, and by how much?
- **predict:** Guess how many swings the keys will make before the swing is half as wide. Then guess the number when a postcard is opened out on the keys like a sail. Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Only the card changes: folded small for the first three runs, and opened flat to face the air for the next three. The thread, the keys, the card itself and the starting mark stay the same. The card rides on the keys in both tests, so the weight is the same. Each test is done three times and the middle count is used.
- **record:** Fill in the table. Write how many times sooner the swing ran down with the sail open, and one sentence on where the energy went.
- **results table:** Card | Swings to half width, try 1 | Try 2 | Try 3 | Middle count
- **s1 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Hang the keys and swing them once so that your child sees the swing slowly getting smaller. Stop them. Ask for both guesses and the reasons, and write them down.  
  *Parent says:* The swing gets smaller by itself. How many swings before it is half as wide? What if the keys carry an open card? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess the swings with the card folded. Guess again with the card open. Say why.
  - Note: One swing means there and back. Do not correct the guess. Many children expect only a small difference.
- **s2 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Tape one end of the thread firmly to the top of the table at its edge. Tie the keys to the other end so that they hang 2 centimetres above the floor. Lay the paper strip on the floor under the swing. Mark 0 under the still keys, and 10 and 20 centimetres to one side. Fold the card three times and tape it flat against the keys.  
  *Parent says:* Let us hang the keys so that they just clear the floor. Now mark the strip. Zero is under the keys. Then mark 10 and 20.  
  *Child mode:* Hang the keys low. Mark 0, 10 and 20 on the strip. Tape on the folded card.
  - Note: Press two or three pieces of tape over the thread so that it cannot slip. The keys must swing along the strip, not across it. Looking straight down from above gives the best view of the marks.
- **s3 (7 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Pull the keys to the 20 mark with the thread tight. Let go without pushing. Count each time the keys come back to your side. Stop counting when they no longer reach the 10 mark. Write the count. Do it three times.  
  *Parent says:* Hold the keys over the 20 mark. Let go. Count each time they come back to you. Stop when they do not reach the 10 mark any more.  
  *Child mode:* Let go at 20. Count each return. Stop when they miss the 10 mark.
  - Ask: Did the keys ever swing back further than the 20 mark where you let go? (good: No, never.; They came back a little short each time.)
  - Note: Expect somewhere between 20 and 80 swings. Each run takes one to two minutes. Count aloud together. If the keys wobble in a circle, stop and start that run again.
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Unfold the card. Tape it to the keys so that its flat face meets the air as the keys swing. Start from the 20 mark again and count in the same way, three times.  
  *Parent says:* Now the card is open like a sail. Same start, same counting. Let go.  
  *Child mode:* Open the card. Tape it to face the air. Count the swings again.
  - Note: Expect only about 3 to 10 swings. If the card twists edge-on, add a second piece of tape so that it stays facing the swing.
- **s5 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Find the middle count for each test. Divide the bigger by the smaller to see how many times sooner the sail swing ran down. Ask your child to write what they think, what they saw, and why.  
  *Parent says:* Tell me three things. What do you think the air does to a swing? What did you count? Why did the open card make such a difference?  
  *Child mode:* Write what you think, what you saw and why. Where did the swing's energy go?
  - Note: After your child has answered, give the idea. At each end of the swing, the keys have stored energy of height. In the middle it is movement energy. It changes back and forth, twice on every swing. Each time, the keys and card push air aside, and some movement becomes warmth in the air. The open card pushes far more air, so the energy leaves far sooner. A little also leaves where the thread bends under the tape. What the test cannot tell you is how the loss is shared between the air and the thread. Ask how your child would find out.
  - Check: With the card folded the middle count was 40 swings. With it open, 5. How many times sooner did the open card run down? → **8 times**
  - Think: A swing in the park also runs down. What would you change to make it keep going longer?

  *Parent says:* I have switched the kettle on. Where is the energy coming from? Name every place you think it will go.  
  *Child mode:* A grown-up switches on the kettle. Where will the energy go? Name each place.
  - Note: If you have no electric kettle, heat a small pot of water on the stove and keep your child an arm's length back. The chain then starts with the stored chemical energy of the gas.
- **s2 (6 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* Stay with the kettle. Listen for the first hiss and the rumble. Hold a hand 10 centimetres from the side, without touching. Watch for steam and for the switch clicking off.  
  *Parent says:* What can you hear? Is the air near the side warm? What is coming out of the spout? Each of those is energy going somewhere.  
  *Child mode:* With a grown-up, listen to the kettle. Feel the warm air near it. Do not touch.
  - Ask: Which things that you noticed were not the job of the kettle? (good: The noise.; The warm outside.; The steam going into the room.)
- **s3 (7 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Draw what you watched. Draw one arrow coming in, 5 squares wide, and label it electrical. Split it into two parts. One arrow is 4 squares wide, labelled heats the water. Thin arrows show the warm air, the sound and the steam.  
  *Parent says:* Let us draw it. The arrow in is five squares wide. About four of the five parts heat the water. Draw that arrow four squares wide. Share the last square between the thin arrows.  
  *Child mode:* Draw a wide arrow in. Split it. Four parts heat the water. One part goes elsewhere.
  - Note: The widths going out must add up to the width coming in, because no energy is lost or made. This kind of drawing is called a Sankey diagram, after the engineer Matthew Sankey, who drew one for a steam engine in 1898.
  - Check: The arrow going in is 5 squares wide. The arrow to the water is 4 squares wide. How wide are all the other arrows together? → **1 square**
- **s4 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Ask where all the energy will be in one hour, when the tea has been drunk and everything is cold again.  
  *Parent says:* In an hour the water will be cold again. Where will all that energy be then?  
  *Child mode:* In one hour the water is cold. Where is the energy then?
  - Note: A good answer is that it has spread into the kitchen air, the cups and the people who drank the tea, and is on its way out of the house.
  - Think: Is the energy in the hot water useful energy or spread-out energy when you forget the tea and it goes cold?
### a3 · The Coin Line: send energy through coins that stay still (15 min)
- **record:** Draw the line of coins before and after, for one coin in and for two coins in. Mark the coins that moved.

  *Child mode:* Line up five touching coins. Guess what happens when one coin hits the end.
  - Note: Common guesses are that the whole row will slide, or that the coins will scatter. Write the guess down.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Flick the sixth coin straight into the end of the row, along the ruler. Watch the far end. Reset the row and do it five times.  
  *Parent says:* Flick it straight in. Which coin moved? Which coins stayed?  
  *Child mode:* Flick the coin in. Which coin leaves? Which coins stay still?
  - Ask: The middle coins hardly moved. How did the energy reach the last coin? (good: It went through them.; Each coin passed it to the next.)
  - Note: With a straight flick, the last coin leaves and the others barely move. A crooked flick scatters them, so keep the ruler as a guide. It is not perfect. Sometimes a second coin creeps a little.
- **s3 (4 min)**

  *Child mode:* Slide two coins in together. Count how many leave the other end.
  - Note: Usually two coins leave. It is harder to do cleanly than one coin, so count the tries where the two went in together.
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Talk about what passed along the row. Then give the idea: each coin was squashed a tiny bit and sprang back, pushing the next.  
  *Parent says:* The energy went through four coins that stayed where they were. It passed along as a quick squash. Sound moves through air in the same way.  
  *Child mode:* The energy passed through the still coins as a quick squash. Sound travels like that too.
  - Think: After a few seconds the last coin stops too. Where is the energy of your flick then?
### a4 · The String Phone: can sound energy travel along a thread? (20 min)
- **safety:** A grown-up makes the hole in each cup with the pencil point, pushing away from the hand.
- **record:** Draw the energy chain from the speaker's voice to the listener's ear, with an arrow for each step. Write what happened with the thread tight, slack and pinched.
- **s1 (6 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* A grown-up makes a small hole in the bottom of each cup. Push one end of the thread through each hole from outside. Tie each end round a piece of matchstick so that it cannot slip out.  
  *Parent says:* I will make the holes. You push the thread through and tie it to the matchstick inside the cup.  
  *Child mode:* A grown-up makes the holes. You tie the thread inside each cup.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Before testing, ask your child to guess. Will a whisper be heard with the thread tight? With the thread loose? Write the guesses and reasons.  
  *Parent says:* One of us will whisper into a cup. Will the other hear it when the thread is tight? What about when it hangs loose? Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Guess. Will a whisper get through a tight thread? A loose thread? Say why.
- **s3 (6 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Stand as far apart as the thread allows and pull it tight. One person whispers into a cup while the other holds a cup to the ear. Then let the thread hang loose and try again. Then pull it tight and have someone pinch the thread in the middle.  
  *Parent says:* Hold the cup to your ear. I will whisper. Now we let it go loose. Now tight again, and I pinch the middle. What changed each time?  
  *Child mode:* Whisper with the thread tight. Then loose. Then pinched. What changes?
  - Ask: Touch the tight thread lightly while someone speaks. What do you feel? (good: It tickles.; It is shaking.)
  - Note: The thread must be straight and must not touch furniture. The whisper comes through clearly when it is tight, and not when it is loose or pinched. If nothing comes through, check that the knots sit against the cup bottoms.
- **s4 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Draw the chain together: voice, air in the cup, cup bottom, thread, second cup bottom, air, ear. Ask what kind of energy it is at each step.  
  *Parent says:* Your voice shakes the air. The air shakes the cup. The cup shakes the thread. What form is the energy all the way along?  
  *Child mode:* Draw the chain from mouth to ear. What form is the energy all along?
  - Note: It is movement energy passed along as a shaking, which is what sound is. So this is nearly all transfer, with little change of form. A pinch takes the shaking into your fingers.
  - Think: A loose thread carries no whisper. What is different about a tight one?

## Challenges
### c1 · The Long Spin: build a top that keeps its energy longest (30 min)
- **problem:** Build a spinning top from things at home. Give it energy with one twist of your fingers. Make it spin for as long as you can before all that energy has spread out as warmth and sound. There is more than one good design.
- **safety:** A grown-up cuts the card and pushes the stick through its centre.
- **your turn:** Build your own top and time it. What did you change that helped the most? Where do you think its energy went fastest: into the tip, into the air or into wobbling?
- **constraints:** Start the top with one twist of the fingers of one hand. No string and no motor.; The top must spin by itself on a plate or table. Nobody touches it after the twist.; Time three spins with a stopwatch. Your best time counts.; Change one thing at a time, so that you know what made the difference.
- **general:** Push the stick through the exact centre of a card disc about 8 centimetres across, so that the top does not wobble.; Keep the disc low on the stick, close to the plate, so that it does not fall over.; Tape coins evenly round the rim. A heavy rim holds more movement energy from the same twist.
- **creative:** Rest an old CD on a marble, with a bottle cap taped on top as a handle. The marble makes a smooth round tip.; Spin the top inside a shallow steel bowl, so that it cannot wander off and hit the rim of a plate.; Compare the same top on a steel plate, a tile, a newspaper and a towel, to find which surface takes its energy fastest.
- **c1s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Read the problem together. Ask your child what makes a top stop, and what might make it spin longer. Write the ideas down.  
  *Parent says:* A top always stops in the end. Where does its energy go? What could you change so that it goes more slowly?  
  *Child mode:* Why does a top stop? Think of ways to make it spin longer.
  - Ask: Where does a top's energy go? (good: Into rubbing at the tip.; Into the air.; Into the sound it makes.)
- **c1s2 (8 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* A grown-up cuts a card disc about 8 centimetres across and pushes a matchstick through its centre. Spin it on the steel plate with one twist. Time three spins and write the best.  
  *Parent says:* I will cut the disc and make the hole. You spin it. One twist, then hands off. I will time it.  
  *Child mode:* A grown-up cuts the disc. You twist it once. Time three spins.
  - Note: A plain card top spins for about 5 to 15 seconds. That time is the one to beat. If it wobbles, the stick is off centre, so make a new hole.
- **c1s3 (12 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Change one thing and time three spins again. Try a heavier rim, a lower disc, a smoother tip or a different surface. Keep a list of each change and its best time.  
  *Parent says:* Change one thing only. What do you want to try first? Why do you think it will help?  
  *Child mode:* Change one thing. Time it again. Write the change and the time.
  - Note: Coins on the rim usually help most, and a CD on a marble can spin for over a minute. Let your child find this out. A change that makes it worse is also a finding.
- **c1s4 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* Look at the list together. Ask which change helped most and why. Listen to the best top as it slows.  
  *Parent says:* Which change gave the longest spin? Why do you think it worked? In the end it still stopped. Where is the energy of your twist now?  
  *Child mode:* Which change helped most? The top still stopped. Where is the energy now?
  - Think: Could any top spin for ever? What would have to be true?

## Worksheets
### Pass it on or change it (core) (core, 16 questions)
1. (mcq) A cricket bat hits a ball and the ball flies off. What is this mainly?  
   Options: A transfer of movement energy / A change of sound into light / Energy being destroyed  
   **Answer:** A transfer of movement energy. *Why:* Movement energy passes from the bat to the ball. The form stays the same, so it is a transfer.
2. (truefalse) In an energy change, the energy turns into a different form.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. A clap changes movement into sound. A bulb changes electrical energy into light.
3. (fill) When energy passes from one thing to another, it is called an energy ____.  
   **Answer:** ['transfer']. *Why:* A transfer is energy passing along. A change, or transformation, is energy taking a new form.
4. (sort) Sort each event by what the energy mainly does.  
   **Answer:** a striker hits a carrom coin → Passes along (transfer); warm hands warm a cold cheek → Passes along (transfer); one swing bumps the next swing → Passes along (transfer); a clap makes a sound → Changes form; a torch cell lights a bulb → Changes form; a marble speeds up down a slope → Changes form; rubbed palms get warm → Changes form. *Why:* In the first three, the energy is the same form before and after, and only moves to a new thing. In the last four, a new form appears.
5. (match) Match each thing to the main energy change it makes.  
   **Answer:** Torch → stored → electrical → light; Electric kettle → electrical → heat; Marble on a slope → stored → movement; Ghatam drum → movement → sound; Solar panel → light → electrical. *Why:* Ask what goes in and what comes out. A solar panel runs a torch's chain backwards.
6. (order) Put the steps of a torch's energy chain in order.  
   **Answer:** ['Stored chemical energy in the cell', 'Electrical energy round the loop', 'Light and warmth from the bulb', 'A slightly warmer wall and room']. *Why:* The energy starts in the cell, is carried round the loop, becomes light and warmth in the bulb, and ends as warmth in the room.
7. (mcq) A paper cup slides and stops. What changed its movement energy into warmth?  
   Options: The cup getting tired / Gravity pulling it down / Friction with the table  
   **Answer:** Friction with the table. *Why:* Friction between the cup and the table changes movement into warmth. Gravity pulls down, and does not slow a cup sliding on a level table.
8. (truefalse) Pushing hard on a wall that does not move does work on the wall.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. In science, work is a force moving a thing. The wall does not move, so no energy passes to it.
9. (fill) The part of the energy that does the job we wanted is called ____ energy.  
   **Answer:** ['useful']. *Why:* For a bulb the useful energy is light. For a fan it is moving air.
10. (draw) Draw an energy chain for a ceiling fan, from the wall switch to the room. Use boxes and arrows.  
   **Answer:** Boxes such as: electrical energy in the wires → motor → moving blades → moving air → warmth in the room. A side arrow from the motor shows warmth and hum.. *Why:* A good chain has an arrow for every step, shows the useful part, and follows the energy until it has spread out as warmth.
11. (write) A football rolls across a field and stops. A friend says its energy is gone. What would you say to her?  
   **Answer:** The energy is not gone. Friction with the grass and the air changed the ball's movement into warmth. The grass, the ball and the air are each a tiny bit warmer.. *Why:* A strong answer says where the energy went and that the amount is the same as before.
12. (mcq) A mixer grinder makes chutney. Which is spread-out energy?  
   Options: The turning blades / The cut coconut / The roar and the warm jar  
   **Answer:** The roar and the warm jar. *Why:* The mixer is built to turn its blades and cut. The noise and warmth are energy that went where it was not wanted.
13. (odd) Which is the odd one out, and why? A kettle, an iron, a geyser, a fan.  
   **Answer:** The fan, because it changes electrical energy mainly into movement. The other three change it mainly into heat.. *Why:* Look at the main form that comes out. Three give heat and one gives movement.
14. (perspective) Two children look at a toy car that has rolled to a stop.  
   **Answer:** Answers vary. A strong answer says the second child matches what measurements find, and that the first child is right that the car's own movement energy ran down. A check is to rub something hard and feel the warmth.. *Why:* The first child describes what we see: the gathered movement energy has run down. The second describes what is measured: the same amount now exists as warmth.
15. (count) A marble pushed a cup 3, 5 and 4 centimetres in three tries. What is the middle value?  
   **Answer:** 4. *Why:* In order the tries are 3, 4, 5. The middle one is 4 centimetres.
16. (write) Name one thing in your home that changes energy from one form to another. Say what goes in and what comes out.  
   **Answer:** Any true example, such as: a television takes in electrical energy and gives out light, sound and warmth.. *Why:* A strong answer names the form going in, the useful form coming out, and at least one spread-out form.
### Chains, arrows and swings (stretch) (stretch, 16 questions)
1. (mcq) A wave crosses a pond and a floating leaf only bobs up and down. What travelled across the pond?  
   Options: Energy / The water itself / The leaf  
   **Answer:** Energy. *Why:* The water and the leaf stayed in their places and only moved up and down. The energy of the wave travelled across.
2. (truefalse) Sound can carry energy through the air without the air travelling to your ear.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. Each bit of air shakes and pushes the next bit. The shaking travels, and the air stays roughly where it was.
3. (fill) A drawing where the width of each arrow shows the share of energy is called an energy ____ diagram.  
   **Answer:** ['flow', 'Sankey']. *Why:* The arrow in is as wide as all the arrows out added together, because no energy is made or lost.
4. (order) A girl throws a ball straight up and catches it. Put the energy forms in order from the throw.  
   **Answer:** ['Stored energy in her arm muscles', 'Movement energy of the rising ball', 'Stored energy of height at the top', 'Movement energy of the falling ball', 'Warmth and sound in her hands at the catch']. *Why:* Her muscles give the ball movement. Going up, movement changes into stored energy of height. Coming down it changes back, and the catch turns it into warmth and a slap of sound.
5. (match) Match each living being to an energy change it makes.  
   **Answer:** Leaf → light → stored chemical energy; Firefly → stored chemical energy → light; Electric eel → stored chemical energy → electrical; Shivering honeybee → stored chemical energy → warmth. *Why:* Every living being takes energy in one form and changes it to others. A leaf and a firefly run opposite changes.
6. (count) A bulb's arrow in is 10 squares wide. Its light arrow is 3 squares wide. How wide is the warmth arrow?  
   **Answer:** 7. *Why:* The arrows out must add up to the arrow in. 10 take away 3 is 7 squares.
7. (mcq) A swing is at the very top of its swing, about to come back. Where is most of its energy?  
   Options: Stored, in its height / In its movement / In its sound  
   **Answer:** Stored, in its height. *Why:* At the top it is highest and, for a moment, still. So the energy is stored in height. At the bottom of the swing it is nearly all movement.
8. (truefalse) A pendulum let go from a mark can swing back higher than that mark by itself.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. It can never have more energy than it started with, and it loses a little to the air on each swing. So it returns a little lower each time.
9. (write) In the Tired Swing test, an open card made the keys stop swinging much sooner. Explain why, using the word energy.  
   **Answer:** The open card pushes much more air aside on every swing. Pushing air changes the swing's movement energy into warmth in the air, so the energy leaves the swing sooner.. *Why:* A strong answer says where the energy went (the air) and why more went there with the open card (more air to push).
10. (sort) Sort each one by how the energy is mainly transferred.  
   **Answer:** lifting a bucket on a rope → By a push or pull (work); a bat hitting a ball → By a push or pull (work); a hot tawa cooking a dosa → By heating; warm hands melting a piece of ice → By heating; sunshine drying clothes on a line → By sound or light; a temple bell heard across a street → By sound or light. *Why:* A force that moves a thing transfers energy as work. A hotter thing touching a cooler one transfers it by heating. Sound and light carry it across a gap as waves.
11. (count) Lifting a bottle of water 1 metre takes about 10 joules. About how many joules to lift it 3 metres?  
   **Answer:** 30. *Why:* Three times the distance is three times the work. 10 times 3 is 30 joules.
12. (draw) Draw an energy flow diagram for an old bulb with a glowing wire. Make the arrows the right widths.  
   **Answer:** One wide arrow in, labelled electrical. It splits into a very thin arrow labelled light and a very wide arrow labelled heat.. *Why:* Only about a twentieth becomes light, so the light arrow is thin and the heat arrow takes nearly all the width.
13. (odd) Which is the odd one out, and why? Holding a bag still, lifting a bag, pulling a cart, kicking a ball.  
   **Answer:** Holding a bag still, because nothing moves, so no work is done on the bag. In the other three, a force moves a thing.. *Why:* Work in science needs movement. Three of these have it and one does not.
14. (perspective) Is the warmth from your body wasted energy?  
   **Answer:** Answers vary. A strong answer sees that 'wasted' depends on the job you have in mind. For moving, the warmth is a loss. For staying alive, it is needed.. *Why:* Both use the same facts. The engineer counts only movement as the job. The biologist counts staying warm as a job too. Useful is about what is wanted.
15. (write) Explain why a bouncing ball bounces a little lower each time.  
   **Answer:** At each bounce the ball is squashed and springs back. Some of its movement energy changes into warmth in the rubber and the floor, and into sound. So it has less energy to rise with.. *Why:* A strong answer names where the energy goes at each bounce, and says the energy is not destroyed.
16. (mcq) You want to find out if a rough table stops the cup sooner than a smooth one. What must you change?  
   Options: Only the surface / The surface and the marble / The start mark and the cup  
   **Answer:** Only the surface. *Why:* In a fair test only one thing changes. Here it is the surface. If the marble or the start mark changed too, you could not tell what made the difference.
### Where does it all go? (challenge) (challenge, 16 questions)
1. (mcq) Julius Robert Mayer said in 1842 that movement and heat are the same thing in two forms. Which finding fits his idea?  
   Options: A heavy wheel turns for ever / Rubbing always makes warmth / Cold flows out of ice  
   **Answer:** Rubbing always makes warmth. *Why:* If movement and heat are one, then lost movement must show up as warmth, and it does. No wheel has ever turned for ever.
2. (truefalse) An energy chain for a bus could begin with sunlight that fell millions of years ago.  
   **Answer:** True. *Why:* True. Diesel is made from tiny sea beings that lived long ago and stored the energy of sunlight. That stored energy is what moves the bus.
3. (fill) The rule that energy is never made and never destroyed is called the ____ of energy.  
   **Answer:** ['conservation']. *Why:* Conservation means the total is kept. Energy only moves and changes form.
4. (order) Put this energy chain in order, starting from the earliest.  
   **Answer:** ['Sunlight falls on the sea', 'Water rises as vapour and falls as rain in the hills', 'Water rushes down through a turbine at a dam', 'A generator makes electrical energy', 'A fan turns in a flat']. *Why:* The Sun lifts the water. The high water stores energy. Falling, it turns a turbine and a generator, and the electrical energy runs the fan.
5. (count) A machine takes in 100 joules. It gives 25 joules of movement. How many joules spread out as warmth and sound?  
   **Answer:** 75. *Why:* Nothing is lost, so the rest must be warmth and sound. 100 take away 25 is 75 joules.
6. (mcq) Bhaskara II described a wheel meant to turn for ever. Why does every such wheel stop?  
   Options: Friction changes its movement into warmth / It runs out of wheel / Gravity switches off  
   **Answer:** Friction changes its movement into warmth. *Why:* On every turn a little movement becomes warmth at the axle and in the air. Nothing puts that energy back, so the wheel slows and stops.
7. (write) In 1930 some atoms seemed to lose energy. Wolfgang Pauli guessed an unseen particle took it. Why was that bold?  
   **Answer:** Nobody had seen such a particle. He trusted the rule that energy is never lost more than he trusted that everything had been seen. The particle, the neutrino, was found in 1956.. *Why:* A strong answer says that he had two choices: give up the rule, or predict something new. He kept the rule, and a test later agreed.
8. (perspective) People who study energy use different words for what you saw today.  
   **Answer:** Answers vary. A strong answer tries one of the ways on the torch and says what it makes clear and what it hides.. *Why:* No view is marked right here, because teachers and physicists still disagree. All three give the same amounts. They differ in the picture they put in your mind.
9. (draw) Draw what happens to the atoms of a table top just under a sliding cup. Show before and after.  
   **Answer:** Before: rows of atoms jiggling a little. After: the same atoms jiggling more, in all directions, under where the cup slid.. *Why:* Warmth is the scattered movement of atoms. Friction turns the one orderly movement of the cup into faster jiggling of many atoms.
10. (mcq) Emmy Noether proved why energy is conserved. What is the reason she found?  
   Options: The laws of nature stay the same over time / Energy is too heavy to vanish / Scientists agreed to say so  
   **Answer:** The laws of nature stay the same over time. *Why:* She showed in 1918 that if the laws of nature are the same from one moment to the next, a number must stay constant. That number is energy.
11. (truefalse) If a marble starts twice as high on a slope, it reaches the bottom twice as fast.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. It arrives with twice the movement energy, but movement energy grows faster than speed. Its speed is only about one and a half times as great.
12. (write) Design a fair test: does a heavier marble push the cup further? Say what changes and what stays the same.  
   **Answer:** Change only the marble: a small one and a big one. Keep the ramp, the start mark, the cup and the table the same. Roll each three times and compare the middle slides.. *Why:* A strong answer changes one thing, keeps the rest the same, repeats each roll, and says what will be measured.
13. (match) Match each person to what they did.  
   **Answer:** Count Rumford, 1798 → showed that rubbing makes heat without end; James Joule, 1840s → measured how much movement makes how much heat; Emmy Noether, 1918 → proved why energy must be conserved; John Henry Poynting, 1884 → showed that a wire's energy travels in the space around it. *Why:* Each one added a piece. Rumford and Joule joined movement to heat. Poynting followed the energy of a circuit. Noether found the reason for the rule.
14. (odd) Which is the odd one out, and why? Warm brakes, a warm phone charger, a warm motor, a warm cup of tea.  
   **Answer:** The warm cup of tea, because its warmth is the useful energy we wanted. In the other three, the warmth is spread-out energy we did not want.. *Why:* Useful depends on the job. Tea is meant to be hot. Brakes, chargers and motors are not built to make warmth.
15. (write) The total energy in the world never changes. So why do people say we must save energy?  
   **Answer:** What we can run short of is gathered energy in a useful form, such as fuel, food and charged batteries. Once it has spread out as warmth it is very hard to use again. Saving energy means spreading it out more slowly.. *Why:* A strong answer separates the amount of energy, which is fixed, from how gathered and useful it is, which runs down.
16. (mcq) You push a toy car 2 metres. Then you push it just as hard for 4 metres. Which push did more work?  
   Options: Both the same / Neither did any work / The second push  
   **Answer:** The second push. *Why:* Work depends on the force and on how far the thing moves. The same force over twice the distance does twice the work.

## Quiz
1. (mcq) A hot dosa is put on a cold steel plate. The plate warms up. What is this?  
   Options: A change of form / An energy transfer / Energy being made  
   **Answer:** An energy transfer. *Why:* Heat passes from the dosa to the plate, and it is heat before and after. That is a transfer. If you chose a change of form, look for a new form. There is none here.
2. (mcq) You ring a cycle bell with your thumb. Which change of form happens?  
   Options: Sound into light / Heat into movement / Movement into sound  
   **Answer:** Movement into sound. *Why:* Your thumb moves the lever, the bell shakes, and the shaking air is sound. If you chose another, say each form aloud and check that it is really there.
3. (mcq) Which energy chain fits a torch?  
   Options: Light → electrical → stored / Stored → electrical → light and warmth / Movement → sound → light  
   **Answer:** Stored → electrical → light and warmth. *Why:* The cell holds stored chemical energy. It becomes electrical energy in the wires and then light and a little warmth in the bulb. The second chain runs backwards, which is what a solar panel does.
4. (mcq) A marble rolls down a slope. What happens to its energy on the way down?  
   Options: Movement energy changes into stored energy / New energy is made by rolling / Stored energy changes into movement energy  
   **Answer:** Stored energy changes into movement energy. *Why:* It loses height and gains speed, so stored energy is changing into movement energy. Rolling makes no new energy. The energy was put in when the marble was lifted.
5. (mcq) A ball rolls across the floor and stops. Where is its movement energy now?  
   Options: Warmth in the floor, ball and air / Nowhere. It is gone. / Back inside the ball as movement  
   **Answer:** Warmth in the floor, ball and air. *Why:* Friction changed the movement into warmth, spread through the floor, the ball and the air. 'Gone' is how it looks, but each time it is measured the warmth matches the lost movement.
6. (truefalse) A flat torch cell shows that energy can be used up and destroyed.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. The energy that was stored in the cell is now warmth spread through the room. What ran out was energy gathered in one place.
7. (mcq) A marble starts 5 centimetres high and pushes a cup 4 centimetres. From 10 centimetres high, about how far will it push?  
   Options: About 8 centimetres / About 4 centimetres / About 40 centimetres  
   **Answer:** About 8 centimetres. *Why:* Twice the height stores twice the energy, and that pushes the cup about twice as far: about 8 centimetres. If you chose 4, you expected height not to matter. Try the test again and compare.
8. (mcq) In the marble and cup test, why is each start mark tried three times?  
   Options: One roll can go crooked / To tire the marble out / To make the cup lighter  
   **Answer:** One roll can go crooked. *Why:* A single roll can be odd. Three rolls let you take the middle value, which a single odd roll cannot spoil. The marble and the cup do not change.
9. (mcq) For a ceiling fan, which is the useful energy?  
   Options: Warmth of the motor / Hum of the motor / Movement of the air  
   **Answer:** Movement of the air. *Why:* A fan is built to move air. The warm motor and the hum are energy that spread out where we did not want it.
10. (mcq) On a cold night in Ooty, a family is glad of the warmth from an old bulb. Is that warmth wasted?  
   Options: Yes. Warmth is always waste. / No. Tonight they want it. / Yes. It is destroyed.  
   **Answer:** No. Tonight they want it.. *Why:* Useful means 'what we wanted'. Tonight the warmth is wanted, so it is useful. Energy is never destroyed, and no form of energy is waste by its nature.
11. (mcq) In which of these is work done on the thing, in the scientist's meaning?  
   Options: Holding a bag still / Lifting a bucket from a well / Pushing a wall that stays put  
   **Answer:** Lifting a bucket from a well. *Why:* Work needs a force and a movement. The bucket moves up, so energy passes to it. The bag and the wall do not move, so no energy passes to them, though your muscles still spend energy.
12. (mcq) Which is the best description of what physicists mean by energy?  
   Options: An invisible liquid in things / A number whose total never changes / The tiredness you feel  
   **Answer:** A number whose total never changes. *Why:* Energy is a number that can be worked out for anything, and its total stays the same through every change. Nobody has found a liquid, and what energy is underneath is still unknown.
13. (truefalse) Scientists know exactly what energy is made of.  
   **Answer:** False. *Why:* False. They can work out the amount exactly, and it always balances. What energy is, underneath the sums, is an open question.

## Applications
- Hydroelectric stations such as Idukki in Kerala change the stored energy of high water into movement and then into electrical energy.
- Wind farms such as Muppandal in Tamil Nadu transfer the movement energy of air to turning blades.
- Solar parks such as Pavagada in Karnataka and Bhadla in Rajasthan change light straight into electrical energy.
- Electric trains and some electric cars use their motors as generators when braking, to change movement back into stored electrical energy.
- Engineers draw energy flow diagrams to find where a factory, a car or a house lets energy spread out, and to cut the loss.
- LED bulbs give the same light as older bulbs for a fraction of the electricity, because less becomes heat.
- Pressure cookers and pots with lids keep heat from spreading into the kitchen, so more of it cooks the food.
- Musicians and makers of instruments such as the ghatam and the veena shape wood and clay so that hand movement changes well into sound.

## Open questions
- **What is energy, underneath the sums?** Physicists can work out the number exactly and it always balances. Nobody can say what it is a number of, or if that question even has an answer.
- **Is the total energy of the whole universe fixed, zero, changing, or impossible to add up?** Einstein's theory of gravity gives no single way to count the energy of stretching space, so careful physicists reach different answers.
- **Why does energy spread out but never gather itself back?** The rule of conservation would allow both. The spreading seems to depend on the universe having started in a very special, gathered state, and nobody knows why it started that way.
- **What is the dark energy that makes space stretch faster and faster?** It was found in 1998 by measuring far exploding stars. It seems to be most of the energy in the universe, and nobody knows what it is.
- **How do leaves pass the energy of light from molecule to molecule with almost none lost on the way?** In 2007 a team reported that leaves may use quantum effects to do it. Later tests questioned this, and the work goes on.

## Connected maths
- In the Marble and the Cup test, three rolls from the top mark pushed the cup 12, 15 and 13 centimetres. What is the middle value? **13 centimetres.**. Put the three in order: 12, 13, 15. The middle one is 13. (queues `math.data.averages.L4`)
- From a height of 3 centimetres a marble pushed a cup 4 centimetres. If the push grows in step with the height, how far would it push from 9 centimetres? **12 centimetres.**. 9 is 3 times 3, so the push is 3 times 4, which is 12 centimetres. (queues `math.fractions.ratio-and-proportion.L4`)
- A kettle takes 100 joules of electrical energy. Four fifths of it heats the water. How many joules heat the water, and how many spread out? **80 joules heat the water and 20 joules spread out.**. One fifth of 100 is 20. Four fifths is 80. 100 take away 80 leaves 20. (queues `math.fractions.fractions-of-a-whole.L4`)
- Three players start the Warmth Bowl game with 12 beans each, and the Sun pile has 10. At the end the players hold 9, 7 and 11 beans and the Sun pile has 2. How many beans are in the bowl? **17 beans.**. At the start there are 36 and 10, which is 46. At the end the hands hold 27 and the Sun has 2, which is 29. 46 take away 29 is 17. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.word-problems.L4`)
- In the Tired Swing test, the keys made 48 swings with the card folded and 6 swings with it open. How many times sooner did the open card run down? **8 times sooner.**. 48 divided by 6 is 8. (queues `math.division.division-facts.L4`)
- An old bulb takes in 60 joules each second and gives 3 joules of light. An LED bulb takes in 9 joules each second and gives 3 joules of light. How many joules become warmth in each bulb every second? **57 joules in the old bulb and 6 joules in the LED bulb.**. 60 take away 3 is 57. 9 take away 3 is 6. Both give the same light, but the old bulb spreads out far more warmth. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.mental-strategies.L4`)
- Draw a bar chart for cup slides of 4, 9 and 13 centimetres from the three marks. Use one square for each centimetre. How many squares taller is the tallest bar than the shortest? **9 squares.**. The tallest bar is 13 squares and the shortest is 4. 13 take away 4 is 9. (queues `math.data.bar-charts.L4`)
