# Plant parts we eat, and how a cactus, a lotus and a banyan use theirs
`sci.plants.plant-parts.L2`

**Summary.** Put a piece of brinjal and a piece of potato in a bowl of water. The brinjal floats and the potato sinks. Every vegetable is a part of a plant: a root, a stem, a leaf, a flower, a fruit or a seed. What is inside each part shows the job it did for the plant. In this unit you sort the food in your kitchen by plant part, and test which parts float. You also meet three plants with very different parts: a cactus in a dry desert, a lotus in a pond and a huge banyan tree.

**Intro.** *Next time someone washes vegetables, watch the bowl. Some pieces float on top. Some pieces go to the bottom. Which ones float in your home?* Every vegetable we eat is one part of a plant. A carrot is a root. Palak is a leaf. A brinjal is a fruit, and chana is a seed. These parts are not all made the same way inside. Some are packed tight with food. Some have tiny spaces full of air. Before you read on, make a guess. Will a piece of potato float or sink? You are going to find out.

## Terms
- **Plant part**: One piece of a plant. Each piece has its own job: root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit or seed. *e.g.* A carrot is the root of the carrot plant. Palak is the leaf of the palak plant.
- **Edible**: Safe to eat. Only some plant parts are edible. *e.g.* The leaf of a drumstick tree is edible. We cook it as murungai keerai.
- **Fruit**: The part of a plant that grows from a flower and holds the seeds. To a scientist, a tomato is a fruit. *e.g.* Cut a tomato, a brinjal or a cucumber. You find seeds inside, so each one is a fruit.
- **Stored food**: Food a plant has made and kept inside a part, to use later. *e.g.* A potato, a carrot and a chana seed are packed with stored food.
- **Air space**: A tiny gap inside a plant part that is filled with air. *e.g.* A leaf has many air spaces inside. That is one reason a leaf floats.
- **Float and sink**: A thing floats if it stays on top of the water. It sinks if it goes down to the bottom. *e.g.* A piece of brinjal floats. A piece of potato sinks.
- **Spine**: A hard, sharp point. On a cactus, the spines are leaves that have changed shape. *e.g.* A cactus has spines instead of flat green leaves.
- **Prop root**: A root that grows down from a branch into the soil and holds the branch up, like a pillar. *e.g.* A banyan tree has many prop roots hanging from her branches.

## Explanations
### Every food from a plant is one of the plant's parts · *defined*
**Definition:** The plant foods we eat are roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits or seeds. Each part had its own job for the plant.

A carrot and a radish are roots. They grew down into the soil and stored food. Sugarcane and lotus stem are stems. Palak, cabbage and curry leaves are leaves. Cauliflower and banana flower are flowers. A cook calls tomato, brinjal and bhindi vegetables. But they grew from flowers and hold seeds, so to a scientist they are fruits. Rice, wheat, dal and peanuts are seeds.
- e.g. Root: carrot, radish, beetroot.
- e.g. Stem: sugarcane, lotus stem, potato.
- e.g. Leaf: palak, cabbage, curry leaves, drumstick leaves.
- e.g. Flower: cauliflower, banana flower, cloves.
- e.g. Fruit: tomato, brinjal, bhindi, cucumber, drumstick, mango.
- e.g. Seed: rice, wheat, toor dal, chana, peanuts.
### Why some plant parts float and others sink · *defined*
**Definition:** A plant part that is packed tight with stored food sinks in water. A plant part with many tiny air spaces floats.

A potato, a carrot and a dry chana seed are packed with food that the plant saved for later. There is little room inside for air, so they sink. A brinjal is soft and spongy inside, with many tiny air spaces. A leaf has air spaces too, because a leaf takes in air to make food. The air helps them float. Cutting a potato into a tiny piece does not help. The tiny piece sinks too, because what is inside is the same.
- e.g. Potato piece: sinks. It is packed with stored food.
- e.g. Dry chana: sinks. A seed is packed with food for the baby plant inside.
- e.g. Brinjal piece: floats. It is spongy, with air spaces.
- e.g. Palak leaf: floats. It has air spaces inside.
### A cactus, a lotus and a banyan: same parts, different homes · *defined*
**Definition:** Every plant has roots, a stem and leaves, but their shapes are very different. Each plant's parts suit the place where the plant lives.

A cactus lives where it hardly rains. Their thick green stem stores water and makes food, and their spines are leaves that lose almost no water. A lotus lives in a pond, with their stem in the mud where there is little air. Long tunnels of air run down the leaf stalks to the stem. A banyan is a huge tree. Roots grow down from her branches into the soil and hold the branches up like pillars.
- e.g. Cactus: a fat, green stem that stores water, and spines instead of flat leaves.
- e.g. Lotus: a stem in the mud, long stalks with air tunnels, and big leaves on the water.
- e.g. Banyan: prop roots that hang from the branches and become new trunks.
### Does a plant feel what each part is doing? · *debated*
Plants feel and decide with every part. A banyan's hanging root senses which way is down and keeps growing towards the ground. A root senses wet soil and grows that way. A leaf senses light. The parts send signals to each other, so the whole plant acts together. Some scientists describe this as automatic responses, and do not use words like feel or decide.
- e.g. A banyan's hanging roots grow straight down towards the soil.
- e.g. A cactus's roots grow fast just after rain, while the soil is wet.
- e.g. A money plant's stem bends towards the window.
- **Scientists who study how plants behave:** A plant senses gravity, water, light and touch with every part, and decides how to grow. Each part sends signals to the others.
- **Many other plant scientists:** These are automatic responses to gravity, water and light. They call them 'responses' and do not use the words 'feel' or 'decide'.

## Mostly agreed
- All the plant food we eat comes from six kinds of plant part: roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds. *How we know:* Cut and look: a carrot grows in the soil like a root, a tomato has seeds inside like every fruit, and a dal grain sprouts like a seed.
- Plant parts packed with stored food usually sink in water. Parts with many air spaces float. *How we know:* In the float test, a potato piece and dry chana sank. A brinjal piece and a palak leaf floated.
- A cactus stores water in a thick stem, and their spines are changed leaves. *How we know:* Cut a cactus stem and it is wet and green inside. Spines grow from the stem where leaves would grow on other plants.
- A lotus has air tunnels in their stalks and underground stem. *How we know:* Cut a lotus stem across and you see a ring of holes. Air travels through these tunnels to the parts in the mud.

## How we found out
**John Dacey; Mevi-Schütz and Wolfgang Grosse in Germany, 1980 for the water lily; 1988 for the lotus.** Water lilies and lotus plants grow their stems in pond mud. Mud has almost no air in it, but the stems need air to stay alive. For a long time, scientists thought air reached them only by creeping slowly down the leaf stalks. In 1980, a scientist named John Dacey studied the yellow water lily. He found that air does not just creep. Young leaves, warmed by the sun, push air down the stalks. The air flows through the stem in the mud, and comes back up and out through older leaves. Dacey called it an internal wind. In 1988, two scientists in Germany, Mevi-Schütz and Wolfgang Grosse, found a flow of air through the lotus too, driven by the warmth of the sun on the leaves. Scientists are still studying exactly how the lotus pushes the air.  
*What people believed before:* That air reaches the stems of pond plants only by creeping slowly down the stalks. Dacey showed that warm young leaves push air down, so it flows through the plant like a wind.

## Challenging perspectives
- **Heavy things sink and light things float.** (A common first guess; *rejected*)  
  For: A big stone sinks and a tiny feather floats, so weight seems to decide.  
  Against: A big piece of brinjal floats and a tiny crumb of potato sinks. What matters is what is inside, not how big or heavy the piece is.
- **A tomato is a vegetable, not a fruit.** (Cooks and shopkeepers; *accepted*)  
  For: We cook tomatoes in rasam and sambar, not in sweets. In the kitchen, a tomato is used like a vegetable.  
  Against: A tomato grows from a flower and has seeds inside. So to a scientist, a tomato is a fruit. Both words are right in their own place.
- **Cactus plants come from the deserts of India.** (Many people; *rejected*)  
  For: Prickly pear cactus grows wild on dry roadsides in many parts of India.  
  Against: Almost all cactus plants first grew in North and South America. People brought prickly pear to India. The thorny plants of the Thar, like khejri, are not cactus plants.

## Roots
We have sorted our food by plant part, tested which parts float, and met a cactus, a lotus and a banyan. Now we ask what the float test tells us about plants, and about ourselves.

**Deep truth.** Our potato piece and dry chana sank in the bowl. They are packed with food the plant stored for later. The palak leaf floated, because it is full of tiny air spaces. A leaf takes in air to make food, so each part is filled for its job.  
*Habit it breaks:* We think of vegetables as food that is the same all through, made for us. The float test showed that each part is filled differently inside: one with stored food, another with air. What fills a part tells us the job it was doing for the plant.  
*Evidence:* In the float test, the potato and dry chana sank, and a tiny crumb of potato sank too. The brinjal piece and the palak leaf floated. Cut open, the potato was firm and packed, and the brinjal was spongy. A lotus stem slice is full of air tunnels.

- *who am i:* Your body is filled for its jobs too. Your lungs are full of tiny air spaces, and your bones are dense and strong.
- *what is life:* A plant makes food and stores some for later, in a root, a stem or a seed. When we eat it, that saved food becomes ours.
- *what is this world:* A cactus, a lotus and a banyan all have roots, stems and leaves, but each is shaped by the place they live.

Most plants start as one seed. From that one seed come roots, a stem, leaves, flowers, fruits and new seeds, each filled and shaped for a different job. The same root, stem and leaf become a water store in a cactus, an air pipe in a lotus and a pillar in a banyan. Everything is conscious, the plant too: each part senses what is around that part and sends signals to the others, so the whole plant acts together.

- **Tirukkural 595:** A lotus stalk grows as long as the water is deep. In the same way, how high a person rises depends on how strong their will is. (A lotus grows their leaf stalks long enough to reach the top of the water. The Tirukkural, from Tamil Nadu, saw that a plant's part grows to fit the place.)
- **Bhagavad Gita 5.10:** A wise person acts in the world but is not stained by it, like a lotus leaf that sits on water and stays dry. (A lotus leaf rests on the water, and water rolls off in drops. The top of the leaf is waxy, so it stays dry.)
- Science (settled): Leaves have many tiny air spaces inside. Air moves through them to the cells that make the plant's food.
- Science (debated): Whether plants feel what their parts are doing, the way we feel our hands, is still argued about.
- Other view, Many plant scientists: They explain the parts of a plant without any mind. Over a very long time, plants with parts that suited their home lived and left more seeds. They do not use feeling or deciding to explain it.
- Practice, Feel the air inside you (3 min): Sit still and put your hands on your chest. Breathe in slowly. Feel your chest get bigger as air fills the tiny spaces in your lungs. Breathe out slowly. A leaf has tiny air spaces too. Breathe in again, and think of a leaf taking in air in the sun.
- Ask: Your lungs have air spaces, and so does a leaf. What else in your body is filled for its job?
- Ask: Is a banyan's prop root still a root once it holds the tree up?

## Story: The Brinjal That Would Not Sink
*Thamarai thandu is the Tamil name for lotus stem. The Thimmamma Marrimanu banyan is in Andhra Pradesh and is one of the largest trees in the world by the area under her branches. Kavya, Paati and Arjun are made up.*

Kavya was seven, and she lived in Madurai with her grandmother. Everyone called her grandmother Paati. On Sunday mornings, Kavya helped Paati wash the vegetables for sambar.

This Sunday her cousin Arjun had come from Bengaluru. He was six. He stood on a stool at the sink, watching.

Paati filled a big steel bowl with water. Kavya dropped in the pieces of brinjal. They bobbed up and floated on top. Then she dropped in the pieces of potato. Plop, plop. They went straight to the bottom.

"The potato is heavy, so it sinks," said Arjun. "The brinjal is light, so it floats."

Kavya looked at the bowl. The biggest piece of brinjal was bigger than any piece of potato. "Let's test it," she said.

She picked out the biggest brinjal piece and pushed it down to the bottom. When she let go, it shot back up. Then she broke off a tiny crumb of potato, as small as a grain of rice. She dropped it in. It sank, slowly, all the way down.

Arjun stared. "But it's so small!"

"So it isn't about big or small," said Kavya. "It's about what is inside."

Paati smiled and cut a brinjal piece open. "Look closely," she said. Inside, the brinjal was soft and spongy, like a bath sponge. "There are tiny spaces full of air in there," Paati said. Then she cut a potato. Inside it was firm and wet and white. "A potato is packed with food the plant stored for later. There is no room for much air."

Kavya put a palak leaf on the water. It floated too. "Does a leaf have air inside?" she asked.

"Yes," said Paati. "A leaf takes in air to make the plant's food. So a leaf is full of tiny air spaces."

Next Paati took out something Arjun had never seen. It was a long, pale stick, like a thin cucumber. "This is thamarai thandu," she said. "Lotus stem." She cut a slice and held it up to the window. The slice was full of holes, in a ring like a flower.

"Why does it have holes?" asked Arjun.

Kavya thought about it. "Lotus plants grow in ponds," she said slowly. "This stem was down in the mud. Maybe the holes are for air."

"You are right," said Paati. "Mud has almost no air. These tunnels carry air down from the leaves on top of the pond. When I was a girl near Thanjavur, our temple tank was full of lotus. We children used to break a leaf stalk and look at the holes inside."

Arjun laughed. "A plant with straws inside!"

Then Arjun remembered something. "In my school there is a cactus in a pot," he said. "It has no leaves at all. Only prickles."

"The prickles are the leaves," said Kavya. She had read it in a book. "A cactus lives where it hardly rains. Thin, flat leaves would dry out. So the cactus has spines, and keeps water in a fat green stem."

"And the big tree near Kavya's school?" asked Paati. "The one with roots hanging down like ropes?"

"The banyan!" said Kavya. "Her roots grow down from the branches. When they reach the ground, they get thick, like pillars."

"In Andhra Pradesh there is a banyan called Thimmamma Marrimanu," said Paati. "She has so many pillar roots that she covers more ground than two football fields. People walk inside her like walking in a forest. And it is all one tree."

Arjun looked at the bowl again. Brinjal on top. Potato at the bottom. A lotus slice with holes. "Every vegetable is a part of a plant," he said. "And each part is different inside."

"Then let's find out which part each one is," said Kavya. And they went to look in the vegetable basket.

**For the parent:** Whether a plant part floats or sinks does not depend on its size. It depends on what is inside it: stored food, or tiny spaces full of air.

- Ask: Why did Arjun think the potato sank?
- Ask: How did Kavya test his idea?
- Ask: Why does a lotus stem have holes?
- Ask: What did Kavya say a cactus's spines are?

## Experiments
### e1 · The Float Test: which plant parts float, and which sink? (25 min)
- **question:** Which plant parts float in water, and which sink? Does a tiny piece do the same as a big piece?
- **predict:** Hold each piece in your hand. Which ones will float, and which will sink? Put them in two groups. Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** Every piece goes into the same bowl of the same tap water. We drop each piece in gently from just above the water, and wait 10 seconds before we decide. The four cut pieces (potato, carrot, brinjal, cucumber) are all about the size of your thumb, so size is not the reason one floats and another sinks. The leaf and the chana go in as they are. Then we change only the size, with one tiny piece of potato and one tiny piece of brinjal.
- **safety:** A grown-up does all the cutting.
- **record:** Draw the bowl. Draw each food at the top of the water or at the bottom, where it really went. Write F for float or S for sink next to your guess.
- **results table:** Food | Plant part | My guess: float or sink | What happened | Tiny piece: float or sink
- **s1 (4 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut one thumb-sized piece each of potato, carrot, brinjal and cucumber. 2. Lay them on a plate with a palak leaf and 5 dry chana. 3. Fill the bowl with water.  
  *Parent says:* Here are six foods. Each one is a part of a plant. Can you tell me which part each one is?  
  *Child mode:* Look at the six foods. Each one is a part of a plant. Which part is each one?
  - Ask: Which part of the plant is the carrot? And the chana? (good: The carrot is a root. It grew in the soil.; The chana is a seed. It can grow into a new plant.)
  - Note: Accept any answer now. The vegetable basket sort and the card game explain the parts. Potato is a stem that grows under the ground, which surprises most people.
- **s2 (4 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Let your child hold each piece. 2. Ask them to put the pieces in two groups: will float, will sink. 3. Write down their groups and their reason, word for word.  
  *Parent says:* Hold each piece in your hand. Which ones will float, and which will sink? Make two groups. Why do you think so?  
  *Child mode:* Hold each piece. Will it float or sink? Make two groups. Say why.
  - Ask: Why did you put that one in the sink group? (good: It feels heavy.; It is hard.; Seeds are small, so I think they float.)
  - Note: Most children say heavy or hard things sink. Do not say if they are right. The test will answer it.
- **s3 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child drops each piece in gently, one at a time, from just above the water. 2. Count to 10 together after each one. 3. Your child says float or sink, and you write it in the table.  
  *Parent says:* Drop the first piece in gently. Let's count to ten. Is it on top, or at the bottom?  
  *Child mode:* Drop one piece in gently. Count to ten. Is it on top or at the bottom?
  - Note: Usually the brinjal, cucumber and palak leaf float. The potato and dry chana sink. Most carrots sink, but a few float. Write down what yours really did. If a piece floats with a bubble stuck to it, push it under once and let go.
- **s4 (4 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. A grown-up breaks off a crumb of potato, as small as a grain of rice. 2. Cut a tiny piece of brinjal the same size. 3. Ask your child to guess first. 4. Drop both in.  
  *Parent says:* Some people say small things float and big things sink. Here is a tiny bit of potato. Will it float or sink? Why?  
  *Child mode:* Here is a tiny bit of potato. Will it float or sink? Say why. Now drop it in.
  - Ask: The tiny potato sank, and the big brinjal floated. So is it about size? (good: No. The tiny potato still sank.; It is what is inside that decides.)
  - Check: A tiny crumb of potato sank. A big piece of brinjal floated. What does this show? → **Size is not what matters. What is inside matters.**
- **s5 (5 min) · grown-up needed**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Cut a brinjal piece and a potato piece in half. 2. Let your child look closely and press each one with a finger. 3. Ask what is different inside.  
  *Parent says:* Look inside the brinjal and the potato. Press each one. What is different?  
  *Child mode:* Look inside the brinjal and the potato. Press each one. What is different?
  - Ask: What is the brinjal like inside? (good: Soft and spongy.; It has tiny holes.; It squashes.)
  - Ask: What is the potato like inside? (good: Hard and firm.; Packed tight.; No holes.)

- **s6 (3 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Ask your child to say what they found in three parts: what I think, what I saw, and why. 2. Write it at the bottom of the table.

  *Child mode:* Say what you found. Which floated? Which sank? Why?

### e2 · Do Seeds Drink? Ten chana in water overnight (20 min, 2 days)
- **question:** Does a dry seed take in water? How much bigger does a line of 10 chana get after a night in water?
- **predict:** Look at the dry chana. After a night in water, will the line of 10 chana be shorter, the same, or longer? Why do you think so?
- **fair test:** We use the same 10 chana before and after. We line them up the same way each time, touching end to end along the ruler. We keep 10 more dry chana in a dry cup, so we can compare.
- **record:** Draw a dry chana and a soaked chana side by side, the real size. Write the length of the line before and after.
- **results table:** Chana | Length of the line before (cm) | Length of the line after one night (cm) | How much longer (cm) | Hard or soft?
- **schedule:** day 1: Line up 10 dry chana along the ruler and measure. Put them in water. Put 10 more chana in a dry cup. (10 min); day 2: Line up the 10 soaked chana again and measure. Compare them with the dry ones. Press one of each. (10 min)
- **s1 (5 min)**  
  *Parent does:* 1. Your child lines up 10 dry chana touching end to end along the ruler. 2. Read the length together and write it down. 3. Ask the question below.  
  *Parent says:* Here is a line of 10 dry chana. Tomorrow, after a night in water, will the line be shorter, the same, or longer? Why?

## Applications
- Cooks soak dal and chana before cooking, because dry seeds take in water and get soft.
- Gardeners grow new money plants, tulsi and mint by putting cut stems in water until roots grow.
- Lotus stem is cooked in many parts of India: thamarai thandu in Tamil Nadu, kamal kakdi in much of North India, nadru in Kashmir.
- Makhana, the puffed snack from Bihar, is the seed of the fox-nut plant, a kind of water lily that grows in ponds.

## Open questions
- **How does a banyan's hanging root know which way is down, and when it has reached the soil?** Scientists know roots sense gravity with tiny heavy grains in their tips. How a hanging root then changes into a thick pillar once it touches the ground is still being studied.
- **Exactly how does a lotus push air down to the stem in the mud?** Scientists have found that warm leaves push air down the stalks. Just how much each part of the leaf does, and how the flow changes from day to night, is still being studied.

- In the float test, 3 foods floated and 3 foods sank. How many foods did you test? **6 foods.**. 3 that floated and 3 that sank. 3 + 3 = 6. (queues `math.addition-subtraction.meaning.L2`)
- A line of 10 dry chana was 9 cm long. After a night in water, the line was 12 cm long. How much longer did it get? **3 cm longer.**. Count on from 9 to 12: 10, 11, 12. That is 3 steps, so the line got 3 cm longer. (queues `math.length.standard-units-length.L2`)
