# Card: explanations, perspectives, agreed, challenging, big and open questions

Read `app/content/WRITER_BRIEF.md` first. This card covers `explanations`, `agreed`, `challenging`, `big_questions`, `open_questions`, and `terms`. These carry the founder's principles most.

## What they hold
- **explanations:** `{concept, status, definition, description, examples[], perspectives:[{who, explanation}], art, context}`. `status`: `defined` (an agreed definition exists), `idea` (we only have ideas: say so), `debated` (give every explanation in `perspectives`).
- **agreed:** `{text, evidence}`: what most scientists agree on, and how we know. The app folds each statement into the explanation it matches as its "how we know" line, so write only NEW things here, with the evidence. Never restate an explanation in other words (the checker warns when half or more repeat).
- **challenging:** `{claim, who, for, against, status, context}`. `status`: `accepted`, `debated`, `minority`, `rejected`.
- **big_questions:** `{q, kind, hint}`. `kind` is `deep` (down into the idea) or `big` (out to life and the universe). Open-ended.
- **open_questions:** `{q, why_open}`: what nobody knows yet.
- **terms:** `{term, meaning, example, art}`. The word, in plain words, with an everyday example.

## Rules
- **No idea held back for age.** The big idea of the lesson appears at Level 1 too: told with fewer words and more things to touch, not left out. Never "too advanced", "when you are older".
- **Truthful.** Never teach something wrong to simplify. Not "five senses" or "six senses" (thousands of kinds of sensors: balance, body position, temperature, chemical sensing). Not "just a theory". When you must simplify, say "Here is what we know so far", and say what is missing.
- **Look again at every explanation, then write `perspectives` or a `one_view` note, never neither.** Use the "Look again" questions in the brief (scale and senses, scientists, other traditions, other beings, time). A plain statement like "a wall does not move" needs the closer look (its atoms never stop moving). Never invent a view to fill a count. Each perspective has a real `who` (a scientist, a tradition, a way of thinking) and a plain `explanation`. Give the strongest version of each, not a straw man. A `debated` card needs 2 or more.
- **Do not present one theory as the truth.** Say what the evidence is, what it cannot show, and who disagrees.
- **Open debates, including among scientists.** `challenging` has at least 2 views, with at least one still `debated` or `minority`. Give evidence `for` and `against`, fairly. A rejected claim is named with why it was given up.
- **Questions that let the child form their own view.** `big_questions`: 1 to 10, more is better and each unique, both kinds, each ends with "?" and has a `hint` that helps a child start thinking and never gives the answer. `open_questions`: 3 or more, each says why it is still open.
- **Lead with the point.** The first sentence says what the card is. Explain every science word once, in plain words, then use the right word (magnets attract and repel, not "pull").
- **The explanation comes after the child's guess and result,** never before.
- **Every card carries its own `context` line:** what it is and why it is here.
- **Stance as fact, then the other view.** "Plants feel, remember and decide. Some scientists call this an automatic response." Never "DeepKids believes".
- **Never deny a mind.** No "can't think", no "no brain" to mean "cannot think".
- **Humans are animals.** "Humans and other animals." No being is lower or simple.
- **Women and underdogs are named** where they took part, and the real history stays exact.
- **Show it.** If an idea cannot be seen (atoms, tides), give an `art` brief for a zoom-in picture.

## Common mistakes
- A `perspectives` list left empty on every card, when some of the ideas really are argued about. Or a made-up "view" added only to fill the list.
- A perspective with `who: "Some people"` and a vague view.
- `challenging` that only lists wrong ideas, never a live debate.
- A `big_question` that a yes or no answers, or a hint that gives the answer.
- A "simple" version that is false, then "actually…" lower down.
- Poetry in an explanation ("leaning on the air").

## What the validator checks
`status` is defined/idea/debated; `perspectives` is a list; `debated` needs 2 or more; `defined` needs a `definition`; `examples` present; `challenging` status valid; at least 1 `big_questions` (a warning below 4 or above 10), each `deep` or `big`; (to do) at least 2 explanations with 2 or more perspectives; 2 or more challenging views each with `who`, `for`, `against`; at least one debated or minority view; 3 or more open questions with `why_open`; both big-question kinds, a `hint`, and a "?"; a `context` line on explanations and challenging cards; errors for "five senses", "six senses", "just a theory", and age-gating phrases; "this unit"/"this topic" in family text (to do); "humans and animals" (to do); denying a mind (to do).
