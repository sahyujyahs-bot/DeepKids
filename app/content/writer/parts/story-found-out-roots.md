# Card: story, how we found out, roots

Read `app/content/WRITER_BRIEF.md` first. Roots detail: `app/content/PHILOSOPHY.md`.

## What they hold
- **story:** `{title, text[], epiphany, ask[], art[], read_aloud: true}`. 450 to 1400 words. The app reads it aloud.
- **how_we_found_out:** `{story, who, when, wrong_idea}`. 80 to 200 words of how people really found this out, including an idea people once held that turned out wrong and how it was shown wrong.
- **roots:** `{context, deep_truth:{truth, habit, evidence}, three:{who_am_i, what_is_life, what_is_this_world}, thread, teachings[], science[], other_view, practice, ask[], art}`.

## Rules: story
- A picture-phrase is allowed here, and only after the plain idea is clear. The science in it is true.
- Made-up people: about 80% women and girls, about 60% South Indian names, places and foods. When someone teaches someone, a woman or girl teaches a man or boy.
- Living beings are she, he or they, never "it".
- It ends with a question (`ask`), not a moral. Meaning comes out of the science, never stuck on.
- The story names every character the first time. Questions elsewhere never ask about the story.
- Do not hold the big idea back for age: the story of a Level 1 lesson still meets it, in a child's words.

## Rules: how we found out
- **Real people and real dates only.** Verify each one. If you are not sure, leave it out.
- Say the **wrong idea** people once held, why it was believable (it fitted what they saw), and how it was shown wrong.
- Name the women and underdogs the usual story leaves out, where the evidence supports it. Do not invent a credit.
- If the history itself is disputed, say "historians disagree" and give both views.

## Rules: roots
- **One deep truth that only this lesson's science could give.** Test: could you write it without knowing what this lesson teaches? Then rewrite it. It does not repeat any other lesson's deep truth, it is not a slogan, and it is 3 to 5 short sentences of under 25 words each that an eight-year-old can understand.
- `habit`: the exact label or split it questions, and why. `evidence`: the finding from this lesson that points to it.
- **Never deny a mind.** Our stance is stated as fact, then the other view. Never "DeepKids believes".
- **2 or 3 teachings** from named sources (the text and the place: Chandogya Upanishad 6.13, Tao Te Ching 42). If you are not sure of the source, leave it out. Draw from many traditions, not one.
- **Always one honest other view** (Charvaka, the Buddha's anatta, Dvaita, physicalism, a neuroscientist), said fairly.
- **Science marked** `settled`, `idea`, `debated` or `open`. Quantum physics never "proves" consciousness: say "observer" means a detector.
- **A short practice** for the body (breath, silence, a way of looking), with `say` and `kid`.
- **2 to 4 open questions** at the end (`ask`). Never answered for the child.
- No religion as an identity: no prayers, rituals or "our god".

## Common mistakes
- A deep truth that fits any lesson ("everything is connected").
- Roots that preach a conclusion instead of asking.
- A story where a boy teaches a girl, or a character who is only there to carry a moral.
- A `how_we_found_out` with no wrong idea, or one over 200 words.
- A quote or source you cannot check.

## What the validator checks
Story words 450 to 1400 and `title`, `epiphany`, `ask`, `art`; `how_we_found_out` has `story`, `who`, `when`, `wrong_idea` and 80 to 200 words; roots has every field, `deep_truth` has truth, habit, evidence, with a deep truth that is not generic and does not repeat another lesson's, up to 5 sentences of under 25 words; 2 to 3 teachings with source, teaching, link; science status valid; practice has title, mins, say, kid; 2 to 4 `ask`; "DeepKids believes" and similar; denying a mind (to do); "it" for a living being (to do); stock lines.
