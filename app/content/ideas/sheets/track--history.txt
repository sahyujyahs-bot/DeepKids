R22: 0) Fresh digs & rewrites (last few years)
R23: Ancient DNA & South Asia.
R24: • A Harappan genome from Rakhigarhi showed no Steppe pastoralist ancestry in that individual; big for timing debates on Indo-Aryan languages (2019). Cell
R25: • Broader aDNA work (523 ancient genomes) mapped South Asian ancestry as gradients of Iranian-related + Southeast Asian–related hunter-gatherers, with Steppe-related ancestry arriving after the Indus decline. This reframed population history and language-spread hypotheses (2019). PMC+1
R26: Sinauli “chariots” (UP). Bronze-Age burials with copper-covered carts (c. 1850–1550 BCE). Scholars dispute whether they’re true horse-drawn chariots or ox-carts. Treat as provisional. Wikipedia
R27: Keeladi/Keeladi–Kondagai (TN). Urban features and Tamil-Brahmi material; radiocarbon points to a 6th century BCE horizon, though the earliest script dating is debated and some contexts are mixed. (New reporting 2025; scholarly caution remains.) The Economic Times+1
R28: Possible Saraswati palaeo-channel (Rajasthan, 2024–25). ASI reported a 23 m-deep palaeochannel under Bahaj village; potentially big if confirmed—but still early days. The Times of India
R29: Gandhāran birch-bark scrolls (1st–3rd c. CE). The 1990s–present decipherment of the oldest Buddhist manuscripts continues to reshape how and where Buddhism spread (Gāndhārī/Kharoṣṭhī). Wikipedia+1
R30: Global benchmarkers (context).
R31: • Oldest known wooden structure, Zambia ~476,000 years (2023): pushes hominin tech back—affects deep prehistory narratives. Nature+1
R32: • Lidar mega-surveys keep rewriting Mesoamerican urbanism (2023–25).
R34: Indian Subcontinent History
R35: 1) Indian & subcontinental history (wide + deep, with fault lines noted)
R36: A. Deep Prehistory → Neolithic
R37: Earliest humans in South Asia; microlithic traditions; domestication packages (millets, rice). On rice: indica domestication involves introgression and hybrid histories—don’t oversimplify. PNAS+1
R38: B. Indus/Harappan Civilization (c. 2600–1900 BCE core)
R39: Urbanism (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira), water systems, standardized weights, trade to Oman/Mesopotamia.
R40: Script undeciphered; Dravidian/other hypotheses exist.
R41: aDNA: Rakhigarhi individual points to no Steppe ancestry in the urban phase; later Steppe ancestry enters post-IVC. Cell+1
R42: C. Post-Harappan & Early Vedic / Iron Age (c. 1900–600 BCE)
R43: Regional cultures (PGW, NBPW), early iron, chiefdoms → mahājanapadas.
R44: Sinauli material (see above) belongs around Late Harappan/Ochre-Coloured Pottery/Copper Hoard contexts; avoid over-reading. Wikipedia
R45: D. Urban South of the Vindhyas early horizon
R46: Keeladi/Keeladi–Kondagai complex: craft, brick structures, water works; claims of early Tamil-Brahmi use before 3rd c. BCE are contested; settle on “6th c. BCE urban horizon; earliest writing remains debated.” The Economic Times+1
R47: E. Imperial formations & cross-roads (c. 6th–2nd c. BCE)
R48: Achaemenid satrapies in NW; Aramaic administration intersects with Indic worlds. Kharosthi script is widely held to derive from Imperial Aramaic under the Achaemenids. Wikipedia
R49: Mauryan Empire; Ashoka’s edicts in Prakrit, Greek, Aramaic (Kandahar bilingual & Greek edicts) demonstrate direct messaging to Hellenic audiences. edizionicafoscari.unive.it+1
R50: F. Hellenistic, Indo-Greek, Saka, Kushan interfaces (2nd c. BCE–3rd c. CE)
R51: Taxila/Gandhara as hubs; Greco-Buddhist art fuses Hellenistic forms with Buddhist iconography; Kushan coinage, Buddhist patronage. Encyclopedia Britannica+1
R52: Gandhāran manuscripts (see above) anchor early Buddhist textuality in the NW. Wikipedia
R53: G. Early Historic trade webs (1st–3rd c. CE)
R54: Indo-Roman trade via Red Sea ports; Muziris Papyrus (Greek contract) evidences pepper/ivory shipments and legal frameworks; Berenike yields 7.5 kg pepper from India in a single jar. Oxford Academic+1
