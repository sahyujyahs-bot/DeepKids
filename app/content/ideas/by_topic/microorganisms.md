### The First Fungi
Quickly following the red algae, the Fungi evolved from eukaryotes with only mitochondria.

The parent eukaryote with mitochondria had innovated food & primitive internal digestion. But its
first child, the fungi, did not opt for that route. Instead, they chose the more historic route of
external digestion by secreting enzymes & breakdown molecules outside the body & absorbing
nutrients.
But why? Because their aspirations were greater than their parents. Internal digestion means a
defined body shape that can house the gut. And digestion would have to happen per meal.

Instead, they developed a whole new way to live & grow - A fungi will start extending on one
side like a tip, a tail, only adding some cell wall. And slowly the tip grows and the fungo mother
will divide her nucleus and give one to the hyphae. Now the hyphae has its own DNA, but
remains forever attached to the mother. This continues. New hyphaes get added like chains,
each having its own nucleus, but all hyphaes are still attached to the mother. They could cover
hundreds of acres this way.

They also highways and transporter proteins to carry the nutrient absorbed in the hypahes to
other older interior cells of fungi.
For internal communication between the cells, they used the same well established chemical
pathways, ion fluctuations and electrical impulses. The same fundamentals that happen within
neurons - tunnels that animals built for internal communication.

When the resources get scarce, the chemical signals activate the genes for reproduction & the
hyphaes pause focussing on growth and focus on reproduction , making spores - tiny cells
packed with their dna & eject into the world, for them to find homes elsewhere.

They, like the aerobic bacteria, and eukaryotes with mitochondria, needed Oxygen to produce
energy and continued consuming them from around them releasing CO2

Fungi - the scavengers with decentralized intelligence , covering acres, responding subjectively
to stimuli across distances. Not only did they cover the soils under the ocean, they also broke
down the quickly gathering bio waste & made more food available for other organisms that
came before & as you will see in future chapters, made life possible on land too.

### The First Animal - Sponge
Soon after the neither plant nor an animal, fungi, came the first animals - sponges.
They were multi-cellular & visible to the eye.
 Intercellular communication that coordinates the whole sponge - cells use chemical signals and
calcium wave propagation to respond collectively
can perform simple reflexes like contracting the body when irritated.
They innovated a new respiratory system by pumping water in through its pores, bathing every
cell with water, so the cells could absorb the free oxygen floating in the water.

The sponges like their eukaryote parents could engulf prey, but didn’t have the internal digestion
engine , instead they used a filtration system, where they collected all the food molecules ,
convert them to nutrients & transport the nutrients to all the cells.

Like all their eukaryotic ancestors, they could reproduce asexually - by cell division, and
sexually - by producing sperm and eggs. Fertilization happened inside the mother - 2nd species
to do so, after the red algaes.
They still did not have specific gender & were hermaphrodites, holding genes to both producing
eggs and sperms, and activated either gene at different times based on resources available,
energy , competition etc

The whole sponge body can also slowly contract by coordinating special contractile cells.
Though extremely slow and limited, this is the first appearance of multicellular body contractions
without true muscle tissue.

They also evolved silica or calcium carbonate spikes and collagen to retain its shape & rigidity.
They had basic immune systems, ways to recognize what was self and foreign, and could reject
cells of another sponge if they entered their system - the biological “sense of self”.

They also had sensors that could detect light & dark - sponges contract in bright light, sensors
that could detect toxins, changes in chemicals, flow rate and much more - all these inherited
from its parent & grandparent species.

They are the first animal, but quite distinct from the rest of the animals to come . One of a kind.

"The History of Evolution of Life" Coloring Book

This coloring book is a carefully researched journey into the fascinating story of life on Earth, based on the best scientific knowledge we have today.
However, there are a few important points to keep in mind as we explore:

1. Evolution is an ongoing discovery.
Evolution is not an exact science; it is a well-supported framework based on evidence we’ve uncovered so far. Our understanding may change as new discoveries are made, and some current beliefs could be revised in the future.

2. Some mysteries of evolution remain unsolved. Even solved mysteries are not set in stone.
No step in evolution is fully understood, and theycontinue to remain subjects of debate, even among scientists. For instance, it's widely believed that life began with RNA molecules, precursors to DNA, but this is not universally agreed upon, and definitive proof is still elusive. So consume the information with an open mind, and attempt to delve further into the questions by reading different perspectives.

3. Evolution is not a straight line.
Contrary to popular belief, evolution is not a simple progression from unicellular organisms to mammals and humans. It is a branching, ever-changing tree where every species, including the earliest unicellular prokaryotes, continues to evolve in its own unique way. We are finding new evidences everyday, that refute earlier understanding of the different aspects of evolution.

## SCI Cards: scientists for this topic (founder's card game; strongest underdog stories first)
- **Carlos Juan Finlay** (3 Dec 1833–1915-08-20, Cuban; Heretic, Ahead of Time, IP Theft Victim, Underdog, Invisible Giant): Proposed in 1881 that yellow fever is transmitted by mosquitoes (Aedes aegypti) — 20 years before it was accepted. His theory was ridiculed for two decades. Walter Reed's team later confirmed it (1900) using Finlay's mosquito samples and methodology — and Reed got most of the credit. *Whisper:* The deadliest killer in a tropical city might be a tiny mosquito, not dirty water or bad air. Sometimes the vector of death is the thing you barely notice. *Story:* For 20 years, Finlay presented his mosquito theory at medical conferences. He was laughed at — the 'mosquito man' from Cuba. In 1900, Walter Reed's US Army Yellow Fever Commission came to Cuba, used Finlay's mosquito cultures, his research protocols, and his theory — and confirmed he was right. Reed initially credited Finlay, but over time, American institutions claimed Reed as the hero. Finlay was nominated for the Nobel Prize seven times but never won. Cuba recognizes him; the US largely doesn't.
- **Esther Lederberg** (1922-12-18–2006-11-11, American; IP Theft Victim, Invisible Giant, Foundational, Underdog): Discovered lambda bacteriophage (a virus that infects bacteria) — fundamental to molecular biology and genetic engineering. Co-invented replica plating — a technique for studying bacterial genetics that became essential to all microbiology. Her husband Joshua Lederberg won the 1958 Nobel Prize partly for work she contributed to; she was not included. *Whisper:* Viruses can hide inside bacteria, dormant, waiting — and this discovery became one of the most powerful tools in genetic engineering. The woman who found it watched her husband collect the Nobel Prize. *Story:* Esther Lederberg grew up in the Bronx during the Depression, so poor she was malnourished. She discovered lambda phage and co-developed replica plating — both foundational techniques in molecular biology. Her husband Joshua Lederberg won the 1958 Nobel Prize for work on bacterial genetics that included her contributions. She was not nominated. After their divorce, she was denied tenure at Stanford while he held a named chair. She spent decades in the shadow of her ex-husband's prize.
- **Katalin Karikó** (1955-01-17–Living, Hungarian-American; Underdog, World Changer, Ahead of Time, Heretic, Applied): Developed the modified mRNA technology that made COVID-19 vaccines (Pfizer/BioNTech and Moderna) possible. Discovered that modifying one nucleotide in synthetic mRNA prevents the immune system from destroying it — allowing it to deliver instructions to cells. This breakthrough took decades and was repeatedly rejected and defunded. *Whisper:* You can teach the body to fight a virus it has never seen — by sending it the instruction manual in mRNA. One chemical tweak, decades in the making, saved millions of lives. *Story:* For over 20 years, Karikó's mRNA research was rejected by funders, dismissed by colleagues, and repeatedly defunded. She was demoted at the University of Pennsylvania. Her grant applications were rejected so often that she was nearly fired. She kept going. In 2005, she and Drew Weissman discovered that swapping one nucleotide (uridine → pseudouridine) in synthetic mRNA prevented the immune system from destroying it. This was the key breakthrough. When COVID-19 arrived in 2020, her technology became the basis of the fastest vaccine development in history. Billions of doses were administered worldwide. She went from near-obscurity to Nobel laureate in 18 years.
- **Barry Marshall** (1951-09-30–Living, Australian; Heretic, World Changer, Underdog, Foundational): Proved that stomach ulcers are caused by the bacterium Helicobacter pylori — NOT by stress or spicy food. When no one believed him, he drank a beaker of H. pylori bacteria, developed gastritis, then cured himself with antibiotics. Overturned decades of medical dogma. *Whisper:* Stomach ulcers are an infection, not a personality flaw. For decades, doctors blamed patients' stress and diet. A bacterium was the real cause — and the cure is antibiotics, not antacids. *Story:* Marshall and pathologist Robin Warren found H. pylori in ulcer patients. The medical establishment refused to believe bacteria could survive in stomach acid. Drug companies selling antacids didn't want a cheap antibiotic cure. When no journal would publish him and no ethics board would let him experiment on humans, Marshall drank the bacteria himself, got sick, biopsied his own stomach, found H. pylori, and cured himself with antibiotics. He was 32.
- **Lynn Margulis** (1938-03-05–2011-11-22, American; Heretic, World Changer, Foundational, Controversial, Ahead of Time): Endosymbiotic Theory — proposed that mitochondria (cell powerhouses) and chloroplasts (photosynthesis units) were once free-living bacteria that were swallowed by larger cells and became permanent partners. This means every cell in your body is a collaboration between different life forms. Her paper was rejected 15 times before publication. *Whisper:* You are not one organism — you are a collaboration. Every cell in your body contains the descendants of ancient bacteria that merged with your ancestors billions of years ago. Life evolves through cooperation, not just competition. *Story:* Her 1967 paper was rejected by 15 journals before the Journal of Theoretical Biology published it. The idea that organelles were once independent bacteria was considered absurd. She was mocked, dismissed, and called a radical. DNA evidence eventually proved her right — mitochondria DO have their own DNA, separate from the cell's nucleus, confirming their bacterial origin. She challenged the Neo-Darwinian focus on competition, arguing that symbiosis (cooperation between species) is a primary driver of evolution.
- **Gagandeep Kang** (1962-11-03–Living, Indian; World Changer, Applied, Underdog, Foundational): First Indian woman elected Fellow of the Royal Society (science category). Led the clinical development of India's first indigenous rotavirus vaccine (Rotavac) — now protecting millions of Indian children from deadly diarrheal disease. Research on gut infections, nutrition, and the oral polio vaccine's effectiveness in Indian conditions. *Whisper:* A vaccine designed for rich countries might not work the same way in poor ones — different gut bacteria, different nutrition, different water. You have to study the specific population you're trying to protect. *Story:* Kang spent decades studying why oral vaccines (like polio) worked less effectively in Indian children than in Western ones — discovering that gut infections and malnutrition altered the immune response. This wasn't a failure of the vaccine; it was a failure to understand the context. She helped develop Rotavac — an affordable rotavirus vaccine made in India, for Indian conditions, at a fraction of the cost of imported alternatives. It's now in India's national immunization program, protecting millions of children from a disease that kills hundreds of thousands annually.
- **Emmanuelle Charpentier & Jennifer Doudna** (11 Dec 1968 / 19 Feb 1964–Both Living, French / American; World Changer, Foundational, Ahead of Time, Forbidden Knowledge): Developed CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing — a precise, cheap, and easy tool for cutting and editing DNA in any organism. CRISPR lets scientists edit the genome like editing a typo in a document. It has revolutionized biology, medicine, agriculture, and raises profound ethical questions about editing human embryos. *Whisper:* Life now comes with an edit button. For the first time in 4 billion years, a species can precisely rewrite the code that defines all living things — including itself. *Story:* Bacteria have an ancient immune system: when a virus attacks, they save a snippet of the virus's DNA and use it to recognize and cut the virus if it returns. Charpentier and Doudna figured out how this system (CRISPR-Cas9) works and showed that it could be reprogrammed to cut ANY DNA sequence — in any organism. Suddenly, editing genes became as accessible as using a word processor. Sickle cell disease can be cured. Crops can be made drought-resistant. Mosquitoes can be engineered to not carry malaria. But the same tool could edit human embryos, raising questions about designer babies that society hasn't resolved. They are the first all-female team to share a science Nobel.
