## SCI Cards: scientists for this topic (founder's card game; strongest underdog stories first)
- **Prafulla Chandra Ray** (2 Aug 1861–1944-06-16, Indian (Bengal); Underdog, Foundational, Applied, Invisible Giant): Discovered mercurous nitrite and its derivatives (1896) — the first chemical compound discovered in India. Founded Bengal Chemical & Pharmaceutical Works (1901) — India's first pharmaceutical company. Wrote 'A History of Hindu Chemistry' — documenting India's suppressed chemical heritage. Pioneer of chemical education in India. *Whisper:* India had a chemical tradition stretching back millennia — zinc smelting, steel making, dyes, medicines — but colonial education erased it. Recovering your own history IS a scientific act. *Story:* Born in Bengal under British rule, Ray faced the systematic dismissal of Indian scientific achievement. He responded by doing two things: world-class original chemistry (mercurous nitrite research) AND historical scholarship documenting India's ancient chemical traditions — metallurgy, alchemy, dyes, and medicines that Europe had either borrowed or suppressed. He founded India's first pharmaceutical company and donated almost all his wealth to education. He returned his knighthood after the British massacre at Jallianwala Bagh.
- **Gagandeep Kang** (1962-11-03–Living, Indian; World Changer, Applied, Underdog, Foundational): First Indian woman elected Fellow of the Royal Society (science category). Led the clinical development of India's first indigenous rotavirus vaccine (Rotavac) — now protecting millions of Indian children from deadly diarrheal disease. Research on gut infections, nutrition, and the oral polio vaccine's effectiveness in Indian conditions. *Whisper:* A vaccine designed for rich countries might not work the same way in poor ones — different gut bacteria, different nutrition, different water. You have to study the specific population you're trying to protect. *Story:* Kang spent decades studying why oral vaccines (like polio) worked less effectively in Indian children than in Western ones — discovering that gut infections and malnutrition altered the immune response. This wasn't a failure of the vaccine; it was a failure to understand the context. She helped develop Rotavac — an affordable rotavirus vaccine made in India, for Indian conditions, at a fraction of the cost of imported alternatives. It's now in India's national immunization program, protecting millions of children from a disease that kills hundreds of thousands annually.
- **Charles Darwin** (12 Feb 1809–19 Apr 1882, English; World Changer, Heretic, Foundational, Controversial): Theory of Evolution by Natural Selection — species evolve over time through variation, inheritance, and differential survival. Published 'On the Origin of Species' (1859). Also: theory of coral reef formation, extensive work on barnacles and earthworms. *Whisper:* You are not separate from nature — you are nature. Every living thing on Earth is your relative. Complexity doesn't require a designer; it emerges from simple rules repeated over deep time. *Story:* Studied to be a clergyman before joining the Beagle voyage at 22. Spent 20 years sitting on his theory, terrified of the backlash. Only published when Alfred Russel Wallace independently arrived at the same idea. Was mocked, attacked by the Church, and caricatured as an ape. Suffered chronic illness for decades (possibly Chagas disease from a bug bite in South America).
- **Tycho Brahe** (14 Dec 1546–24 Oct 1601, Danish; Foundational, Controversial): Most accurate naked-eye astronomical observations in history. His precision planetary data (especially of Mars) became the foundation Kepler used to derive his laws of motion. *Whisper:* Sometimes the greatest contribution isn't the answer — it's gathering the evidence so precisely that the right answer becomes inevitable for someone else. *Story:* A Danish nobleman who lost part of his nose in a duel and wore a metal prosthetic (possibly Saturn or brass). He built a castle-observatory on an island, kept a pet moose (which reportedly died falling down stairs after drinking beer), and employed a psychic dwarf named Jepp. He died mysteriously after a banquet — modern theories range from mercury poisoning to a burst bladder.
