## Concept activities: Gravitational bending of light (general relativity)
- **about-the-concept:** Massive bodies curve spacetime, bending the path of light—observed during eclipses and around galaxies.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Einstein’s general relativity; gravitational lensing; deflection of starlight.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Gravity reveals that even light follows the fabric of space; mass and energy shape reality itself.
- **experiments:** Recreate lensing with a wine-glass base to bend light from a candle; place a clear marble over a page to mimic gravitational lens; use an online simulator of gravitational lensing.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Use magnifying glass to distort a printed image and compare to real lensing; photograph water-filled glass bending background lines; watch videos of Einstein’s 1919 eclipse experiment.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Observe a total or partial solar eclipse when available; look for mirage-like distortions over hot roads; visit a planetarium for a lensing demonstration.
- **dance:** Dance where dancers warp a stretchy fabric while a beam of light (flashlight) moves across; group choreography showing spacetime ripples.
- **drama:** Skit of light trying to escape a massive star and being gently curved; drama of astronomers proving Einstein right in 1919; mime of space being stretched.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint cosmic arcs like those seen in Hubble lensing photos; draw spacetime grid bending around a planet; create abstract art of warped light.
- **music:** Compose music starting with straight melody lines that bend into glissandos; create a soundscape of slowly warping tones; improvise a “space ripple” piece.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice slow breathing while visualising space curving; meditate on the pull of gravity on light; alternate heavy grounding poses with light floating poses.
- **physical_games:** “Gravity tag”: players pull on a stretchy cloth creating curves others must follow; relay race where path must bend around obstacles representing mass.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Board game where players place “mass tiles” that bend light-path marbles; quiz on Einstein’s predictions; card-matching gravitational lens images to sources.
- **reading:** Relativity for Kids by Jerome Pohlen; NASA resources on gravitational lensing; popular science articles on Einstein’s eclipse proof.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem from a photon’s perspective as it bends around a star; story of the 1919 eclipse expedition; journal of night-sky observations.
- **more:** Build a DIY lensing demo with a glass sphere; photograph star trails distorted by heat haze as an analogy; explore online simulations of gravitational lensing.
- **other:** Visit a science museum relativity exhibit; attend an astronomy club star party; watch a documentary on Einstein’s general relativity.

- **Emmanuelle Charpentier & Jennifer Doudna** (11 Dec 1968 / 19 Feb 1964–Both Living, French / American; World Changer, Foundational, Ahead of Time, Forbidden Knowledge): Developed CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing — a precise, cheap, and easy tool for cutting and editing DNA in any organism. CRISPR lets scientists edit the genome like editing a typo in a document. It has revolutionized biology, medicine, agriculture, and raises profound ethical questions about editing human embryos. *Whisper:* Life now comes with an edit button. For the first time in 4 billion years, a species can precisely rewrite the code that defines all living things — including itself. *Story:* Bacteria have an ancient immune system: when a virus attacks, they save a snippet of the virus's DNA and use it to recognize and cut the virus if it returns. Charpentier and Doudna figured out how this system (CRISPR-Cas9) works and showed that it could be reprogrammed to cut ANY DNA sequence — in any organism. Suddenly, editing genes became as accessible as using a word processor. Sickle cell disease can be cured. Crops can be made drought-resistant. Mosquitoes can be engineered to not carry malaria. But the same tool could edit human embryos, raising questions about designer babies that society hasn't resolved. They are the first all-female team to share a science Nobel.
