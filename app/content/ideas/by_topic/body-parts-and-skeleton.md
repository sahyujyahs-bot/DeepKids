## Concept activities: Homeostasis
- **about:** Your body’s balance-keeper. It keeps temperature, water, and energy just right so you can play, think, and sleep.
- **principles:** (1) Feedback loops (when too hot → cool down). (2) Set points (like a comfy room temp). (3) Sensors–controllers–effectors (skin → brain → sweat glands).
- **philosophy:** Balance is not stillness; it’s gentle adjusting like a surfer staying on a wave. We learn to notice signals and respond kindly to the body.

## Evolution picture book: introduction
"The History of Evolution of Life" Coloring Book

This coloring book is a carefully researched journey into the fascinating story of life on Earth, based on the best scientific knowledge we have today.
However, there are a few important points to keep in mind as we explore:

1. Evolution is an ongoing discovery.
Evolution is not an exact science; it is a well-supported framework based on evidence we’ve uncovered so far. Our understanding may change as new discoveries are made, and some current beliefs could be revised in the future.

2. Some mysteries of evolution remain unsolved. Even solved mysteries are not set in stone.
No step in evolution is fully understood, and theycontinue to remain subjects of debate, even among scientists. For instance, it's widely believed that life began with RNA molecules, precursors to DNA, but this is not universally agreed upon, and definitive proof is still elusive. Did living cells ccome first or the undead virus come first? debated. We believe Sponges were the first ever animal. But there is also a group that debates comb jellies came first. We don't know definitive answers to any question in evolution yet. About every step of evolvution of life that we have pieced together so far are still heavily debated in the scientific community. So consume the information with an open mind, and attempt to delve further into the questions by reading different perspectives.

3. Evolution is not a straight line.
Contrary to popular belief, evolution is not a simple progression from unicellular organisms to mammals and humans. It is a branching, ever-changing tree where every species, including the earliest unicellular prokaryotes, continues to evolve in its own unique way. We are finding new evidences everyday, that refute earlier understanding of the different aspects of evolution.

With these points in mind, let’s dive into the incredible history of life and explore the wonders of evolution together!

- **Mary Anning** (21 May 1799–9 Mar 1847, English; Underdog, Self-Taught, Invisible Giant, Foundational): Discovered the first complete Ichthyosaur skeleton (age 12), first complete Plesiosaur, first British Pterodactylus. Her fossil finds forced scientists to accept extinction as real and challenged the biblical timeline of Earth. *Whisper:* The Earth is old beyond imagination, and it is littered with the bones of creatures that no longer exist. Extinction is not failure — it is the normal fate of species. *Story:* Poor, working-class, and female in early 1800s England — three strikes against being taken seriously in science. She sold fossils from Lyme Regis cliffs to support her family. Male scientists bought her finds, published about them under their own names, and didn't credit her. She couldn't join the Geological Society (women were banned). She was struck by lightning as an infant and survived.
- **Muthulakshmi Reddi** (30 Jul 1886–1968-07-22, Indian (Pudukottai, Tamil Nadu); Underdog, World Changer, Foundational, Heretic): First Indian woman to be admitted to a men's college (Madras Medical College, 1907). First woman legislator in India. First woman to chair a legislative body in India. First female surgeon in India. Founded the Adyar Cancer Institute (1954). Led the abolition of the Devadasi system in Madras Presidency. Fought for women's education, marriage reform, and healthcare access. *Whisper:* Being the first woman in a room full of men who don't want you there isn't just personal courage — it changes the architecture of the room for every woman who comes after. *Story:* Born to a Devadasi community (temple dancers who were often exploited), Reddi fought her way into the most exclusive medical college in Madras — as the only woman. She became the first female surgeon in India. But she didn't stop at medicine — she entered politics, becoming India's first woman legislator, and used her platform to abolish the Devadasi system that had exploited women like those in her own community. She founded the Adyar Cancer Institute in 1954, which today treats over 100,000 cancer patients annually, most of them poor, many for free.

- **Charaka** (c. 300 BCE (estimated)–c. 200 BCE (estimated), Indian; Foundational, World Changer, Ahead of Time): Charaka Samhita — one of the foundational texts of Ayurveda.
