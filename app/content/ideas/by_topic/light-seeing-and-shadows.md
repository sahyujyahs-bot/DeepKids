## Grade concept grid: Gravitational lensing
**Grade 1.** *concept:* Mass bends light, like a glass lens. A big galaxy can “stretch” and “mirror” the picture of a farther galaxy. Philosophical truth: What’s heavy shapes what’s seen. Salient points: Curved paths for light near big things. Debated: Is the bend from matter we can’t see? (Often yes.) Exceptions: Not glass refraction—this is spacetime curvature. In art/life: Fun-house mirrors stretch images. In nature: Heat mirages bend light (different mechanism).
**Grade 2.** *concept:* Gravity’s pull warps space so light takes curved routes; aligned just right, one galaxy can appear as many arcs or rings. Philosophical truth: Perspective depends on the path. Salient points: Einstein ring/arcs, multiple images. Debated: Do small clumps cause extra distortions? Exceptions: Needs near-perfect alignment for dramatic effects. In art/life: Camera lenses distort edges; painters warp perspective for effect. In nature: Raindrops lens sunlight into rainbows (different physics).
**Grade 3.** *concept:* Strong lensing (arcs, multiple images), weak lensing (tiny shape shears, statistical), microlensing (brief brightening by stars/planets). Philosophical truth: Hidden mass reveals itself by how it deforms reality’s image. Salient points: Deflection angle ∝ mass; time delays measure H₀; mass mapping without light. Debated: Substructure vs smooth halos; line-of-sight systematics. Exceptions: Mass-sheet degeneracy; intrinsic alignments. In art/life: Anamorphic drawings; fisheye lenses. In nature: Ice lenses focus sunlight—same idea of “bending,” different cause.
**Grade 4.** *concept:* Cosmic shear across wide fields traces dark matter; cluster lenses expose dark matter maps; microlensing finds exoplanets. Philosophical truth: What can’t be seen can be measured by its consequences. Salient points: Shear 2-pt/3-pt functions, convergence κ maps, cross-correlations with galaxies/CMB. Debated: S₈ tension (lensing vs CMB); baryonic feedback corrections. Exceptions: Photo-z errors, PSF systematics. In art/life: Data-sculpture of lens maps; warped grids. In nature: None exact; analogy is refraction, not curvature.
**Grade 5.** *concept:* Joint lensing with RSD, BAO, clusters, and GWs will test gravity and dark energy; time-delay lenses refine H₀. Philosophical truth: Converging methods beat any single view. Salient points: Multi-probe cosmology, model-independent mass mapping, lensing of standard sirens. Debated: Modified gravity vs ΛCDM + systematics. Exceptions: Mass modeling degeneracies remain; need high-res spectroscopy/imaging. In art/life: Multi-layer compositing mirrors multi-probe inference. In nature: Webs distort prey images—metaphor for path-dependent perception.

## Concept activities: Light, Photon, Speed of Light
- **about-the-concept:** Light is electromagnetic radiation; photons are its energy packets; nothing travels faster than c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Wave–particle duality; constant speed of light in vacuum; energy–frequency relation (E=hf).
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Light is the universe’s ultimate messenger; limits define possibility; speed reveals fundamental order.
- **experiments:** Measure speed of light with a microwave oven and chocolate (standing-wave melt spots); shine a laser over a known distance and time the reflection with a stopwatch app; use a fibre-optic toy to explore photon travel.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Make a pinhole camera to capture light without lenses; use a CD to create rainbow spectra; shine a flashlight through different coloured plastics to see absorption.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Watch sunrise and time the first rays; observe distant lightning and compare light vs. thunder arrival; visit a planetarium to see stellar light paths.
- **dance:** Dance where dancers dart in straight lines like photons; sudden freezes to symbolise light’s finite speed; duet switching from wave-like to particle-like movement.
- **drama:** Skit of photons racing across space to reach Earth; drama of Einstein announcing constant light speed; mime of light bouncing between mirrors.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint rays streaming across space; draw photon as both particle and wave; create a speed-of-light comic strip.
- **music:** Compose music that alternates very fast notes and sudden silences to mimic light’s speed; record metallic percussion to symbolise photons; create a “speed run” rhythm piece.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice trataka (steady candle-gazing) to focus on light; visualise a photon journey with each breath; slow breathing imagining infinite yet finite speed.
- **physical_games:** “Photon tag”: players run only in straight lines; relay where teams pass a “light baton” with no curves; flash-light chase game.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on light’s speed in different media; card-matching game pairing light phenomena to wavelength; board game racing photons across the universe.
- **reading:** Light by David Bodanis (child-friendly sections); NASA’s “Imagine the Universe” photon resources; simple physics books on Einstein’s relativity.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem of a photon’s 8-minute trip from Sun to Earth; story of light leaving a distant galaxy; journal describing moments when light feels slow.
- **more:** Build a DIY fibre-optic lamp; photograph long-exposure light trails; use free apps to measure light intensity.
- **other:** Visit an observatory; watch a laser light show; explore online videos of particle accelerators producing photons.

## Concept activities: Mixing Paints vs Lights

- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Different paths lead to the same perception; presence and absence can both create richness.
- **experiments:** Shine red, green and blue torches on a white wall to show additive mixing; mix paints of primary colours to show subtractive results; overlap coloured cellophane sheets on a flashlight beam.
