## Concept activities: Electric & Magnetic Fields
- **about-the-concept:** Regions around charges or magnets where forces act at a distance.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Coulomb’s law; magnetic field lines; interaction of electric and magnetic fields.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Invisible connections shape reality; forces can act without touch.
- **experiments:** Sprinkle iron filings around a bar magnet to reveal field lines; rub a balloon on hair and attract small paper bits; build a simple electromagnet with a nail, wire and battery.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Use a fridge magnet to move a paperclip without touching; create static sparks with wool sweaters; test which kitchen items magnets attract.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Observe auroras if possible; watch lightning to witness electric fields; visit a science museum electricity exhibit.
- **dance:** Dance of invisible pulls and pushes; partner choreography where one leads without physical contact; wave-like movement representing field lines.
- **drama:** Skit of two charges calling each other without touching; drama of a compass needle reacting to unseen magnet; mime of sparks jumping.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint swirling field lines; draw magnetic poles and loops; create abstract art of electric sparks.
- **music:** Compose electronic music with rising electric hums; create a percussion piece with sudden “magnetic snaps”; layer sounds to mimic charged particles.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice alternate-nostril breathing while imagining an energy field around the body; meditate on subtle connections; slow breathing visualising magnetic loops.
- **physical_games:** “Magnet tag”: players are North or South poles and must attract/repel; static balloon hair race; relay using magnets to move paperclips.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz matching everyday phenomena to electric or magnetic cause; board game building a field map; card game pairing particles to fields.
- **reading:** Electric Universe by David Bodanis (kid-friendly sections); simple books on electricity and magnetism; NASA EM spectrum resources.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem about invisible threads of power; story of a spark travelling through air; journal of hidden forces noticed in daily life.
- **more:** Build a simple electric motor; photograph iron filings patterns; record static crackles with a microphone.
- **other:** Visit a planetarium aurora show; attend a high-voltage demo; interview an electrical engineer.

## Concept activities: Electric Charge

- **principles-within-the-concept:** Coulomb’s law; conservation of charge; static electricity.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Invisible polarity drives connection and separation; opposites create balance.
- **experiments:** Rub a balloon on hair to attract paper bits; use a plastic comb on wool and bend a water stream; build a simple electroscope with foil.

## Concept activities: Conductivity, Resistance, Superconductors, Semiconductors, Nanotech
- **about-the-concept:** How easily materials let charge flow: conductors carry current, resistors oppose it, superconductors have zero resistance, semiconductors control current; nanotech manipulates matter at atomic scale.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Ohm’s law; band theory; Meissner effect; quantum effects at nanoscale.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Flow and control—technology harnesses nature; at tiny scales, matter behaves in surprising ways.
- **experiments:** Build a simple circuit and measure current through copper vs. pencil graphite; cool a superconducting magnet demo video to see levitation; use a solar cell (semiconductor) to power a LED.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Compare how metals vs. plastics conduct using a battery and bulb; observe how pencil leads vary in resistance; explore a transistor radio to see semiconductors in action.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Visit a university lab or science museum with superconducting levitation demo; observe lightning conductors during a storm; see nano coatings on lotus leaves repelling water.
- **dance:** Dance alternating free-flowing (conductive) and resistant movements; group choreography of sudden zero-resistance glide.
- **drama:** Skit of electrons trying different materials; drama of a train levitating on a superconductor; mime of nano robots building structures.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint metallic vs. matte textures; draw levitating trains; create nano-inspired fractal patterns.
- **music:** Compose electronic music with smooth conductive lines and sudden resistive stops; create soundscape of superconducting “silence”.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice breathing visualising free flow of energy; yoga poses with minimal resistance (fluid vinyasa); meditation on hidden quantum worlds.
- **physical_games:** “Conductor tag”: players pass an “electric baton” only through conductive teammates; relay with obstacles representing resistance; game of building nano structures from blocks.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on material conductivity; board game designing a superconductor train line; card-matching materials to conductivity level.
- **reading:** Superconductors for Kids (science outreach PDFs); simple books on semiconductors; kid-friendly nanotech resources.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem about electrons racing without friction; story of nano builders; journal of gadgets using semiconductors.
- **more:** Build a graphite pencil resistor; photograph water beading on lotus leaf; record sounds of electric circuits.
- **other:** Visit a semiconductor fabrication lab (public tour); attend a nanotech exhibition; watch videos of levitating magnets.

## Concept activities: Safety with electricity (no touch wires)
- **about-the-concept:** Understanding hazards of electric current and safe handling of electrical devices.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Current through body can harm; insulation and grounding prevent accidents; circuit breakers protect.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Respect for invisible power; caution is wisdom.
- **experiments:** Demonstrate how a fuse breaks circuit with a thin wire (under supervision); use an LED and battery to show controlled current; show how rubber gloves insulate (without using live wires).
- **activities-around-the-house:** Inspect household plugs for damage; label “do not touch” on live wires; practice switching off main power safely.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Watch linemen working safely on power lines; observe grounding rods at substations; visit a science museum safety exhibit.
- **dance:** Dance with sudden stop-start moves showing danger and caution; freeze-dance when “electric shock” is called.
- **drama:** Skit of a superhero electrician teaching safe habits; drama of a family preventing an electrical accident; mime of touching a live wire and stopping just in time.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint warning signs with bold symbols; draw lightning bolts with safety icons; create posters for electrical safety.
- **music:** Compose a jingle or rap about electrical safety; percussion piece with sudden silence for “cut the power”; create soundscape of crackles and silence.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice grounding yoga poses to feel stable; slow breathing visualising safe inner current; meditate on respect for unseen forces.
- **physical_games:** “Safety tag”: players must freeze when “live wire” is called; relay race switching off pretend breakers; obstacle course avoiding “live wires” (ropes).
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on safety rules; board game where players avoid electrical hazards; card-matching do’s and don’ts.
- **reading:** Electrical Safety for Kids (children’s booklets from power companies); simple physics texts on circuits.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem warning of hidden sparks; story of a child learning to stay safe; journal of household safety checks.
- **more:** Build a model house circuit with fuses; photograph safe wiring practices; create a checklist for home safety.
- **other:** Visit an electricity board training centre; attend a science fair safety demo; watch a video on power-line maintenance.

## SCI Cards: scientists for this topic (founder's card game; strongest underdog stories first)
- **Hedy Lamarr** (1914-11-09–2000-01-19, Austrian-American; Invisible Giant, Ahead of Time, Underdog): Co-invented frequency-hopping spread spectrum technology — originally designed to make radio-guided torpedoes unjammable in WWII. This technology became the foundation for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and GPS. *Whisper:* A signal that keeps changing its frequency is nearly impossible to block or intercept. Hiding in plain sight — by being unpredictable — is a form of security. *Story:* Famous as 'the most beautiful woman in Hollywood,' she was also an inventor. She and composer George Antheil patented frequency-hopping using a mechanism inspired by player piano rolls. The US Navy rejected it during WWII. Decades later, the military adopted it — and it became the basis for modern wireless communication. She received no money and almost no recognition during her lifetime.
- **Rajeshwari Chatterjee** (1922-01-24–2010-09-03, Indian (Karnataka); Underdog, Applied, Invisible Giant, Foundational): Pioneer of microwave engineering in India. Developed microwave components (antennas, waveguides) at IISc Bangalore when India had virtually no microwave technology capability. First woman to earn an engineering degree from IISc. Her work enabled India's radar, satellite communication, and defence electronics capabilities. *Whisper:* The invisible waves that carry your phone calls, TV signals, and radar images — someone had to build the first Indian-made devices to generate, shape, and receive them. *Story:* In an era when women in Indian engineering labs were unheard of, Chatterjee earned her degree from IISc, went to the University of Michigan for her PhD, and returned to build India's microwave engineering capability from scratch. She designed antennas, waveguides, and other components when India couldn't import them. Her students went on to build India's radar systems, satellite communication infrastructure, and defence electronics. She was also a passionate advocate for women in engineering.
- **Michael Faraday** (22 Sep 1791–25 Aug 1867, English; Self-Taught, Underdog, World Changer, Foundational): Electromagnetic induction (moving a magnet near a wire creates electricity) — the principle behind every electric generator and transformer. Discovered benzene. Laws of electrolysis. Invented the Faraday cage. Concept of field lines. *Whisper:* Electricity and magnetism are not separate — they are two faces of one force. Move one, and the other appears. The universe is deeply interconnected. *Story:* Born into poverty, son of a blacksmith. Apprenticed to a bookbinder at 14, where he read every science book he bound. Attended public lectures by Humphry Davy, took meticulous notes, bound them beautifully, and sent them to Davy asking for a job. Got hired as a lab assistant. Had almost no formal mathematics — he thought in pictures, not equations. Maxwell later translated Faraday's intuitive field concepts into the mathematical equations that unified electromagnetism.
- **Nikola Tesla** (10 Jul 1856–1943-01-07, Serbian-American; IP Theft Victim, Underdog, Ahead of Time, World Changer): Alternating Current (AC) power system — the foundation of modern electricity grids. Tesla coil, rotating magnetic field, induction motor, radio (contested with Marconi), wireless energy transmission concepts. *Whisper:* Energy doesn't need wires to travel — it moves in waves and fields. The universe is fundamentally electromagnetic, not mechanical. *Story:* Worked for Edison, who promised him $50,000 if he improved his DC generators. Tesla did. Edison said it was a joke. Tesla quit and dug ditches for a living. He later won the 'War of Currents' against Edison's DC system. Died alone in a hotel room, impoverished, in love with a pigeon. Marconi got the Nobel Prize for radio using Tesla's patents — later overturned by the Supreme Court.
- **Alfred Wegener** (1 Nov 1880–1930-11-01, German; Heretic, Ahead of Time, World Changer, Foundational): Continental Drift — proposed that all continents were once a single supercontinent (Pangaea) that broke apart and drifted to their current positions. Presented matching fossils, rock formations, and coastlines as evidence. Ridiculed for 50 years until plate tectonics proved him right. *Whisper:* The ground beneath your feet is not fixed — continents move. The Earth's surface is a jigsaw puzzle, and it was once one piece. Stillness is an illusion of timescale. *Story:* A meteorologist by training — not a geologist — which gave the establishment an excuse to dismiss him. He noticed that Africa and South America fit together like puzzle pieces and found matching fossils on both continents. Geologists mocked him: 'If continents moved, what force could possibly push them?' He couldn't answer that (the mechanism — convection in the mantle — wasn't discovered until later). He died on a Greenland expedition in 1930, his theory in ruins. Plate tectonics vindicated him in the 1960s.
