## RNA's story of life (founder's picture book, narrated by RNA)
### The First Fungi
Quickly following the red algae, the Fungi evolved from eukaryotes with only mitochondria.

The parent eukaryote with mitochondria had innovated food & primitive internal digestion. But its
first child, the fungi, did not opt for that route. Instead, they chose the more historic route of
external digestion by secreting enzymes & breakdown molecules outside the body & absorbing
nutrients.
But why? Because their aspirations were greater than their parents. Internal digestion means a
defined body shape that can house the gut. And digestion would have to happen per meal.

Instead, they developed a whole new way to live & grow - A fungi will start extending on one
side like a tip, a tail, only adding some cell wall. And slowly the tip grows and the fungo mother
will divide her nucleus and give one to the hyphae. Now the hyphae has its own DNA, but
remains forever attached to the mother. This continues. New hyphaes get added like chains,
each having its own nucleus, but all hyphaes are still attached to the mother. They could cover
hundreds of acres this way.

They also highways and transporter proteins to carry the nutrient absorbed in the hypahes to
other older interior cells of fungi.
For internal communication between the cells, they used the same well established chemical
pathways, ion fluctuations and electrical impulses. The same fundamentals that happen within
neurons - tunnels that animals built for internal communication.

When the resources get scarce, the chemical signals activate the genes for reproduction & the
hyphaes pause focussing on growth and focus on reproduction , making spores - tiny cells
packed with their dna & eject into the world, for them to find homes elsewhere.

They, like the aerobic bacteria, and eukaryotes with mitochondria, needed Oxygen to produce
energy and continued consuming them from around them releasing CO2

Fungi - the scavengers with decentralized intelligence , covering acres, responding subjectively
to stimuli across distances. Not only did they cover the soils under the ocean, they also broke
down the quickly gathering bio waste & made more food available for other organisms that
came before & as you will see in future chapters, made life possible on land too.

### The First Animal - Sponge
Soon after the neither plant nor an animal, fungi, came the first animals - sponges.
They were multi-cellular & visible to the eye.
 Intercellular communication that coordinates the whole sponge - cells use chemical signals and
calcium wave propagation to respond collectively
can perform simple reflexes like contracting the body when irritated.
They innovated a new respiratory system by pumping water in through its pores, bathing every
cell with water, so the cells could absorb the free oxygen floating in the water.

The sponges like their eukaryote parents could engulf prey, but didn’t have the internal digestion
engine , instead they used a filtration system, where they collected all the food molecules ,
convert them to nutrients & transport the nutrients to all the cells.

Like all their eukaryotic ancestors, they could reproduce asexually - by cell division, and
sexually - by producing sperm and eggs. Fertilization happened inside the mother - 2nd species
to do so, after the red algaes.
They still did not have specific gender & were hermaphrodites, holding genes to both producing
eggs and sperms, and activated either gene at different times based on resources available,
energy , competition etc

The whole sponge body can also slowly contract by coordinating special contractile cells.
Though extremely slow and limited, this is the first appearance of multicellular body contractions
without true muscle tissue.

They also evolved silica or calcium carbonate spikes and collagen to retain its shape & rigidity.
They had basic immune systems, ways to recognize what was self and foreign, and could reject
cells of another sponge if they entered their system - the biological “sense of self”.

They also had sensors that could detect light & dark - sponges contract in bright light, sensors
that could detect toxins, changes in chemicals, flow rate and much more - all these inherited
from its parent & grandparent species.

They are the first animal, but quite distinct from the rest of the animals to come . One of a kind.

## Evolution picture book: introduction
"The History of Evolution of Life" Coloring Book

This coloring book is a carefully researched journey into the fascinating story of life on Earth, based on the best scientific knowledge we have today.
However, there are a few important points to keep in mind as we explore:

1. Evolution is an ongoing discovery.
Evolution is not an exact science; it is a well-supported framework based on evidence we’ve uncovered so far. Our understanding may change as new discoveries are made, and some current beliefs could be revised in the future.

2. Some mysteries of evolution remain unsolved. Even solved mysteries are not set in stone.
No step in evolution is fully understood, and theycontinue to remain subjects of debate, even among scientists. For instance, it's widely believed that life began with RNA molecules, precursors to DNA, but this is not universally agreed upon, and definitive proof is still elusive. Did living cells ccome first or the undead virus come first? debated. We believe Sponges were the first ever animal. But there is also a group that debates comb jellies came first. We don't know definitive answers to any question in evolution yet. About every step of evolvution of life that we have pieced together so far are still heavily debated in the scientific community. So consume the information with an open mind, and attempt to delve further into the questions by reading different perspectives.

3. Evolution is not a straight line.
Contrary to popular belief, evolution is not a simple progression from unicellular organisms to mammals and humans. It is a branching, ever-changing tree where every species, including the earliest unicellular prokaryotes, continues to evolve in its own unique way. We are finding new evidences everyday, that refute earlier understanding of the different aspects of evolution.

With these points in mind, let’s dive into the incredible history of life and explore the wonders of evolution together!

- **Mary Saturna Ross** (1908-08-09–2008-04-29, American (Cherokee Nation); Underdog, Invisible Giant, Applied): First known Native American female engineer. Worked at Lockheed's classified Skunk Works division on the design of the Agena rocket stage (used in the Gemini program and for satellite launches). Contributed to preliminary design concepts for interplanetary space travel, including flyby missions to Mars and Venus. *Whisper:* The rockets that carried America to space were partly designed by a Cherokee woman working in the most secret engineering lab in the country — and nobody knew for decades. *Story:* Ross was a great-great-granddaughter of Chief John Ross of the Cherokee Nation. She studied mathematics and engineering, then joined Lockheed during WWII. She was selected for the elite Skunk Works team — the only woman among 40 engineers. Her work was classified for decades. She designed ballistic missile systems and early concepts for interplanetary flight. After retiring, she spent decades advocating for Native American and female participation in engineering. Most of her specific contributions remain classified. She lived to be 99.
- **Annie Jump Cannon** (11 Dec 1863–1941-04-13, American; World Changer, Foundational, Underdog, Invisible Giant): Created the stellar classification system still used today — O, B, A, F, G, K, M (Oh Be A Fine Girl Kiss Me). Classified over 350,000 stars by hand — more than any other person in history. Her system organizes stars by temperature and spectral type and remains the foundation of all stellar astronomy. *Whisper:* Stars are not all the same — they come in types, from blue-hot giants to red-cool dwarfs. One woman sorted 350,000 of them by hand and created the alphabet astronomers still use. *Story:* Nearly deaf from childhood scarlet fever, Cannon was one of the 'Harvard Computers' — women hired to do tedious astronomical calculations for low pay. She could classify a star's spectrum in just 3 seconds by glancing at its photographic plate. Over her career, she classified 350,000 stars — a record that will never be broken because machines now do the work. Her OBAFGKM classification, initially dismissed as mere 'cataloging,' became the Periodic Table of stars. Every astronomy student memorizes her sequence.
- **Jane Goodall** (1934-04-03–Living, English; World Changer, Heretic, Foundational): Proved that chimpanzees make and use tools — overturning the definition of humans as the only tool-using species. Discovered that chimps hunt, eat meat, have complex social hierarchies, wage wars, show empathy, and mourn their dead. Her 65+ years of continuous research at Gombe is the longest-running animal study in history. *Whisper:* The line between humans and animals is thinner than we thought. Chimpanzees make tools, grieve, wage war, and show compassion. We are not as special as we believed — and that's not diminishing, it's connecting. *Story:* Sent to Tanzania in 1960 by Louis Leakey with no degree and no formal training — just a notebook and binoculars. Within months she observed chimps making and using tools (stripping leaves from twigs to fish for termites). When she told Leakey, he said: 'Now we must redefine tool, redefine man, or accept chimpanzees as human.' The scientific establishment attacked her for naming her subjects instead of numbering them and for attributing emotions to animals. She was right, they were wrong. Over 65 years, her Gombe research revealed a mirror — showing us that much of what we call 'human' exists in our closest relatives.
- **Charles Darwin** (12 Feb 1809–19 Apr 1882, English; World Changer, Heretic, Foundational, Controversial): Theory of Evolution by Natural Selection — species evolve over time through variation, inheritance, and differential survival. Published 'On the Origin of Species' (1859). Also: theory of coral reef formation, extensive work on barnacles and earthworms. *Whisper:* You are not separate from nature — you are nature. Every living thing on Earth is your relative. Complexity doesn't require a designer; it emerges from simple rules repeated over deep time. *Story:* Studied to be a clergyman before joining the Beagle voyage at 22. Spent 20 years sitting on his theory, terrified of the backlash. Only published when Alfred Russel Wallace independently arrived at the same idea. Was mocked, attacked by the Church, and caricatured as an ape. Suffered chronic illness for decades (possibly Chagas disease from a bug bite in South America).
- **Tholkappiyar** (c. 300 BCE (estimated)–Unknown, Indian (Tamil Nadu); Foundational, Ahead of Time, Invisible Giant, World Changer): Authored Tolkāppiyam — one of the oldest extant works on linguistics and grammar in any language. Systematically classified Tamil phonetics, morphology, syntax, and poetics. Developed a complete theory of semantics and pragmatics. Classified human emotions into primary and secondary categories — prefiguring modern psychology's emotion classification by millennia. *Whisper:* Language has structure. It follows rules as precise as mathematics — and the first person to systematically prove this did it not for Greek or Latin, but for Tamil, over 2,000 years ago. *Story:* In the Sangam age, when Tamil literature was flourishing in southern India, Tholkappiyar wrote the first known systematic grammar of any Dravidian language. But Tolkāppiyam is far more than a grammar — it classifies human emotional states (meypāṭu), describes the relationship between land, climate, and human behavior (tiṇai), and develops a complete theory of meaning in communication. This is proto-linguistics, proto-psychology, and proto-ecology in a single text. Western linguistics credits Pāṇini (Sanskrit) as the first grammarian; Tholkappiyar's work in a completely different language tradition is equally systematic and arguably broader in scope.
- **Ibn Sina (Avicenna)** (c. 980 CE–June 1037 CE, Persian (Afshana, modern Uzbekistan); World Changer, Foundational, Ahead of Time, Invisible Giant): Canon of Medicine (Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb) — a five-volume medical encyclopedia that classified 760 drugs, described contagious disease transmission, quarantine principles, clinical trials, and psychological disorders. It was the standard medical textbook in European universities for 600 years (until the 1700s). Also contributed to philosophy, physics, and geology. *Whisper:* Diseases spread through invisible particles in water and air — and can be stopped by isolating the sick. A Persian physician understood germ theory conceptually 800 years before Pasteur formalized it. *Story:* A child prodigy who had memorized the Quran by age 10 and surpassed his teachers by 14. He wrote 450 works on nearly every subject. His Canon of Medicine systematized all known medical knowledge and added his own observations: he described meningitis, recognized that tuberculosis was contagious, understood that diseases could spread through water contamination, and proposed quarantine periods of 40 days (the origin of the word 'quarantine' from the Italian 'quarantina'). European medical schools used his textbook for six centuries — sometimes not crediting the author.
- **Charaka** (c. 300 BCE (estimated)–c. 200 BCE (estimated), Indian; Foundational, World Changer, Ahead of Time): Charaka Samhita — one of the foundational texts of Ayurveda. Described 341 plant-based medicines, 177 animal-derived medicines, 64 mineral preparations. Systematic diagnosis based on pulse, observation, and questioning. Described digestion, metabolism, immunity, and genetics conceptually. Proposed that the body is a field and diseases have identifiable, treatable causes — NOT divine punishment. *Whisper:* Disease has causes that can be understood, identified, and treated. The body is not a mystery punished by gods — it is a system that can be studied. Prevention matters more than cure. *Story:* Charaka compiled and systematized medical knowledge from centuries of Indian practice. His Samhita describes concepts we now recognize: immunity (vyadhikshamatva), metabolic constitution types (prakriti), the gut as central to health, and the idea that a doctor should treat the patient, not just the disease. He insisted that physicians take an oath of ethical practice — centuries before the Hippocratic Oath. His pharmacopoeia described herbal medicines that modern pharmacology is now validating (turmeric/curcumin, ashwagandha, neem).
