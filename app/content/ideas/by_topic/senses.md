## Grade concept grid: Reality
**Grade 1.** *concept:* What’s real is what we see, touch, hear. Dreams feel real until we wake. *truth:* Reality is what our senses agree on—for now. *salient:* Real vs imaginary. *debated:* Are dreams part of reality? *exceptions:* Illusions, magic tricks. *art_life:* Puppet shows, plays blur pretend and real. *nature:* Mirrors, reflections, shadows.
**Grade 2.** *concept:* Reality is made of things and happenings. Some are visible, others hidden (like air). *truth:* What we don’t see can still exist. *salient:* Matter, energy, invisible forces. *debated:* Is everything we see the full truth? *exceptions:* Mirage, echo. *art_life:* Animation makes drawings come alive. *nature:* Camouflage and mimicry trick eyes.
**Grade 3.** *concept:* Reality has layers—physical, mental, digital. *truth:* Perception constructs experience. *salient:* Relativity of observation; simulation hypothesis. *debated:* Is there one objective reality or many? *exceptions:* Quantum superposition—multiple states coexist. *art_life:* Virtual worlds, theatre, illusion art. *nature:* Reflection in water, mirages, camouflage.
**Grade 4.** *concept:* Physical laws describe patterns of reality. Still, the observer influences outcomes. *truth:* Reality might depend on consciousness. *salient:* Quantum measurement, relativity of frames. *debated:* Is matter solid or probability cloud? *exceptions:* Double-slit experiment’s paradox. *art_life:* Cinema, perspective drawing—frames define what’s “real.” *nature:* Bioluminescence, perception-based signals in animals.
**Grade 5.** *concept:* Reality could be a participatory construct—an emergent web of relationships. *truth:* “To be” means “to relate.” *salient:* Relational quantum mechanics, subjective realism. *debated:* Is there an ultimate reality beyond perception? *exceptions:* Quantum decoherence limits shared reality. *art_life:* Meta-theatre, immersive experiences, simulation art. *nature:* Ecosystems—reality as interdependence.

## Concept activities: Safety with electricity (no touch wires)
- **about-the-concept:** Understanding hazards of electric current and safe handling of electrical devices.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Current through body can harm; insulation and grounding prevent accidents; circuit breakers protect.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Respect for invisible power; caution is wisdom.
- **experiments:** Demonstrate how a fuse breaks circuit with a thin wire (under supervision); use an LED and battery to show controlled current; show how rubber gloves insulate (without using live wires).
- **activities-around-the-house:** Inspect household plugs for damage; label “do not touch” on live wires; practice switching off main power safely.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Watch linemen working safely on power lines; observe grounding rods at substations; visit a science museum safety exhibit.
- **dance:** Dance with sudden stop-start moves showing danger and caution; freeze-dance when “electric shock” is called.
- **drama:** Skit of a superhero electrician teaching safe habits; drama of a family preventing an electrical accident; mime of touching a live wire and stopping just in time.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint warning signs with bold symbols; draw lightning bolts with safety icons; create posters for electrical safety.
- **music:** Compose a jingle or rap about electrical safety; percussion piece with sudden silence for “cut the power”; create soundscape of crackles and silence.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice grounding yoga poses to feel stable; slow breathing visualising safe inner current; meditate on respect for unseen forces.
- **physical_games:** “Safety tag”: players must freeze when “live wire” is called; relay race switching off pretend breakers; obstacle course avoiding “live wires” (ropes).
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on safety rules; board game where players avoid electrical hazards; card-matching do’s and don’ts.
- **reading:** Electrical Safety for Kids (children’s booklets from power companies); simple physics texts on circuits.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem warning of hidden sparks; story of a child learning to stay safe; journal of household safety checks.
- **more:** Build a model house circuit with fuses; photograph safe wiring practices; create a checklist for home safety.
- **other:** Visit an electricity board training centre; attend a science fair safety demo; watch a video on power-line maintenance.

## SCI Cards: scientists for this topic (founder's card game; strongest underdog stories first)
- **Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)** (c. 965–c. 1040, Arab (Basra, modern Iraq); World Changer, Foundational, Ahead of Time): Book of Optics — proved that vision works by light entering the eye (not beams leaving it, as Greeks believed). Established the scientific method: hypothesis → experiment → conclusion. First to explain the camera obscura. Pin-hole camera. *Whisper:* Light comes TO the eye, not FROM it. Truth is found by testing ideas against reality, not by following authority. The scientific method is humanity's most reliable way of knowing. *Story:* Claimed he could regulate the Nile's flooding. When the Caliph called his bluff, he realized it was impossible and faked madness to avoid execution. Under house arrest for ten years, he wrote his masterpiece — the Book of Optics — which influenced Roger Bacon, Kepler, Descartes, and Newton. He essentially invented the scientific method 600 years before Europe's Scientific Revolution.
- **Obaid Siddiqi** (1932-01-07–2013-07-26, Indian (Basti, Uttar Pradesh); Foundational, World Changer, Applied): Pioneered the use of Drosophila (fruit flies) to study the genetics of sensory perception — taste, smell, and neurological function. Discovered temperature-sensitive paralytic mutants in flies, opening new approaches to studying neural development. Founded TIFR's National Centre for Biological Sciences (NCBS), which became India's premier biology research institute. *Whisper:* A fruit fly can teach you how brains work. The same genes that control a fly's ability to taste sugar also operate in human neurobiology — because evolution conserved these molecular tools across hundreds of millions of years. *Story:* Siddiqi took a radical approach: use the simple fruit fly to understand the genetics of how organisms sense the world. He found mutant flies that couldn't taste, couldn't smell, or became paralyzed at high temperatures — each mutation revealing a gene essential for neural function. His broader contribution was institutional: he built NCBS from nothing into one of India's best research centers, creating a culture of excellence in Indian biology that persists today.
- **Tycho Brahe** (14 Dec 1546–24 Oct 1601, Danish; Foundational, Controversial): Most accurate naked-eye astronomical observations in history. His precision planetary data (especially of Mars) became the foundation Kepler used to derive his laws of motion. *Whisper:* Sometimes the greatest contribution isn't the answer — it's gathering the evidence so precisely that the right answer becomes inevitable for someone else. *Story:* A Danish nobleman who lost part of his nose in a duel and wore a metal prosthetic (possibly Saturn or brass). He built a castle-observatory on an island, kept a pet moose (which reportedly died falling down stairs after drinking beer), and employed a psychic dwarf named Jepp. He died mysteriously after a banquet — modern theories range from mercury poisoning to a burst bladder.
