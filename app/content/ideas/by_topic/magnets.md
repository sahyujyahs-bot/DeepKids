## Concept activities: Electromagnetic
- **about-the-concept:** The force between charged particles; responsible for light, magnetism, electricity and chemistry.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Coulomb’s law; magnetic fields; electromagnetic waves.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Attraction and repulsion create both structure and communication; invisible currents power life.
- **experiments:** Build a simple circuit with battery and bulb; create a magnet from a nail and wire; show static electricity by rubbing a balloon on hair.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Test conductivity of household items; use magnets to pick up paperclips; experiment with a simple compass and bar magnet.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Visit a power station or science museum electricity exhibit; watch lightning in a storm; observe northern lights (if possible).
- **dance:** Dance with sparks of quick movement and flowing current gestures; paired improvisation where one attracts and one repels.
- **drama:** Skit of electrons and protons debating attraction; drama of lightning striking; mime of invisible magnetic fields.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint electric arcs; draw field lines around magnets; create collage of lightning photos.
- **music:** Compose electronic music with sudden surges; create a piece alternating attraction (harmony) and repulsion (dissonance); drum rhythm imitating electric pulses.
- **yoga-breathing:** Practice alternate-nostril breathing imagining current flow; breath work visualising magnetic field around the body; meditation on inner electricity.
- **physical_games:** “Magnet tag”: players carry magnets and attract or repel; relay race passing electric “charge baton”; static balloon hair game.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Board game building electric circuits; trivia quiz on electromagnetism pioneers; card-matching phenomena to magnetic or electric causes.
- **reading:** Electric Universe by David Bodanis (child-friendly excerpts); simple books on electricity and magnetism; NASA EM spectrum resources.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem about invisible currents; story of a lightning bolt’s journey; journal of household uses of electromagnetism.
- **more:** Build a DIY electromagnet; photograph lightning or auroras; watch high-voltage demonstrations online.
- **other:** Attend a science centre electricity show; visit a radio observatory; interview an electrical engineer.

## Concept activities: Electromagnetic Induction

- **principles-within-the-concept:** Faraday’s law; Lenz’s law; electric current induced by moving magnets.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** Change creates energy; motion and magnetism interweave to light the world.
- **experiments:** Move a magnet through a coil to light a small LED; swing a magnet near a compass and observe needle deflection; build a simple hand-crank generator.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Slide a magnet in and out of a coil connected to a multimeter; watch induction stove heating; observe doorbell mechanism.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Visit a hydroelectric plant; watch wind turbines spin generators; observe power lines carrying generated current.
- **dance:** Dance of magnets and coils chasing each other; group choreography of circular motion producing spark.
- **drama:** Skit of Faraday discovering induction; drama of magnet and coil creating electricity; mime of changing fields birthing current.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint spirals of coil and magnet; draw arrows of induced current; create art of lightning-like induction patterns.

- **yoga-breathing:** Practice circular breathing visualising currents induced by change; meditate on the power of transformation; yoga flows of coiling and uncoiling.
- **physical_games:** “Generator tag”: players spin arms to “generate” electricity; relay with magnet-and-coil tasks; hand-crank race to light small bulbs.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on Faraday’s law; board game building power grids; card-matching induction scenarios.
- **reading:** Faraday, Maxwell and the Electromagnetic Field (kid-friendly excerpts); simple electricity & magnetism books; science museum resources.
- **writing-observations-stories-poems-answers:** Poem about moving magnets awakening light; story of Faraday’s discovery; journal of moments when motion sparked ideas.

## Concept activities: Electromagnetic waves & spectrum
- **about-the-concept:** Light, radio, microwaves, X-rays, etc.—all are electromagnetic waves differing in wavelength and frequency.
- **principles-within-the-concept:** Maxwell’s equations; wave–particle duality; spectrum from radio to gamma.
- **philosophy-underlying-the-concepts:** All forms of light are one phenomenon; diversity is unity expressed in different frequencies.
- **experiments:** Split sunlight with a prism to see visible spectrum; build a simple radio crystal set; observe microwaves warming food.
- **activities-around-the-house:** Use a remote control to detect infrared with a phone camera; compare LED bulbs’ colours; listen to AM/FM radio for different frequencies.
- **activities-outside-the-house-in-nature:** Watch stars and auroras; visit a planetarium or radio telescope; observe rainbow after rain.
- **dance:** Dance with movements representing long to short wavelengths; group choreography from slow radio waves to quick gamma jumps.
- **drama:** Skit of the EM spectrum family reunion; drama of photons travelling across space; mime of wave and particle forms.
- **painting-drawing:** Paint a rainbow spectrum; draw abstract EM wave patterns; create collage of satellite and light images.

- **yoga-breathing:** Practice breathing imagining waves of energy through body; meditate on unity of all light.
- **physical_games:** “Spectrum tag”: zones of playground represent parts of spectrum; players move at different speeds; relay sending “signal” across bands.
- **games-of-any-kind:** Quiz on EM spectrum uses; board game collecting different frequencies; card-matching everyday tech to spectrum region.

## SCI Cards: scientists for this topic (founder's card game; strongest underdog stories first)
- **Michael Faraday** (22 Sep 1791–25 Aug 1867, English; Self-Taught, Underdog, World Changer, Foundational): Electromagnetic induction (moving a magnet near a wire creates electricity) — the principle behind every electric generator and transformer. Discovered benzene. Laws of electrolysis. Invented the Faraday cage. Concept of field lines. *Whisper:* Electricity and magnetism are not separate — they are two faces of one force. Move one, and the other appears. The universe is deeply interconnected. *Story:* Born into poverty, son of a blacksmith. Apprenticed to a bookbinder at 14, where he read every science book he bound. Attended public lectures by Humphry Davy, took meticulous notes, bound them beautifully, and sent them to Davy asking for a job. Got hired as a lab assistant. Had almost no formal mathematics — he thought in pictures, not equations. Maxwell later translated Faraday's intuitive field concepts into the mathematical equations that unified electromagnetism.
- **Nikola Tesla** (10 Jul 1856–1943-01-07, Serbian-American; IP Theft Victim, Underdog, Ahead of Time, World Changer): Alternating Current (AC) power system — the foundation of modern electricity grids. Tesla coil, rotating magnetic field, induction motor, radio (contested with Marconi), wireless energy transmission concepts. *Whisper:* Energy doesn't need wires to travel — it moves in waves and fields. The universe is fundamentally electromagnetic, not mechanical. *Story:* Worked for Edison, who promised him $50,000 if he improved his DC generators. Tesla did. Edison said it was a joke. Tesla quit and dug ditches for a living. He later won the 'War of Currents' against Edison's DC system. Died alone in a hotel room, impoverished, in love with a pigeon. Marconi got the Nobel Prize for radio using Tesla's patents — later overturned by the Supreme Court.
- **Abdus Salam** (1926-01-29–1996-11-21, Pakistani; Underdog, World Changer, Foundational, Controversial): Electroweak Unification — showed that electromagnetism and the weak nuclear force are two aspects of a single force, the electroweak force. This was a massive step toward a unified theory of everything. Co-developed independently with Sheldon Glashow and Steven Weinberg. *Whisper:* Forces that seem completely different — light and radioactive decay — are actually one force wearing two masks. The universe is simpler than it appears, and unification is its deepest theme. *Story:* Abdus Salam was Ahmadi Muslim — a minority sect declared 'non-Muslim' by Pakistan's government in 1974. When he won the Nobel Prize, Pakistan barely acknowledged it. His gravestone originally read 'First Muslim Nobel Laureate' — the government ordered 'Muslim' to be removed. He spent his life trying to build science infrastructure in the developing world, founding ICTP in Trieste, which has trained thousands of scientists from poor countries. A prophet in every land except his own.
