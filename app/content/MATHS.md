# DeepKids maths: the philosophy, the threads every unit carries, and how a unit is built

Read this with `PHILOSOPHY.md`, `WRITING.md`, `TRACKS.md` and `units/SPEC_UNIT.md`. The curriculum map is `curriculum/math.json` (25 topics, 214 planned subtopic-level units, Levels 1 to 5 = Classes 1 to 5). Maths units use the same unit file format as science, so the app opens them the same way.

## What the founder wants maths to be (her words, kept close)
1. **Maths is subjective too.** It is not objective, as most people believe. Like any human language, it is an abstraction that hides subjectivity inside it. What we count, which rules we choose to start from, which things we call "the same", how we write numbers: people chose these. Gödel's theorems belong here, and so do chaos and fractals.
2. **Geometry, algebra and number are deeply linked to the universe and to everyday life.** This must come across in every unit, not as a closing remark.
3. **Maths is in every life activity:** sports, stitching, cooking, dance forms and more.
4. **Humans are not the only ones doing maths.** Every other being does. Spiders build webs, ants and termites build homes, plants arrange their leaves, fish and birds make patterns to attract a mate. Maths is in everyone already.

## How each thread shows up in a unit (every unit carries all four)
- **Roots card (subjective, abstract, universal).** Written as PHILOSOPHY.md says: our stance is stated plainly as ours, the science or mathematics is stated as found, and one honest other view follows. Ask the child what counts, what we agreed to call "one", "equal" or "the same", and what would change if people had chosen differently (other number systems, other bases, the Indian and the international way of grouping big numbers, zero's long journey). At Levels 4 and 5 add the deeper ideas in plain words: some questions in a system cannot be settled inside the system (Gödel's incompleteness, told simply and honestly: in any set of rules strong enough for arithmetic there are true statements the rules cannot prove; this does not by itself prove that maths is subjective, so say it as a pointer, not as proof), chaos (tiny changes in the start, large changes in the end), fractals (the same pattern again and again at smaller sizes), different geometries on a flat page and on a ball. Never bend the mathematics to fit the stance (see "Never bend the facts" in PHILOSOPHY.md).
- **"Maths in life" part (people).** At least one real activity from daily life or a craft, played, sewn, cooked, danced or measured: sports (cricket angles and speed, kabaddi counting, kho-kho patterns), stitching and weaving (symmetry, tessellation, counting threads), kolam and rangoli (symmetry, grids), cooking (fractions, ratios, measuring), dance (rhythm, tala cycles, symmetry in formations), music, markets and money, travel and time. It is a real task the family does, not a word problem dressed up.
- **"Everyone does maths" part (other beings).** At least one living example, accurate and specific: spiders and web geometry, bees and hexagons, termites and mound shapes, plants and leaf and seed spirals, shells and spirals, birds and flight paths, fish and patterns made to attract a mate. Verify each claim with a real source before it goes in. State the claim as exactly as the evidence allows (for example, pufferfish males make circular ridged patterns in the sand; do not call it fractal unless a source says so; an ant colony's mound is not "a house the height of a person" unless the species and the source say so). Mark honest uncertainty `idea` or `debated`.
- **Universe and everyday life.** Geometry and algebra are shown doing work: shape and strength in a bridge or a roof, the number pattern in a leaf, the equation that says how a thing moves or grows, the sizes of the world from a grain of sand to the galaxy.

## How a maths unit is built (the same file format as a science unit, used differently)
Every unit has all of the usual elements (`SPEC_UNIT.md`). In maths they work like this.
1. **Opening puzzle** (`summary`, `intro`): one real question the unit answers.
2. **Discover it** (an `experiment` or `activity` marked as the spine investigation, 20 minutes or more, result unknown to the child beforehand): the child finds the pattern or rule with household things (beans, steel cups, string, paper, a dice, a ruler) before any rule is stated. Predict first, with a reason.
3. **Name it** (`explanations`, `terms`): a short, plain explanation with worked examples and a picture model (number line, bar model, array, grid, fraction strip). Each new word defined once.
4. **Practise it** (`worksheets`, `games`, `quiz`): graded sets of short problems, easy to hard, with answers and a reason for each answer; quick mental-maths routines; games played with a dice, cards or a board that build fluency. In the app the checkable problems mark themselves, no AI needed.
5. **Use it** (`maths` problems, `applications`): problems from Indian daily life (money in the market, time on a train, a recipe) that grow out of what the child did in this unit.
6. **Think about it** (`big_questions`, `challenging`, `challenges`): "convince me", "spot the mistake", open puzzles, and the question of whether this is true everywhere or only because we agreed so.
7. **Maths in life** and **Everyone does maths** (see above): placed as `activities` or `projects` and as `spotted`.
8. **Roots** and **How we found out** (`roots`, `how_we_found_out`): where the idea came from and who found it, with Indian mathematicians credited where the evidence supports it, and women and underdogs the usual story leaves out.
9. **Check** (`quiz`, `outcomes`): every outcome has quiz items; a wrong answer adds the unit to the refresher list.
Sessions are shorter than in science: about 20 to 30 minutes, with a daily short practice. The `plan.spine` is about two sessions.

## Rules that do not bend
- Plain words, correct maths, no flowery language (WRITING.md). Every sentence must be mathematically true at the level taught; where the idea is simplified, say it is simplified.
- Real people, dates and findings only; verify every fact; mark uncertainty honestly.
- Without AI first: practice, answer checking and hints work on the phone for every family.
- 60 percent South Indian examples; the Indian and the international number systems both shown where numbers get big.
- Pictures: public domain, CC0 or CC BY with credit, or our own drawn pictures. Never copy non-commercial or share-alike text.
