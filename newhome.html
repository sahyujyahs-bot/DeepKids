The problem

Everyone Agrees.
Everyone Is Wrong.

The textbook took it from an older textbook. The worksheet took it from the textbook. The top search result took it from the worksheet, the video took it from the search result, and the app took it from the video. One person simplified something until it broke, thirty years ago, and nobody since has gone back to check.

So a child asks a real question and gets handed the same sentence five times, in five fonts, and mistakes the repetition for proof. Agreement isn't evidence. It's just copying.

What's actually happening

Gravity is dragging you towards the centre of the Earth. What holds you up is the ground shoving back — the Normal Force. You are standing in a tug of war, and the copied sentence deleted one side of it.

What it costs to get it right

You Have To Put The
Easy 99% Down

There's no shortcut past this, which is exactly why so few products bother. Every DeepKids box goes through all five before it gets printed.

Step one

Set aside everything that's easy to find

The first page of results, the summaries, the explainer videos, the other kids' books on the same subject. All of it is downstream of the same few sources. Start by refusing to use any of it.

Step two

Read widely, especially people who disagree

Many books, from many decades, by people who argue with each other. Where two credible sources contradict each other, that contradiction is the interesting part — not something to smooth over.

Step three

Go to the papers, not the write-up of the papers

The journalism about a finding is not the finding. We read the research itself, including the bit at the end where the authors list everything their own study can't tell you.

Step four

Think it through from first principles, alone

Then put every source down and reason it out from scratch. If the accepted explanation and our own reasoning don't meet, one of them is broken, and we don't get to print until we know which.

Step five

Challenge what's paraded — including the "facts"

Some of the most confidently repeated ideas are the softest. Where the science is genuinely unsettled, we say so in the box, because "nobody knows yet" is a real answer and children deserve to hear it.
